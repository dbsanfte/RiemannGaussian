`ArithmeticOperatorCrtBoundaryAudit` now makes one distinct-column failure

```
sum_(1<=n<=X) divisorObservation(f,n) divisorObservation(g,n+h)
  = sum_(d,e<=X+h) f(d) g(e) incidenceCount(X,h,d,e).
```

Every nonzero incidence has `gcd(d,e)|h`; this does not pay its physical
cutoff boundary. At `X=4*2^J`, consider primes in the disjoint bands
`(X/2,3X/4]` and `(3X/4,X]`. Both columns are large odd primes, so their
actual successor incidence is **exactly zero**; their coprime CRT mean

supplies enough logarithmic mass in each band to prove that the full
termwise completion debit, **including every prime power**, satisfies

```
fullBoundaryDebit(X,y) >= X/64
```

eventually, for every fixed height. Its normalization by every dyadic
`X^beta`, `beta<1`, diverges. This is an actual-prime, cofinal obstruction
to **absolute CRT-boundary payment**, not a bound or a source computation
for the joined signed correlation. It rules out completing the periods
and then spending a rowwise norm allowance. The whole unmasked signed
boundary and the prescribed source-sensitive packet remain unestimated;
the distinct-column branch is not closed by this result alone.

## Literature strength checks

Kummer's carry identity is classical; no novelty is claimed for it. See
[Sury's treatment](https://www.isibang.ac.in/~sury/kummerlegendre.pdf).
The GCD matrix positivity literature distinguishes positivity from the
stronger total-nonnegativity restrictions on ordered prime-exponent vectors;
see [Guillot–Wu](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.01947).

For growing Ramanujan expansions, convergence is real mathematical input,
not a consequence of a finite Fourier identity. Laporta's prime-correlation
application requires an explicit Delange convergence premise, and describes
its strong cancellation conclusion as equivalent to a Hardy–Littlewood
prime-pair conjecture. It is not an unconditional prime-cancellation theorem
to import into this campaign. See
[the paper, Theorem 1 and the discussion following it](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01581).
This does not prove that every growing-modulus approach has that strength.


The nonlinear LCM-cutoff square is the classical Selberg-sieve algebra,
not a new arithmetic inequality. See Heath-Brown's
[lectures, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0209360).
Its remainder terms are separate input; the square identity does not pay
a height-twisted prime discrepancy. The same lectures explicitly delimit
the parity obstruction to the information used and discuss additional
input, so no blanket impossibility of sieve methods is asserted here.

The stronger modular benchmark
[Shao–Teräväinen, Theorems 1.3–1.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.05954)
retains nilsequence/polynomial twists and growing moduli, but its stated
errors are `x/(log x)^A` with fixed-complexity and W-trick hypotheses.
Even granting a bound of that rate for this campaign's required weight
would not by itself beat `x^beta`, `beta<1`, at all heights. This is a
rate comparison, not a claim that our Mellin phase satisfies those
nilsequence hypotheses or that their full congruence input is equivalent
to an ordinary PNT envelope. No such theorem is imported as a Lean axiom.

The fixed-shift literature is also a separate strength check.
[Matomäki–Radziwiłł–Tao, Theorem 1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.01315)
gives prime/divisor asymptotics for almost all shifts in growing ranges,
with logarithmic savings for the Mangoldt cases. This neither supplies a
bound at the one retained successor shift nor automatically handles the
prime-leg-only divisor convolution. The paper explicitly distinguishes
power-saving divisor estimates from the weaker Mangoldt estimates.
No full Hardy–Littlewood conjecture is claimed necessary for our weaker
campaign endpoint.

The classical additive prime-sum bounds are also not a ready-made
Mellin non-coherence theorem. [Tao, Theorem 1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1201.6656)
provides explicit smoothed Vaughan bounds, including `x/sqrt(q)`,
`sqrt(x*q)` and `x^(4/5)` terms, with specified rational-approximation,
cutoff and auxiliary-prime hypotheses. The small-q refinement still
contains `x/q`. A normalized principal prime-modulus source has size
proportional to `1/(q-1)`; amplifying it back to unit size multiplies an
`x/q` allowance by `q-1`, leaving at least `x/2` for `q>=2`.
This is a paper-level **rate preflight for that principal-channel use**,
not a proved transfer of the theorem to our factorial/height/mask weights,
and not a rejection of signed major-arc cancellation or every additive
Fourier approach. The theorem has not been imported as a Lean axiom.

[Lau, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.07556) does provide a fixed
power saving for smoothed `d_k(n)d(n+h)`, with integer `k>=4` and stated
shift/smoothing hypotheses. Its saving is useful in its actual class;
it cannot be differentiated in an unstated complex convolution parameter
to produce a logarithmic-zeta/prime estimate. Such a uniform analytic
interpolation would be new input. Neither paper is installed as an axiom.

For higher Selberg orders, [Banks–Sinha, Lemma 2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.11768)
gives the positive comparison between Mangoldt convolution powers,
generalized Mangoldt coefficients and logarithmic powers. The new Lean
audit derives those comparisons and the exact integer recurrence rather
than importing them as axioms. Their Theorems 1.3 and 1.4 tie appropriate
UNIFORM twisted generalized-Mangoldt asymptotics to RH; those asymptotics
are not an unconditional payment available to this campaign. This does
not establish equivalence for an unstated weaker fixed-height bound.

The co-Poisson/Müntz literature has the same zeta-factor strength check.
[Báez-Duarte, Theorems 1.2, 1.3 and 2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0505453)
gives a Mellin product with zeta and an RH approximation criterion for
suitable kernels. The sufficiency theorem there assumes compact support;
it cannot simply be applied to a Gaussian. The missing approximation
estimate is arithmetic input, not a consequence of Poisson symmetry.
[Burnol, Theorem 1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0103058) retains squared
zero multiplicities in a lower bound for the approximation distance.
These are paper-level strength checks, not imported Lean axioms or a
claim that every nonlinear Gaussian/lattice approach collapses. The new
Lean heat audit proves only the stated linear convolution no-go. The
floor-approximation audit independently derives the single right-half-zero
Cauchy--Schwarz distance barrier for every finite balanced floor code; it
does not import Burnol's stronger multiplicity formula or prove distance
decay.

For Farey geometry, [Cheung–Quas, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.14976v1)
proves weak mixing of the BCZ return map. It supplies no fixed power-saving
bound for this campaign's weighted cutoff; ordinary mixing is left open.
[Chahal–Chatterjee–Chaubey, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.00228v3)
gives a subexponential Weyl-sum estimate in its stated restricted Farey
class. Their Section 4 treats GRH-equivalent distribution statements.
These are strength checks, not independent source-scale payments or
theorems assumed in Lean. Neither paper's scope licenses replacing a
native ordered-gap observable by a complete Fourier projection.

For one-point Farey masks, [Braun, equations (1) and (3)](https://www.peterbraun.com.au/newer%20pdf%27s/A2013/Farey%20series%20v2.pdf)
uses an exact Mobius/grid convolution and inverse-zeta factor for its stated
Liouville-weighted rational test. Its RH-equivalent growth assumptions are
not an unconditional estimate for this campaign. The new Lean audit
proves the arbitrary native one-point profile identity independently;
neither the paper's equivalence nor a continuation for an arbitrary hard
asymmetric arc is installed as an axiom. Ordered gap or inverse-residue
information cannot be inferred from that one-point identity.

For the distinct inverse-residue candidate, [Kıral, Proposition 8](https://arxiv.org/html/1504.01860)
gives the opposite-sign Kloosterman continuation its genuine zeta-zero poles
at rho/2, of the actual zero multiplicity. Theorem 2 gives a weighted
partial-sum bound `sum_(c<=X) S(m,-n;c)/c = O(X^(1/6+epsilon))` for fixed
m,n. An Abel conversion would give `O(X^(7/6+epsilon))` for the raw sum;
this is a strength check, not a compiled estimate, and is weaker than the
campaign `o(X^beta)`, beta<1, target. It is not advertised as the strongest
current benchmark. The continued spectral expansion and defining Dirichlet
series have disjoint convergence regions; neither direct insertion into a
positive spectral formula nor treating scattering poles as real eigenvalues
is licensed. The local finite-matrix and nonreal-coordinate proofs are
independent of the paper. Its continuation, source theorem and estimates
are NOT imported into Lean or credited as a new arithmetic saving.
