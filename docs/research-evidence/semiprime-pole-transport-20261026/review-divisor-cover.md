26 October 2026. Root review. **PASS after clarifying the first nonunit
index and the preserved scalar-base contract.** No new coverage or
acquisition theorem is credited to this audit.

The primary source identifies Chris Umans and Siki Wang. Its Conjecture
5.1, Theorems 5.2 and 5.5, and the scale and rank hypotheses were checked
directly. The conditional exponent with an o(1) term is not the requested
strict soft bound. The repository's October 16 paid group-cover transfer
already removes singleton prefactoring under its stated decoder contract;
the present audit preserves that credit.

For the native smaller scalar order r, r<P<=sqrt(N)<=K^3. Three prime
factors greater than K would contradict this bound. More directly, losing
any divisor greater than K leaves order below K^2. The actual scalar
screen therefore detects a loss at the smaller component, regardless of
the other component's order. A saturated leaf at the FIRST nonunit index
means the two exact local orders are equal. Their coprimality forces that
index to be one. An arbitrary common multiple would not justify this
conclusion; the corrected report must retain first-index localization.

The prior contracts were checked in the frozen native-cover-review and
review-torus-transfer reports. The licensed scalar extractor accepts a
specified unit with both odd local orders above 16H and a short nonzero
global annihilator. The preceding base in prefix-product localization
still meets these requirements: a continuing screen certifies both orders
large, and powering preserves oddness and coprimality. It is unnecessary
to assert that every preceding projection preserved both orders exactly.
The final short label annihilates that preceding base on the saturated
branch. No long product exponent is passed to a short-label interface.

The weaker prime-cover promise does not imply beta^s=beta^t: it only
reduces the local order of beta^(s-t). The extra bounded-order clock in
the proposed batch product is therefore real. Conversely, a new algorithm
need not acquire that full product; this is a missing operation in the
tested handoff, not a lower bound on every prime-cover consumer.

proved, but the report supplies no identity covering the interior label.
Its claims remain conditional supplier interfaces and prior-credit checks.
