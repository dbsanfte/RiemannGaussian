# Umans–Wang divisor covers versus native short pole labels

26 October 2026. Focused primary and prior-credit audit, scratch only. The authors are **Chris Umans and Siki Wang**, not Williams. **Outcome:** native roughening permits a strictly narrower *prime-factor projection* target than their full divisor cover, and the exact per-projection factor consumer is proved below. But it changes the acquisition problem: a prime-divisor cover does not produce a zero group difference, and batching its partial-order tests is still unpaid. No constructed target-scale cover or new factoring rate is claimed.

Theorem5.5 gives soft-O(N^(max(alpha,beta)/2+o(1))) conditionally. At alpha=beta=1/3 this is N^(1/6+o(1)), NOT automatically soft-O(N^1/6), nor an every-input uniformly constructed cover. Section6's easy smooth-factor subset is not full coverage; its prime and balanced-semiprime cases remain singled out as difficult. These are hypotheses/results of the paper, not imported native suppliers.

## 2. A genuinely weaker native target: one prime factor of the smaller scalar order

Use ORIGINAL K preparation. Let P be the smaller factor and r=ord_P(beta). Then

    16K²<r<=P-1<K³,

and every prime divisor of r is >K after paid roughening. Therefore r has at most TWO prime factors counted with multiplicity. Any nontrivial loss under beta -> beta^A removes a prime >K, leaving

    ord_P(beta^A)=r/gcd(r,A)<K²<16K².                (1)

This holds for any explicitly supplied nonzero O(log N)-bit A, or a soft-K-bit A if the wider licensed decoder contract is retained and charged. No factorization of A is needed.

An actual consumer for (1) is: compute beta^A; execute the old scalar short-order screen through16K², in soft K. If it returns a proper gcd, factor. Otherwise locate the FIRST NONUNIT INDEX i of that scalar screen. If its gcd is saturated (N), both local exact orders equal i: neither component vanished at an earlier index. Their inherited coprimality therefore forces i=1. Coprimality alone would not force an arbitrary common multiple or lcm to be1. Thus beta^A=1 globally, and the original licensed nonzero-annihilator extractor factors once, inside original soft K for short A. Negative A uses its absolute value. This is the existing smaller-order-loss theorem expressed as a supplier interface, not a new scalar screen.

Consequently a constructed POLYLOGARITHMIC menu of short exponents, one of which shares ANY prime with r, suffices in soft K. It need not contain a multiple of all r. This asks less coverage than the full Umans–Wang divisor property and accepts unfactored leaves. The native promise r|P-1 with PQ=N is retained; we do not replace it by arbitrary unconstrained small integers and claim an all-semiprime result.

## 3. Execute the tempting structured prime-cover handoff: the new unpaid operation

Suppose S,T are cheaply enumerated exponent descriptions and only a PRIME divisor ell|r is promised to divide some A=s-t. This does NOT imply beta^s=beta^t mod P unless r|A. Therefore the already paid cross-difference polynomial/MPE machinery does not find that pair. Its row product can be a global unit even though a candidate exponent causes partial order loss. This is the exact point at which weakening the cover breaks the old acquisition handoff.

To test a candidate pair one must instead detect

    exists i in[1,16K²]: beta^(i*s)=beta^(i*t) mod P. (2)

The per-pair short screen is soft K, not polylogarithmic. Calling it on all |S||T| candidates is not a target-scale algorithm. The collective finite product required to batch it is

    product_(i<=16K²) product_(s,t)
                         (beta^(is)-beta^(it)).       (3)

Its i=1 slice is acquired by ordinary cross-MPE, but the remaining clock is a new long joint query. Substituting (3) as a supplied oracle would reintroduce the very arithmetic acquisition task. Likewise raising beta to the explicit PRODUCT of all candidate differences requires its total exponent bit length; it is not acquired by the modular integer resultant for that product. No unknown-order group exponentiation of a huge resultant is free.

Thus the weaker coverage target is real, but it has a different, explicitly unpaid batch consumer. The full Strong group-order cover can bypass (2) because it gives a genuine group collision immediately. A prime-cover conjecture by itself cannot be advertised as an unconditional improvement to that framework.

## 4. Test the native pole labels as the missing small projection menu

At a virtual native witness the scalar norm label has the short form

    L=ell+a+cN-HI,

and r divides L. The mixed pair itself also has short integer coordinates. If I or an equivalent clock phase/row selector were acquired, the existing native decoder already finishes; packaging this L as a divisor-cover leaf is circular.

There is one fully paid projection operation: for ANY acquired O(K) list of O(log N)-bit nonzero labels L_j (for example actual public row centers or a bounded number of actual clock endpoints), their integer product M has soft-K bit length. A product tree, beta^M and the short-order screen fit soft K. If this yields a global annihilator with wide exponent M, do NOT invoke a short-label extractor on M: binary-search the prefix-product screens, terminating early on a proper factor, to the first loss of the large-order invariant. The previous powered base still has both local orders >16H, odd, K-rough and mutually coprime: powering replaces each order by a divisor, and the preceding prefix screen certified the large-order invariant. It need not preserve the original exact order values. These are precisely the admissibility invariants accepted by the inherited short-label decoder. Its next SINGLE input label is a nonzero short annihilator; apply that decoder to THIS admissible previous base. Prior contracts are recorded in [native-cover-review, partial-order audit](/home/dbsanfte/riemann/formal/docs/research-evidence/semiprime-knot-direct-20261026/native-cover-review.md) and [review-torus-transfer, first-nonunit gate](/home/dbsanfte/riemann/formal/docs/research-evidence/semiprime-even-acquisition-20261026/review-torus-transfer.md), alongside the credited shared-label-cleaning preparation. The logarithmic number of screens still costs soft K. On continuation, gcd(r,M)=1, otherwise (1) forces that factor branch. This is already the shared-label-cleaning preparation, with the improved smaller-order-loss conclusion; adding finitely many public endpoints changes no exponent. It supplies a certificate that those labels are units modulo r, not the missing interior label ell+a+cN-HI.

Completing EACH row by all I in its long interval would make the product exponent have J*h short-label factors, rather than soft K bits. The group-polynomial/resultant audit already charges that mass. Interpreting the product of its *modular integer values* as the exponent is invalid. Native conic stationarity holds modulo H, not modulo the unknown prime divisors of r; no proved identity here makes the required interior L share a factor with the short center/endpoint product.

The prime-order branch emphasizes the exact arithmetic issue: if r is prime and none of the acquired short labels is zero modulo r, all their projections are automorphisms of that local subgroup. This is compatible with an interior witness, whose E-HI can still be zero. It is not an arbitrary fake-root counterexample or a proof that further native constraints cannot produce another short menu.

## 5. Precise remaining arithmetic lemma

A useful replacement for the full conjecture would be an ACTUALLY CONSTRUCTED polylogarithmic menu of nonzero O(log N)-bit labels A_t (or wider labels only under the separately retained, charged wide-annihilator contract), such that for every prepared N=PQ one A_t shares a prime with the smaller scalar order, or a proved collective algorithm for (2) on a larger structured menu within soft K. Native rows and pole transport offer short descriptions for such labels but currently do not supply that coverage or batched test.

This formulation distinguishes a potential easier native target from a theorem restatement: its individual consumer is executed, its failure under old cross-MPE is explicit, and its relation to the already paid label-cleaning projection is accounted for. It removes neither the unknown clock nor the original construction requirement. The all-semiprime soft-K goal remains open.
