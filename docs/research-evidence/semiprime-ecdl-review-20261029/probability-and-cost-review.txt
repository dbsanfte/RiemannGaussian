Scope: full paper.txt read (1076 lines), with detailed audit of Sections3.2–3.5. No factoring-goal source edits, imports or builds. No browsing used. This is an independent mathematical review, not a formal kernel proof or an independent proof of the assumed tensor-contraction theorem.

Verdict
The randomized reduction in Lemmas9–13 is rigorous as written for the stated prime-order/cofactor-one setting, conditional on the exact deterministic tensor-contraction solver. I found no fatal gap in its coverage, relation dependence, nonzero-V or expected-time arguments. It proves a Las Vegas expected bound for EVERY valid fixed input curve/target in the stated family, not merely an average-curve heuristic. It does not supply a deterministic worst-case ECDLP algorithm, an unconditional tensor solver, or an improved deterministic semiprime-factoring rate.

Precise hypotheses
1. Theorem3 (p5; Lemmas5–7 pp6–7; proof p8): d>=1, arbitrary d-bit unsigned a,b and 2d-bit z. It is an unconditional parsimonious encoding of ab=z with <1100(d+1) Boolean edges, maxdegree<136(d+1)^(3/4), and uniformly polynomial-time vertex predicates. It is an encoding theorem, not a standalone subexponential multiplication/search theorem. The ring is Z/(2^J+1); J and L are powers of two, L divides2J, and the proof establishes a PRINCIPAL root and invertible L, rather than assuming that ordinary root-of-unity order suffices over a composite ring. Canonical residues and unique carries are essential to parsimony.
2. Theorem4 (statement pp8–9, proof p16): nonsingular short-Weierstrass E/F_p, p>3 prime, full group order r odd prime, generator P, valid Q in that group, and known r (Section3 opening p8); Q=O is handled directly. b=ceil(log2 p). Its explicit proved asymptotic parameter range, from Lemmas11 and13, is sufficiently large b and 2<=m<=ceil((log2 b)^(1/4)). The statement's phrase 'not too large' should be read with that explicit range; I did not infer a theorem for unrestricted m.
3. The tensor-search bound is CONDITIONAL on Theorem2 (p3), which requires an exact deterministic contraction solver, degree O(s^0.9), polynomial-size descriptions and polynomial-time predicates, with time exp(O(s/sqrt(log s))) and polynomial space. The paper expressly does not independently verify that theorem (p16). Lemma1's deterministic witness search does not require parsimony. Lemma11 pp12–13 invokes only that existence/search part.
4. Lemma10 pp11–12 additionally requires r>=100, m>=2, and L=(r-1)tau/s >=max(r^(1/m),10m). The chosen tau in Lemma13 pp14–15 ensures this; tau<=s and r^(1/m)>=10m hold for sufficiently large b in the explicit m range.

Randomized hashed factor bases: pp10–11, Lemma9
The compressed point encoding enc(A)=2x+(y mod2) is injective for nonidentity curve points: the only two ordinates for a fixed x are y and p-y, which have opposite parity for odd p; y=0 is the unique-root case. With prime2p<=s<=4p, enc(A)<s. Sampling alpha!=0 and beta uniformly gives a permutation of F_s, not an independent random subset. For each slot, Pr[A in F_i]=rho=tau/s. For distinct A,B, the exact joint probability is tau(tau-1)/(s(s-1))<=rho^2. Independent slot seeds give independence BETWEEN slots. The deterministic size cap |F_i|<=tau follows from injectivity/permutation; excluding alpha=0 is significant for that uniform cap.
The proof never assumes full random-set independence or higher-wise independence. Repeated point values ACROSS different slots are permitted because the slot seeds are independent. That is sufficient for the second-moment argument.

Coverage and entropy: pp11–12, Lemma10
Put n=r-1. The exact tuple counts are K_k(O)=(n^k+(-1)^k n)/r and K_k(R!=O)=(n^k-(-1)^k)/r. Their use of scalar multiplication to show equal nonzero-target counts is justified by the prime cyclic group. Hence mu>= (1-1/n)L^m/r for m>=2.
For pairs of decompositions, identical tuples contribute mu. Distinct equal-sum tuples cannot agree in exactly m-1 positions. With exactly j<=m-2 agreements, the completion count is bounded by (1+1/n)n^(m-j)/r. The survival probability is at most rho^(2m-j), using ONLY the within-slot two-point bound and between-slot independence. Summing agreement patterns gives the stated second-moment upper bound and
  Pr[X>0] >= (1-1/n)/(r/L^m+(1+1/n)(1+1/L)^m) >= 2/5.
All combinatorial overcounting is in the safe direction. No smoothness heuristic, expander hypothesis, presumed relation entropy, or presumed matrix rank enters this result. The number of hash seeds is much smaller than the number of possible subsets, but the proof needs only these verified first/two-point moments.

Fixed seeds, relation collection and linear dependence: pp13–15
The fixed-target/random-seed coverage is converted correctly to fixed-seed/random-target coverage. If f is the fraction of decomposable targets, E_seed[f]>=2/5 and 0<=f<=1 imply Pr[f>=1/5]>=1/4. This does NOT claim every seed is good, nor simultaneous coverage of every target.
Conditional on a fixed good seed, fresh uniform u,v produce independent uniform R=[u]P+[v]Q, so successes are independent Bernoulli trials of probability f>=1/5. N=sum_i|F_i| is then fixed. The expected attempts for N+1 successes are at most5(N+1), and Markov gives probability>=3/4 of completion within20(N+1) attempts.
The N columns are pairs(i,A), retaining separate columns for the same curve point in different bases. N+1 rows always give a nontrivial LEFT dependency over F_r. No independence or full-rank assumption on the rows is required. Even repeated/identical rows are harmless. Cancelling every labelled column also cancels the corresponding actual group points.

Nonzero V and hidden scalar randomness: p14, Lemma12
For a fixed unknown xi, (u,v)->(R=[u+xi*v]P,v) is a bijection from F_r^2 to G x F_r. Thus conditional on R, v remains exactly uniform. After fixing the seed and FULL sequence of targets (including failed attempts), deterministic decomposition and deterministic row-dependency selection determine the successful indices, matrix and dependency lambda without seeing u,v. The selected v_j remain independent uniform; lambda has at least one nonzero entry; V=sum lambda_j*v_j is therefore uniform F_r. Consequently V!=0 with EXACT probability1-1/r conditional on successful collection.
This is not a claim that the relation matrix contains fresh row entropy. The unused scalar blinding is what prevents the cancellation equation from being trivial. It is important operationally that pivot/tie/dependency selection not be made using the stored u/v values; the paper explicitly enforces that separation. A randomized independent choice of a dependency could also be conditioned on, but the paper's deterministic choice is sufficient.
For r>=100, a trial succeeds with probability at least (1/4)(3/4)(1-1/r)>1/6. Fresh restarts give a geometric tail and almost-sure termination. Every returned xi is correct by U+Vxi=0 and invertibility of nonzero V modulo prime r. Curve/target averaging is not needed.

All cost components: pp14–16, Lemma13 and Remark2
- tau=ceil((s/(r-1))*max(r^(1/m),10m)); Hasse and s<=4p make s/(r-1)=O(1), hence N<=m*tau=O(m*r^(1/m)) in the stated large-b range. This is a deterministic per-trial cap, not merely E[N].
- Exact factor-base enumeration loops over t_hash<tau, inverts the affine hash, discards z>=2p, computes x=floor(z/2), and extracts the root of the curve RHS with specified parity if it exists. It scans tau hash values, NOT r curve points; injectivity prevents duplicates. Quadratic-residuosity/root extraction and selecting a prime s are randomized expected-polynomial preprocessing, and are correctly included in the Las Vegas expectation. No GRH or unproved number-theoretic heuristic is needed for these routines. Parity and the zero-RHS case need the stated handling.
- Sorting/indexing columns and group scalar multiplication are polynomial overhead per enumerated point or target. One may store relation rows sparsely while collecting them, then materialize the dense matrix inside the stated Gaussian-elimination budget; otherwise a naive dense-row write is not literally poly(b) per call, but is still absorbed by the O(N^3*b^2) linear-algebra stage.
- Lemma11 uses O(mb)-edge networks, degree O(b^(3/4)), uniform polynomial descriptions/predicates, and O(mb) contraction calls per deterministic witness search. The factor-base LIST is NOT embedded in the network; hash membership is checked arithmetically with O(b) edges per slot. The s field modulus has O(b) bits. Fixing witness bits does not enlarge degree or edge count.
- O(N+1) target attempts per trial and O(mb) calls per query yield the stated expected O(m^2*b*r^(1/m)) contraction calls.
- Elimination costs O(N^3) operations in F_r, hence O(m^3*r^(3/m)*b^2) bit operations with ordinary quadratic arithmetic, and O(m^2*r^(2/m)*b) matrix bits. Gaussian elimination is NOT polynomial in b, and the paper includes it; the overall algorithm is NOT polynomial-space merely because the contraction solver is.
- Per-trial construction cost may correlate with trial success. E[total]=E[C]/p_success nevertheless follows from fresh independent trials and the renewal identity; no independence between C and success is needed.
- Computing/rounding m and tau needs no real-number oracle. For instance, exact integer-power comparisons can determine the least tau satisfying ((r-1)*tau)^m>=r*s^m and (r-1)*tau>=10m*s; their integers have polynomially many bits in the stated m range. This implementation detail does not alter the exponent.

The 1/4 exponent
The query stage contributes b/m + O(mb/sqrt(log b)) to the binary exponent (including the number of queries). Elimination contributes3b/m plus polynomial factors. Thus the total is 2^{O(b/m+mb/sqrt(log b))}. Balancing 1/m with m/sqrt(log b) gives m=Theta((log b)^(1/4)), producing 2^{O(b/(log b)^(1/4))}. Constants3, solver constants and ceilings alter only hidden constants, not the1/4 exponent. Matrix space at this choice has the same O-exponent. The final proof's handling of finitely many small sizes by exhaustive search is legitimate for an asymptotic claim.

Clarifications / nonfatal omissions
A. Theorem4's 'm not too large' is less precise than the explicit range used in its lemmas. The final asymptotic statement uses an admissible m, so this is not a failure of that statement.
B. Fixed cofactor c (p9) is a sketch, not the main theorem's complete proof. With group order c*r and large prime r, A=[c]S gives the intended prime-order subgroup (gcd(c,r)=1 eventually); witnesses S may be nonunique, but Lemma11 needs no parsimony. To keep the literal Fi subset G definition during factor-base enumeration, add a polynomial-time subgroup filter, or allow unused columns for the enumerated superset. This is routine for fixed c and leaves the asymptotic bound, but should be stated rather than treating the subgroup as the entire curve in enumeration.
C. No derandomization is provided. Existence of many good seeds is not an efficient deterministic seed-finding algorithm. Straight enumeration of s^{2m} affine-hash seeds has exponentTheta(mb), and enumeration of all scalar pairs is likewise too costly for the claimed saving. These observations do not rule out a new derandomization argument; none appears here. A bounded-failure randomized cap is also not a deterministic worst-case guarantee.
D. The unproved external tensor theorem remains the decisive condition. This audit of the reduction cannot certify that theorem or turn a report-level complexity reduction into our kernel-checked full machine.

Comparison with the paused deterministic semiprime target
No improvement follows. The paper addresses ECDLP, gives no reduction from arbitrary binary semiprime factorization to its prime-order-curve inputs, and promises randomized EXPECTED time rather than deterministic worst-case time. Even under a hypothetical b=Theta(ell) transfer, b/(log b)^(1/4) is asymptotically LARGER than ell/sqrt(log ell), by a factorTheta((log ell)^(1/4)); it is a weaker numerical saving, not a stronger one. Both claims depend on the same contraction-type engine, so this is not independent evidence proving that engine.
Theorem3's unconditional linear-edge multiplication encoding, combined CONDITIONALLY with Theorem2 and deterministic bit self-reduction, suggests the matching sqrt(log)-denominator factoring scale directly; it does not strengthen that scale or close our literal implementation obligations. This observation is only a review/comparison, not resumed factoring work.

Positive reusable ideas
- Linear-size, moderate-degree multiplication predicates by ONE balanced DFT split, canonical ring words and uniquely forced carries; careful principal-root proof works over the composite ring.
- Permutation hashing supplies both a deterministic factor-base size cap and sufficient negative pair correlation, without pretending that the sets are fully independent random subsets.
- Independently hashed slots make repeated points across slots safe and allow a clean second-moment proof for every fixed target.
- Deterministic nuisance-variable cancellation requires only a left kernel, not full-rank or independent relation assumptions; label duplicated base occurrences explicitly.
- Hidden affine scalar blinding preserves one full field element of entropy after conditioning on every target and every deterministic search outcome. This is the precise mechanism making the final cancellation useful.
- Separate existence/search from exact counting: witness extraction does not need parsimonious auxiliary witnesses. This matters for fixed-cofactor certification and other many-witness gadgets.
