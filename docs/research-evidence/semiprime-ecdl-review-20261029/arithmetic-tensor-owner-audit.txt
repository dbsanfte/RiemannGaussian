1. Integer multiplication: Theorem 3 (statement p.5, conclusion p.8), Lemmas 3–7 (pp.4–7).

The one-split DFT construction is the important structural point. It does not claim a linear-time multiplication circuit. It puts a small dense transform inside each polynomial-time vertex predicate, reducing the number of stage boundaries that cost Boolean edges.

Parameter derivation checked:
  J = least power of two with J^2 >= 64(d+1), k=J/4, n=ceil(d/k),
  L = least power of two >=2n, L1=2^floor(log2(L)/2), L2=2^ceil(log2(L)/2).
  Lemma 5: J>=16; J^2<256(d+1); n<J/16+1;
  L<4n<J/4+4<=J/2; max(L1,L2)<=sqrt(2L)<=sqrt(J).
All these inequalities hold also at the J=16 boundary, with the stated strict/non-strict directions.

The ring is A=Z/(2^J+1), and omega=2^(2J/L). Lemma 5.3 does NOT improperly assume the modulus prime. For 0<j<L, write j=2^v*j' with j' odd and use h=L/2^(v+1); omega^(jh)=-1. Translation by h permutes the geometric sum, so S=-S. Since 2 is invertible modulo the odd modulus, S=0. Also L is a power of two and hence invertible. This gives the principal-root condition needed by inversion in Lemma 3. The four-step indexing in Lemma 4 is correct: j=L2*j1+j2 and t=t1+L1*t2 leave the discarded exponent term L*j1*t2.

Lemma 5.4 prevents modular aliasing of convolution coefficients:
  c_l < n*2^(2k) = n*2^(J/2) < 2^J.
Consequently the inverse transform's canonical residue is the actual nonnegative integer coefficient, not only its congruence class. Padding L>=2n prevents polynomial wraparound.

Carries (equation (5), p.6; Lemma 6, p.7): Interpret the carry equations as INTEGER equalities, not congruences. With B=2^k, k>=4 and kappa_0=0, forward division with remainder uniquely fixes z_l in [0,B) and kappa_(l+1). Inductively c_l,kappa_l<2^J gives kappa_(l+1)<2^(J+1-k)<=2^J. Thus J+1-bit carry words suffice. Telescoping gives ab=sum_l z_l*B^l+kappa_(2n)*B^(2n); because ab<2^(2d)<=B^(2n), the terminal carry is zero, and the padded output bits above position 2d-1 are zero. This verifies existence, absence of spurious products and uniqueness of carries.

The exact advertised constants in Lemma 7 check out:
  edges <=4d+8L(J+1)<=4d+4J(J+1)<=4d+(17/4)J^2<1100(d+1).
The eight-L tally is 4L forward-transform words, L pointwise-product words, L internal inverse-transform words, at most L coefficient words and at most L carry words.
  degree <=2*sqrt(J)*(J+1)<136(d+1)^(3/4).
Here J+1<=(17/16)J and J^(3/2)<64(d+1)^(3/4). Product/carry vertices have fewer than 4(J+1) ports and obey the same bound. Interface padding is a public constant inside predicates, not additional free bits.

Parsimony in Lemma 6 is supported: fixed a,b uniquely determine every canonical transform/product/coefficient word and every carry. Fixed-width natural carry/limb words have unique bit representations. Canonical ring outputs are essential; accepting arbitrary representatives modulo 2^J+1 would indeed introduce multiplicity. The paper explicitly requires their canonical representations.

2. Bit costs and modular composition: Lemma 2 (pp.3–4), Lemma 8 and Corollary 1 (p.8).

O(d) counts Boolean EDGES, not bit operations, code size or table rows. A transform predicate has O(L2^2) modular operations on O(J)-bit words. Elementary bit arithmetic and public-power preparation give, for example, O(d^(3/2)*polylog(d)) bit time per transform predicate; a coarser fixed polynomial is enough for Theorem 2. Roots, twiddle powers and L^-1 are public, polynomial-time constructible constants; they are not unknown answers. Graph incidences and predicate code/parameters admit a uniform polynomial-bit description. Thus no hidden exponential predicate-evaluation oracle is required by this arithmetic construction.

For modular multiplication, the guessed d-bit quotient theta is uniquely floor(xy/q)<q for x,y<q. The equality xy=theta*q+z must be an exact integer equality (use overflow-rejecting ripple addition, or a 2d+1-bit sum with its high bit fixed zero). For modular addition, beta is uniquely the carry across q. Canonicality checks x,y,z<q and deterministic equality-copy trees preserve uniqueness. Corollary 1 correctly conditions parsimony on uniqueness of auxiliary field words u; arbitrary existential systems are not asserted parsimonious.

3. Point validity/addition: Corollary 2, pp.9–10, equations (6)–(8).

Canonical affine coordinates plus a distinguished infinity flag avoid projective-scale multiplicities. At infinity, iota=1 forces x=y=0 and the curve equation is gated off. Otherwise iota=0 forces the actual curve equation. All coordinate words are canonical residues modulo p.

The zero-test constraints a*zeta=0, a*e=1-zeta, zeta*e=0 uniquely fix BOTH zeta and e, including a=0, where zeta=1,e=0. The five selectors are disjoint and exhaustive on valid input points: first infinity, second infinity with first nonidentity, opposite points, unequal-x generic addition, and doubling.

The exceptional cases work: both infinities select only the first case; a y=0 point doubled is in the opposite-point case and yields infinity. In the doubling case, equal x and the curve equation imply y2=+/-y1; y1+y2 !=0 implies y2=y1!=0, so 2*y1 is invertible because p>3. The generic denominator x2-x1 is nonzero. The respective slope is unique and the standard output formulas apply. The extra constraint (1-sigma4-sigma5)*lambda=0 explicitly removes the otherwise free inactive slope, so the three exceptional branches do not acquire a factor p in their extension count. All ungated polynomial intermediates can be constrained as ordinary deterministic field computations, even when the final equation is gated.

For the m-point chain, take S1:=A1 by wiring/aliasing, then m-1 additions uniquely determine all later partial sums. The text's m-1 count and claim of determined partial sums implicitly require this identification.

4. Qualifications, not blocking gaps.

The following are small implementation/formalization details rather than a failed asymptotic argument: explicitly declare J+1-bit unsigned carry words; make the integer addition's final-carry rejection explicit; wire S1=A1 rather than leave S1 a free guessed point; attach unary-true leaves to interface edges if the graph convention disallows dangling legs. These choices add no asymptotically harmful edges or degree. The latter may add O(d) trivial vertices, so the explanatory O(sqrt(d)) arithmetic-vertex count need not count terminal dummy vertices; Theorem 3 itself only needs the edge/degree bounds.

The fixed-cofactor paragraph on p.9 does not preserve literal parsimony: for sufficiently large prime r coprime to fixed c, multiplication by c on E(F_p) of order c*r has a kernel of size c, so A=[c]S has c witnesses S. With m certified points this can multiply extensions by c^m. This is NOT a blocking issue for the main search reduction: Lemma 1's deterministic existence/witness search expressly does not require parsimony. It would need qualification if one tried to reuse the cofactor certificate for a literal parsimonious-count claim. The prime-order Corollary 2 above has no such problem.

No actual blocking arithmetic/exception/parsimony flaw was found in the reviewed main prime-order construction. This review does not certify all factor-base probability or extraction claims later in the paper.

5. Transfer to the paused deterministic total-bit target.

Conditionally on Theorem 2 (p.3) being a correct TOTAL-BIT deterministic contraction algorithm with the stated uniform polynomial-time predicate interface, the multiplication construction gives a direct semiprime reduction at the requested scale. Let ell be the input bit length. Encode a*b=N with a,b ell-bit words, hardcode N as a 2ell-bit output, and impose 2<=a<=b and b>=2 using Lemma 2. This has s=O(ell) edges, s>=Omega(ell), and maximum degree O(ell^(3/4)), hence eventually meets O(s^0.9). For a semiprime there is a nontrivial satisfying factor pair; ordering also makes the pair unique, although witness search does not need uniqueness.

Use Lemma 1 deterministic prefix fixing, with O(ell) exact contraction calls. Network construction, pin updates, count-bit reading and the final division/verification cost polynomial in ell. The conditional total is therefore 2^(C*ell/sqrt(log(ell+2)))*(ell+1)^d for fixed constants C,d (with bounded small sizes absorbed into a constant). Even explicit local truth-table generation, if an implementation needs it, costs poly(ell)*2^O(ell^(3/4)); its exponent is lower order than ell/sqrt(log ell), since sqrt(log ell)/ell^(1/4)->0. This observation concerns time and is not a separate polynomial-space claim.

This is a constructive encoding/conditional complexity transfer, NOT verification of Theorem 2 or of any existing finite-machine compiler. Using the paper as an independent proof of that engine would be circular. The paper's own ECDLP Theorem 4 (pp.8–9) is randomized, Las Vegas/expected-time, and gives the weaker exponent b/(log b)^(1/4); it cannot itself be substituted for a deterministic worst-case factoring source.
