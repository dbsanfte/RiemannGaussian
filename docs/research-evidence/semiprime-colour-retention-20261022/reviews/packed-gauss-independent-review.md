## All-character projector and correction

Let B=A[Z]/Φ_m(Z), A=(Z/N)[X]/(X^k−1). It is important that this means the monic primitive-m-root algebra, not merely Z^m=1: G_0=−1 uses Φ_m(Z)=0. The conceptual root-adjoining paragraph in the report has this intended meaning; explicitly naming B would remove any possible ambiguity.

For h_a=k/gcd(a,k), a h_a=0 mod k, so E_a is the average over the cyclic subgroup generated by X^a. Every summand of E_a² occurs exactly h_a times, hence E_a²=E_a. After base change to either hidden characteristic and its algebraic closure, E_a is exactly the indicator of ξ^a=1. The averaging denominators are invertible because h_a|k and gcd(N,k)=1. There is no implicit inversion of ξ^a−1 in the production algorithm.

At a given ξ, E_a E_b is exactly the both-trivial projector. If ξ^(a+b)=1 and either character is trivial, both are trivial. Hence E_(a+b)−E_a E_b is exactly the nontrivial inverse-character projector; it is neither negative support nor an extra exceptional case.

Checking the four disjoint possibilities gives the claimed coefficient c_ab:

- Both nontrivial and product nontrivial: the correction vanishes, leaving J_ab.
- Exactly one trivial: J_ab=−1 and the correction vanishes.
- Both trivial: J_00=m−2 and subtracting m−1 gives c_ab=−1.
- Nontrivial inverse pair: J_ab=−ξ^(a ell(−1)); the correction multiplies J_ab by m, giving c_ab=−m ξ^(a ell(−1)). Multiplying by G_0=−1 gives the standard inverse-pair Gauss product.

Thus G_aG_b=c_abG_(a+b) on every root specialization. Since N is squarefree and gcd(N,k)=1, the roots of X^k−1 are distinct over each hidden characteristic and these specializations detect equality in A, and likewise in B after the conceptual Z base change. The reasoning does not require the algorithm to know P or Q. It remains valid if P divides m−1 but does not divide k; neither character orthogonality nor its averaging divides by m−1.

Unit safety is correct. In each specialized component G_0=−1 and every nontrivial G_a has inverse supplied by G_-a divided by m ξ^(a ell(−1)). Both m and the root of unity are units. Hence c_ab is a unit. The more direct identity J_ab J_-a,-b=m applies in the both-nontrivial, product-nontrivial case; exactly-one-trivial components instead have J=−1 and are covered separately. The report's wording already separates these cases. No production step needs these inverses.

## Binary algorithm

For E=0, C_0=−1 gives 1=C_0G_0. For E=1, C_1=1. Assuming G_1^e=C_eG_e, squaring and the proved c_(e,e) identity give C_(2e)=C_e²c_(e,e). Multiplication by G_1 gives C_(2e+1)=C_(2e)c_(2e,1). The stored subscript need only be e mod k because all G and c subscripts are modulo k. Starting after the leading binary1 uses at most two histograms per subsequent bit. This proves the exact output, including the trivial and lower-order characters, without dividing by a symbolic Gauss sum.

Specialization X=1 gives C_E(1)=(−1)^(E+1), consistent also at E=0. For E=k, G_(k mod k)=G_0=−1, hence G_1^k=−C_k. Both signs in the report are correct.

## Construction and true bit charge

The auxiliary arithmetic is fully public. Trial factoring m−1, then testing at most m primitive-root candidates using its known prime divisors, costs soft O(m) in log m bit overhead. Walking the chosen primitive root acquires the actual m−1-entry logarithm table. No hidden-prime discrete logarithm, unspecified complete sum, or uncharged primitive-root existence search is used.

For each requested (a,b), loop over the m−2 public values t≠0,1 and add to one of k bins. Indices require O(log m+log k) bits and counts O(log m) bits before reduction. The claimed soft O(m log m) histogram charge is conservative. Each required projector uses at most k entries; h_a^(-1)=gcd(a,k) k^(-1) modulo N, so one extended gcd for k suffices. Products of projectors or their corrected Jacobi sums take a constant number of cyclic polynomial convolutions per c_ab.

With B=ceil(log_2N), cyclic multiplication costs soft O(k(B+log k)) bits by integer packing and reduction. Constantly many such products per binary step, plus at most two histograms per step, yield the displayed

    soft O(m log m + log(E+1)[m log m+k(B+log k)])

bound. Only constantly many k-coefficient arrays need be kept at once. The log table costs O(m log m) bits and the modular arrays O(kB); intermediate histogram counters and convolution carry bits add logarithmic overhead. Thus the stated soft O(m log m+kB) storage is valid in the stated native polynomial-size regime. Input E itself takes O(log(E+1)) bits, as explicitly included.

One C_E has exactly k modular coefficients. It simultaneously represents all character specializations, but does not materialize k vectors of k coefficients. Applying a supplied unit-index rho_s permutes those k coefficients in O(k) index operations. Thus the no-k²-output claim is correct with the report's explicit prohibition on materializing every conjugate or treating an arbitrary downstream all-pairs task as free.

The stated production algorithm does not ask for an inverse power or an inverse Gauss/Jacobi sum, so no additional inverse algorithm or source theorem is required for this claim.

## Native-phase boundary

True Frobenius has both sigma_P and rho_P. Direct substitution gives G(χ)^P=χ(P)^(-P)G(χ^P) mod P, hence C_P(χ)=χ(P)^(-P). Raising this identity to Q and then applying the definition of C_Q for χ^P yields

    C_N(χ)=χ(P)^(-N) C_Q(χ^P) mod P.

The displayed C_Q term is essential. Computing C_N is not computing C_P, and packing the character parameter does not recover P mod k or make an m-by-k native tag family a scalar-size query. The report retains this limitation correctly. The same statement is compatible with the commuting-action audit in high_order_colour/report.md: computing a coefficient-field correction cheaply is different from removing its unknown local index.
