# Primary black-box gcd interfaces versus the paid channel-vector oracle

2026-10-16. No nearlinear common-channel gcd algorithm found. The exact common-channel locator theorem is independently reviewed in ../reviews/common-channel-locator-review-b.md.

Our input is unusually strong but not an explicit polynomial list: for B~J~sqrt L, one arbitrary scalar X returns the ENTIRE vector (N_0(X),...,N_(B-1)(X)) in soft O(J) ring work. Each channel has degree O(J). Their monic field-side common gcd H is squarefree and is exactly the powered hit locator; the retained source bounds deg H=o(J). This is small degree, not a sparse acquired representation. No hidden field arithmetic or factorization is supplied.

## Primary results freshly checked

Kaltofen--Trager, Computing with Polynomials Given by Black Boxes for Their Evaluations: Greatest Common Divisors, Factorization, Separation of Numerators and Denominators (1990), author PDF https://kaltofen.math.ncsu.edu/bibliography/90/KaTr90.pdf . Full PDF/text saved and inspected, especially section 3 around pp.310--312. The black-box gcd construction uses random line images, a random linear combination to reduce a family, and univariate/bivariate image gcd/interpolation. It establishes randomized polynomial-time implicit computation, not a number of input evaluations polylogarithmic in the degree or output-sensitive only in deg H. Its rational/field and normalization contracts do not furnish a composite-ring nearlinear gcd with factor-aware pivots. Applying its generic image acquisition with Theta(J) scalar points to our vector oracle costs soft O(J²), even though all B channel values arrive together. This is a cost of that implementation, not a theorem that every structured oracle algorithm needs that many points.

Huang--Gao, Sparse Polynomial GCD Algorithms Asymptotically Linear in All Fundamental Parameters, September 2026, arXiv:2609.08074v1, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08074 . Full primary PDF/text saved and inspected. Theorem 3 assumes input polynomials A,B over a field and charges their input term counts T_A,T_B, the gcd term count T_G, total degree D and field-size factors. In the infinite-field statement its displayed leading term is soft O(n*T_G*D), together with input-term and logarithmic terms; the finite-field statement is a bit bound with additional log|K| factors. Its characteristic condition is satisfied by our actual hidden fields when applied to degree-O(J) images, but those fields and coefficient inputs are not already available. The theorem is not a cheap evaluation-oracle-to-gcd conversion. Even a bound T_G<=deg H+1=o(J) only gives a product T_G*D, potentially almost quadratic at the proven density exponent; it does not imply soft O(J). Acquiring explicit sparse inputs remains an additional requirement. No hardness theorem is used to exclude our small-degree structured source.

## A useful reduction that does fit the vector oracle

Over one hidden field, two independently random scalar linear combinations of the B channels have gcd H with high probability. To see the precise interface, choose a first combination A. Except with probability <=1/|K| it is nonzero. Conditional on nonzero A, each root of A/H that is not common to every channel is a nonzero linear constraint on a second random coefficient vector; it vanishes with probability <=1/|K|. There are at most d=O(J) such roots in an algebraic closure. Thus the second combination adds an extra common factor with probability <=d/|K|. Repeated-root multiplicity is likewise controlled by the first nonzero derivative/Hasse-jet condition beyond H; the channels have minimum valuation zero after division by H, so the same root-event bound covers any spurious factor. Random coefficients modulo N reduce uniformly to both fields, permitting a union of the field-side failure estimates, but not granting a ring-gcd algorithm.

At any scalar X, both combinations are evaluated using ONE paid vector call and O(B) dot-product work. This avoids a B-fold oracle cost. It does not lower the cost of acquiring their degree-O(J) coefficient representations from Theta(J) points, or supply a small-output black-box gcd routine with only polylogarithmically many vector calls. Random combination coefficients and their bit costs are ordinary public metadata; the hidden H need not be known to form them.

## Concrete remaining positive target

A successful algorithm here needs to exploit the structured family beyond generic black-box gcd. Possibilities include a nearlinear simultaneous multipoint extension of the vector oracle, a compressed common-factor routine with a proved charged number/cost of structured calls, or a factor-selective consequence avoiding explicit H. Generic dense gcd is already nearlinear after two degree-O(J) coefficient lists are supplied; their acquisition is the missing stage. Nothing in the primary algorithms checked converts the present scalar vector oracle and the o(J) output-degree promise into soft O(J) total work.

This audit does not claim a deterministic or randomized oracle lower bound for the actual channels, nor that all J node values are necessary. Composite-ring nonunit leading coefficients, gcd=N saturation and extension queries need explicit contracts in any proposed use. The new positive binomial remainder oracle and the exact collision locator remain potentially useful interfaces.
