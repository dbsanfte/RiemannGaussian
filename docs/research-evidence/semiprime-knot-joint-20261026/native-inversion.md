## 1. The actual arithmetic promise used

    HI=aP+bQ+lambda,
    E−HI=(P−1)(bQ−a), E_swap−HI=(Q−1)(aP−b).

They survive integer-zero deletion, complete-swap pruning and any previously certified unit diagonal pruning. At both cleaned local orders r*, H and I are invertible, since every retained E is invertible and E=HI modulo the witnessed local order. ## 2. Executed swap fusion: one base, two winding offsets

For a complete swap pair write e=E, f=E_swap. Enlarge the public root-label list by ef for each pair, if necessary. This is only O(L) extra O(logN)-bit labels; rerunning the paid factor-or-roots procedure costs soft k and preserves the same original witnesses by powering. With x=rho_(ef), root uniqueness gives

    rho_e=x^f, rho_f=x^e,
    rho_e rho_f=x^(e+f), rho_e/rho_f=x^(f−e).         (1)

All identities are exact, including signed labels. The native pair supplies the explicit short-bit formulas

    e+f=(a+b)(N+1)+2lambda,
    e−f=(a−b)(1−N).                                 (2)

Thus this is a legitimate simultaneous cabling interface, with acquired scalar initialization: the pair at index i is

    (b−x^(Hfi))(b−x^(Hei)), b=x^(ef).                (3)

Because e,f are coprime to the retained subgroup orders, its local zero set is equivalently that of

    (x^(e−Hi)−1)(x^(f−Hi)−1).                       (4)

This equivalence follows from injectivity of e- and f-powering in the group, not from division by geometric quotients. At a collision the quotient may be nonunit, especially if its exponent is divisible by the residue characteristic. Retain actual original labels to decode each branch of (4).

Equation(4) has not reduced the index range: it is the original pair in a new row-dependent base x. Equations(1),(2) pay all phases but do not make e+f or e−f constant across pairs. A common-target formulation has become a per-pair common-base formulation, while the original common-base query already existed globally before inverse labels. No mass or exponent reduction follows from this fusion alone.

The standard torus-knot Alexander cancellation is not a missing scalar algorithm for (3): its denominator factors (1−T^e),(1−T^f) have the SAME argument and no differing offsets. ## 3. Multiplicative index reindexing, with the uncovered portion explicit

A natural next candidate is i=uv. For any acquired q_E, the factor is

    b−q_E^(uv) = b−(q_E^u)^v.                        (5)

Take U=floor sqrtM and V=ceil(M/U). Acquire all u in1..U and v in1..V as public bounds. This costs soft sqrtM descriptors, and exact powers have short exponents. But the rectangular product over u,v counts integers with multiplicity and omits primes exceeding max(U,V). It cannot replace the interval1..M. We do not claim that failure refutes a more selective multiplicative cover.

The native discriminant does give the exact additional condition on its missing index:

    (HI−lambda)^2−4abN=(aP−bQ)^2.                  (6)

Primitivity of (a,b) limits common content in the supplied row; it does not factor this integer I. Scaling a,b by d and taking the uncentered root gives lambda_d=d lambda−H floor(d lambda/H), hence

    I_d=d I−floor(d lambda/H),                      (7)

## Addendum: what the full native label product can actually certify

The horizontal axes make the known roughening explicit, including deletion. For (a,b)=(k,0), lambda=tk, 0<=t<k, the labels are E=k(t+1). The public integer-zero deletion removes the endpoint E=H; all E=k*s, 1<=s<k, survive that deletion. Complete-swap pruning may remove a horizontal query row if its vertical partner was independently deleted, so one should not silently rely on every such pair after arbitrary pruning. The clean robust implementation may include these publicly acquired axis labels as AUXILIARY root-cleaning inputs regardless of whether their query rows were removed. That costs soft k and does not reintroduce a deleted query leaf. This recovers factorial/small-coindex roughening already credited, not a new stronger native sieve.

There is a useful exact conditional structural reduction. At the SMALLER factor P<=sqrtN<=k³, after this known roughening every prime divisor of its scalar order exceeds k. Its order therefore has at most two prime factors counted with multiplicity. Now apply cleaning by the nonaxis-label product M_nonaxis:

* If a two-prime order loses any primary factor, the surviving order is prime, unless it drops below the prepared threshold and the first-drop routine factors N.
* A square prime order sharing a prime with M_nonaxis is killed completely, hence produces that factor-return branch.
* If the order is prime and there is no center collision beta^E=1 at any supplied label, no label is divisible by that order. Cleaning is then an automorphism and changes no order. The same identity-on-order branch also occurs for a two-prime order whose support misses every label.

Thus nonaxis label incidence gives a conditional prime-order reduction on the smaller component, or a factor. This is a legitimate use of the split, but not an acquired test deciding which structural case applies and not a reduction of the physical query. In particular, the full catalogue does not here provide a proof that its nonaxis labels meet one prime factor of every possible two-prime local order.

For a proposed stronger roughness threshold U, the missing arithmetic assertion is precise: every relevant order prime ell in(k,U] dividing P−1 or Q−1 must divide at least one supplied label a+bN+lambda. The native root equation is modulo H, whereas this incidence is modulo ell. At the P-component it reads

    a+bQ+lambda=0 mod ell.

The virtual cover only supplies E=HI modulo ell; after cleaning it supplies an invertible I, not a center hit. Forcing the stronger incidence therefore requires selecting a DIFFERENT actual conic point whose index is0 modulo ell. That is the additional native selection lemma, not a consequence of merely listing all H-roots. No such selection lemma, >k roughness guarantee, or prime-order structure theorem for both components is established in this pass.


## Sharpening: any smaller-side order loss already returns a factor

The previous conditional prime-order reduction can be strengthened. After the familiar k-roughening, let r_P be the current scalar or projected clock order at the smaller factor P. Then r_P<P<=sqrtN<=k³, and every prime divisor of r_P exceeds k. If powering by any supplied label removes a nontrivial primary factor, the removed quotient is at least k+1. Consequently the new order is strictly less than k³/(k+1)<k²<16H. For the paid supplied-short-label cleaning schedule, the already paid first-drop procedure therefore factors N; it cannot leave a retained prime order above the prepared threshold after a genuine smaller-side loss.

Thus, on the continuing branch, arbitrary nonaxis-label cleaning is an AUTOMORPHISM at the smaller component: its order is unchanged. This strengthens and supersedes the earlier “retained order becomes prime” alternative for that component. It neither proves the unchanged order prime nor supplies an acquired order; the identity-on-order branch can have two large prime factors. The conclusion uses the smaller-side bound and does not automatically apply to the larger factor.
