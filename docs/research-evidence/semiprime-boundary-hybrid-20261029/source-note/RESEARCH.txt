DETERMINISTIC COLOUR/BOUNDARY HYBRID: A PROVED LEAF-EXPONENT IMPROVEMENT
Scratch investigation, 29 October 2026

Status and scope
================

This note proves a general deterministic exact-contraction algorithm. Its
exponent is M/2 + O((M^2 Delta)^(1/3)), instead of enumerating all 2^M global
assignments. Under the Riemann repository's current degree and leaf caps, this
becomes 2^((1/2 + o(1)) s/sqrt(log_2 s)) for an entire leaf contraction,
including coefficient arithmetic and constructing the partition.

This is a paper proof with an exact Python prototype, not a new Lean theorem
or an integrated factoring implementation. It improves the LEAF exponent.
The global class 2^O(ell/sqrt(log ell)) is unchanged by the results here.

A second result tightens the actual recursion CALL-COUNT bound: the coefficient
98304 in its natural exponential can be replaced by 393216/7. This recovers
slack from the existing factor-16 graph shrink theorem and a geometric sum.
It is an improved analysis of the existing tree, not a runtime change.

The colour partition here is an algorithmic grouping of tensor vertices. It
does not import the old arithmetic residue-colour catalogue, nor assert that
a Jones/Habiro/skein identity became part of the factorer. The useful carryover
from the knot/colour investigations is exact contraction through boundaries,
with every boundary factor and intermediate jet coordinate retained.

Boundary contraction itself is an established technique; no literature-wide
novelty claim is made. For context see Markov and Shi, "Simulating quantum
computation by contracting tensor networks", SIAM J. Comput. 38 (2008),
https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0511069 . The specific bound below is proved
directly, without assuming a supplied tree decomposition or separator oracle.

1. The exact object being contracted
===================================

Let G=(V,E) be a finite loopless multigraph with M=|E| binary edges and maximum
degree Delta. Parallel edges and isolated vertices are permitted. Each edge
has TWO distinct incident vertices. At each vertex v an explicitly stored
table T_v : {0,1}^{E(v)} -> R is provided, where R is a commutative semiring.

We compute

    Z = sum_{x in {0,1}^E} product_{v in V} T_v(x restricted to E(v)).       (1)

For the bit-complexity statement assume one R-value has a canonical encoding
of b bits, and addition/multiplication are implementable in poly(b) bit work.
Finite modular truncated polynomial rings used by the repo satisfy this,
with b the length of the WHOLE coefficient vector, not just one coordinate.
Over arbitrary unbounded rings, the arithmetic-operation statement still
holds, but a separate coefficient-growth bound would be needed for bit cost.

No loops or higher-arity incidences are silently accepted. The repo's
well-formed bipartite tensor networks meet this two-endpoint hypothesis.

2. The contraction identity and its precise work parameter
=========================================================

Choose K>=2, and assign each vertex one of K colours. C_g denotes colour g,
numbered 0,...,K-1. For a vertex set S, E(S) consists of edges with both ends
in S, and boundary(S) consists of edges with exactly one end in S.

Build the exact boundary tensor

    B_S(y) = sum_{z in {0,1}^{E(S)}} product_{v in S} T_v((y,z)|E(v)).       (2)

For a single colour class C_g this takes, up to polynomial factors, 2^{I_g}
work, where

    I_g = |{e : at least one endpoint of e lies in C_g}|.                 (3)

It does NOT enumerate assignments to edges disjoint from C_g.

For disjoint vertex sets S,T, finite distributivity gives

    B_{S union T}(y)
      = sum_{z in {0,1}^{E(S,T)}} B_S((y,z)|boundary(S))
                                    B_T((y,z)|boundary(T)).             (4)

Here E(S,T)=boundary(S) intersect boundary(T). This is the complete set of
variables that become internal upon merging. The new boundary is the
symmetric difference of the two old boundaries. No omitted scalar,
normalizing determinant, inverse, or nonzero test occurs in (4).

Start with B_{C_0}; merge C_1,...,C_{K-1} in order. For j=2,...,K let
P_{j-1}=C_0 union ... union C_{j-2}. The j-th merge costs 2^{U_j} times a
polynomial, with

    U_j = |boundary(P_{j-1}) union boundary(C_{j-1})|.                    (5)

The final boundary is empty, and its scalar entry is exactly (1).
Consequently, with

    W = max( I_0,...,I_{K-1}, U_2,...,U_K ),                            (6)

both work and storage are at most poly(|V|,M,K,b) 2^W. Isolated vertices
contribute their scalar tables, including zero and non-units. An empty colour
class contributes 1, not a spurious factor of 2. The M=0 case is just the
product of all isolated scalar tables.

3. A cheap deterministic partition with a uniform bound on W
===========================================================

Independent uniform random vertex colours are used ONLY to prove that a
polynomial-time conditional-expectation algorithm succeeds. Runtime does
not sample random colours, search all K^|V| assignments, or accept a partition
as advice.

For one edge, the probability of touching C_g is 2/K-1/K^2, so

    mu(I_g) = M(2/K-1/K^2).                                           (7)

For U_j, place a colour into one of the three regions: old prefix (j-1
colours), new class (one colour), or future (K-j colours). An edge is counted
exactly when its endpoints lie in different regions. Therefore

    mu(U_j) = 2M [j(K-j+1)-1]/K^2.                                   (8)

Since j(K+1-j) <= (K+1)^2/4, every one of the 2K-1 loads X has

    mu(X) <= (1/2+1/K)M.                                             (9)

We need a simultaneous bound on all these loads, not just on a single cut.
Each X is a sum of M Bernoulli indicators. Indicators on vertex-disjoint
edges are independent. Every individual variance is at most 1/4, and every
covariance is at most 1/4. The number of unordered pairs of edges sharing an
endpoint is at most sum_v binom(deg(v),2). Parallel pairs being counted twice
in this last upper bound is harmless. Hence

    Var(X) <= M/4 + (1/2) sum_v binom(deg(v),2)
            = (sum_v deg(v)^2 - M)/4
            <= M Delta/2.                                          (10)

Define the nonnegative potential, centred at the UNCONDITIONAL means,

    Phi = sum_{all 2K-1 loads X} (X - mu(X))^2.

By (10), E Phi <= (2K-1)M Delta/2. Fix vertices one at a time. Given all
colours fixed so far, compute the K possible conditional expectations of
Phi for the next vertex, and select a least one. Their average is the current
conditional expectation. Thus the selected value never increases it. At
termination the actual Phi is at most its original expectation, so every X
satisfies

    |X-mu(X)| <= sqrt((2K-1)M Delta/2).

Combining with (9) proves the explicit, deterministic width bound

    W <= (1/2+1/K)M + sqrt((2K-1)M Delta/2).                           (11)

The potential can be evaluated in polynomial time. Expand each X^2 into
single-edge and edge-pair indicators. Each product depends on at most four
unfixed vertex colours; enumerate at most K^4 possibilities. There are
O(K M^2) terms, K choices per vertex, and |V| vertex steps. The deliberately
loose total is O(|V| M^2 K^6) elementary rational-arithmetic operations.
All conditional probabilities have denominator K^j for j<=4. Means have
denominator K^2, so multiplying the complete potential by K^4 makes every
calculation integral, with bit lengths O(log(M+1)+log(K+1)). There is no
unaccounted exponential partition search.

The prototype uses a faster equivalent variance formula: only pairs sharing
a vertex need joint expectations; disjoint pairs remain independent after
conditioning. Its assertions check each conditional-average identity exactly.

4. Choosing K: the exponent theorem
==================================

For M>0, 1<=Delta<=M. Put

    x = (M/Delta)^(1/3),  K = max(2,ceil(x)),  A = (M^2 Delta)^(1/3).

Then x<=K<=2x, so M/K<=A and

    sqrt((2K-1)M Delta/2) <= sqrt(K M Delta) <= sqrt(2) A.

Theorem. The exact contraction (1) has a deterministic algorithm with

    T, S <= poly(|V|,M,b) 2^{M/2+(1+sqrt(2))(M^2 Delta)^(1/3)}
          <= poly(|V|,M,b) 2^{M/2+3(M^2 Delta)^(1/3)}.                 (12)

Here T and S are bit work and bit storage under the coefficient-encoding
assumption in Section 1. With unit-cost semiring operations, the corresponding
arithmetic-operation bound also follows. The entire input is paid for: a
vertex's degree is no greater than its colour class's incident-edge load,
so every explicitly stored local table has at most 2^W entries.

For Delta=o(M), (12) is 2^{M/2+o(M)}, up to the stated coefficient and vertex
factors. Without that condition, use the smaller of (12) and brute force.
There is no claim of improvement for every small or high-degree graph; a
star can already contain an explicit 2^M table at its central vertex.

The easy sufficient condition 3(Delta/M)^(1/3)<1/2 makes the displayed
exponential term strictly smaller than 2^M. Its asymptotic constant 1/2 is
not a practical twofold runtime improvement; it concerns a leading exponent.

5. A bit algorithm rather than a free table-lookup model
======================================================

The Python prototype uses ordinary dictionaries/lists for convenience.
The bit bound in (12) can be implemented with sequential records and sorting:

* For a bucket, enumerate its N=2^{I_g} incident-edge assignments, represented
  by O(M)-bit records. For each vertex in the bucket, form N local-address
  requests, sort them by local address, and merge with that vertex's explicitly
  stored table. There are at most N table entries since deg(v)<=I_g. Attach
  the values, sort back by assignment identifier, and multiply into the
  assignment's accumulator. Sort by boundary address and sum equal-key runs.

* For a merge, enumerate N=2^{U_j} union-boundary assignments. Retrieve the two
  input boundary entries by the same sorted request/merge process, multiply,
  then sort by the new boundary key and sum runs. Both input tables have at
  most N entries. Boundary masks, addresses, and restrictions take poly(M)
  bit operations; no enormous tensor entry is supplied by an oracle.

* Standard finite-tape merge sorting uses O(log N) sequential merge passes,
  with polynomial record-copy/comparison cost per record. Record length is
  O(M+b), log N<=M, and modular coefficient operations keep values canonical
  and bounded by b. Thus these costs contribute only polynomial factors.
  Sequentially process vertices and groups; no product of two exponentially
  large request lists or free random-access assumption is needed.

This is an explicit bit-algorithm construction and cost proof for the new
leaf algorithm. It is not a claim that the repository's existing native
program has already been lowered to this implementation or certified in Lean.

6. Substitution into the current Riemann leaves
=============================================

All referenced sources are in /home/dbsanfte/riemann/formal/RiemannGaussian.
Source hashes and the inspected repository HEAD accompany this note.

SemiprimeD1LeafCounter.run builds all 2^M global assignments and sums them.
Its LeafRun even stores that whole table. The word/tape recursive caller,
however, consumes the leaf's contracted words and report, not the global
assignment table. A replacement needs a leaner leaf result and a proved
payload equality; preserving the unnecessary LeafRun.table output would
erase the improvement.

SemiprimeD1TensorNetwork.WellFormed provides exactly the two-endpoint incidence
condition: each variable belongs to one left and one right vertex, with
local port lists matching these incidences. It gives a loopless bipartite
multigraph, even though the theorem above also allows non-bipartite graphs.

SemiprimeD1OriginalStateResources.Bound.degree and eventually_original give,
for every actually reached network, the uniform cap

    lambda = ceil(log_2(s+2)),
    Delta <= degreeCap(s) = 2^floor((9 lambda+9)/10) = O(s^(9/10)).       (13)

The current cap is 9/10, not the older 3/4 appearing in earlier investigations.
SemiprimeD1Schedule.leafSize and SemiprimeD1LeafRate give, for the actual
sufficient-fuel controller and all stopped leaves,

    M <= L_s := floor(s/(floor(sqrt(lambda))+1))+1
             <= s/sqrt(lambda)+1.

This is NOT asserted for arbitrary manually truncated recursion fuel: a
zero-fuel root can intentionally invoke a large brute-force leaf. The public
controller supplies the scheduled fuel.

Write L=s/sqrt(lambda). Applying (12) uniformly over stopped leaves gives

    M/2 + 3(M^2 Delta)^(1/3)
      <= L/2 + O(s^(29/30)/lambda^(1/3)) + O(1)
       = (1/2+o(1))L,                                                (14)

because s^(-1/30) lambda^(1/6) tends to zero. Small leaves need not themselves
have Delta=o(M); monotonicity in M and the ORIGINAL-scale cap yield the uniform
bound (14) anyway. Actual degrees can always be capped additionally by M.

Coefficient arithmetic remains subleading. The existing invariant bounds
vertices by 5s, the full jet rank by (4s)^(lambda+1), and scalar precision by
s*201(lambda+1)^2*2^(4lambda). Thus a full coefficient value has
b=2^O(log^2 s) bits. Schoolbook truncated-jet arithmetic is polynomial in b
and the axis metadata. Its log-cost is O(log^2 s), and explicit local-table
inventory contributes O(Delta) in the exponent. Both are o(L). The broader
existing scalar envelope 2^{(lambda+1)^3} is also 2^{o(L)}.

Therefore the complete new leaf work is at most

    2^{(1/2+o(1))L},                                                (15)

where the previous eager-leaf envelope was 2^{(1+o(1))L}. This is the positive
algorithmic result of this investigation: a uniform factor of two in the
LEADING LEAF EXPONENT, not just a finite collection of faster examples.
Convergence can be slow with the s^(9/10) degree cap; the finite-size formula
(11), or its min with brute force, is the honest quantitative bound.

Correctness substitutes structurally into the recursive counter because
(2)-(4) compute the identical element of its current coefficient ring. The
count and first moment survive whenever that ring has the packed marker.
ALL current jet coordinates are retained, not discarded based on a final
readout cancellation. Equality of canonical words additionally requires
using the existing canonical coefficient encoding, an integration proof not
performed here. No old collision-acquisition or knot-normalization oracle
is reintroduced.

7. A separate tightening of the actual recursion call exponent
=============================================================

This section concerns the current actual child list, with every grid point,
mode and all 64 groups still included. It is independent of the new leaves.

SemiprimeD1TensorNetworkRecursion.child_shrink already proves

    16 * scope(child graph) <= edgeCount(parent).

SemiprimeD1RecursiveCallState.child_rows identifies each actual child's rows
with exactly that graph. The currently used child_shrink_eight weakens the
factor to 8. The uniform eventual packing guard and reachable degree invariant
already discharge the stronger theorem's hypotheses, just as they discharge
the weaker one. Consequently, induction along actual child-list membership
proves, for EVERY reachable call at depth d with original root size <=s,

    M_d * 16^d <= s.                                                 (16)

Root: d=0 and M_0<=s. Step: child size <= parent size/16, and actual depth
increments by one. Every parent has the inherited degree cap and well-formed
rows, so the same eventually-valid original-input packing guard applies.
This proof introduces no favourable-instance or chosen-mode assumption.

Let b0=floor(lambda/131072), u_d=floor(b0/2^d). At active nodes u_d>0. The
existing exact-floor lemma SemiprimeD1PathRate.sqrt_floor_le states

    sqrt(b0) <= 2^(d+1) sqrt(u_d).

Using (16), it follows that

    M_d/sqrt(u_d) <= (2s/sqrt(b0)) (1/8)^d.                           (17)

The current menu bound is exp(48 M_d/sqrt(u_d)). The already-paid grid/group
factor G_s=2^(4lambda+7) therefore bounds actual branching by

    G_s exp(A_s (1/8)^d),  A_s=96s/sqrt(b0).                          (18)

There are at most lambda+1 child generations. The number of nodes at depth t
is at most the product of the first t branching bounds. Summing over depths,
and using G_s>=1 and sum_{d>=0}(1/8)^d=8/7, gives

    count <= (lambda+2) G_s^(lambda+1) exp((8/7)A_s).                 (19)

This is the same elementary finite-tree induction as the existing count_le,
with ratio 1/8 and its tighter geometric sum. It bounds all retained calls,
not a single favourable path or only one child per mode. The existing fuel
cap lets the same inequality hold uniformly over supplied fuels for call
COUNT, even though the leaf-work bound requires scheduled sufficient fuel.

Finally the already-proved floor bound sqrt(lambda)<=512 sqrt(b0) gives

    (8/7) A_s <= (393216/7) s/sqrt(lambda).

Thus, using 2^x<=exp(x) for the same grid term as the repository,

    count <= (lambda+2)
      exp( (393216/7)L + (4lambda+7)(lambda+1) ).                    (20)

The current displayed coefficient is 98304; the new one is exactly 4/7 of
it. This is a 3/7 reduction in that proved leading CALL-COUNT upper-bound
coefficient. It is analysis slack removed, not a claim that the running tree
now visits 3/7 fewer calls. More accurate square-root/floor estimates could
further tighten constants without changing the asymptotic class.

8. What these results do and do not say about the complete exponent
=================================================================

The main scale is unchanged:

    L = Theta(ell/sqrt(log ell)),  since s=2^20 ell.

The recursive modes still carry an exponential-in-L worst-case envelope.
Halving a leaf exponent is not enough to replace sqrt(log ell) by log ell
or to prove 2^{O(ell^alpha)} for alpha<1. Nothing in this note establishes
such a stronger complexity class or a new factoring record.

There is a further accounting detail: the current numeric whole-counter
envelope is

    localEnvelope(C,k,s) * (calls+1)^3,

not localEnvelope times calls. It retains a C*degreeCap local-table reserve
and a cubic multiplicity reserve. Consequently one must NOT report the
combined leading coefficient as "98304+log(2)" for this envelope.

For fixed C,k, the old numerical envelope, using its displayed tree bound,
is at most

    exp( [3*98304 + log(2) + o(1)] L ).                             (21)

If a new implementation is joined to the corresponding envelope with only
the leaf exponent replaced using (14), the improved numerical envelope is

    exp( [3*(393216/7) + log(2)/2 + o(1)] L ).                      (22)

Equation (22) is proved as a bound on that DEFINED numeric envelope, using
(14) and (20). It is an integration target for the changed counter, not an
already-proved bound on the current program's unchanged clock. Tightening
the call-count analysis alone can improve the old numeric envelope while
retaining its old leaf exponent. The new leaf routine must still be connected
to the recursive program and its resource report before attributing (22)
to an implemented counter.

The repo's existing WordTapeOriginalRate endpoint explicitly bounds a mixed
retained report, with full primitive source/control/memory simulation still
separate. This note does not silently close that separate proof obligation.

9. Executable validation and remaining work
==========================================

Run, entirely inside this scratch directory:

    python3 -B test_boundary_hybrid.py

The recorded run in validation.json passed:
  - 48 exact comparisons with global brute-force contraction, on empty,
    isolated, parallel, disconnected, cyclic, complete, star and bipartite
    graphs, with K=2 and K=4, over modular rings and whole truncated jets;
  - 16 exhaustive checks of load means and conditional potentials;
  - all 35 colourings of a small graph for K=2 and K=3;
  - isolated zero/non-unit scalar preservation;
  - a square-zero-marker example returning count=2 and first moment=15;
  - intermediate mixed jet products, without projecting to the final moment;
  - malformed endpoint/loop rejection and automatic K>2 selection.

The prototype verifies that executed assignment dimensions equal the load
definitions used in the proof. These finite tests support code correctness;
the whole-family exponent follows from Sections 2-6, not from test coverage.
The prototype is not a Lean proof or a calibrated primitive clock simulator.

To integrate the positive algorithmic result, the main thread would need to:
  (a) implement the boundary-leaf result type and deterministic partition;
  (b) prove exact canonical-word equality to the existing leaf semantics;
  (c) account for sorted joins and polynomial partition work in the chosen
      concrete cost model, including all local-table reads;
  (d) replace both inactive and fuel-exhausted leaf call sites, and transport
      the recursive correctness/report joins;
  (e) use the actual factor-16 child shrink and the tighter geometric bound.

Those are implementation/formalization tasks for this specific leaf gain.
A stronger overall asymptotic class still requires a new mathematical bound
on recursive mode acquisition/contraction (or another global bottleneck).

A possible next hybrid is to compute local child responses as functions of
only their incident mode coordinates, then contract the resulting mode graph.
It would require a uniform bound on weighted boundary loads AND the cost of
building all local response tables. Modes can in principle concentrate on
few groups; this note does not prove those new bounds or assume them away.

All work here is confined to this scratch directory. No main-tree source,
Git state, running agent, or publication process was changed.
