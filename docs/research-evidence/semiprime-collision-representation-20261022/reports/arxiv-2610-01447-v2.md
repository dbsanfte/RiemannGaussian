## Main conclusion for our project

The paper claims a dimension-independent Poincare/Cheeger bound for isotropic LOG-CONCAVE REAL probability measures. It supplies no integer-factorization algorithm, exact modular collision evaluator, or direct replacement for the native J~m,L~m² query. Its strongest potentially transferable mechanisms are exact compensation for centering losses, averaging a block before restarting an iteration, and keeping a multiscale recursion's accumulated loss bounded. Applying those mechanisms here requires a new public positive-energy or log-concave-carrier construction preserving actual scalar collisions. That construction has not been found.

This is an applicability reading, not certification of the claimed KLS breakthrough. Statements below distinguish the authors' theorem/proofs from proposed project bridges. The requested v2 itself was read at substantive theorem/proof passages, not substituted by its abstract. Sections 1--5 and references were read by this reviewer; the existing collaborating reviewers cover section 6 and sections 7--9, with their reports recorded below when delivered. This reviewer also read Proposition 6.22 and its proof and the final Proposition 9.25/9.26 proof chain for the synthesis.

## Exact theorem hypotheses and proof chain

**Theorems 1.1 and 9.1** (printed pp.2 and99; formal statement in section9): the claimed bound is uniform in dimension for isotropic log-concave probability measures on R^n. The test functions are locally Lipschitz with finite Dirichlet energy. This is a functional inequality, not a bit-complexity algorithm. No randomized/quantum factoring model is proposed. Stochastic processes occur in the proof, but their exact implementation cost is not supplied as an oracle for our problem.

**Sections 2--3, pp.8--18:** the authors define real covariance, log-concavity and Appell polynomials with lower-derivative means subtracted. Appell functions are explicitly not assumed orthogonal. They justify strong-convex regular approximations, Bochner identities, a compact-resolvent weighted diffusion H=-Delta+grad W dot grad, and its inverse square root. These hypotheses matter: modular row distributions, discrete lattice-point measures, and complex character weights do not automatically satisfy them. A full-dimensional log-concave continuous measure has convex support; actual finite modular collision support is not itself such a measure.

**Theorem 4.1 and Lemmas 4.2--4.3, pp.19--24:** the polynomial variance claim is bounded by 1024^d(d!)^4 times the squared Hilbert--Schmidt leading tensor norm. Covariance-normalized stochastic localization creates strongly log-concave posteriors; the proof tracks all derivative moments with one covariance metric and includes cross variations. This is not a fast algorithm evaluating the moments: tensors, posterior covariances and integrals are proof-side analytic objects.

**Theorem 5.1, Lemma 5.2, pp.24--26:** degree-dependent polynomial coefficients imply a curvature-dependent Poincare bound. The key normalization is exact. For a centered family u, v=||u||², b=||H^-1/2 u||², beta=v/b, define h=sqrt(beta)H^-1/2u and chi=||H^1/2u||²-beta v. Then chi>=0 and the normalization defect z satisfies ||grad z||²=chi/beta. The hierarchy tracks mass and energy separately:

    v_(r+1)=v_r-p_r,
    e_(r+1)<=e_r-a v_r-chi_r.

No discarded means are silently ignored. The combination is essential to their contradiction proof.

**Lemmas 5.3--5.5, pp.26--29, and proof of Theorem5.1, pp.29--32:** subset-incidence spectral bounds recover partially symmetrized tensors. Adjacent-swap defects are propagated with their explicit iteration count; a lag kernel bounds their aggregate without charging a separate constant per degree. The result is a global loss budget. Neither the subset-incidence operator nor its singular-value bound is a collision incidence matrix for our native rows; identifying those would be an additional theorem.

**Proposition 6.22, pp.52--54 (proof read):** an averaged inverse-gradient block has first-seven centering loss and starting excess O(z^-3), while a whole-block propagation constant is bounded by2. The averaged object is an ORTHOGONAL DIRECT SUM of iterates, not a signed sum that could cancel source mass. The construction starts with a top right singular vector of a compact analytic operator power and an existence-defined radius z. Those are not publicly acquired in our setting. This proposition is a real compensation mechanism but not an implementable fast block algorithm merely because the proof names a family.

**Section9 final chain, pp.133--138 (relevant concluding proofs read):** the authors retain coefficient caps on disjoint degree intervals and bound the combined tail once, then assign geometrically decreasing outer loss budgets. Proposition9.25 maintains a prescribed-depth profile with bounded accumulated amplitudes/offsets; Proposition9.26 makes finite choices of depth and outer rounds and passes by regular approximation. The distinction between analytic loss constants and arithmetic operation count is crucial. Uniform bounded loss does not make a branching computation near-linear unless the block operators themselves have a paid compressed implementation.

## Concrete bridge A: a public log-concave lattice carrier

A possible use would start with an explicitly supplied convex body or log-concave density, a public rounding map into native pair/index candidates, and a proof that at least inverse-polylog mass lands on useful ORIGINAL scalar collision leaves. Covariance control and cheap membership/density oracles must be acquired in softm. Given such a bridge, the paper's claimed KLS estimate could improve the conductance analysis of an existing sampling/rounding routine. That would still be a randomized route unless separately derandomized; it would not immediately meet the current deterministic all-input target.

The natural row lattice and public factor interval are available, but no such high-mass rounding carrier is supplied. Uniform continuous measure on a convex relaxation places zero mass on exact isolated equality loci unless a positive-volume decoding neighborhood is proved. Uniform enumeration of J*L native cells has only the coverage guarantee of at least one useful cell, which does not imply inverse-polylog collision mass. Convexification or adding Gaussian noise does not prove that rounding preferentially reaches that cell. An attempt to define a potential favoring gcd-positive leaves must pay evaluation and normalization of that potential; the unacquired long collision product cannot be used as its oracle.

This is a missing-bridge statement, not a theorem excluding every log-concave transport. KLS controls conductance/variance under its assumptions; it does not lower-bound the mass of an arbitrary designated event. Even a fixed well-behaved interval distribution can have arbitrarily small measurable subsets. Therefore a dimension-free bound alone cannot supply the missing event-mass theorem.

The native lattice reductions already operate in fixed small dimension. Removing a polylog or dimension-dependent conductance factor there would not by itself erase the m^1/2 gap between the known query and softm, and polylog improvements are already suppressed in the project's soft notation. A relevant carrier must reduce acquisition/search mass or give a new exact contraction, not only improve a hidden logarithm.

## Concrete bridge B: a source-preserving compensated energy recursion

The more promising transferable proof pattern is to seek a PUBLIC Hilbert-space operator for the native paired-difference family with all of the following properties:

1. its input, output actions and block startup are acquired in softm, with carrier size charged;
2. an explicit nonnegative energy/mass quantity detects useful same-leaf scalar collisions or factors;
3. each compression has an EXACT defect identity and a loss budget preserving that collision certificate;
4. averaged blocks control both startup loss and propagated defect, and restrictions to pair groups retain the certificate/localization;
5. positive energy or termination can be converted to a proper gcd or original nonzero annihilator without acquiring the entire long product.

The project has paid some matrix-vector actions, but not an operator satisfying these five properties. Its products of differences detect a union of zero leaves, while generic variance centering is additive. Character sums may cancel distinct leaves or place weights on unrelated components. In particular replacing a product-zero condition by a small variance, or using a hidden local zero projector, would not be a public source-preserving construction. The paper provides neither that identification nor the required discrete operator.

If a valid operator were found, the paper's exact-defect accounting suggests how to avoid repeatedly paying a fixed loss per recursion depth: average blocks, retain all centering terms, and sum one propagated kernel. This is a concrete design pattern worth retaining. It is not an acquired new collision observable today.

## Source/model limits and status

The paper's author references to faster convex-body sampling/volume concern real convex geometry. There is no theorem giving an exact modular resultant from supplied row exponents, no finite-character Fourier identity, no cheap hidden-prime order/trace oracle, and no deterministic exact-arithmetic runtime theorem for this project. A Poincare inequality can be meaningful for an eventual auxiliary sampler, but supplied-oracle, covariance, precision, rounding mass and failure-probability costs would all have to be counted. It cannot substitute for preprocessing or factor recovery merely by analogy.

Provisional project verdict: useful analytic compensation ideas, but no currently valid theorem bridge to near-linear native collision acquisition. The strongest next question is whether a paid source-preserving positive-energy block operator exists; log-concave transport additionally needs a proved collision-mass bound and would initially change the computational model. No factoring exponent or frozen claim is changed by this assessment.

## Integrated sections-7--9 reading and final assessment

The existing-agent report `kls-sections7-9.md` completes the substantive later-section reading. Section7 transfers an existential controlled posterior to a dimensional profile. Section8 still has a fixed multiplicative cost per outer round. Section9's real additional work is to eliminate that accumulation while controlling the growing thresholds: Proposition9.23 (pp.129--132) keeps the SAME actual family, retained prefix and matched energy budget, and charges a finite chain of coefficient caps through disjoint degree bands and one combined tail. Proposition9.25 (pp.133--137) has summable amplitudes/reserves but growing starting depths; Lemma9.24 removes fixed polynomial argument inflation BEFORE contracting it. Proposition9.26 (p.138) selects finite parameters only after a uniform all-stage profile is established. These details prevent replacing substantive uniformity with the phrase 'iterate until small.'

The project analogue must retain the SAME labelled native row/index object. Separate cheap character or norm conditions cannot be combined as if their zeros came from the same leaf. If a future acquired nonnegative INTEGER collision count were approximated with a certified total absolute error below1/2, rounding could give exact detection; for normalized rare-hit mass the accuracy must scale with the total number of cells. The KLS theorem does not supply that approximation or the public count. Positive mass retention and bounded analytic amplitudes are not bounds on acquired vector lengths, convolution degrees, covariance computation, or precision.

A public high-mass log-concave carrier or such an exact positive-energy contraction remains to be constructed. Frozen project files and its claimed rate are unchanged.
