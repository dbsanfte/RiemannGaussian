**PASS for root's separation theorem on the pure-K two-monic input.** This pays global row separation and actual tag interpolation in soft J on a good-family continuation. It does not acquire the long norm, and the derivative-screen algorithm itself has prior credit.

## Both good orientations and exact distinctness

Write P<Q. At the nonsplit prime, family A is good when that prime is P, and family B is good when it is Q. In BOTH cases its row root is a power of the same local pi with integer exponent

    e_j=Q+c_j P+ell_j,

where c_j=a_j for A or b_j for B. On pure K=2·3^e the c-values form the single step-6 AP N mod6, each with precisely the two opposite phase tags. This use of the AP and two-element fiber is important; it should not be claimed from an arbitrary conductor-aware modulus without a separate argument.

For different coefficients, |c_j-c_l|>=6, while the centered phase difference has absolute value <H. Therefore

    |e_j-e_l| >= 6P-|ell_j-ell_l| > 6P-H > 0.

For equal c, the two phases are exactly +/-ell. Their difference is nonzero: ell²=4cN mod H makes ell a 3-unit, excluding ell=0 and the centered half-period. Its absolute value is <H. Thus all exponent labels differ.

Since P<=sqrt(N)<=K³ and |c_j-c_l|<K,

    |e_j-e_l| < K P+H < 2H² < ord_R(pi).

The good-family roots are therefore ALL distinct in the nonsplit field, not merely distinct at the one hit. The same inequalities work for either good orientation; no smaller-nonsplit assumption was inserted.

## Paid global certificate and what it initializes

Acquire F=product(X-y_j), F', and all F'(y_j) by monic MPE, then their product Delta. This is soft J without prior separation. At the good field Delta is nonzero. Consequently gcd(N,Norm(Delta)) is proper or one, never N. A proper gcd terminates; on a unit norm Delta is an algebra unit, so every factor F'(y_j), and every pair difference in those factored derivative values, is a unit globally. This uses the elementary commutative-ring fact that factors of a unit product are units, not a field-only implication.

Thus the actual tag interpolation polynomial

    W(X)=sum_j [w_j/F'(y_j)] F(X)/(X-y_j)

is acquired in soft J by certified inverses and a weighted product tree, with W(y_j)=w_j globally. No J² pair expansion is needed. The same certificate also makes H0=XF'-JF invertible modulo F, and Hw=W*H0 modulo F. These are useful paid normalizations; inverses of the long collision remainder g are still forbidden.

The good family is unrecognized. Apply the bounded certificate to both families; a saturated norm on the wrong family can be declined, not inverted or assigned a generic annihilator decoder. At least one family has the proper-or-unit guarantee above. A unit certificate on either attempt is sound globally, regardless of goodness.

## Stronger clock-block separation: valid but conditional on scale

Consider the actual expanded roots y_j q^r for 0<=r<b (opposite shift convention gives the same bounds). Their good-field exponents are e_j+Hr. For different c, the sufficient condition

    bH < 6P-H                                      (1)

excludes integer equality, since the added shift difference has magnitude at most (b-1)H. This is conservative: the sharper no-equality bound from these estimates is bH<6P. For equal c and different phase tags, their phase difference lies strictly between -H and H and is nonzero, so adding an integer H shift cannot cancel it. Same-tag distinct shifts are nonzero as well.

Under (1), the absolute expanded difference is <KP+bH<KP+6P<2H² for K>16, so no order wrap occurs. Therefore these expanded roots are distinct at the good field. This is a genuine block-separation promise under the displayed inequality, not just a formal generic-position claim.

However P is unknown and the existing interface gives only P>16H. That licenses a fixed constant b (for example b=95 satisfies (1)), not b growing as a power of K. A public stronger lower bound P>=P0 would license corresponding b; it must come from an actual paid supplier/screen or a valid bin restriction. It cannot be inferred from K~N^(1/6).

To turn the block promise into global certified inverses, one direct algorithm builds

    F_b(X)=product_(r=0)^(b-1) q^(rJ) F(q^(-r)X)

and screens its derivative at all Jb expanded roots. This costs soft(Jb), with the same proper-or-unit good-field guarantee. Thus polylogarithmic b fits soft K, while a power-growing b does not have a newly paid soft-K certificate from this argument. A compressed discriminant/shift-resultant supplier would be an additional operation requiring proof and initialization. The local separation theorem alone supplies neither that global certificate at lower cost nor long-clock contraction.

**Disposition:** the native separation and soft-J W acquisition are correct, useful preparation and an honest extension of the new uniform promise. No full-query improvement follows; retain the original soft-K^(5/4) weighted bound and soft-K^(3/2) monic fallback.
