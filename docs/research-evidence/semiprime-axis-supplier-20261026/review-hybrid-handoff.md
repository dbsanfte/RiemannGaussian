## First-order values and one-prime validation

The marker first slice at the good field is exactly A(1-Yr), with A
nonzero; thus V=A and U=Ar. The other prime may be split or singular
and need not have the same hit pattern. Norm(V) cannot vanish at the
good nonsplit prime, so its gcd is unit or proper, never saturated
there. After a certified inverse, the tau coefficient of U/V vanishes
at that prime. It yields a proper gcd or a global base scalar. The
integer scan through n candidates then gives a proper factor or a
correct nonsplit-factor residue on the promised attempt. A false
attempt may look numerically valid; only the good attempt's provenance
is guaranteed. All such candidates must be preserved for the bounded
HH validator, rather than trusting a false attempt as a certificate.

Complementing the candidate by N/r modulo n handles the larger factor
without changing its provenance. Nonunit residues cannot describe a
prime factor on the prepared branch and are safely discarded. The
algorithm promises verified factor or bounded failure on wrong inputs.
