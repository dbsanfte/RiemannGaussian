## 1. What one-prime information now suffices

Let B=(Z/NZ)[tau]/(tau²-D), Jacobi(D,N)=-1. Exactly one prime R of N is nonsplit. It may be P or Q. Suppose an acquired pair U,V in B obeys, at this ONE prime,

    V!=0, U/V=r=R mod n, 0<=r<n, n|K.

No promise is required at the other prime. Norm(V) is nonzero at the good field. Its gcd therefore factors or certifies a global algebra unit; a saturated norm is bounded failure on an unpromised pair. On the unit branch alpha=U/V=alpha0+alpha1*tau. Since alpha1=0 modulo R, gcd(alpha1,N) factors or proves alpha1=0 globally. In the latter case scan the integer labels 0<=s<n using gcd(alpha0-s,N). The correct s gives a proper factor or a GLOBAL match, hence an actual one-factor residue. Wrong inputs return only factors, one candidate, or bounded failure. Cost soft n suffices, with no additive-zero annihilator claim.

Complement r by N/r modulo n if needed; the supplied residue is for either factor. The reviewed HH consumer then applies. Its one-residue threshold is n of order K^(1/3), not the old partial-tag decoder's K^(1/2). No other-prime eigenline, simultaneous orientation or common physical collision index is necessary.

This validates the old projective ratio target at a weaker modulus, but does NOT acquire the ratio. The old unique-hit proof was specifically smaller-nonsplit, so changing its consumer alone does not make that old source uniform.

## 2. Keep the exact torus setup and modulus ledger

Use K for the old signed-weight native scale, H=K². In its archived setup K can include the discriminant conductor, unlike the scalar packet's special K=2*3^e. Do not silently identify those moduli. The old unconditional discriminant scan, norm-one point/order supplier, scalar beta and coupled screen cost soft K on the prepared branch, with their inherited finite guards. Set

    pi=beta*W, barpi=beta/W, q_t=pi^H.

At the nonsplit prime R, the odd scalar and norm-one orders divide R-1 and R+1, hence are coprime and ord_R(pi)>256H². This coprimality is automatic at that prime, not between different primes. The supplied screen gives the actual short q_t units. The scalar beta remains available for HH: with q_s=beta^H and n|K, ord(beta^(n²))>=ord(q_s)>16H at BOTH primes.

The [old signed-weight proof](/home/dbsanfte/riemann/formal/docs/semiprime-factorisation-investigation.md:43815) already uses whichever prime is nonsplit in its correct public size bin. It already pays the unconditional one-discriminant preparation. Neither fact is new orientation work.

For unit tag j and aK-lattice row a*j²=b*N mod K, acquire centred ell with

    f_j(X)=aX²+ell X+bN,
    f_j(j)=0 mod H, f'_j(j)=0 mod K,
    V_j=pi^ell * barpi^(a+bN).

Frobenius proves the actual R-side clock relation at I=(aR+bN/R+ell)/H. In a correct weighted bin, ANY hit forces this equality as an INTEGER, by the old small-gap versus ord_R(pi) bound. This exact provenance, not a scalar conic-root comparison, is the ingredient used below.

## 3. Main multiple-hit bridge: content-sector labels

Partition the acquired NONAXIS Gauss rows by g=gcd(a,K). The lattice congruence implies gcd(b,K)=g. The weighted norm determinant gives |ab|<=K/sqrt(3), so g<=sqrt K. Put n_g=K/g and w_j=[j]_(n_g).

At any exact R-side hit f_j(R)=0. Writing z=R-j gives

    a z²+f'_j(j)z+f_j(j)=0 mod K².

At a prime ell dividing K, put e=v_ell(K), r=v_ell(g). If v_ell(z)<e-r, the first term has uniquely smaller valuation than the other two. This is impossible. Therefore n_g divides R-j. Thus EVERY hit row in this sector shares the SAME integer coarse label w_j=R mod n_g, even with multiple hits and regardless of whether R is the larger factor. In particular n_g>=sqrt K exceeds the new HH single-tag threshold.

Axes can be included separately; they are not a coverage exception in this exact TORUS argument. Normalize a Gauss axis to (a,b)=(+/-K,0) or (0,+/-K), which is a shorter lawful axis if necessary. Horizontally ell=-a*j mod H, so f_j(R)=+/-K*R*(R-j) mod H; vertically ell=-b*N*j^-1 mod H, giving f_j(R)=+/-K*N*(1-R*j^-1) mod H. Both force R=j mod K. Use n_axis=K and w_j=j. This does NOT identify arbitrary scalar-axis alias phases: the exact nonsplit f_j(R)=0 proof is essential.

For one bin/sector, shift its original signed clock as usual to x_i=q_t^i and row y_j=q_t^M V_j. Define the finite weighting

    P(delta,Y)=product_(j,i)
       [1-y_j/x_i+delta*(1-Y*w_j)].

If d>=1 is the actual good-field hit count, then d<=J_sector<=K<R because each row hits at most one clock node. Its first nonzero slice is

    [delta^d]P=A*(1-Y*r)^d, r=R mod n_g, A!=0.

Hence only TWO coefficients are needed:

    V=d*[delta^d]P(delta,0),
    U=-[delta^d Y]P,                 U/V=r at R.   (1)

No unique-hit promise or full hit-polynomial output is required. The degree d is actual data, not assumed known. Scan constant-Y coefficients by precision doubling. A coefficient zero at the nonsplit field but nonzero in B globally exposes a proper integer gcd of its two coordinates. At the first good nonzero coefficient, its norm is nonzero there, so its norm gcd factors or makes A a global unit. The count d<K is a unit at both primes. The case d=0 gives no hit and must be rejected, not interpreted as a residue ratio.

### Whole-menu consumer, including conductor-aware K

Validate one supplied pair per bin/sector using section 1. Total integer-label scanning costs at most

    sum_(g|K) K/g <= K*(1+log K)

per logarithmic bin bank, plus the axis scan. Each valid-looking output gives at most g lifts modulo common modulus K (axis gives one). The full palette size is bounded by

    S<=O(log N)*(1+sum_(g|K,g<=sqrt K)g)
      <=O(log N)*sqrt K*tau(K).

For K=2*3^e this is soft sqrt K. For conductor-aware K use the elementary uniform divisor bound tau(K)<=C_epsilon K^epsilon with epsilon=1/12; absorb the logarithm into another K^(1/12) with a fixed computable constant. Thus S<=C'K^(2/3) uniformly, with constants depending only on the fixed exponent choices. The constant can be constructed from finitely many small primes in the standard proof of the divisor bound; there is no hidden input-dependent exception. Deduplicate the actual palette. Taking the fixed constant in HH's interval parameter large enough (r=ceil(16C'K/S)) pays ONE shared HH call in soft K. False-sector outputs need not trigger separate full HH calls. At least one correct bin/sector is covered, including axes, if the promised coefficient pairs are acquired.

This is a genuine many-hit/one-prime provenance interface. It does not improve a selected-row consumer: an already selected original useful row was already cheap to finish.

## 4. Simpler uniform unique-hit alternative: two explicit monic families

The parent supplied a cleaner complementary construction, checked here. For EVERY unit j modulo K take

    family A: b=1, a=[N*j^-2]_K;
    family B: a=1, b=[j²*N^-1]_K.

Use the same centred ell construction and fixed barpi roots from section 2. In family A the coefficient a is a unit; in family B a=1. All rows have a,b positive and less than K. If the nonsplit factor is P, family A has

    aP+Q < K^4+K^4/16=17H²/16.

If it is Q, family B has the identical bound for Q+bP. The inequalities use P<=sqrt N<=K³ and Q=N/P<N/(16H)<=H²/16. The true integer I lies in [0,2H]. For ANY hit of the relevant family in that clock, its gap has magnitude <4H², below ord_R(pi). Thus it is an exact integer root, and the unit-a valuation argument forces j=R mod K. One row per tag and clock separation imply exactly ONE hit at the good field. The other family need not satisfy that promise and has bounded verified failure.

These two attempts remove the smaller-nonsplit orientation premise WITHOUT a QRH palette. They apply the OLD general signed-weight/Frobenius construction; they are not a new point supplier. The second fixed-barpi family is not silently identified with the older canonical chi_D(j)=+1 numerical source, whose W-exponent can differ. Nor is conductor compatibility for an independently fixed scalar modulus silently imported.

Mark w_j=[j]_n for any n|K with n>=K^(1/3). The choice n=K is always available; a divisor near the smaller threshold is not assumed for an arbitrary conductor-aware K. Here d=1 on one relevant family, so U=-[delta Y]P and V=[delta]P(delta,0) give the one-prime coarse tag. Only the constant-rank ring B[delta,Y]/(delta²,Y²) is needed. The old scalar algorithm over that ring has initialized cost soft(K+sqrt(K*H))=soft K^(3/2), not soft K. No d-search or triangular growing jet is required in this alternative. Its genuinely missing step is acquisition of that TWO-coefficient weak pair at soft K on at least one unrecognized family; no row, index or full gcd is required.
