## Correction/addendum: native quadratic exponents do have a formal rank-two map

The parent supplied an important correction to Section5's broad acquisition wording. Euler's exponent gives beta^(P²)=beta^((N+1)P−N), since the difference is −P(P−1)(Q−1); similarly for Q. Consequently

    beta^((aP−bQ)²)
      =beta^[−N(a+b)²] gamma^[(N+1)(a²+b²)]
           delta^[(N+1)(a²−b²)].

The right side IS an acquired element of the same formal rank-two algebra, in soft J total coefficient work for the supplied rows. There is therefore no missing FORMAL exponent-square map here. The numeric delta remains unacquired. The statement in Section5 should be read with this correction, not as ruling out such a formal map.

This particular higher equation supplies no extra orientation test at a supplied physical linear hit. At D=A its right side equals beta^((aN−b)²), and the linear hit implies HI−lambda=aN+b modulo the local beta order. Thus beta^((HI−lambda)²−4abN)=beta^((aN−b)²) follows. At D=A^-1 the corresponding identities use a+bN and a−bN. Hence at each local known branch the quadratic comparison is already implied by its linear comparison. The formal map is a valid acquired refinement; this quadratic equation alone does not select a mixed branch or remove the unknown physical index.
