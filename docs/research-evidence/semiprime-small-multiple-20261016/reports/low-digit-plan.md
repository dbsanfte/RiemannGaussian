# Low-digit position/origin plans cannot supply few short geometric carriers

2026-10-16. Classical source arithmetic. Root proposed triple elimination; this note independently checks it and replaces an invalid fixed-menu multiplier by a stronger residue-compatible count. No new prime-distribution theorem, backend construction, or factoring clock is claimed.

## Statement and inherited premises

Work on the SAME original balanced, order-qualified supply of ordered prime pairs (P,Q), with P,Q=Theta(M³), N=PQ, and every current auxiliary odd prime m=Theta(M). Its original size is Omega(M⁶/log²M). Retain the majority family of the pinned native-center report, using its polynomial menu envelope K_*=O(M^.020) and energy threshold lambda_m=m^.04. Its new energy loss O(M^5.980), singular loss O(M^5.020), and the inherited soft O(M^5.994) losses are all negligible compared with this original supply.

The literal projected public gamma has distinct prime local orders R_P>P^.677 and R_Q>Q^.677. Consequently R_P,R_Q>c M^2.031, and writing P=1+vR_P, Q=1+wR_Q gives positive v,w<=C M^.969. These are proof-side properties of the inherited actual source, not supplied algorithmic inputs.

For each (m,h), with 0<=h<m² and m not dividing h, prescribe a library of at most B(M) plans. The library is fixed as N varies subject to N=h modulo m². Each plan supplies, at every canonical first-current tag 1<=j<m:

- an integer origin k_j;
- a carrier assignment i(j) in {1,...,k};
- an integer label 0<=r_j<T_{i(j)}, with T=sum_i T_i.

The origins, assignments, labels, lengths and grouping may depend arbitrarily on (m,h) and the plan choice, but not on further input digits. Uniformly each integer has (log M)^O(1) bits, and k²T<=M(log M)^O(1). The polynomial exponents in these bounds are fixed. A full-input or factor-advised choice among the prescribed B(M) plans is allowed.

A plan succeeds on N if there exist units c_i,rho_i modulo N, allowed to depend arbitrarily on the full input and even the hidden factors, for which

 gamma^(-N-delta_j+m²k_j)=c_{i(j)} rho_{i(j)}^(r_j)   for every tag j.       (1)

Then the number of original order-qualified pairs on which ANY plan at ANY CURRENT candidate m succeeds, after removing the preceding energy exceptions, is

 soft O(B(M) M^4.969).                                                   (2)

In particular B(M)<=M gives soft O(M^5.969)=o(M⁶/log²M). Removing these successes from the SAME retained original supply leaves Omega(M⁶/log²M) inputs for which every current candidate defeats every such plan. The energy premise is asserted only at current candidates; the final counting argument overcounts witnesses over all comparable m, not only a fictitious fixed K_* menu.

There is no restriction on numerical origin magnitude or relative span beyond the polylogarithmic bit bound. Carrier starts and ratios may be arbitrary units, lie outside the gamma subgroup, and be privately advised. Positions and origins depending on further digits of N are outside the theorem. The bound is a static plan obstruction, not an operation lower bound or a restriction on all implicit algorithms.

## 1. A raw first-current witness survives the inherited light-tag reduction

The native first pair at every tag is (a_j,1), where a_j is the canonical representative N j^(-2) modulo m. It belongs to publicPairs even when it is not the selected short row. Every light tag therefore still contains this canonical first current. The light tags depend only on N modulo m and number g>=2m/3.

The exact lift is

 D_j=(N-a_j j²)/m,
 b_j=centered(m,-j^(-1)D_j),
 delta_j=a_j+m b_j-2a_j j.

It depends only on N modulo d=m²: increasing N by d leaves a_j unchanged, changes D_j by m, and leaves its centered b_j unchanged. Signed origin choices do not affect this fact.

For a quadruple of first currents, their t values all equal one, so the tS term of the native-center report's E_mod cancels. Thus its catalog bound yields, on the g labeled light tags,

 # {delta_1+delta_2=delta_3+delta_4 modulo d}
       <=E_*=lambda_m C_m^4 m^(5/2),      C_m=64(floor(log₂m)+1)^4.        (3)

This is a labeled raw congruence count, not a deduplicated-set estimate. It holds simultaneously at every current candidate before choosing any origin plan. For ANY integer origins, put f_j=-delta_j+d k_j. An integer additive equality among f_j implies the congruence in (3); hence the labeled integer energy of these f_j is at most E_*. No order/no-wrap assumption or origin-span bound is needed for this integer implication.

## 2. At most O(m) low-digit annihilator candidates

Restrict a plan to the light tags, retaining labels with multiplicity. In each assigned carrier choose a base tag 0. For every tag j with r_j=r_0, retain the difference f_j-f_0. If there is a tag 1 with r_1!=r_0, choose the first such tag and also retain, for every other tag j, the anchored cross-difference

 K_j=(r_1-r_0)(f_j-f_0)-(r_j-r_0)(f_1-f_0).                             (4)

Discard zero candidates. There are at most 2g=O(m) candidates. They depend ONLY on (m,h,plan): the common -N in (1) cancels, and delta_j is exactly d-periodic. Their bit lengths are polylogarithmic in M under the metadata hypothesis.

If (1) holds, every retained candidate K is an annihilator of gamma modulo N. Equal-position differences cancel the same carrier value. For (4), raise the two equalities gamma^(f_j-f_0)=rho_i^(r_j-r_0) and gamma^(f_1-f_0)=rho_i^(r_1-r_0) to the opposite integer powers. Commutativity cancels rho_i, regardless of its relation to gamma. Thus gamma^K=1, and BOTH R_P and R_Q divide K.

At least one candidate must be nonzero for a successful plan on the retained energy family, eventually in M. Otherwise each carrier group has f_j affine in r_j over the rationals: equal labels have equal f; two unequal labels fix the affine map; groups with one distinct label are constant. Sums within a group take at most 2T_i-1 distinct integer values, even when the affine slope is rational or zero. If g_i source indices are in group i, weighted Cauchy--Schwarz and then Hölder give

 E_integer>=sum_i g_i^4/(2T_i)>=g⁴/(2k²T).

In conjunction with (3), this forces

 k²T>=g⁴/(2E_*)>=c m^(3/2)/(lambda_m C_m^4)
                    =m^1.46/(log m)^O(1),                              (5)

contradicting k²T<=M(log M)^O(1) and m=Theta(M) for all sufficiently large M. No distinct-target lower bound is used. In particular collapsing multiple tags to the same value does not avoid this argument.

## 3. Count possible successful inputs using their fixed low product residue

Fix m,h, a plan, and one of its nonzero K. The integer |K| has only polylogarithmically many ordered pairs of distinct prime divisors above cM^2.031: its bit length is polylogarithmic, and every such prime consumes Omega(log M) bits. This is a counting fact; no public factoring of K is invoked.

Fix one such ordered pair (R_P,R_Q). Candidate factors must be

 P=1+vR_P, Q=1+wR_Q,   1<=v,w<=C M^.969,
 PQ=h modulo m².                                                       (6)

There are only O(M^.969) possibilities in (6), rather than O(M^1.938). Indeed fix v. A candidate P divisible by m is disallowed by the qualified source. Otherwise P is a unit modulo m², and its fixed product residue determines Q=h P^(-1) modulo m². The prime R_Q is different from m, so this determines w modulo m². The permitted w range has length O(M^.969)<m², and therefore contains at most one such w. Primality and bin restrictions can only decrease this count.

For each m there are O(M²) choices h, B(M) plans per h, O(M) anchored candidates per plan, and only polylogarithmically many divisor pairs per candidate. There are O(M) comparable integers m; counting all of them also bounds all candidate primes, however the actual input-dependent menu varies. Thus

 O(M) * O(M²) * B(M) * O(M) * soft O(M^.969)
       =soft O(B(M) M^4.969),

as claimed. Every count is an absolute overcount of the same original ordered factor supply. No selector-box estimate or prime equidistribution assumption is needed in this final counting step. The inherited selector argument is used only to establish the simultaneous energy-qualified family in (3).

For B(M)<=M the loss soft O(M^5.969) is negligible. A smaller plan library improves the loss accordingly. More generally B(M)<=M^(1.031-epsilon) for a fixed epsilon>0 is allowed, subject to the same uniform encoding and k²T bounds.

## 4. Novelty and limits

The inherited cone estimate, native catalog and light tags, majority supply, projected gamma orders, and setup bounds are unchanged. The new step is that a successful low-digit metadata plan yields a nonzero, low-digit-determined annihilator, and its two large prime-order divisors restrict possible actual factor pairs. Using h=PQ modulo m² is essential to the sharper cofactor count. Multiplying a per-modulus count by K_* would have been invalid for the varying menu; the proof explicitly sums ALL O(M) comparable moduli instead.

The earlier bounded-wide-origin certificate can publicly strip a sufficiently small annihilator using a factorial residue. That size restriction is unnecessary here because this theorem only counts large prime divisors of polylog-bit K; it does not claim cheap factor extraction from an arbitrarily large K. Arbitrarily long binary encodings are also outside the contract.

This excludes low-digit-prescribed positions/origins even if their group generators are fully advised, and permits an advised selection from a library of M plans. It does not exclude full-N-dependent metadata, enormous libraries, high-doubling nonlinear carriers, or direct scalar norm computation. No full-query acquisition bound, all-semiprime factoring theorem, new Lean declaration, build, or new numerical experiment follows.
