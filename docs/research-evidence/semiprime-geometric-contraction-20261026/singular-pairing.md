    Xi(u,v)=det(I-uA-vM)

## 2. Execute the pairing with the clock projector

Let x_i=q^i, W(X)=product_(i=0)^(h-1)(X-x_i). The clock evaluation-at-zero weights are

    k_i=-W(0)/(x_i W'(x_i)).

They are unit amplitudes, with explicit finite q-factorial descriptions. Individual requested weights are paid in soft sqrt(h) using the inherited finite-product backend; an h-entry weight vector is NOT acquired at that cost. Its boundaries W(0)=(-1)^h q^(h(h-1)/2) and W'(0)/W(0)=-sum_i q^-i are acquired by powering and a geometric-sum formula using the screened unit 1-q^-1.

The natural singular Cauchy pairing of the two known projector families is

    B=sum_(j,i) l_j*k_i/(T_j-x_i).                    (5)

Equation (5) is first a rational identity on regular universal data. Its componentwise pole at an actual collision is not a permitted numerical inverse. A formal deformation supplies the meaning at that collision, with its own amplitude; it cannot be substituted as a physical first jet without specifying that deformation.

Nevertheless the proposed contraction can be executed algebraically in BOTH orders. Finite Lagrange partial fractions give

    sum_j l_j/(x-T_j)=(1-D(0)/D(x))/x,
    sum_i k_i/(x-x_i)=(1-W(0)/W(x))/x.               (6)

Hence exactly

    B=W'(0)/W(0)+D(0)*sum_i k_i/[x_i D(x_i)]
     =-D'(0)/D(0)-W(0)*sum_j l_j/[T_j W(T_j)].      (7)

All four outside constants in (7) are acquired GLOBAL units or acquired logarithmic derivatives; D(0) is a unit since the actual T_j are units. The first equation collapses the entire row alphabet with no unknown cofactor. The second collapses the entire geometric clock alphabet. This is a finite residue-preserving transposition identity after a consistent formal regularization, with all displayed normalizations paid. It is not an infinite partition expansion or an assumed h-entry matrix.

The FIRST unpaid operation is now explicit: either acquire the weighted clock sum with denominator D(x_i), or the J weighted reciprocal clock products W(T_j). The latter can be executed by the old joint engine in soft(J+sqrt(Jh)); the former is its selected Cauchy/Newton counterpart, not a supplied soft-J quadrature rule. No cancellation in (7) sets either interior sum equal to its boundary constants. At a collision the corresponding term is a pole, so treating both sums as zero would delete precisely the signal.

## 3. Can a second known projector move the evaluation point onto the intersection?

There is a precise attempted move. Replacing evaluation at zero in (4) by evaluation at a supplied c gives the paid vector L_j(c), and its Cauchy transform becomes

    sum_j L_j(c)/(x-T_j)=[1-D(c)/D(x)]/(x-c).         (8)

This is acquired at any ONE supplied c in soft J, subject only to the already gated row differences. But using c=theta=q^I presupposes the missing clock intersection. Known short endpoint modes (3) only replace the numerator in (8) by supplied short polynomials; they do not choose that c.

Trying to acquire it through a centered q-shift is equally concrete. The affine shift x -> qx+(1-q)c has a known evaluation-at-c projector, but its row/kernel ratio is

    W(qT_j+(1-q)c)/W(T_j).                          (9)

The finite GP endpoints telescope only at the original center c=0. For an unknown c=theta the numerator in (9) is an affine image of the full h-root clock; no acquired two-endpoint formula has been derived. Its short descriptor is not its value at the J supplied rows. Thus this particular projector transport restores exactly a long ratio, rather than providing a paid physical projector. This is an executed missing input, not a claim that every affine or DAHA construction must have that cost.

The second known clock projector also does not avoid this issue: its full finite pairing is already (7). Its explicit boundary factors are acquired, but the selected interior Cauchy contraction remains.
