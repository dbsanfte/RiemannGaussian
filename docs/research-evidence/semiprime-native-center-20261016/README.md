# The actual native row centers still require large geometric carriers

2026-10-16. New classical arithmetic scope, independently reviewed. The
preceding adaptive-Euclid result was progress: it covered all advised ray
choices but left the native public/reflected row centers outside its
polylogarithmic-origin-span hypothesis. This proof handles those actual
centers, including mixed advised choices, on the SAME original qualified

The result excludes the few-small-geometric-carrier route for the native

## The new arithmetic count

[Complete proof](reports/native-centers-and-origin-span.md). The low-digit
light-ray catalog is unchanged. Its cone points are (a,t,u=a*j), with
u^2=n*a*t. Instead of forcing the INTEGER t sums equal, the proof counts
the complete congruence energy of t*(P+Q-1)+delta modulo m^2.

It averages both higher factor digits and the low factor p at fixed
n=p*q. The higher-digit map has determinant q-p; the singular case
P=Q mod m costs only O(K_*M^5) original pairs. Three quadruple cases
then give at most 10m^4/lambda bad ORDERED factor-residue pairs modulo
m^2, at threshold lambda*polylog(m)*m^(5/2). One projection has at most
four source entries per fiber; the final degenerate case uses weighted
cone energy. The varying selector transfers this to O(K_*M^6/lambda)
bad original pairs without a prime-distribution assumption in boxes.
This exceptional set is proof-side factor-residue data, not an N-only
test that the algorithm can evaluate for free.

For native publicShift and reflectedShift, their exact midpoint formulas
cancel the centered b term from the effective global phase. Every such
phase lies in one common interval whose pair differences are less than
16m^4<D. Thus the native centers create no global wrapping and their
weighted target energy is bounded by that congruence energy. There is
no relative-origin-span assumption for these particular centers.

Repeated target values retain their SOURCE multiplicity. A weighted
Cauchy--Schwarz/Holder argument gives, for every geometric cover,

    k^2*T >= m^(3/2)/(lambda*polylog(m)).

Here k is the number of progressions and T their total listed length.
This uses the number of selected light TAGS; it does not reuse the old
distinct-target bound, which need not hold after native centering.
The complete multicenter packet cover contains a one-center-per-tag
light witness. No energy bound for the entire untrimmed multicenter
union, or collision coverage after trimming, is asserted.

For polylogarithmic menus, lambda=K_*log^4 M retains an original majority
and yields k^2*T=soft Omega(m^(3/2)). The larger frozen envelope
K_*=O(M^.020), lambda_m=m^.04 yields m^1.46/polylog(m), with new losses
O(M^5.980) and O(M^5.020). Rays, native center choices and covering
progressions may all be privately advised after seeing the full input.

## Wider origins have a quantified escape

For one chosen origin per retained source entry, with numerical span H,
the proof gives the same bound divided by W, the number of possible
global phase wraps. Precisely
W=2floor(2V/D)+1 for V=(m-2)*(P+Q-1)+6m^2+m^2H, and
W=O(1+m^2H/D). Translated pair-sum Cauchy--Schwarz bounds every off-zero
congruence energy by the zero one, uniformly over advised origins.

Under the polylogarithmic assumptions, a few nearlinear-size carriers
would therefore require H>=D/(m^(3/2)*polylog m), already at least
m^(5/2)/polylog m from D>16m^4. The stronger inherited order gives
m^2.562/polylog m. This is a restriction on numerical span, not a bit-
complexity lower bound for acquiring origins. Very wide advised origins
and other kinds of shared computation remain open.

proof does not exclude implicit scalar norms or an additive extractor.
