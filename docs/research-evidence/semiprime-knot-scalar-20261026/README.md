Literal reuse of stationary events cannot remove its descriptor cost under
the full continuous-tag covering premise. This is a scoped constructor
result, not a lower bound for factoring.

## A precise event-count bound

Let k=2*3^e, gcd(N,k)=1, and delta<k. A nonzero integer pair (a,b) covers
the tag/scale points satisfying

    sigma in (Z/kZ)^*, 1<=t<=4,
    aN=b*sigma^2 mod k, |a*t-b|<=delta.

If R descriptors cover this entire product domain, the proof gives

    k <= C1*delta^2 + C2*delta*sqrt(R*tau(k)).

At delta=C*sqrt(k/r), for sufficiently large r, this implies

    R = Omega(kr/tau(k)) = Omega(kr/log k).

The argument allows arbitrary coefficient sizes and repeated vectors,
and counts the union of their validity intervals. Normalize the content
h to gcd(h,k), which preserves the residue condition and widens each
interval. Small coefficients cover O(delta^2) total tag-times-length
measure. At primitive level a0, there are O(a0*tau(k)) descriptors and
each covers at most O(delta/a0) measure. Summing the largest R capacities
proves the inequality. See [the proof](event-count.md) and its
[independent review](event-review.md).

The premise is essential: the actual factors of a fixed semiprime give
only two linked arithmetic points, not all independent tags and real
scales. A semiprime-specific coverage theorem or an implicit contraction
of a larger family remains possible. The review gives an explicit
rational-scale example illustrating why the bound cannot be transferred
to isolated points without additional constraints.

## Exact tests of the knot leads

The [character report](character-norm.md) proves that native integer
q-strings have length at most two. After both paid shift-zero separation
and shift-one deflation, the same statement holds locally, with Omega(k)
distinct points. Thus the simple constant-string specialization is not
available on that continuing branch. Longer-distance sharing remains
unresolved. The [review](character-review.md) checks all factor and
nonzero-annihilator branches, including hidden modular coincidences.

The [induction report](induced-norm.md) keeps the base factor 2^m, the
central sign, and all collision zeros. Safe 2-primary completion yields
F_d(Y)=product_j(Y-x_j^d) in soft O(J log d), preserving the native zero
predicate. The explicit clock-halving identity replaces F by F(X)F(qX),
so degree doubles and the old square-root cost remains. The
[independent review](induction-review.md) checks these statements.

The [quantum Frobenius audit](quantum-frobenius-audit.md) adds Higgins's
2025 miraculous-cancellation paper to the search. It distinguishes its
auxiliary root-of-unity parameter and power-sum output from our long
arithmetic norm. The [coverage report](primitive-coverage.md) and
[source review](coverage-review.md) retain the exact modulo-k^2 phase,
common center for swapped rows, and licensed recovery conditions for the
alternate stationary source.

## What would advance the goal next

Two concrete solution routes survive these checks:

1. An acquired identity that couples different native clock positions,
   with soft O(J) data and legal corrections at every collision, giving
   prefix cost soft O(J+sqrt(m)).
2. A coverage construction using the relation between actual factor
   residues and scales, with soft O(k) descriptors and total interval
   width soft O(k^2). It must not require the full continuous-tag cover
   excluded above.

Neither is supplied here. Another short character, cover or tensor
description alone would not establish either cost bound.
