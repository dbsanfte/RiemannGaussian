# Formal-log and modular-composition escape audit

2026-10-15. Elementary algebraic claims below are derived here. No general scalar complexity lower bound is asserted.

Let R=Z/N, H monic of degree J with H(0) a unit, A=R[X]/H and x=[X]. These assumptions hold for H=prod_j(X-Y_j) with every Y_j a unit.

## Exact obstruction to transporting a truncated series

Theorem. For every K>=1, the image of the ideal (X^K) under R[X] -> A is the whole of A. Consequently reduction modulo H does not factor through truncation modulo X^K, even at the level of the resultant/norm.

Proof. H(0) unit implies x is a unit by the relation H(x)=0. Thus x^K A=A. For any prescribed jet T (degree<K) and arbitrary a in A, choose a polynomial B representing x^(-K)(a-T(x)). Then T+X^K B has the prescribed jet and residue a. In particular identical jets permit residues 0 and 1, whose multiplication determinants are 0 and 1 in R. This remains true for jet T=1, so fixing constant coefficient 1 does not repair it.

More explicitly, taking B to represent -x^(-K) makes 1+X^K B have residue zero despite its K-jet being 1. Taking B=0 gives residue 1. These are genuine polynomials. This is an obstruction to a universal jet-to-residue/norm conversion, not an information-theoretic obstruction when q,L and the special q-product promise are also supplied.

Reciprocating X preserves unit roots: x^(-1) is also a unit. The normalized reciprocal H* again has unit constant coefficient. Truncating the top coefficients of a long polynomial therefore has the same problem; knowing only J low or J high coefficients does not establish the desired norm. A Mobius change sending a single target to zero does not make all other distinct targets nilpotent.

For P_L(X)=prod_(i<L)(1-q^i X), its formal logarithm modulo X^K is

    -sum_(1<=k<K) S_k X^k/k,
    S_k=sum_(i<L) q^(ik).

When k and 1-q^k are units, S_k=(1-q^(Lk))/(1-q^k). Thus the truncated log and exp can indeed be computed cheaply. They determine P_L mod X^K, exactly the insufficient quotient above. The ideal generated by x in A is A, so its adic topology is not a separated topology in which this infinite series could converge to an element of A. Writing log and exp directly in A is therefore unjustified.

If x were nilpotent of index K and 1,...,K-1 units, truncation WOULD suffice, and formal log/exp would be legitimate in the nilpotent ideal. A unit x cannot be nilpotent in a nonzero ring. Over a field the quotient H=(X-a)^J makes x-a nilpotent, but x itself remains a unit when a!=0. Reexpanding each factor around a introduces constants 1-a q^i and rational sums involving their inverses; it does not preserve the cheap geometric S_k formula. Any nonunit constant has to be treated by gcd, not inverted.

In fact P_L mod X^K can already be computed division-free in soft O(K log L) operations by geometric doubling, because the carrier (X^K) IS stable under scaling. Thus cheap jet acquisition is unconditional and is not the bottleneck.

Failure of a denominator inversion is not uniformly a factor: gcd=1 permits inversion, proper gcd gives a factor, gcd=N gives no proper factor. For 1-q^k the latter may simply mean a genuine short order of q; the apparent geometric quotient has a removable singularity. S_k can always be computed by a division-free doubling recurrence in O(log L) operations. For k nonunit, formal log/exp itself is not uniquely justified by the characteristic-zero identity; one cannot cancel k by fiat.

A known order t<=soft O(J) is a separate positive special case: evaluate F on one geometric period by chirp convolution in soft O(J+t), multiply its t values and its needed prefix, and raise the full-period product to floor(L/t). This gives soft O(J+t+log L), without formal log. It does not establish such a small period for the hard family.

Finite-field multiplicative logarithms are different from formal logarithms. A group homomorphism F_p* -> (F_p,+) is necessarily zero because the group orders p-1 and p are coprime. Discrete logarithms instead take values modulo p-1 and require factor/field information and an acquisition algorithm; they do not justify the above formal series at unit arguments.

## Why fast modular composition alone does not close doubling

To use P_(2t)(X)=P_t(X)P_t(q^t X) from only P_t mod H, substitution X -> q^t X must descend to an endomorphism of A. Exact necessary and sufficient condition:

    H(q^t x)=0 in A, equivalently H(X) divides H(q^t X).

Proof: two representatives differ by H B, and their substituted residues differ by H(q^t x) B(q^t x). Taking B=1 gives necessity; the same formula proves sufficiency. Degree equality and monicity make the condition H(q^t X)=q^(tJ) H(X). With H(0) unit it implies q^(tJ)=1; coefficient-wise it also requires (q^(tk)-q^(tJ))h_k=0 for every k. Thus this condition is explicit and testable, and cannot be assumed for arbitrary supplied unit targets.

Fast modular composition computes the composition of supplied degree<J representatives. It cannot make this result independent of representatives when the condition fails. Counterexample: representatives 0 and H define the same element of A but substitute to 0 and H(q^t x), which can differ.

Moving carriers gives a correct identity: to acquire P_t(q^t X) mod H(X), acquire P_t(Z) mod H_t(Z), where H_t(Z)=q^(tJ) H(q^(-t) Z), and then substitute Z=q^t X. But the ordinary doubling tree now asks for the product on two different target carriers. Without a supplied relation, at depth k it can have 2^k shifted carriers. This is not a lower bound on all moving-carrier algorithms; it identifies the missing compression theorem and prevents simply counting one composition per doubling level.

The new primary modular-composition result is van der Hoeven–Lecerf, *Fast modular composition using spiroids*, https://www.texmacs.org/joris/fftcomp/fftcomp.html . Even granting its deterministic degree D nearlinear D^(1+5/sqrt(log D)) polylog cost over Z/N, this solves individual valid compositions, not the preceding carrier-acquisition problem. It is also D^(1+o(1)), not soft O(D): the factor D^(5/sqrt(log D)) is superpolylogarithmic. Therefore even a hypothetical polylog-number composition construction using this bound alone would not meet the strict soft O(m) target at D~m.

## Scope

These are rigorous obstructions to (1) transporting a finite X-adic jet to a quotient with unit X, and (2) fixed-carrier scaling composition absent invariance. The special q-product may still admit an independent algorithm exploiting all of q,L,H, changing carriers with provable compression, source-specific cancellations, or other structure. None of these obstructions is an arbitrary scalar lower bound.
