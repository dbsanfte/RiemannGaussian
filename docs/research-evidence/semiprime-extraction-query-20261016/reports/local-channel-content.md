# Common-channel acquisition: exact local-content and sparse-probing limits

2026-10-16. No new global nearlinear locator/factor acquisition algorithm was found. This report analyzes two concrete uses of the newly paid channel oracle: local common-content extraction from jets, and blind powered-node gcd probing. The obstructions below are arithmetic statements on the retained actual source, not generic interpolation or circuit lower bounds. Global structured Hermite--Padé, nonlocal reconstruction and new additive observables remain open.

## 1. Supplied positive interface

The frozen block-query bundle proves, for dyadic B~J~sqrt L, that the whole vector (N_r(X))_(r<B) at ANY supplied scalar X has a formal division-free X-dependent program of soft O(J) size, after paid X-independent unit inversions/constants. Its coefficient polynomials have degree O(J). In either actual hidden field their monic common divisor is exactly

 H(Y)=product_(j:F(z_j)=0)(Y-z_j^B),
 F(t)=P_L(t;q).

The public nodes z_j^B are distinct after public offset deduplication. The fieldwise H is squarefree and selects the collision rows, unlike ordinary nonzero-row moments. The retained unit-row-density theorem bounds the number of hit rows in each field by M^.997, while J~M. This is a supply/exception theorem, not a runtime promise for every qualified input.

At a powered original target, exact transport gives

 N_r(z_j^B)=u_(j,r) F(z_j),
 u_(j,r)=z_j^(-r)E_B(z_j)/B,

with every u_(j,r) a cheaply acquired unit modulo N. Thus H=gcd(D_B,N_r) in either field for ANY single channel r, where D_B is the public monic powered-node polynomial. This removes a need to acquire every channel polynomial, but it does not acquire even one polynomial remainder N_r mod D_B. The complete vector at one point is not that remainder.

## 2. Exact local-jet content theorem

Let K be either actual odd-characteristic hidden field and x0 in K. Since H is the polynomial gcd of the channels, write N_r=H U_r with gcd_r U_r=1. Therefore

 min_r ord_(Y=x0)N_r = ord_(Y=x0)H
  = 1 if x0 is a collision node, and 0 otherwise.    (1)

For every precision h>=1, the ideal generated by N_r(x0+epsilon) in K[epsilon]/epsilon^h is consequently the ideal generated by epsilon^v, with v given by (1) (and epsilon=0 if h=1). At a noncollision center at least one channel has nonzero constant coefficient, so a channel jet is a unit. At a collision center H is simple and at least one U_r(x0) is nonzero, giving exactly first-order content for h>=2. Higher local precision does not create a new remote root in this local gcd.

In particular choose ANY public safe center x0=-gamma^a, with arbitrary integer a. The actual gamma subgroup has odd order in both hidden fields, as do all powered targets z_j^B. Since characteristic is odd, -gamma^a cannot equal any z_j^B. Thus H(x0) is nonzero in both fields, so the ideal generated by ALL channel jets is the WHOLE truncated ring

 (N_0(x0+epsilon),...,N_(B-1)(x0+epsilon))
       = (1) in (Z/N)[epsilon]/epsilon^h             (2)

for EVERY precision h, even though the global locator H is nonconstant. To see the composite statement, the constant values generate the unit ideal modulo N by CRT; some constant linear combination has constant coefficient 1 and hence is an invertible truncated series.

Equation (2) defeats the specific proposal to replace GLOBAL polynomial content by local-series gcd/content at these always-safe centers, even with adaptive precision. It does NOT defeat rational reconstruction or a global Hermite--Padé computation using the coefficient/degree constraints: a polynomial factor with nonzero constant coefficient is simply a unit in the local ring. With enough Taylor coefficients one can of course reconstruct the polynomials and compute their global gcd; that is a different algorithm with separately charged acquisition.

Nor does (2) assert that all individual channel constants are units at a safe center. They need only generate the unit ideal together. An individual nonunit value or pivot could still expose a proper factor. Therefore this local-content theorem is NOT a no-factor guarantee for every jet-based computation.

## 3. Paid jet clock, without a false precision shortcut

Run the uniform point-evaluation program over R[epsilon]/epsilon^h at X=x0+epsilon. Constants and their unit inverses are independent of X and remain valid. This computes all channel jets at a cost soft O(hJ) base-ring operations, i.e. soft O(hJ log N) bits and soft O(hJ) residue storage by truncated polynomial arithmetic. Polylogarithmic h is still soft O(J); h~J yields a valid quadratic-scale method by this direct construction. This is an upper bound, not a lower bound for a better structured jet algorithm.

At a collision-free target center z_j^B, transport implies EVERY channel's constant coefficient is a unit modulo N, so each jet is locally invertible. At a P-only or Q-only collision target, each channel constant has the corresponding proper gcd by transport, and a single acquired channel constant already suffices to factor. Identifying an informative center remains the missing acquisition step; high-order local gcd is not needed once such a center is found.

## 4. Blind random node probes do not exploit the retained sparsity efficiently

Consider the specific policy that draws k original target tags uniformly and independently, queries channel values at their powered nodes, and accepts only when one of those values has a nonunit gcd with N. This covers either an individual-channel or whole-vector constant-value gcd test, not arbitrary analysis of the numerical unit values or their derivatives.

At every row that is unit in both fields, all channel values are units by transport. On the retained family at most 2 M^.997 original tags can hit either field. Thus the probability that k such probes reach any nonunit channel value is at most

 2 k M^.997/(m-1) = O(k M^(-.003)).                 (3)

For polylogarithmic k this tends to zero. Sampling without replacement has the same union bound. This is a concrete actual-family failure of a blind rare-hit probing policy, NOT a bound on an adaptive input-dependent algorithm, nor on extracting nonlocal information from nonzero values. The o(J) locator degree makes uniform hitting less likely; it does not supply collision-selective moments or sparse coefficients.

## 5. Remaining constructive target

A global algorithm may exploit the explicit split D_B, the structured channel circuit, or cheaply constructed syzygies. For example, random scalar combinations could present a common-gcd candidate pair, but their global polynomial coefficients and a composite-safe gcd are not supplied by a few oracle evaluations. The coprime parts need not have low degree when the common factor is small. The preceding observations do not exclude a stronger structured algorithm.

The exact positive acquisition target remains one channel modulo D_B (equivalently original row payload), its collision locator directly, or a sufficient factor-selective global consequence. No such softly linear construction was obtained. This report does not restate a generic black-box evaluation lower bound, infer cost from polynomial degree, or claim the source is hard for all additive/content methods. No repository edits, backend proof generation or numerical experiments were made.
