## New primary algorithm checked: modular factorial halving

Yann Tal, [Computing Modular Factorials Below the Square-Root Barrier, ECCC TR26-211](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/211/download), September 2026, has divisor-conditioned factorial algorithms over a **given prime**. Theorems 1.1–1.2 take a divisor of p-1 and construct compatible data within their bounds; Theorem 1.3 takes an explicit extension field and compatible character data for its higher cyclotomic case. Proposition 4.3 and Appendix H instead give a uniform deterministic logarithmic improvement via modular halving and simultaneous shifted-prefix evaluation, for factorials modulo known p or p². This is not a guaranteed exponent improvement or an unknown-semiprime geometric-product theorem.

The specifically reusable mechanism to test is its shifted prefix

    U_h(Z)=prod_(j=1)^h(j+p*Z) mod p²
          =A_h+p*Z*B_h,
    A_h=h!, B_h=h!*sum_(j=1)^h 1/j  (h<p).

Here B_h is normalized by removing the factor p: the source writes its second coordinate with that p already included. The two coefficients suffice for all shifts because every second-order shifted term contains p². Modular halving moves even/odd integer factors into shorter intervals using the public integer p; simultaneous prefix evaluation shares their two-coordinate state. Its finite-ring truncation is genuine, not merely a short notation for a long function.

### Exact parity branch and its cost

For clarity, the multiplicative version of interval halving itself is legal, without any inversion:

    P_m(F;t,q)=P_ceil(m/2)(F;t,q²)
                 *P_floor(m/2)(F;t*q,q²),
    P_s(F;t,q)=prod_(i=0)^(s-1)F(t*q^i).

For the physical indices 1 through m, instantiate t=eta*q. Both branches are genuine positive-native phases. The odd-order square root of q is acquired as eta, but it does not make one branch a nontrivial root-of-unity rotation whose factors are certified units. Consequently this identity cannot delete either branch. Combining B consecutive phases gives the known block polynomial

    G_B(X)=prod_(r=0)^(B-1)F(q^r*X), deg G_B=J*B.

Acquiring its full coefficients and evaluating at m/B block starts costs soft O(J*B+m/B+J), optimized at B about sqrt(m/J), yielding the old soft O(J+sqrt(Jm)). All products are division-free; block starts may repeat and monic remainder divisions do not invert a collision. Leftovers require fewer than B direct factors, whose joint geometric evaluation fits the same bound. This block identity and its optimization are **prior credit**, recorded only to charge the attempted modular-halving adaptation. Tal's shared two-coordinate nilpotent state does not replace G_B for this input.
