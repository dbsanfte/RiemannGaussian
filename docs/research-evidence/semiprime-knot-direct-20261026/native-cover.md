# Native partial-order cover: exact scaling test and remaining arithmetic incidence

26 October2026. Read the archived joint packet, scalar catalogue/swap erratum, and shared-label/smaller-order continuation results. This bounded pass does not establish a soft-k whole query. It tests one concrete native cover proposal—use primitive-content scaling plus inverse labels to move a guaranteed negative witness into a shorter/multiplicative index set—and rejects that shortening by an exact sign/carry theorem. No fake root set or numerical example is used.

## 1. Actual order-incidence target

On the smaller component P, after familiar k-roughening, the current scalar/projected clock order r_P<P<=k³ has all prime divisors>k. Removing any nontrivial order factor leaves less than k², so the first-drop separator factors N. Therefore a sufficient new cover would supply soft-k short nonzero labels K such that one prime ell|r_P divides one K. This is weaker than requiring the full r_P to divide a label. It still needs an initialized aggregate/projection and short-label first-drop recovery; listing a formal product is insufficient.

For a genuine native conic witness,

    E=a+bN+lambda, HI=aP+bQ+lambda,
    lambda²=4abN modH, H=k²,
    E−HI=(P−1)(bQ−a).

Thus any ell|r_P obeys E=HI modulo ell. Full-label cleaning which returns no factor makes E and I invertible modulo r_P; in particular this very witness does NOT provide a center incidence ell|E. Stronger roughness would require a different acquired label or actual conic point, not merely repowering this unit label. The smaller-order theorem is used as a consumer, not counted as a new cover.

## 2. Exact all-semiprime scaling theorem for the negative window

Take an ACTUAL retained nonaxis witness with I<=−1 and 0<lambda<H. Let d>=1 be a positive integer such that the scaled coefficient pair da,db is still admitted. The required uncentered root is

    lambda_d=d lambda−H floor(d lambda/H),
    I_d=dI−floor(d lambda/H).                        (1)

This follows from its actual integer stationary equation, not just a congruence. Since the floor is nonnegative,

    I_d<=dI<=I, hence |I_d|>=|I|.                    (2)

Positive integer scaling therefore NEVER shortens this guaranteed negative witness. The coefficients also pay the usual d² in |ab|. Keeping the unreduced root d lambda instead gives I_d=dI, which still cannot shorten it.

Doing the opposition first does not evade the result. Opposition maps (a,b,lambda,I) to (−a,−b,H−lambda,1−I). Scale that row and oppose it back. The scaled root is nonzero because it remains an admitted nonaxis row and the native lambda=0 lemma still applies. Its resulting negative index is

    1+dI−ceil(d lambda/H)<=dI<=I,                    (3)

because lambda>0 gives ceil(d lambda/H)>=1. Thus the entire proposed “opposite, multiply, recenter, return” operation also fails to reduce the negative horizon. The lambda=0 axes are already separately paid and must not be used to extend this nonaxis proof. Dividing a genuine common content can shorten a row, but that is the prior content/coset operation; primitive content1 offers no integer division.

This is a uniform native statement for every semiprime and every actual witness in this operation's domain. It rules out only this specific proposed shortening mechanism. It does not prove all alternative representatives have large indices, nor exclude choosing another unrelated conic vector.

## 3. Why multiplicative covering does not follow from invertibility

Shared-root preparation turns a witness into the unique I-th root of b in its local retained group. It does not give an integer factorization I=uv or acquire the physical-index root list. On the unreduced scaling path, every ell>k dividing r_P is coprime to I and to every admissible d<=sqrt(k/|ab|)<ell, hence ell does NOT divide dI. This scaling cannot create the desired partial-order center hit either.

With the actual recentering, a center hit requires

    dI=floor(d lambda/H) mod ell.                   (4)

The carry is in{0,...,d−1}, and the admissible d are at most sqrt k. Equation(4) is an extra modular selection condition; neither I being a unit nor lambda²=4abN modH implies it. The corresponding short-index partial screen E−Hn,1<=n<=ceil sqrtk, requires I=n modulo ell. The catalogue only proves |I|<M, not this residue-window promise. A direct claim that inverse labels or an admissible scaling automatically supplies the smaller index is therefore unsupported, even before paying aggregation.

A rectangular multiplicative cover likewise misses its large prime indices. No native theorem established here says at least one actual stationary I avoids those missing indices. The full-cover fallback remains the already paid signed evaluator; no partial cover is silently substituted for it.

## 4. Exact factor-correlated window a new proof could exploit

For the particular stationary root of an actual factor pair, lambda=−(aP+bQ) modH and

    I=ceil((aP+bQ)/H),
    aP−bQ=0 mod k.

The second condition makes (aP+bQ)²=4abN modH. For a candidate order prime ell, an actual centered partial-order hit at this stationary row is therefore equivalent to the following concrete constrained lattice/window condition:

    aP−bQ=0 mod k,
    aP+bQ in union_(t in Z)(H ell t−H, H ell t].      (5)

The half-open convention includes lambda=0 at the upper endpoint; nonaxis lambda=0 is impossible in the actual hard branch. The old Gauss proof supplies a short vector in the first lattice, with |ab|<k and |I|<M. It does not select the ell-spaced width-H windows in (5). Thus an all-semiprime partial-order cover needs an additional factor-correlated selection theorem or a different label construction. This restates the missing implication precisely on ACTUAL P,Q, not a full independent tag/scale domain and not a generic degree-counting argument.

Existing auxiliary cyclotomic/Frobenius and finite Alexander cyclic-norm formulas inspected in the preceding packets do not select these integer windows. Their complete character cycles have an exact acquired cyclic norm but no original-index boundary matching (5). Reusing them here without a new selection identity would return the already-audited different predicate. No fresh literature formula was used as an uninitialized shortcut in this pass.

The smaller-component automorphism theorem does not itself supply that selection. No whole-family soft-k cover or query is proved, and no preparation result is presented as a goal advance.
