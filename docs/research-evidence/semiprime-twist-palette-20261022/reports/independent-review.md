# Independent review of the nonlinear quartic palette

22 October 2026. Reviewed `residual_characters/nonlinear-quartic-palette.md` against the pinned cross-twist-symbol and corrected-cross-order-root notes. No further examples, implementation, or repository changes.

Verdict: the palette identities and claimed native soft budget are correct. The palette acquires its unit/character gauges, not the missing collision observable. Two short clarifications would make its proof and full bit charge explicit.

1. **Unit safety includes split Gaussian primes.** For t,a in the prepared scalar odd-order subgroup, Norm(t+a i)=t²+a² cannot vanish modulo p: otherwise (t/a)²=-1, giving order four for t/a in an odd-order subgroup. In the split algebra F_p[i]≈F_p×F_p, nonzero norm means BOTH components are nonzero. Thus the Gaussian denominator is a unit, not merely a nonzero element. The same argument applies to every q-power boundary factor, including negative powers and arbitrarily large palette index. No additional order-length restriction is used. Differences in (1) are scalar x-y times Gaussian units, so they vanish in both components at p exactly for the original scalar collision, without extra single-component zeros.

2. **Equation (2) has the correct sign and quartic convention.** Direct subtraction gives -2a i(x-y), exactly as displayed. Rational units have full-rational-denominator Gaussian quartic symbol one, S_N(i)=Jacobi(2,N)=j2, and S_N(bar alpha)=S_N(alpha)^(-1) for Gaussian units. Since each alpha_a(t) is a unit, (1) gives precisely j2 S_N(alpha_a(x))S_N(alpha_a(y)) when x-y is a unit. Define the extension S_N(h)=0 when Norm(h) is nonunit modulo squarefree N; this proves the displayed zero-mask identity as well. It does not turn the global fourth root into a CRT tuple of local phases.

   **Proof repair:** the later sentence deducing the general norm-one formula merely from bar(w-z)=-(w-z)/(wz) is incomplete. That relation proves only the square of the desired phase and leaves a ± ambiguity. Use Gaussian UNIT lifts w=alpha/bar alpha and z=beta/bar beta, whose existence and lift independence are proved in `corrected-cross-order-root.md`. Then

       w-z=(alpha*bar beta-beta*bar alpha)/(bar alpha*bar beta).

   The numerator is i times a rational scalar. If w-z is a unit, that scalar is a unit because i and both denominators are units. Its symbol is therefore exactly j2; the denominator contributes S_N(alpha)S_N(beta). This establishes the full phase and sign. The correction changes the proof, not the claimed identity. For nonunit differences use the zero extension above. The corrected-root lift algorithm's exceptional w=-1 branch remains valid in the split algebra by its norm-one argument, not a generic inference from zero norm.

3. **Whole palette construction is paid.** A(Y)=product_j(x_j+iY) is degree J; balanced product construction and monic remainder-tree evaluation at nu points cost soft O(J+nu) operations in the fixed rank-two ring. Its nonmonic leading coefficient is harmless; only remainder-tree divisors need to be monic. Repeated evaluation nodes are allowed. B_0 is one degree-one geometric product in soft O(sqrt L), plus a modular power u^L. Equation (3) follows exactly by expressing B_k=u^L q^(kL) product_(h=-k)^(L-1-k)(q^h+i). The recurrence adds one boundary and removes one boundary, both certified units. Updating all boundary powers and all a_k costs O(nu) modular multiplications, plus O(log L) initial powering and a constant number of unit inversions. No length-L list is required. It is useful to check aggregate norms of all A_k,B_k before any inverse; their product definitions already prove them units under the promises. This check costs O(nu) gcds and does not alter the budget.

4. **Full BIT charge: add an explicit size sentence.** Put B=ceil(log_2 N), E=O(log((J+1)(L+1)(nu+1))). Monic polynomial operations over Z/N[i] cost soft O((J+sqrt L+nu)(B+log(J+L+nu))) bits with integer packing/quasilinear polynomial multiplication. Each of the nu companions in (4) uses O(log(JL+1)) Gaussian modular multiplications for its three powers, plus unit inversions; hence an additional O(nu E M(B)) bit charge up to logarithms. The 2nu Gaussian quartic symbols in (6), represented with O(B)-bit coordinates, cost O(nu B³) conservatively. Input/output alone costs Omega((J+nu)B) and is included. Therefore a safe displayed total is

       soft O((J+sqrt L+nu)(B+E)+nu E M(B)+nu B³).

   In the NATIVE regime J,L,nu≤N^O(1), E=O(B); for nu=soft O(J+sqrt L), this is soft O(J+sqrt L) when logarithmic factors in N are suppressed, as the report states. Without that polynomial-size-index assumption, replace each casual O(log N) exponent/descriptor assertion by E; it is not valid for arbitrary enormous L. Formula (6) likewise charges ordinary fourth-root exponents and the Jacobi(2,N) query, with no hidden local cardinality input.

5. **Root promise versus localization remains correctly separated.** If the original covered root query has a collision, the product of all symbol entries is zero for every palette member. All diagonal gauges are nevertheless acquired units. This known zero does not decide which restricted child still has a collision. The report explicitly leaves these child queries, useful gcd extraction, and saturated-leaf recovery unpaid. Equation (5) is a source-preserving Gaussian unit multiple of the original scalar product even at a nonunit product; no cancellation of that product occurs. A localized Gaussian difference preserves the original scalar event, so the original exponent certificate remains applicable; this does not certify an exponent for an unrelated torus element.

No substantive mathematical repair to (1)--(6) is needed. Recommended exact edits are the unit-lift proof for the arbitrary norm-one phase, the explicit zero-extension convention, and the polynomial-size-index/bit-cost sentence. The plus/minus native anchor notation in this review's predecessor `weighted-cauchy-continuation.md` has also been marked explicitly: its minus targets are old z_j anchors, whereas folding uses the plus-exponent convention.
