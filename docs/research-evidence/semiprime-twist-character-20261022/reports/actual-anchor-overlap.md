## 3. A paid way to equalize all nonzero pair centers

There is a real constructive alternative to scalar centering. Suppose supplied anchor pairs have

with total center descriptor length B=sum_j bitlen(c_j). Let A=product_j c_j, and r_j=A/c_j. A product tree plus outside-product exponent propagation constructs ALL

in soft O(B) modular-bit work, including polylogarithmic N factors. At every tree level the sibling exponent lengths sum to O(B); there are logarithmically many levels. The algorithm stores the product tree and leaf residues, not all J long integer exponents r_j; only a selected r_j is materialized by A/c_j at recovery time. This does not cost J times bitlen(A). Negative centers use unit inverses; zero centers already have product 1 and require a separate class.

Now raise the entire j-th row pair and its stride to r_j. Their centers become COMMON:

 z_j^(r_j) (z'_j)^(r_j)=gamma^A,

Every original local equality survives arbitrary nonzero integer powering. Powering may enlarge zero masks if r_j shares a local order factor, but a new selected equality still supplies a genuine annihilator for the ORIGINAL gamma: r_j times its nonzero integer exponent difference. Its bit length is O(B+the original exponent length), so when B=soft O(m) the inherited decoder is still paid. A proper gcd already factors N. A selected integer-zero exponent remains excluded by the inherited direct-root/trivial-leaf handling.

Thus actual distinct reciprocal centers CAN be equalized with paid metadata while directly preserving original collision events. The price is not an uncharged square-root oracle: it is that the common q-grid has become row dependent. The q_j are supplied c_j-th roots of one common value q^A. Computing them is paid; computing all their long products is not. No identity evaluating this variable-base family within soft m is supplied here.

This is a constructive normalization interface, not a factoring result. The previous distinct-center report only excluded a common center with the original fixed grid. Scalar centering instead retains a common stride but moves the query origins. The two trades must not be conflated.

## 4. Boundary identities must retain interior events

For fixed F(X), a rational coboundary F(X)=G(qX)/G(X) gives a telescoping orbit ratio only where all canceled denominator factors are units, or with an explicit nonunit-resolution mechanism. A common root canceled between numerator and denominator can be an interior original hit. Its formal cancellation does not certify absence of that hit. At such a value the displayed ratio is 0/0; evaluating the canceled rational function can return a unit while the original product is nonunit.

A positive-support alternative is possible when MANY anchors lie on long q-chains: the union of their length-L orbit supports can have only L+J rather than JL factors. A product over that union can retain OR-of-zeros without signed denominator cancellation; multiplicity may be discarded for squarefree-N detection. Such long-chain coverage would be genuinely useful. The literal canonical source's chains of length at most two yield only a constant-factor reduction by this mechanism.

Reciprocal stability alone folds factors into quadratic norms; it does not identify an OR-of-zeros product with its boundary ratio. Even an exact identity F(kappa/X)=unit*X^(-J)F(X) can lose all zeros if one divides the two sides and keeps only the unit.
