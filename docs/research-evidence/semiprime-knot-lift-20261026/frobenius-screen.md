# A target-scale Frobenius screen for the actual native clock

26 October 2026. Constructive native prepass derived while auditing twist lifts and cyclic knot norms. This uses the ordinary multiplicative q-factorial algorithm, not a new q-factorial theorem. It proves a factor-or-stronger-interface result in soft O(k) bit complexity. It does not acquire the remaining complete collision query or prove sixth-exponent factoring on every semiprime.

## 1. Paid input and public algorithm

Use the actual prepared scalar contract: N=P Q is a product of distinct odd primes, with P<Q used only in the proof; N<=k^6; H=k^2; q is an acquired unit with local orders r_P,r_Q>16H. In particular P,Q>16H. No factor, order, or factor-size advice is supplied to the algorithm. The existing oddness and cross-prime coprimality are compatible but are not needed for this screen.

Compute publicly

    A=16k^2+1, B=floor(N/A^2), w=q^N mod N,
    Z_B(w)=product_(j=1)^B (w^j-1) mod N,
    g=gcd(Z_B(w),N).                                  (1)

All integers in the descriptors have O(log N) bits. On a valid continuing input B>=1 because P,Q>=A and N>=A^2. Also

    B < k^2/256 < 16k^2.

The empty B=0 product is defined as one in an implementation, but is outside the supplied continuing contract. The powers in (1) are not formed as giant integers: compute w by modular binary exponentiation, then use the initialized product algorithm below.

## 2. Exact factor-or-interface theorem

Under the stated input,

    g=Q if P divides r_Q, and g=1 otherwise.           (2)

In particular g is NEVER N. If the screen returns one, both local q-orders are coprime to N. Thus x->x^N is a bijection of each local subgroup generated by q, and q^N generates exactly the same local subgroup as q.

Proof. Since r_P divides P-1<Q, neither P nor Q divides r_P. Consequently

    ord_P(w)=r_P>16k^2>B.                             (3)

At the larger prime, Q cannot divide r_Q, and P is the only possible common prime divisor with the squarefree N. Thus gcd(r_Q,N) is either one or P, regardless of the multiplicity of P in r_Q. Therefore

    ord_Q(w)=r_Q                if P does not divide r_Q,
    ord_Q(w)=r_Q/P              if P divides r_Q.       (4)

In the latter case the reduced order d satisfies

    d <= (Q-1)/P < N/P^2 <= N/A^2,

so the integer d is at most B. Thus one factor w^d-1 in (1) is zero modulo Q. In the former case ord_Q(w)>16k^2>B and none is zero modulo Q. Equation (3) ensures every factor is nonzero modulo P in both cases; their field product is also nonzero. This proves (2), including properness and the continuing order-coprimality certificate. No product value is inverted, and no arbitrary auxiliary zero is assigned an original-annihilator decoder: the gcd itself is the proper factor Q.

The same argument works for any acquired unit whose local orders exceed the public cutoff and whose factors exceed the stated public lower bound. In this packet the named input is q, the actual reciprocal-pair and geometric-query clock. For u=q^-1 the subgroup and orders are identical, so the certificate applies directly to the twist-lift multiplier u^N. It also extends to beta and eta: q=beta^H and eta=beta^(H/2), the order quotient ord(beta)/ord(q) divides H, and gcd(H,N)=1 since both prime factors exceed H. Hence no prime dividing N can disappear when passing from beta to q. A failed screen proves N is coprime to both local beta orders and thus to both eta orders as well.

## 3. Initialize the whole product in soft O(k)

Choose b=ceil(sqrt B), t=floor(B/b), and acquire

    F_b(X)=product_(r=1)^b (w^r X-1).

A balanced product tree acquires all b+1 coefficients using soft O(b) ring operations; each factor's leading coefficient is a known unit. This construction uses multiplication only. Acquire the t points x_h=w^(hb), 0<=h<t, by a single powering and consecutive multiplication. A monic subproduct/remainder tree evaluates F_b at all those points in soft O(b+t) ring operations, even if points coincide or their differences are zero divisors. Its divisors are monic, so it never needs a difference inverse or an inverse of w^j-1. Then

    product_(h=0)^(t-1) F_b(x_h)
      = product_(j=1)^(tb)(w^j-1).

Multiply the at most b-1 remaining factors directly, obtaining exactly Z_B. For B=1, the same scheme works; b=1,t=1. The modular binary powering, product tree, multipoint tree, tail and final gcd cost soft O(sqrt B) ring operations, with polylogarithmic factors in N at the bit level. Since sqrt B<k/16, this is soft O(k) bits under the standard fast integer/polynomial arithmetic model. Intermediate scalar and polynomial coefficient reductions stay modulo N. The implicit product's B leaves are not all enumerated.

This is the classical baby-step/giant-step product method, with the native exponent N used before the scan. Prior algorithmic credit belongs to Strassen-type product evaluation and its q-holonomic extension; see Bostan–Yurkevich, *Fast Computation of the N-th Term of a q-Holonomic Sequence and Applications*, Journal of Symbolic Computation 115 (2023),96–123, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsc.2022.07.008 and https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.08656 . The division-free monic implementation just described states the composite-ring legality directly rather than importing a field-only inverse step.

## 4. What this changes for the knot route

The lift audit found that s->s+tN moves an invariant along the multiplier W=u^N. Before this screen, W can generate a proper subgroup of <u> modulo Q when P divides the local order. The screen either factors such an input in the target time or eliminates that exceptional case rigorously. On every continuing input, W generates <u> in BOTH components, so membership in <W> is exactly membership in <u>. The proof does not supply an inverse of N modulo the unknown local orders or solve a discrete logarithm.

The remaining lift equations have explicit rational targets acquired from each known native root v and its inverse. They still require subgroup membership and a lift-selection rule. In addition, an LG zero must be connected to valid original recovery and the complete physical-index product must be acquired cheaply. Those steps remain open. The prepass is a uniform algorithm with a conditional factoring branch, not a proof that all semiprimes have the special cross-prime divisibility P|r_Q, and not an improvement in the worst-case complete-query exponent.

This result is independent of constructing more knot examples or certifying more finite coverage. Its positive content is the exact factor-or-interface theorem (2) for every prepared input, with the entire test cost paid at the target scale. A focused executable kernel and independent mathematical review accompany it; neither substitutes for the still-open complete acquisition theorem.
