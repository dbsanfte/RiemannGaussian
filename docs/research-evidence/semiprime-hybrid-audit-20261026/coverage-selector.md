# Coverage/selector hybrid audit: which interfaces actually compose?

26 October 2026. Research audit prepared in scratch and archived after cross-review; no numerical tests or campaign. **Finding:** several formulations do have complementary promises, but the available outputs do not yet discharge one another's missing selection/support requirements at soft k. One new, explicitly costed weaker completion contract for the colour-independent kernel query is given in Section 5. It is not an acquired readout.

Its nonsplit torus side makes every correct-bin saturated leaf an exact integer quadratic factor equation. The later scalar catalogue removes the bin/torus dependence using nonzero global-annihilator recovery, at the same rate. [Coarse decoder](/home/dbsanfte/riemann/formal/docs/semiprime-factorisation-investigation.md:43117), [unique-hit target](/home/dbsanfte/riemann/formal/docs/semiprime-factorisation-investigation.md:42255).

These promises are not interchangeable: completeness of coverage, uniqueness, correct tag classification, and cheap support acquisition are different inputs.

## 2. Unique-hit/coarse-tag output does not transfer to the scalar grids for free

The old tag-gradient consumer works because the good nonsplit MINUS family has exactly one hit AND its row tag is the actual factor residue. The proof uses pi=beta*W and barpi=beta/W: coprime scalar/torus orders give a product order exceeding 256H², forcing the local congruence to be an exact integer stationary equation. Both facts are used before the gradient ratio can be called P mod m. [Uniqueness proof](/home/dbsanfte/riemann/formal/docs/semiprime-factorisation-investigation.md:42175), [tag direction](/home/dbsanfte/riemann/formal/docs/semiprime-factorisation-investigation.md:42679).

Oct26 scalar grids instead have ordinary beta order only bounded below by16H. A local alias is a perfectly useful factoring leaf AFTER localization, but need not classify its colour as P mod m. Therefore interpolating colour tags on these roots and invoking the old weak derivative ratio is missing both uniqueness and correct-tag identification. Merely showing the query is nonunit supplies neither. The universal kernel query has no colour tag at all.

Importing the torus base to restore the old proof destroys the kernel's common-start identity. On a scalar phase-kernel row, sigma=a+bN and lambda+sigma=H*C. Its torus node would be

    pi^lambda*barpi^sigma = (pi^H)^C * W^(-2sigma),

not simply q^C. The extra W factor varies with the row/colour. Even the universal first HNF generator has sigma=lambda=H/2, giving beta^H rather than pi^H. The second retains the c_j-dependent sigma=(k/2)(c_j+N). Thus the old unique-hit information and the new O(1) common seeds are not jointly supplied by either construction. This is an explicit base/character mismatch, not a claim that a new torus-kernel design is impossible.

Even granting selection of one universal-kernel row b would not finish at soft k: its remaining clock length W=O(k^(5/2)) has one-row cost soft sqrt(W)=soft k^(5/4). The old pair-first consumer fit soft k because its horizon was only O(k^(3/2)). That consumer cannot be transplanted with its old cost unchanged.

## 3. Complete root cosets and short Gauss coverage do not supply each other's masks

The Oct24 complete phase catalogue includes every conic root for each admitted signed pair. Full root cosets tile exactly into one longer geometric interval, with a proved source-index decoder. This is a genuine cancellation, but total weighted row-clock mass retains the soft k^(5/4) rate. [Catalogue and tiling](/home/dbsanfte/riemann/formal/docs/semiprime-factorisation-investigation.md:43977), [exact contraction](/home/dbsanfte/riemann/formal/docs/semiprime-factorisation-investigation.md:44031).

The common-character/Gauss boxes are not complete cosets in those phase coordinates. Their supplied domains are sheared parallelograms with row-dependent exact clock translations; one must retain the origin omission and public nonliteral filter. A formal lattice intertwiner does not supply all points of a full coset or certify missing comparisons unit. Conversely using the complete catalogue gives the old tiling/mass, not a sparse subfamily of the new boxes.

The actual shared-slice audit further shows Omega(k³) DISTINCT INTEGER leaf labels already on a fixed-scale subfamily of the current boxes. Literal common-support merging cannot shrink THAT whole family to soft-k² mass, irrespective of the normalizing basis. This is NOT a lower bound for implicit scalar computation or a smaller coverage-preserving subset. [Scoped actual-support proof](/home/dbsanfte/riemann/formal/docs/research-evidence/semiprime-hybrid-audit-20261026/slice-union.md:30).

The finite index-image masks are also not independent extra arithmetic restrictions. For unit rows, the colour quadratic image is exactly the earlier intrinsic conic-square condition. Iterating it does not multiply independent density savings. Its AP-grouped complete query already retains the old bound. A new small covering palette for ACTUAL hits could still help, but has not been proved.

The structured quadratic-class families likewise choose coefficients with a fixed inverse-square tag dependence, while weighted Gauss rows vary through an acquired Euclidean choice. Replacing their data by the Gauss short rows does not preserve the old exact quadratic-class progression. Keeping the old class progression retains its longer clock and existing acquisition cost. [Quadratic class input](/home/dbsanfte/riemann/formal/docs/semiprime-factorisation-investigation.md:43217).

## 4. What the universal kernel completion really gains and loses

Write its actual acquired parameters as

    V=O(sqrt k), W=O(sqrt(N/k)+sqrt k), C=ceil(N/(2k)),
    Z=prod_(|b|<=V) prod_(|h|<=W, h!=Cb)(1-q^(Cb-h)).

The target is guaranteed NONUNIT, every literal zero is omitted BEFORE evaluation, and every other leaf has its own nonzero short label H*(Cb-h). Descriptors and starts are genuinely acquired; there is no hidden colour/domain oracle. The old joint evaluator has V*W=O(sqrt N+k), hence soft N^(1/4) readout. This is a completed compressed cover, not the old unsupported generator-screen promise.

The trade is visible geometrically. An original Gauss lattice has weighted covolume kN and short norm O(sqrt(kN)), giving physical index scale O(k^(3/2)). A generic scalar phase kernel has index about k² inside it, so the inherited Gauss SHORT-VECTOR UPPER BOUND at that covolume becomes larger; this is not a necessity or lower-bound claim. Section 7 uses a universal subkernel and a new inner-lattice vector to obtain its explicit O(k^(5/2)) clock. The original short Gauss witness does not automatically lie in that kernel. Multiplying or transporting it to make the phase zero changes its clock range and exact carries. This is the executed cost trade, not a lower-bound theorem for all kernels or correlated covers.

The cubic cover does not remove this cost either: its chosen explicit construction has soft N^(2/9) rows/windows and a DIFFERENT clock beta^(k³). Feeding that acquired family to an additional norm algorithm already pays its input scale. Moving its clock to beta^(k²) sends index i to k*i. Keeping that sparse AP simply recovers the original beta^(k³) evaluation; filling the interval introduces extra comparisons and supplies no shorter evaluated clock. A new sparse restriction or an independent shared acquisition algorithm would be required; no free composition follows from their separate coverage proofs. These are costs of the specified executed constructions, not lower bounds on all implicit compositions.

## 5. A narrower hybrid target with a FULL soft-k consumer

There is a useful lower-information target specific to the universal kernel cover. Suppose a readout, itself acquired in soft k time with bounded attempts, returns ONE residue rho modulo k such that some guaranteed nonliteral kernel hit has

    h=rho mod k.

Keep all O(sqrt k) acquired b starts and only that AP clock inside [-W,W]. The new length is O(W/k+1), with common base q^k. Its total row-clock budget is

    V*(W/k+1)=O(sqrt N/k+sqrt k)=O(k²)

when N<=k⁶. The inherited monic joint evaluator, signed starts, and clipped tails therefore acquire its scalar/nonunit predicate in soft k. Each b has at most one literal h=Cb to omit; split its interval directly, with the established variable-window/tail algorithm. All extra tests are original kernel leaves and retain H*(Cb-h)!=0. Prefix nonunit localization and the once-only T_ext fit soft k. Wrong residues still run within the same bound and return only validated factors or failure.

A polylogarithmic palette of such residues works with the same soft bound. No colour, row, group root, unique hit or exact index is needed. This is substantially narrower than an arbitrary degree-k polynomial/geometric-orbit oracle: it is a coarse clock-residue readout for ONE explicitly acquired two-GP scalar family.

Alternatively, if the readout supplies b as well, a residue h modulo R with R>=ceil(W/k²) suffices. Query that ONE start on its AP clock of length O(k²); its one-row evaluator costs soft k. In the hard N about k⁶ regime R=O(sqrt k) suffices. This is another proved consumer, not an algorithm providing those outputs.

The old native palettes do NOT currently produce this residue: universal h=I-t-epsilon*b belongs to a different UNKNOWN inner-lattice vector used only in the coverage proof. No paid map transports the old MINUS-row index palette, or a generic colour image, to this h residue. Interpolating an h-mod-k weight along the long q-clock is also not the old cheaply acquired row-tag polynomial: the long clock weights themselves would need initialization. A correct P mod m readout would of course finish by the classical decoder directly, but none of these constructions acquires it.

## Conclusion

The walls are partly complementary, but the natural compositions have precise missing interfaces. Torus uniqueness loses the common scalar seeds; scalar coverage lacks correct-tag uniqueness; full-coset contraction loses the sparse finite mask; common-coordinate support keeps its exact lifts; universal kernel seeds buy complete coverage only with a longer clock. These are scoped mismatches, not proof that hybrid methods cannot succeed.

The new concrete bridge is Section 5's universal-kernel coarse-clock-residue contract with a fully paid soft-k recovery algorithm. An actual acquisition of that residue, or another coupled support theorem that replaces it, would be genuine progress. This audit supplies the composition and identifies the still-missing output honestly; it establishes no sixth-exponent algorithm or new query rate.
