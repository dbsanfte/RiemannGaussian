# Hybrid audit: cheap q-binomial moments / TQ core versus unique-hit locators

The most direct weight-alignment composition gives BOTH candidate outputs identically zero on every good unique-hit input. The outputs are not literally the same scalar renamed: a unique-hit projective locator has a weaker requested output specification than acquiring the full product (no computational separation is proved), and has a fully paid decoder. ## 1. Exact interfaces and promise overlap

Its good-side promise is: the SMALLER prime is the nonsplit field, and exactly one simple row/root and one index I collide. This promise is not global uniqueness and is not supplied on wrong orientations. The polylogarithmic orientation palette remains conditional on its stated QRH import; bounded wrong-label validation is safe.

Paid outputs, if acquired, may be any of:

    T_g=sum_(i<n) g(x_i)*product_(r!=i)F(x_r),
    H_g=sum_(i<n) lambda_i*g(x_i)*product_(r!=i)F(x_r),
    lambda_i=1/G_n'(x_i), G_n(X)=product_(i<n)(X-x_i).

Here the subscript1 means the CONSTANT polynomial g=1. At the good field, T_(g=1) and H_(g=1) are nonzero; T_X/T_(g=1)=H_X/H_(g=1)=x_I. For the acquired interpolation polynomial g(V_a)=original tag a, the corresponding ratio returns that tag. The old three-state recurrences start at actual public vectors and compute these outputs in soft(J+sqrt(Jn)); after a valid pair, original row/root validation and one-row localization cost soft(J+sqrt n). Exact Bezout adjugates differ from H_1,H_X only by a public convention sign. A linear subresultant is another sufficient input, not an acquired one.

The Oct26 moment theorem accepts ANY acquired degree-J polynomial with a guarded q-clock; it can therefore be applied DIRECTLY to this old F, rather than assuming the new scalar knot F has the old unique-hit theorem. Set m=n-1 and z_1=1 in its moment notation. Then3J<m eventually,2m<16H, and the old actual seed/unit guards justify every used factorial inverse. The entire short moment alphabet and degree-at-most-J core cost soft(J+sqrt n)=soft k per orientation. Arithmetic over fixed-rank B changes no exponent. Thus this pairing has genuine interface overlap; it is not rejected merely because the two original reports used different row families or horizons.

The moment parameter is m=n-1. For the TQ composition one may separately use an n-point string z_i=q^(i-1),1<=i<=n,z_0=q^-1; its guards through2n are still available. ## 2. The strongest direct composition: align the two weight systems

Put m=n-1 and f_m=(q;q)_m, a certified unit. The cheap Oct26 moment weights are

    nu_i=(-1)^i*q^[i(i+1)/2-2mi]*[m choose i]_q,
    mu_t=sum_(i=0)^m nu_i*F(x_i)*x_i^t.

From the ACTUAL clock derivative in the old cofactor report,

    G_n'(q^i)=(-1)^i*q^[im-i(i+1)/2]
                      *(q;q)_i*(q;q)_(m-i),

one gets the exact acquired-weight relation

    nu_i=f_m*x_i^(-m)*lambda_i.                   (1)

This is a real bridge, not a formal similarity between unrelated moments. It suggests tilting the new moments by x_i^m to align them with the old Bezout weights. Execute it:

    mu_(m+s)=f_m*sum_i lambda_i*x_i^s*F(x_i)=0
                        whenever J+s<m.          (2)

The zero is EXACT over B: for any polynomial P of degree<m, Lagrange interpolation gives sum_i P(x_i)/G_n'(x_i)=0. Here P=X^s F. Equivalently the new coefficient formula contains B_t=(q^(t+1-2m);q)_m, which vanishes throughout t=m,...,2m-1; all j+m+s fall in that universal zero band.

In particular the aligned candidate pair s=0,1 is(0,0) on EVERY actual good unique-hit input once J+1<m. It cannot satisfy the old locator's required nonzero denominator. No numeric example, arbitrary-root model or generic-degree heuristic is used. This is a decisive mismatch for the specific "moments already supply the cofactor weights" composition.

The missing operation is explicit: moments weight F(x_i), whereas the locator weights product_(r!=i)F(x_r). On the good unique hit those latter weights vanish except at I and have one nonzero survivor; the former have a zero AT I and generally retain all the nonhits. Correct weight alignment alone does not reverse that support.

The weakest unweighted moment-gradient attempt also executes: differentiating mu_0(F+u+vX) yields S(1),S(X), whose ratio is B_1/B_0, independent of F. It is a clock-dependent average, not a proved hit selector. It can be fed to the bounded validator as an optional cheap candidate, but there is no coverage theorem and no rate improvement from that branch.

## 3. Positive exact bridge through the FULL moment determinant

Let H_n(F)=(mu_(a+b))_(0<=a,b<n). The full-support identity is

    det H_n(F)=D_clock*product_(i<n)F(x_i),
    D_clock=product_i nu_i * product_(i<j)(x_j-x_i)^2,

where D_clock is a mathematical unit. Therefore the exact FIRST VARIATIONS are

    partial_u det H_n(F+u+vX)|0=D_clock*T_1,
    partial_v det H_n(F+u+vX)|0=D_clock*T_X.         (3)

Their projective ratio is the desired x_I. Numerical acquisition of D_clock is unnecessary for this ratio. This is the strongest valid Frankenstein identity found: the full determinant's projective gradient matches the old sufficient output, through collisions, with no desired-value inverse.

But the cheap sequence mu_0,...,mu_(2J) initializes only short Hankel matrices. For s<=J their determinant is the Cauchy–Binet sum over s-point SUBSETS. Its gradient is a sum of subset-complement products, not the full leave-one-out sums in(3). At s=1 this reduces to the F-independent average just noted. At the full support size n, derivatives(3) require the long determinant/contraction; taking its small descriptor or adjoint does not acquire them.

On the good unique-hit field the full modified measure has exactly n-1 nonzero weights, so its minimal infinite moment recurrence has order n-1: distinct clock poles have nonzero residues. Its Hankel corank-one kernel is the coefficient vector of G_n(X)/(X-x_I), a degree n-1 polynomial. This scopes the failure of direct SHORT-Prony/kernel extraction; it is not a bound on arbitrary algorithms using the additional known F.

The exact J-dimensional Christoffel dual determinant in the Oct26 report has entries v_a^h*G_n(v_a). Those G_n(v_a) are precisely the old long orbit evaluations. At the unique collision one row vanishes, but locating that row requires their zero masks; the short moment convolution has not supplied them. Thus the exact duality returns to the linear-subresultant/remainder target rather than initializing it.

## 4. Execute the small TQ core plus projective-gradient composition

After screening a constant number of ORIGINAL endpoints in soft J and handling any nonunit by the old leaf consumer, the unique hit may be assumed interior for this test. The exact TQ pencil has

    det M_epsilon(F)=P_ret(F)*C(epsilon)*T_epsilon(F),
    P_ret(F)=product_(i=2..n)F(x_(i-1)).

The retained factor INCLUDES the last original endpoint F(x_(n-1)); only the first endpoint is supplied by T_0(F)=F(x_0). Screening the last endpoint unit is permitted to make the good hit strictly interior, but it is NOT removed from P_ret. Removing it would require an extra accounted factor. C is known and independent of F; all coefficients of the degree-at-most-J core T_epsilon are acquired in soft J. The core is LINEAR in F. At the good interior hit P_ret=0. For a perturbation delta F=g,

    D_g det M_epsilon
       =C(epsilon)*T_epsilon(F)*D_g P_ret,
    D_g P_ret=g(x_I)*product_(r=1..n-1,r!=I)F(x_r). (4)

Hence, if the displayed common core factor is nonzero, the ratio of the FULL derivatives for g=X and g=1 is exactly x_I. At epsilon=0, C(0)=1 and T_0(F) is the screened endpoint F value, so it can be chosen a unit; no speculative generic epsilon is needed.

However differentiating the CHEAP core gives T_epsilon(g), not the derivative in(4). In fact the core-derivative term P_ret*C*T_epsilon(g) is ZERO at the collision. The missing derivative D_g P_ret is the old complementary-product locator up to the known-unit first-endpoint factor F(x_0), which cancels in its projective ratio. Thus applying Baur–Strassen or square-zero arithmetic only to the acquired small-core circuit computes the wrong first variation for the locator. It does not obtain the full determinant's projective gradient.

If T_epsilon(F) also vanishes, the full first derivative can vanish and provides even less; no additive core zero is assigned an original beta-annihilator decoder. Dividing by P_ret or using A^-1 in the normalized pencil would be illegal at this same hit. Universal zeros of C supply no help, since they annihilate every F variation too.

The small core is therefore useful normalization/linear-transform information, while the locator asks for the complementary-product FIRST VARIATION of the removed normalization. This identifies exactly the term a successful new hybrid must compute; it is not a statement that no hybrid can do so.

## 5. Precise next hybrid target and cost

A sufficient new operation is a soft(J+sqrt n) evaluator of the PROJECTIVE pair

    [D_1 P_ret : D_X P_ret]

(or its tag-polynomial version), using the acquired F, q-binomial moments/core, and bounded wrong-orientation validation. It may avoid the exact product, exact adjugate, or common amplitude. The old three-state algorithm already provides it at soft(J+sqrt(Jn)); no inspected identity above removes that degree-dependent contraction.

A less specialized sufficient operation would acquire the rank-one first-variation tensor in(4) from the small core plus singular pencil WITHOUT dividing by the vanished normalization. No such algorithm or finite-ring normalization theorem is supplied by the current reports. That is more focused than requesting another determinant identity, and permits genuinely complementary new data rather than insisting on the whole product.

The old conditional orientation palette is still a separate promise-supply wall. Conditional on the good label, recovery is paid; neither the cheap moments nor TQ core settles that orientation issue. Even granting the promise, the explicit tested compositions above do not initialize the two locator outputs. These conclusions are scoped to the executed bridges, not generic lower bounds or a claim that every past representation has exactly the same obstacle.
