# Reformulations investigated on 24–26 October 2026

This inventory uses those three calendar dates. It groups repeated
versions of one mathematical representation together and distinguishes
exact rewrites, alternative covered families, weaker requested outputs,
and proposals whose required arithmetic property failed. The numbered
groups are an organizational choice, not 34 independent algorithms.

The common task is to extract a useful factor collision from a uniformly
covered family at soft O(k), k of order N^(1/6). In a principal scalar
form, the unpaid quantity is a finite product of F(q^i), or enough
information about its nonunits to recover a factor. An acquired small
description is different from an acquired value or decision bit.

## Reading the record without double-counting

The 24 October work is frozen in the
[recurrence checkpoint](../semiprime-recurrence-checkpoint-20261025/README.md),
including its errata. Later packets are linked from the dated journal.
The present audit adds the colour-free kernel completion, the explicit
hybrid tests and the shared-slice checks; it does not rewrite earlier
frozen reports.

Four qualifications matter when comparing apparent wins:

* The corrected swapped catalogue guarantees a common globally zero
  **index**, not necessarily the same leaf.
* The later Cauchy diagonalizer is not a second independent acquisition
  method. Fixed-q mask acceleration requires its specified factorized
  coefficient-index mask, not arbitrary folded target moments.
* Conditional orientation/Hecke or class-group imports remain
  conditional where their upstream theorem was not independently
  established. They do not silently supply a complete factoring cover.
* Neither the research route's soft N^(5/24) bound nor a soft N^(1/4)
  auxiliary completion is presented as a general factoring record.

The [main audit](README.md) compares actual supplied data and required
outputs, then executes the three strongest candidate handoffs. It
explains why some entries share one wall and others fail at distinct
arithmetic or promise requirements.
