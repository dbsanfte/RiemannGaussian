# Direct native lattice route: shorten the integer quotient before collision search

2026-10-22. Focused primary-literature follow-up and exact source reduction. No frozen files changed, no backend or example campaign. I did not obtain a proof of the sixth-rate factorizer.

## Exact native small-root problem

Use the existing native public row j,a,b,c,ell with P=j+mh, ell=mb-2aj, c=(N+jmb-j^2a)/m^2. The true row gives

 a h^2+b h+c = I(j+mh).                                  (1)

Indeed substitution of P into aP+Q+ell=m^2 I, followed by multiplication by P and PQ=N, gives (1). Thus the direct bivariate integer equation is

 F_j(h,I)=a h^2+(b-mI)h+c-jI=0.                          (2)

In a balanced factor range P~N^1/2 and m~N^1/6, h=O(m^2) and I=O(m^2). All m public j branches are already affordable. Finding their actual small integer roots with a proved soft O(m) TOTAL bound would bypass the geometric interval query and recover P=j+mh directly. A second independent lattice relation cannot be assumed: equation (2) is just the original modular hyperbola in these coordinates.

For example its discriminant condition is

 (b-mI)^2-4a(c-jI) is an integer square.

This reparameterizes the same search; no square-root/lattice acquisition theorem follows merely from its degree two. A resultant made only from multiples of F_j is identically zero and supplies no new information.

## Inspected primary route: use the second rank-three lattice vector

Primary paper inspected directly: Gao–Feng–Hu–Pan, *On Factoring and Power Divisor Problems via Rank-3 Lattices and the Second Vector*, arXiv:2512.19076v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2512.19076v1 . I read the technical overview and the actual lattice, successive-minimum argument and collision reduction in Lemma4.4/Proposition4.5, plus Algorithm5.1/Proposition5.2. The latter requires a supplied residue class. Their second reduced vector avoids a trivial linear relation, gives a quadratic quotient search, and retains a baby/giant collision stage. Their unconditional balanced-semiprime headline exponent is 1/5, not1/6. The earlier repository small-aux-literature.md already records the supplied-class limitation; the quantitative comparison below is a fresh application to the present row scale, not a claim that this paper is new to the repository.

## Quantitative application to the present scale

Here is the explicit actionable construction, using different letters from the native row. For each candidate j form f_j(x)=x+j m^-1 modulo N and the weighted coefficient lattice generated by N,f_j,f_j^2 at a public bound X~P/m. Its determinant is N X^3. The known short linear vector represents mx+j. Obtain an independent quadratic

 g_j(x)=A_j x^2+B_j x+C_j,

with A_j nonzero under the paper's stated parameter conditions. On the true row P divides g_j(h), so set g_j(h)=sigma P. The second-minimum estimate gives, in a fixed factor-size dyadic range,

 |sigma|=O(N^1/2/m^1.5).                                 (3)

All constant-rank reductions and coefficients are acquired in soft O(m), including polylogarithmic factor-size branching. With m~N^1/6, (3) is O(m^1.5): it really shortens the original native I interval of length O(m^2).

It also provides explicit giant exponents without knowing h:

 E_j=C_j m^2+B_j m(1-j)+A_j(1-j)^2.

Since mh=P-j=1-j modulo P-1 and g_j(h)=sigma P, Fermat gives

 beta^E_j = beta^(m^2 sigma) modulo P.                   (4)

Negative bounded sigma may be handled by shifting a common interval and multiplying target values by a known unit. The paper supplies the nontrivial quadratic recovery route when matching saturates modulo N; a proper local match is handled by its gcd collision routine. Prepared order does not erase the unknown sigma or make its entire interval a supplied object.

The explicit standard algorithm pays m giant steps and K=O(m^1.5) baby steps, hence soft O(m^1.5), rather than soft O(m). Using the existing implicit geometric-query machinery still leaves the charged target/interval acquisition, not a free integer root. Optimizing m in the explicit cost m+N^1/2/m^1.5 yields the familiar N^1/5 scale. Thus importing this real lattice improvement does not establish the desired exponent.

## A precise lattice theorem that WOULD finish this route

A sufficient direct improvement is a deterministic construction of soft O(m) public nontrivial quadratic relations, covering every true factor row, for which the actual quotients g_j(h)/P lie in an explicitly acquired union of soft O(m) integer values, with total construction soft O(m). In particular a uniform bound |g_j(h)|<=O(Pm) would suffice: enumerate that short sigma interval explicitly and use the existing near-linear explicit-list gcd collision/recovery routines. This is a complete consumer contract, with no unknown I left and with all list lengths paid.

The inspected second-minimum proof yields only O(P m^1.5) at m~N^1/6. In balanced notation its known short vector has scale m^3, determinant scale m^12, and the resulting bound on the next independent vector is scale m^4.5. The sufficient target is scale m^4. Improving that estimate for the acquired family, or acquiring a sparse quotient menu instead, is the exact extra arithmetic theorem needed. These numbers diagnose the gap in THIS construction; they are not lattice lower bounds or a proof that another higher-rank/structured construction cannot work.

The native equation (2) alone does not supply that extra theorem. Nor can one invoke a general multivariate lattice heuristic to guarantee an independent integer relation. I found no inspected primary theorem that supplies the required shorter quotient family with these all-input bounds and total cost. The outcome is therefore a concrete, genuinely acquired quotient-shortening construction and a precisely charged sufficient improvement, not the claimed 1/6 factorization algorithm.
