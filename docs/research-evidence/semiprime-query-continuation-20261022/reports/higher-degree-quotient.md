# Higher-degree modular-root lattices: quantitative independent-vector and lift ledger

Let N be comparable to m^6, a balanced hidden prime P comparable to m^3, X comparable to P/m comparable to m^2, and j=P mod m. Fix CONSTANT integers 1<=s<=d. Let f=x+j*m^-1 modulo N and use the weighted degree-d lattice generated by N^(s-i) f^i for 0<=i<=s and f^s x^k for 1<=k<=d-s. Its determinant is

 det L=N^(s(s+1)/2) X^(d(d+1)/2),
 log_m det L = D=d(d+1)+3s(s+1)+O(1/log m).              (1)

Every lattice polynomial satisfies P^s divides g(h) on the true h=(P-j)/m. A short vector with weighted norm O(P^s m) would therefore give an explicitly bounded quotient sigma=g(h)/P^s of size O(m). This suffices for m-sized explicit baby/giant matching, provided the polynomial recovery is nonzero as proved below.

## All known polynomial multiples and their actual sizes

Put L0=mx+j, a primitive integer polynomial. The known sublattice S consists of L0^s q(x), deg q<=d-s. It is contained in L by expanding L0=m f-N*c with integer c. Gauss's lemma shows that every integer polynomial in its rational span has INTEGER q, so this is the full lattice intersection with that trivial polynomial subspace.

For a nonzero q of degree k, the highest weighted coefficient has size at least

 m^s X^(s+k), comparable to m^(3s+2k).                  (2)

The basis L0^s x^k has these weighted scales and is asymptotically triangular/orthogonal for fixed d,s. Thus the trivial directions have scales m^(3s),m^(3s+2),...,m^(3s+2(d-s)). They must not be replaced by the determinant mean of the full lattice.

In particular, within a desired O(m^(3s+1)) norm ball, the only possible trivial direction is the single constant multiple of L0^s. This observation is useful: a genuinely small second independent vector WOULD solve the quotient problem. It does not prove that such a vector exists.

## Correct second-independent-vector determinant bound

Use a dichotomy. If the shortest vector is already a useful nontrivial vector of norm at most the target, retain it. Otherwise its first scale is the known m^(3s). Minkowski's second theorem and lambda_i>=lambda_2 for i>=2 give, up to fixed-dimensional constants,

 lambda_2 <= (det L/lambda_1)^(1/d),
 log_m lambda_2 <= e(d,s)=d+1+3s^2/d.                   (3)

LLL changes only constants because d,s are fixed. For the quotient this yields exponent

 e(d,s)-3s = 1+d+3s^2/d-3s
                 >=1+(2 sqrt(3)-3)s >1.               (4)

For d=2,s=1, this gives norm m^4.5 and quotient m^1.5, exactly the already-audited quadratic second-vector scale. A determinant MEAN incorrectly predicting quotient m at this pair has omitted the first trivial vector.

This is an audited guarantee of this determinant/successive-minimum argument, not a lower bound on achievable lattice vectors. For higher parameters it may not even certify nontriviality: (3) must be compared with the next trivial scale m^(3s+2). That exponent gap is strict for d2,s1; it is not generally strict for s>=2. A larger independent vector can land inside the known higher-degree trivial subspace. One must test divisibility/recovery rather than call every second vector informative.

## Projecting out ALL trivial directions gives a real small quotient lattice, but not a short lift

The weighted covolume of S has exponent

 S_exp=sum_(k=0)^(d-s)(3s+2k)=(d-s+1)(d+2s).

The orthogonally projected quotient lattice has dimension s and determinant exponent

 D-S_exp=s(5s+1-d).

Its mean scale is therefore m^(5s+1-d). This tempting positive gain is REAL for projected vectors. However a projected vector is not an integer polynomial g. Recovering an integer lift may add a point in a fundamental domain of S, whose largest basis scale is m^(2d+s). The projected shortest-vector bound does not bound those lifted coordinates or g(h)/P^s. Rationally subtracting arbitrary multiples of L0^s does not repair this: denominators and the residual integer quotient must be charged, and the original lattice integrality cannot be erased.

A constructive completion could exploit the semiprime source to choose a lift whose actual quotient is short even when its coefficient norm is not. The oriented quadratic/window construction does exactly that locally by cancelling derivatives. I have not derived an analogous all-row higher-degree lift with O(m) quotient range and soft O(m) total window/vector count. The projection calculation specifies a potentially useful place to seek such a lift; it does not already supply it.

## Saturated recovery extends rigorously to fixed degree

For a supplied polynomial g and centered true quotient sigma, clear all public powers of m and form

 G(Z)=m^d g((Z-j)/m)-sigma*m^d Z^s.                     (5)

This is an integer polynomial of bounded degree. If it is nonzero, recover its integer roots deterministically in polynomial logarithmic bit work and verify divisors of N. The exact zero-polynomial exception is g(x)=sigma*(mx+j)^s; it is directly testable. A known higher-degree multiple L0^s q with NONCONSTANT q is not an identically-zero recovery polynomial, but its quotient q(h) still needs the proved short bound. No known direction is counted useful merely from lattice membership.

The Fermat giant step can be constructed from m^d g((1-j)/m), and the baby step is beta^(m^d sigma): since P^s=1 modulo P-1, the true row forces their equality modulo P. For a retained quotient interval of diameter Dq, a full-N match sigma' equals the actual sigma whenever ord_P(beta^(m^d))>Dq. With dyadic m and beta of prepared odd local order, powering by m^d preserves that local order, so the retained >16m^2 bound covers O(m) quotient intervals. Thus arbitrarily large polynomial giant exponents do not invalidate recovery: uniqueness uses the SHORT quotient difference, not the giant exponent size. Non-dyadic choices must separately check powering loss.

All centering corrections must be actual known constant multiples of L0^s if they are to shift sigma by a known integer. Nonconstant trivial corrections change sigma by q(h), which is unknown; treating them as freely known quotient shifts would be circular.

## Constructive endpoint

For any fixed d,s the lattice and the known-trivial sublattice are publicly acquired per row in polynomial logarithmic work, so m rows are affordable. The projected quotient lattice is also an explicit fixed-dimensional object. The still-required algorithm is an integer lift/center selection giving a proved short ACTUAL quotient on an all-input covering family, with total acquired row/window/vector descriptions soft O(m). Equations (3)–(4) do not provide it; the attractive projected mean does not include lifting.

No such lift or higher-degree all-input cover was proved in this pass. The positive contributions are the exact full-trivial quotient geometry, the corrected independent-vector bound, and the degree-d saturated recovery theorem with its explicit zero-polynomial and local-order conditions. These avoid a false determinant gain while retaining a concrete short-lift target for further constructive work. No impossibility theorem is claimed.
