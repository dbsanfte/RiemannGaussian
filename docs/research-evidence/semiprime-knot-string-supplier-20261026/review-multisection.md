# Independent paper review of multisection.md

26 October 2026. Read-only mathematical review, including string-theory section 6. No tests or repository edits.

Verdict: the finite formulas and stated non-advancement are correct. In particular the report does NOT silently assume that the prepared local q-orders are coprime to k. Its faithful-phase claim explicitly requires that additional premise; the useful-phase decoder does not.

* Puncture: at Z=a_b^{-1}, F'(Z)=-a_b product_(c!=b)(1-a_c Z), so the transition -F'(Z)/a_b is exact even with numerical aliases among the other roots. Integer C>0 gives at most one literal row at a clock time; aliases are not extra literal deletions. Direct row splitting avoids evaluating a globally zero product and subsequently dividing it. The AP partition preserves those omissions in every child. The soft(V+sqrt(V(W/d+1))) bound uses the existing variable-length shared monic kernel, not just the displayed scalar recurrence.
* Phase filter: local primitive components have exact order d because p does not divide d. Coprime local order(q),d implies simultaneous original hit and h=rho mod d. Without coprimality a phase hit implies q^{d(Cb-h)}=1; the retained leaf has Cb-h nonzero, so dH(Cb-h) is a legal nonzero short exponent label. Localization of a nonunit extension norm may thus use a scalar gcd and the existing extractor without claiming a faithful original residue. No order-roughness is automatic here.
* q-Lucas: the relevant deformation base is Q=zeta/q, with Q^d=q^{-d}. The native short-order promise makes q^d-1 a unit for d<=k; Q is consequently not a primitive dth root in these components. Thus the stated cyclotomic specialization cannot be applied to this string. This conclusion is about that specialization only.
* Section 6 normalization: D(t)=(1-t)^r0 P(t) gives [u^r0]D(1+u)=(-1)^r0 P(1), including possible additional local collision zeros. The displayed dual-Cauchy alphabets and scalar start R^{-V}q^W are correct. The ordinary inverse vertex scalar is illegal to evaluate at t=1 without the indicated regularization. Integral identity validity does not establish a fast Hasse-coefficient algorithm.
* Finite Ward endpoints: shifting z to qz replaces exponent range [-W,W] by [-W+1,W+1], hence F(q^{-W}z)D(qz)=F(q^{W+1}z)D(z), exactly as written. Substitution of the literal factor (1-z)^r0 yields (10), with no missing q-power or reversed endpoint. A finite telescoping sum keeps B_(W+1)-B_(-W); deleting a summand B_(h+1)-B_h subtracts that pair, so the claimed additional hole terms are required. D(0)=1 supplies no evaluated scalar at the collision node z=1. Neither endpoint evaluation nor a normalized quotient proves interior coverage.

Cost/scope: the complete initialized multisection remains soft N^(1/4), and the old native complete query remains soft k^(5/4). T_ext must be included upon factor return, at most once. The norm implementation is deliberately conservative; no rank-independent norm or Fourier readout is established. Boundary/Schur descriptions and the exact regularized coefficient are acquired as descriptions, while their soft-k scalar evaluation is explicitly unacquired. No correction necessary on these points.

Minor precision for future citations: the native bounds J=O(sqrt k) and M=O(sqrt(N/k)+sqrt k) are the actual size statement; writing M=O(k^(5/2)) is its N<=k^6 upper bound, with comparable size only in the hard N~k^6 regime. The report already qualifies that regime.
