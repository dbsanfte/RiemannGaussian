# Fresh primary search: no paid mixed-contraction theorem identified

2026-10-16. Focused literature/interface audit against the actual long query, not a general impossibility result. The target remains J~sqrt L values of F(z)=P_L(z;q) over Z/N in soft O(J), with setup and restriction support paid. The previous packet already pays block actions, square block inverses, a rank-two q-pencil, and its normalized quasiseparable right operator on the unit-boundary branch. This report does not rebrand those as a new query algorithm.

The search found no new primary theorem that turns those operators into their mixed diagonal at the desired clock. Four concrete candidate interfaces, including two sources with 2026 revisions, were checked directly below. PDFs and extracted texts actually used are adjacent and pinned in manifest.json. These are applicability findings for the specified theorems; they are not exhaustive literature or scalar lower-bound claims.

## 1. Rational coefficient preprocessing: a new 2026 theorem, but the wrong acquisition scale

Ahle--Knudsen, Fast Evaluation of Polynomials with Rational Preprocessing, arXiv:2609.06022, September 2026: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06022 . Inspected the primary introduction, model and main theorem statements. Over a field of characteristic zero or p>n, an explicitly parameterized monic degree-n polynomial admits evaluation schemes with floor(n/2)+1 charged nonscalar multiplications, after rational preprocessing of its coefficient vector. The model separately treats fixed scalar operations and additions; rational preprocessing is a rational section of the coefficient parameterization on its stated domain, not an oracle acquiring missing coefficients.

The actual hidden characteristics exceed L, so characteristic alone is not the problem. Taking n=L gives Theta(L) multiplications per point, already larger than a single q-factorial baby-step/giant-step query. Taking n~J for individual block polynomials requires their independently prepared coefficient/gate data; there are Theta(J²) block entries, while the new paid primitive supplies weighted actions without materializing them. The paper does not give a shared compiler from our rational-Newton description to all those row circuits in soft O(J). Passing to Z/N additionally requires each preprocessing denominator to be a unit or an explicit factor/saturation branch. Hence this recent theorem supplies no acquisition improvement here. It does not exclude a different compressed preparation.

## 2. Elliptic butterflies: genuinely fast basis transforms with supplied orbit/basis data

Couveignes--Lercier, Elliptic Butterflies, arXiv:2510.27375, revised March 2026 (the primary abstract page says to be published in Journal of Complexity): https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.27375 . Full primary PDF downloaded; inspected the problem definitions, section2 summary and recursive construction contract. It gives d log d straight-line evaluation/interpolation in specially chosen elliptic-function bases. The setup includes an elliptic curve over a field of odd characteristic, a rational point of known dyadic order d, an evaluation coset b+<t>, and degree-d divisor/basis coordinates; the recursive maps use the corresponding two-isogeny tower. Its reduction problem takes the coordinates of the function being reduced.

These transforms are useful when their basis/orbit data and function coordinates are supplied. The source gamma instead has unknown odd local prime orders, and the actual target list is not supplied as that elliptic coset. More importantly F is an implicit product of length L, not an already acquired d-coordinate elliptic function for d~J. Taking a representation of dimension L already pays the forbidden degree-L data. Taking dimension J presupposes a reduction from F to it, which is precisely the missing acquisition. No conversion from the paid block operator or arbitrary original D to the paper's supplied coordinate input is proved there. No known-order torsion point, extension, basis conversion or local field is granted free over the composite ring. This is an exact input-interface mismatch, not an obstruction to all elliptic algorithms.

## 3. Truncated resultants: cheap jets after the resultant inputs are supplied

Moroz--Schost, A Fast Algorithm for Computing the Truncated Resultant, ISSAC2016 / arXiv:1609.04259: https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.04259 . Inspected the primary theorem and algorithm assumptions. For supplied P,Q in K[x,y] of degree bounded by d, it computes Res_y(P,Q) modulo x^k in soft O(kd) field operations, with the stated characteristic condition (zero or at least k). Its half-gcd/normalization and initial-condition steps are actual operations on the supplied polynomial coefficients.

For the direct original resultant Res(F,D), the y degree bound includes L. Even k=1 gives a degree-L input/clock, not soft O(J). For a degree-O(J) coarse numerator the theorem could make small parameter jets efficient, but that numerator's coefficient list is not yet acquired. The paid arbitrary-point CHANNEL VECTOR is not an explicit degree-O(J) polynomial in its scalar argument, nor the truncated y-coefficient list this algorithm requires. Likewise writing det(I+diag(theta)S) as a resultant would need a new paid low-degree resultant representation; its existence is not supplied by this theorem. On Z/N, field normalization/pivots require their own unit/factor contract. No result here rules out discovering precisely such a representation.

## 4. Fast Ore/linearized operations: their q-transform is not this q-product

Puchinger--Wachter-Zeh, Fast Operations on Linearized Polynomials and their Applications in Coding Theory, arXiv:1512.06520 (saved v3): https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.06520 . Inspected the primary model, operation table and q-transform/evaluation statements. Its linearized polynomial is sum a_k x^(q^k) over a supplied finite extension F_(q^m); multiplication is composition and q denotes the known base-field cardinality. The related skew-polynomial algorithms use a specified automorphism, with normal-basis/subfield promises explicitly charged in the specialized transform results. General multiplication and evaluation are subquadratic degree algorithms, not a soft O(J) theorem for our filtered q-factorial.

Our q=gamma^(m²) is a supplied multiplicative unit, not a supplied Frobenius or finite-field cardinality. One may formally choose the automorphism t->qt over a rational-function coefficient field, but using the small quotient by the original D then requires that automorphism to descend. For a split degree-J polynomial, that means its target set is q-invariant. The actual local order R>J makes a nonempty such set impossible. This is the already known fixed-carrier condition, mentioned solely to audit the Ore algorithm's input, not claimed as a new obstruction. Moving carriers, larger coefficient fields and other Ore formulations remain open and must pay their degrees and conversions. Known extension/cardinality/order information from the coding-theory theorem cannot be inferred from the original semiprime input.
