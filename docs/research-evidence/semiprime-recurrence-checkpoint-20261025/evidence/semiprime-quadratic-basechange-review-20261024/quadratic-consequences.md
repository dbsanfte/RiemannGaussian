## Exact descent to every quadratic field

Let K be quadratic with fundamental discriminant D. Its conductor over Q is |D|, so K is contained in F=Q(zeta_lcm(12,|D|)). F/K is abelian. For a finite-order Hecke character chi on K, let chi_F=chi composed with Norm_(F/K). Abelian induction gives the complete finite Hecke-L identity

    L_F(s,chi_F)=prod_(psi in Gal(F/K)^hat) L_K(s,chi*psi).

Here psi is the corresponding finite-order Hecke character of K; the identity includes the ramified local factors with their proper conductors. The trivial psi factor is L_K(s,chi).

Away from s=1 every factor on the right is holomorphic, so a zero of the trivial-psi factor in Re(s)>1-delta would force a zero of the left side. The only potential poles of other finite-order factors are principal-character poles at 1. At 1 use the standard nonvanishing of nonprincipal finite-order Hecke L-functions; zeta_K's pole is allowed. Hence every finite-order Hecke L-function on EVERY quadratic K inherits the same width delta=10^-6.

This is an analytic deduction only: no algorithm constructs F, lists its characters, or pays its cyclotomic degree. It remains valid when chi_F happens to be principal.

## Uniform explicit formula directly on K

For a fixed nonnegative smooth phi supported in (1,2), standard degree-two zero counting and the inherited strip give

    sum_a Lambda_K(a) chi(a) phi(Norm(a)/x)
      =1_(chi principal)*x*Phi(1)
         +O_phi(x^(1-delta)*log(2|D|Norm(f_chi))).

The implied constant is absolute once phi is fixed; infinity-type/sign contributions are bounded in degree two. This follows by the same smoothed explicit-formula argument already written in the supplied factorization-analytic.tex, using the Hecke conductor instead of just the Dedekind discriminant. It incurs no degree-of-F loss and does not presume GRH. All constants needed for algorithmic cutoffs can be made effective using the explicit zero-count bounds; the merely qualitative field-dependent onset in the original proof is not used as an algorithmic cutoff.

## A deterministic subroutine that this really funds

For imaginary quadratic K, its ordinary ideal class group is generated by prime ideals of norm at most

    B=(C*log(2|D|))^(1/delta),

with an effective absolute C. To prove this, if the generated subgroup were proper, choose a nonprincipal class character chi trivial on it. Both principal and chi smoothed sums would agree for x<=B/2, since every contributing prime ideal has norm below 2x and chi is 1 on each such ideal. But their explicit formulas differ by x*Phi(1)+O(x^(1-delta)log(2|D|)), positive once x^delta exceeds a suitable absolute multiple of log(2|D|). Contradiction. This argument uses a character annihilating a proper subgroup, not a class-density 1/h estimate.

For a promised distinct odd semiprime N, take D=-N if N=3 mod4 and D=-4N if N=1 mod4; these are fundamental negative discriminants. Enumerate rational primes p<=B, computing gcd(p,N) first. A proper gcd factors immediately. For split p, find a square root of D modulo p deterministically by exhaustive search (cost at most p arithmetic steps, requiring no nonresidue oracle), construct its prime-ideal binary quadratic form, and reduce it. Inert prime ideals are principal and can be omitted; handle ramified 2 directly, and any odd ramified prime already yields a factor of N. One conjugate split ideal suffices because the other is its inverse. This acquires a proven generating list in O(B^2 polylog N), hence polynomial in log N with an enormous fixed exponent. Standard form composition/reduction is polynomial in log N and needs no factorization of N.

Thus the new analytic scope funds deterministic generation of a varying-discriminant class group by an actually acquired polylogarithmic-norm prime-ideal list. This is a concrete factoring-relevant input subroutine, not a presentation, class-number computation, relation search or factorization algorithm.

## What it does not fund by the same conversion

For ONE prescribed ideal class A, character orthogonality gives smoothed prime-ideal count

    x*Phi(1)/h_K+O(x^(1-delta)log(2|D|)).

The direct positivity threshold is x^delta much larger than h_K log|D|. It is not polylogarithmic uniformly in D: h_K can be of order sqrt|D| up to logarithmic factors. Thus a polylogarithmic least prime in every class does not follow from this estimate.

Likewise the small generating list does not contain relations or annihilators for free. A concrete deterministic fallback can find a nonzero annihilating exponent for each generator by baby-step/giant-step using an elementary class-number upper bound H0=O(sqrt|D|log|D|). Each search costs soft sqrt(H0)=soft |D|^(1/4); multiply the obtained positive exponents to get a global class-group annihilator. The number and bit lengths of generators/exponents are polylogarithmic, so this fallback remains soft |D|^(1/4), not soft N^(1/6). No factorization of these exponents is needed to assert annihilation, but no faster relation acquisition has been provided here.

Lenstra-Pomerance1992 explicitly proves an EXPECTED-time probabilistic factoring theorem, using random forms and class-group relations. Its primary paper is https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/TOPICS/factoring/rigorous.pdf . The newly available zero-free strip cannot be substituted for the random relation search to obtain a deterministic algorithm without an additional construction.
