## 1. Actual cheap nodal embedding, and what its division polynomial computes

First acquire h_j(i) for 0<=i<=13 and every row (14 values). This is soft J=soft k: fixed-many rank-two powers/norms per row. Any nonunit is handled as an ORIGINAL leaf immediately. On the continuing hard branch these indices are inside L (indeed N>256k^4 and N<=k^6 force k>16). Hence on the continuing branch V_j-1 and all these norm values are units. The seed screen already handles z^i-1 for 1<=i<L.

On the nodal cubic

    E0: y²=x³+x²,
    phi(v)=(4v/(v-1)², 4v(v+1)/(v-1)³),

multiplication of the unit parameter v is the group law on its smooth locus; v=1 gives the identity. Every required division in phi(V_j) and phi(z) is now certified. ALL these point coordinates cost soft k. This is a deliberately singular curve, not an unacquired ordinary elliptic curve or a CM-order promise.

The actual division polynomial has the explicit specialization

    psi_n(phi(v))
      =2^(n²-1)*v^[n(n-1)/2]*(v^n-1)/(v-1)^(n²),    n>=1.       (1)

For example the initial identities are psi_1=1 and psi_2=2y; substitution in the standard doubling/addition identities proves (1), or it follows directly from the multiplicative parameter and its normalized divisor. It is a rational identity with an explicitly unit denominator at our supplied v. Thus this is a genuine initialized O(log n)-operation evaluator even at a zero of v^n-1. Its support is v^n=1, NOT the finite set v=z^i for 0<=i<L.

Taking n=L cannot repair this: the actual guard says z^L-1 is a unit. The orbit generated by phi(z) is not an L-torsion subgroup. At an own hit V_j=z^I, psi_L(phi(V_j)) vanishes only if z^(LI)=1, an extra relation not implied by the native cover. Full E[L] or a cyclic torsion kernel cannot be substituted for the finite geometric segment without a separate acquisition and coverage theorem.

An x-only comparison has an additional explicit companion:

    x(v)-x(r)=4(v-r)(1-v*r)/[(v-1)²(r-1)²].        (2)

It tests v=r OR v=r^(-1). It is not exactly the oriented leaf V_j=z^i. This attempt therefore does not remove the companion by cancellation or silently enlarge the original bounded index set. The norm-leaf consumer applies to the original oriented differences, not to an arbitrary division-polynomial zero.

The actual translated group-parameter endpoint is z^n*(v*z^(-n)-1)=v-z^n, acquired by binary powering in O(log n) operations with only unit inverses. A translated nodal-net description of that endpoint therefore supplies ONE original difference. The actual product requires all those endpoints. This distinction is the first checked mapping boundary, not merely a comparison of polynomial degrees.

## 2. Execute the cumulative-product Somos-4/QRT mapping

The natural proposed fast object is the ACTUAL cumulative product P(n), not h(n). Suppose it satisfied a constant Somos-4 relation

    P(n+2)P(n-2)=a*P(n+1)P(n-1)+b*P(n)².         (3)

On an interval where all h are units, substitution of the product definition and exact cancellation of those units gives the necessary residual

    R(n)=h(n)h(n+1)-a*h(n)h(n-2)
                         -b*h(n-2)h(n-1)=0.      (4)

This is also the usual QRT condition for u(n)=P(n+1)P(n-1)/P(n)²=h(n)/h(n-1). Nothing about a common fourth-order LINEAR recurrence alone proves (4).

There is a fully paid constant-size test for (4). Every product of two shifted h sequences has its spectral bases among the pairwise products of 1,t,z,w. A monic scalar annihilator of order 10 is acquired immediately:

    K(T)=(T-1)(T-t)²(T-t²)
       *(T²-sT+t)*(T²-t*sT+t³)
       *(T²-(s²-2t)T+t²).                        (5)

Repeated factors are harmless. Equation (5) follows by listing unordered pairs, with t occurring both as 1*t and z*w; no spectral inverse is used by the algorithm. Thus R(2),...,R(11)=0 certifies (4) for EVERY later n by a monic recurrence. All those residuals use only h(0),...,h(12), already acquired.

The ten constraints are linear in the two unknown scalars a,b. Solve them by constant-size guarded elimination modulo N. A proper pivot gcd factors N. Globally zero coefficients are dropped; a unit pivot is inverted; an inconsistent row rejects the fit; free variables may be set to zero. The first coefficient h(2)h(0) is already a unit. All remaining gcds and operations are constant in number per row. No original decoder is invoked on a saturated auxiliary determinant or residual.

Initially this suggested a paid no-hit selector: if (4) is globally certified and a first zero h(r) occurred after the initial unit segment in either local field, then R(r) forces b=0, R(r-1) forces a=0, and R(r-2) contradicts the preceding nonzero h values. Equivalently, absorbing zeros in P make (3) impossible once a first hit occurs at r>=4. So a certified fit could safely discard that row without acquiring its unit product. However the actual constructor permits a stronger check: NO continuing row can pass this constant-coefficient fit. The proposed selector is therefore vacuous here, not credited as pruning.

## 3. Exact rejection on the actual nonsplit side, including spectral collisions

Work for proof only in the unknown field B_p*. Set v=V_j and c=v*bar(v), with c nonzero. If (3) held and some future h had a first zero, the absorbing-zero argument above would already contradict it, since all 14 acquired starts h(0),...,h(13) are nonzero. Otherwise all h values are nonzero and (4) follows for all n. It suffices to rule out (4).

First suppose z!=w. A spectral monomial z^a*w^b has field norm t^(a+b). Since ord(t)>C>4, spectral bases from different total degrees 0,...,4 cannot coincide. Within degree 1 the two bases z,w are distinct. Standard linear independence of distinct exponential sequences over a field therefore isolates the degree-0 and degree-1 coefficients of R. This is a proof-side Vandermonde argument, not a computed hidden-field inverse.

The degree-0 channel has unit coefficient c² and gives b=1-a. For a cross channel (1,r), r=z or w, multiply its coefficient by the unit r². It gives

    r²(r+1)-a(r²+1)-b(r+1)
      =(r-1)[r²+(2-a)r+1]=0.                    (6)

The coefficients c*bar(v) and c*v of these two channels are nonzero. The seed screen makes r-1 nonzero, so (6) yields

    a=2+z+z^(-1)=2+w+w^(-1).

Subtracting gives (z-w)(t-1)=0 after multiplication by a unit, impossible. Possible coincidences among degree-2 bases, including z²=w², do not affect the isolated degree-0/1 channels.

It remains to treat z=w=B0. Then B0 is fixed by bar, t=B0², and

    h(n)=c-d*B0^n+B0^(2n), d=v+bar(v).

The short seed guard makes B0^r-1 nonzero for r<=C. If d!=0, the degree-1 cross channel gives

    a=2+B0+B0^(-1).

The degree-3 cross channel (B0,B0²), after b=1-a, gives

    (B0³-1)*[(B0+1)(B0³+1)-a]=0.

These two values differ by

    (B0+1)(B0^4-1)/B0,

which is nonzero by the guard. If d=0, use the two-base sequence h(n)=c+t^n instead. Its constant, cross and highest-square channels require

    b=1-a, a=2+t+t^(-1), t^4=a*t+b.

The last condition then reduces to

    t^(-1)*(t²-1)*(t³-1)=0,

again impossible. No odd-order projection or survival assumption for W was needed; the zero-trace case was handled rather than excluded.

This rejects constant Somos-4 for every actual row after the initial original-leaf unit screen, including the case of a possible later collision. It also rejects any fixed UNIT quadratic gauge A0*B1^n*C1^(n²)*P(n): such a gauge merely rescales the two constant coefficients in (3). It does not reject arbitrary n-dependent coefficients or another nonlinear observable.

## 4. The constant Somos-5 variant was also executed

For

    P(n+3)P(n-2)=a*P(n+2)P(n-1)+b*P(n+1)P(n),

the no-hit unit cancellation gives, after shifting the index to m,

    h(m)h(m+1)-a*h(m-3)h(m)
                         -b*h(m-3)h(m-2)=0.      (7)

It has the same order-10 annihilator (5) and constant-cost fitting procedure: the ten tests m=3,...,12 use the already acquired h(0),...,h(13). Somos-4 uses only the first 13 of these 14 starts. A first absorbing zero after the initial unit segment forces b=0, then a=0, then a nonzero left side (r>=5), so future hits cannot evade this test.

In the z!=w case, the isolated degree-0 channel again gives b=1-a. A degree-1 channel gives

    (r²-1)*[r²+(1-a)r+1]=0,

hence a=1+z+z^(-1)=1+w+w^(-1), again impossible unless z=w. In that remaining case with d!=0, substituting a=1+B0+B0^(-1) in the degree-3 channel leaves the nonzero unit product

    (B0²-1)*(B0^6-1)*(B0+1)*(B0²+1)/B0.

Indeed before substitution the degree-3 equation is

    (B0+1)(B0^9-1)-a*(B0²-1)(B0³+B0+1)=0;

the displayed factorization follows directly. If d=0, the two-base channels instead require t^6=a*t²+b, whose residual is

    t^(-1)*(t³-1)*(t^4-1),

also nonzero. This rejects this second concrete constant-Somos mapping and its fixed unit quadratic gauges. It is not a classification of all higher-width or nonautonomous Somos systems.

## 5. Alternate attempt: turn the segment into Tate torsion

A more ambitious completion avoids the previous mismatch formally. Replace the seed temporarily by an indeterminate Z and take a Tate period Q=Z^L. The class of Z in a hypothetical uniformization G_m/Q^Z then has order L, and its powers form exactly the desired residue classes of exponents modulo L. This is a real candidate for a torsion norm, not just relabeling the given finite-field orbit.

Its exact theta identity exposes the input. Define formally

    theta(x;Q)=product_(n>=0)(1-xQ^n)
                         *product_(n>=1)(1-Q^n/x).

Partitioning exponents by their residue modulo L proves

    product_(r=0)^(L-1) theta(x*Z^r;Z^L)=theta(x;Z).       (8)

The finite desired product corresponds only to product_(r<L)(1-xZ^r). The remaining theta factors are actual infinite tails. They cannot be dropped as units at a native hit, or used to infer a bounded original exponent from a class modulo the new Tate period. Formula (8) is exact, but it has changed a finite segment into an entire integer-orbit identification.

More fundamentally the required Tate curve/point values have NOT been acquired over B. Their coefficients and coordinates are formal power series in Q (or convergent analytic series in a separate topology). The actual Q=z^L is a UNIT in the finite ring B. There is no compatible specialization of the full formal power-series algebra sending Q to that unit: if q0 is a unit of a finite ring, some d has q0^d=1, while 1-Q^d is a unit in B[[Q]] and would map to zero. Truncation does not fix this: Q^M maps to the unit z^(LM), so a discarded formal tail is not zero modulo N.

Consequently the hoped-for O(log L) division-polynomial ladder lacks its ACTUAL curve coefficients and marked points, as well as a finite-index support identity. Supplying an abstract theta/Tate circuit or a short formal prefix does not initialize them. This is the exact failure of this proposed completion; it is not a prohibition on other algebraic curve constructors. In particular the previous supplied anomalous-CM division-polynomial test remains a different conditional route with its own curve-acquisition premise.

A divisor/Miller formulation reaches the same finite-segment boundary without infinite series. If Q_i=Q+iP on a supplied curve, the desired orbit divisor has zeros Q_0,...,Q_(n-1), not n copies of one point. Already a two-point block uses the actual line quotient g_(Q,Q+P), and the next block needs g_(Q+2P,Q+3P). A merge has to acquire the shifted right orbit divisor as well as the line joining the two divisor sums. The ordinary Miller doubling of n[P]-[nP]-(n-1)[O] instead repeats one point; it is not that orbit divisor. On the acquired nodal curve this orbit construction returns the actual degree-n polynomial product_(i<n)(V-z^i). The complete explicit construction costs soft(L+J); retaining the old baby/giant cut costs soft(J+sqrt(JL)). No right-hand divisor was initialized by a single left-hand evaluation.
