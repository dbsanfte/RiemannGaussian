# Rational-Newton input actions plus relation-matrix composition

26 October 2026. Paper-only hybrid test; no repository edits, tests, numerical campaign or implementation. This uses [qbinomial-joint.md](qbinomial-joint.md) and the newly paid native row separation, not the earlier “outer polynomial unavailable” dismissal.

**Result.** The coefficient matrix now has a genuine acquired rank-one invertible Sylvester description, fast actions and all boundary generators. Powered roots and the inverse graph are also acquired in soft J. However the 2026 composition algorithm does not itself turn these scalar-vector actions into the polynomial-coefficient contraction required by its first partial-sum stage. The direct source bound on the actual large bidegree is worse than the old engine. A quotient correction arising on graph compression is explicit and its **operator** has rank J; this does not claim the actual special coefficient correction has rank J or rule out a new fast fused contraction. No K^1.343 or whole-query improvement is established.

## 1. Input, exact two-tail graph evaluation and paid inverse graph

Use pure K=2·3^e, J=phi(K)=K/3, h=2K²+1, B=K, M=2K and rho=q^-1. Let the actual coefficient matrix be

    C_(s,e)=a_(Bs+e),  0<=s<M, 0<=e<B,
    A=rho^h,
    Gdigit(U,V)=sum_(s,e) C_(s,e) U^s V^e.

The full polynomial is EXACTLY

    G_h(X)=Gdigit(X^B,X)+a_(h-1)X^(h-1)+a_h X^h.       (1)

The kernel report acquires C and C^t actions, all factorial starts, and these tails in soft K, without its MB coefficient array.

On the native good-family attempt the derivative/discriminant screen returns a proper factor or all original row differences units in soft J. Every local y_j and its ratios lie in the group generated by the supplied K-rough beta and W. Since B=K, raising to B is injective in that group at EVERY local component. Thus

    U_j=y_j^B

also have all pair differences units globally on that continuation. This does not rely on an inverse exponent being supplied. Acquire

    f_B(U)=product_j(U-U_j),
    H(U_j)=y_j, deg H<J

by product trees, derivative MPE and actual unit-certified interpolation, soft J. H is a unit modulo f_B; its inverse can likewise be interpolated from the supplied y_j^-1. f_B(0) is a unit. Wrong-family failures are bounded/declined; one good family passes or factors.

Consequently the exact desired short polynomial, if composition could be done cheaply, is

    Gdigit(U,H(U))+a_(h-1)U^M+a_h U^M H(U) mod f_B.  (2)

Both tail terms cost soft J by modular powering. From (2), MPE at U_j would give all G_h(y_j) in soft J and then either projective norm pair. This is a valid initialized graph handoff.

## 2. The coefficient matrix really has acquired rank-one displacement

Let S be the lower shift with (CS)_(s,e)=C_(s,e+1), zero in its last column; put Q=diag(rho^e) and Z=diag(rho^(Bs)). Directly applying the coefficient ratio yields

    C*(S-A I)-Z*C*((rho S-I)Q)=d*e_last^t,          (3)
    d_s=-(1-rho^(B(s+1)))*a_(B(s+1)).

The last coefficient a_(BM)=a_(h-1) is one of the paid tails. Thus d is acquired, including the last row. The sign and final-column term are essential; dropping that column would change the finite polynomial.

Both L=S-AI and R=(rho S-I)Q are triangular with unit diagonals. Equivalently

    C*T-Z*C=d*(e_last^t R^-1), T=L R^-1.

The diagonal entries of T are A*rho^-e. Their differences from row nodes Z_s are known units: the exponent h-e-Bs ranges from 2 to h. Thus this is an invertible Sylvester operator, not the earlier diagonal-kernel displacement failure. The bidiagonal solves and generators are paid in soft K. This is stronger input than a generic black-box action or the old unacquired long-remainder generator.

### Its exact generating-function boundary

Write alpha(U)=sum_(s<M)a_(Bs)U^s and d(U)=sum_(s<M)d_s U^s. Equation (3) is exactly

    (1-A V)Gdigit(U,V)-(1-V)Gdigit(rho^B U,rho V)
       =alpha(U)-alpha(rho^B U)+V^B d(U).           (4)

Restrict U=V^B. The right side telescopes over s to

    -(1-rho^(BM))*a_(BM)*V^(BM).

Adding the two known tails from (1) restores the full homogeneous q-difference relation for G_h. Thus the rank-one input structure and its finite boundary are real, and the polynomial curve U=V^B itself is compatible with the joint scaling. The remaining problem is finite **modulus** reduction, not a missing tail in (4).

## 3. Execute the 2026 source stages on this input

Primary source reread: Neiger–Salvy–Schost–Villard, [arXiv:2601.17422v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17422v1), Lemma 2.7, Proposition 3.1, §3.1 Steps 1–3 and §5 Steps 7–8. Its short-input generic bound is soft J^((omega+3)/4). The source works over fields and tests its genericity conditions; it does not prove the native graph H satisfies them or provide a deterministic composite-ring fallback at that bound.

In §3.1 the y-degree is written in three base-mu digits. With V-degree d, mu>=d^(1/3), its first stage forms partial sums

    s_i2(U,V0)=sum_i1 s_i1,i2(U,V0)*A_i1(U,V0),

then combines these with B_i2(U,V0). Here the original U-width is m and the relation degree is delta. The explicit bound is soft mu^omega*(delta+m*mu). For our actual m=M=Theta(J), d=B=Theta(J), even the favourable generic delta=Theta(J/mu) and mu=Theta(J^(1/3)) give the **direct source bound**

    soft J^((omega+4)/3),

not J^((omega+3)/4). Reducing U modulo f_B first changes only constants and leaves a J-by-Theta(J) coefficient matrix.

To reach its smaller exponent, one must FIRST supply a representative of bidegree (mu,delta) with mu about J^(1/4), delta about J^(3/4), total size O(J). Lemma 2.7 reduces an input whose first-variable width is already <mu. Gdigit has width Theta(J), so that hypothesis is not currently supplied by its fast matrix action.

### The precise structured-input contraction missing from Step 1

If e=e0+mu*e1+mu²*e2, fusing source Steps 1–2 asks for

    sum_e g_e(U)*V0^e0*A_e1(U,V0)*B_e2(U,V0),
    g_e(U)=sum_s C_(s,e)U^s.                       (5)

C times a **constant** coefficient vector is paid. In (5) the coefficient vector depends on the SAME U variable as the output polynomials, and on V0. Thus it requires polynomial-coefficient actions of C with matched coefficient convolutions, not one invocation of its known scalar-vector action. Expanding those coefficient vectors and applying C coefficientwise is a lawful completion, but it retains the large contraction (about J polynomial-coordinate applications in the favourable relation-size regime), rather than proving a sub-K^1.5 bound. Applying the kernel circuit over a rank-J truncated coefficient algebra similarly pays its precision rank.

This is the new genuine handoff: implement the fused structured reduction (5), or an equivalent smaller representative, from the rank-one description (3). The source's dense polynomial-matrix products and generic short-input theorem do not already implement it. No assertion that such a structure-aware reduction is impossible is made.

## 4. Why graph compression does not automatically retain rank one

Here is a concrete check of the proposed displacement-preserving compression. Work at the good field, or on a further paid global unit branch. Let E evaluate degree-<M polynomials at U_j, and I interpolate their values to degree <J. For any input p write exactly

    p=r+f_B*t, deg r<J.

Then

    p(rho^B U_j)=r(rho^B U_j)
                    +f_B(rho^B U_j)*t(rho^B U_j).  (6)

The quotient term is required even though the algebraic curve in (4) is scaling-compatible. It carries precisely the information lost by reducing the first variable before shifting it.

All f_B(rho^B U_j) are nonzero at the good field. Indeed equality rho^B U_j=U_l implies (rho*y_j)^B=y_l^B; B=K power-injectivity gives rho*y_j=y_l. The actual one-step no-overlap theorem (6P>2H, automatic from P>16H) excludes this between ANY rows; the q-order bound excludes the same-row case. MPE of f_B at the shifted nodes costs soft J and gives a proper factor or certifies all these values globally units on the good-family attempt.

The correction OPERATOR in (6) has full row rank J. Restrict to inputs p=f_B*t with deg t<J, allowed since M>=2J. Their unshifted evaluations are zero; their shifted values are

    diag(f_B(rho^B U_j))*Vandermonde(rho^B U_j)*t_coeff.

Both factors are invertible at the good field, and after the optional unit screen globally as well. Thus the lost quotient space cannot be represented as a rank-one correction operator for arbitrary inputs. This is stronger than simply saying the root set is not shift-closed.

Crucial scope: it does NOT prove that applying this correction to the special coefficient columns C has rank J, that its scalar contribution is expensive, or that NSSV's relation bases cannot compute it implicitly. A new algorithm could exploit cancellations peculiar to C. It shows why one may not inherit constant displacement rank through this particular finite compression without acquiring/proving those cancellations. The missing correlated quotient contraction is another explicit form of (5).

The ACTUAL boundary matrix still has fast actions. For a supplied column vector c, form p(U)=sum_e c_e g_e(U) using the acquired C*c circuit, divide p by the monic f_B, evaluate its quotient t at all shifted nodes rho^B U_j, and multiply by the acquired f_B values. Each step costs soft K. Reversing this linear circuit gives its transpose at the same cost. Hence full row rank does not mean that useful structure or cheap actions were lost. It is specifically the **correlated paired/diagonal contraction** of these special columns, not ordinary boundary matrix-vector multiplication, that remains unpaid. The actual columns may have additional cancellations; the rank proof asserts none about them.

## 5. Genericity, ring guards and fully charged fallback

H, f_B and their unit inverses are paid, but the small generic relation-degree pattern needed for the source exponent is not. The source's NoCert check is an algorithmic detection/fallback mechanism, not proof of genericity for these finite native roots. Randomization or a change of graph coordinate would need its own deterministic guarantee and cost before affecting the all-semiprime target.

The source also works over fields. A valid adaptation must certify every divisor in the actual quadratic composite algebra, return proper gcd factors when supported on one prime, and distinguish genuine global zero from singular/non-generic pivots. A globally nonzero final determinant does not certify every intermediate pivot. None of these safeguards proves the required short-degree genericity on the surviving good component. Declining a failed attempt and retaining the old complete query is safe but does not improve its worst-case bound.

A fully initialized direct completion is still available: acquire the row G_h values with the old monic block engine in soft K^1.5, interpolate (2), and postprocess the weak pair in soft J. The kernel's literal per-root contraction costs K². The direct large-bidegree source estimate is worse. No presently proved composition hybrid beats these bounds, and the best shorter weighted route remains K^1.25.

## Exact next operation

The outer coefficient matrix is no longer unavailable: its generators, invertible displacement, scalar actions and finite boundary are paid. What remains is **structure-aware relation reduction with polynomial-coefficient matched actions**, such as (5), or a direct selected diagonal/norm contraction that avoids it. Providing that operation together with native genericity/ring-safe initialization could produce a real new bound; ordinary scalar C-actions plus the existing generic dense-input theorem do not yet do so.
