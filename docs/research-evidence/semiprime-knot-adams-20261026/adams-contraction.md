## 1. Actual continuing contract

Write N=P Q, P<Q, and G_P,G_Q for the local subgroups generated by beta. The failed screen certifies gcd(N,|G_P|*|G_Q|)=1, in addition to the earlier odd local orders and q=eta² orders >16k². Thus x↦x^N is bijective on each subgroup containing every actual root and clock. No inverse exponent, local order, factor, or additive field automorphism is supplied.

The original acquired polynomial is

    K(T)=∏_(j=1)^L (v_j−T)(v_j−T^(-1))/(v_j−1)²,
    P_m=∏_(i=1)^m K(eta^(2i+1)).

Every v_j,v_j−1 is a unit. Powering all v_j and the clock by N preserves each local comparison, including its zero/nonunit status. The resulting original-length product remains initialized at cost softly O(L), but this alone is not shorter.

## 2. A new paid finite-alphabet inverse (not an inverse group exponent)

For this section use the complete polynomial root alphabet x_1,...,x_J, with J=2L, including the recentered inverses. First deduplicate literal equal integer exponent labels for a zero-only query, retaining multiplicities/metadata if a numerical norm is needed. A global equality of distinct exponent labels yields the already licensed nonzero polynomial-bit beta-annihilator; a proper gcd yields a factor.

Pairwise **unit** separation can be paid without a quadratic scan. Acquire F=∏(X−x_j), its derivative, and all F'(x_j) by monic multipoint evaluation. If their product is a unit, all pair differences are units. Otherwise product-tree localization finds a nonunit derivative; scanning its J linear difference factors either factors N or produces the preceding nonzero global-annihilator branch. The polynomial work and one complete scan cost softly O(J); including a possible licensed extractor the factor-or-separation bound is softly O(J+k), hence softly O(k) on the native family. No field Euclidean pivot or inverse of a nonunit difference is assumed.

On the continuing branch y_j=x_j^N also have pairwise unit differences: powering is injective in each hidden subgroup. Acquire them and F_N(Y)=∏(Y−y_j) in softly O(J). Fast interpolation then acquires a **coefficient list** B(Y), degree <J, with

    B(y_j)=x_j.

Every interpolation derivative denominator F_N'(y_j) is a certified unit. Monic product trees, multipoint evaluation and batched inversion therefore give softly O(J) ring operations, or softly O(J log N) bits including root powers. These claims concern standard fast-polynomial arithmetic, not an unexpanded symbolic inverse.

Consequently there are explicit inverse R-algebra isomorphisms

    R[Y]/F_N → R[X]/F,       Y↦X^N,
    R[X]/F   → R[Y]/F_N,     X↦B(Y).                  (1)

The identities B(X^N)=X mod F and B(Y)^N=Y mod F_N follow from unit-separated evaluation. This is useful acquired label transport after the new screen. It does not acquire an inverse to N on the whole subgroup, and B(q^(Ni)) need not equal q^i at off-alphabet clock points.

## 3. Execute transfer transport: the correction is singular before deflation

Pulling a powered-clock factor through (1) gives

    q^(Ni)−X^N=(q^i−X) D_N(q^i,X),
    D_N(U,V)=Σ_(r=0)^(N−1) U^(N−1−r)V^r.             (2)

This is an exact finite identity. At a collision U=V in R,

    D_N(U,U)=N U^(N−1)=0.

Thus the formal divided-difference correction is **not** a unit at precisely the relevant collision, despite injectivity of N-powering on the native subgroup. A norm transitivity argument cannot drop it as an invertible boundary. Constructing its degree-N expansion is neither necessary nor cheap; its binary geometric-sum descriptor does not change this zero.

There is a more informative local identity supplied by the semiprime structure. Modulo P, Fermat gives U^N=U^Q for actual subgroup elements, so

    U^N−V^N=(U−V) D_Q(U,V)  mod P.                  (3)

Now D_Q is a unit **even at a collision**: there its value is Q U^(Q−1), nonzero modulo P. Away from collisions, D_Q=(U^Q−V^Q)/(U−V) is nonzero because gcd(Q,|G_P|)=1. Modulo Q the corresponding formula uses D_P. This proves a CRT unit correction exists for every actual comparison. It is the locally Frobenius-deflated correction, not the universal polynomial D_N of (2).

For clarity, this yields an exact zero-preserving normalized knot identity. Define K_N by replacing every v_j by v_j^N in the displayed factorization. Its normalizers remain units by the screen. For any native T, modulo P one has

    K_N(T^N)=K(T)*U_P(T),
    U_P(T)=∏_j D_Q(v_j,T) D_Q(v_j,T^(-1))
                    /D_Q(v_j,1)²,                   (4)

and U_P(T) is a unit. The Q-component has the exchanged formula. Thus an actual U(T) in R^× exists with K_N(T^N)=U(T)K(T), and products retain zeros/nonunits. This holds at collisions without canceling K(T). It is a stronger finite statement than just equality of zero sets.

**Zero-query scope:** the public value of the unit in (4) is NOT required for the original zero/nonunit-only task. Local injectivity already proves that K_N(T^N) and K(T) have exactly the same local zero pattern, and therefore their complete products have the same zero/nonunit status. The K_N, eta^N query is lawful without computing a numerical correction. Formula (4) supplies an optional stronger numerical identity: its unit depends on unknown prime cofactors and is not acquired by (1), while substituting the formal D_N correction would be wrong at collisions. Acquiring this optional normalization is not a necessary backend task or the missing prefix primitive. The actual unresolved step is a shorter contraction of the entire m-position clock. At a localized powered comparison, subgroup injectivity implies the corresponding original comparison; its retained root/index labels and the computed original difference recover the existing signed-gap decoder, without an inverse exponent or a public correction value.

The finite-alphabet inverse B also cannot repair this by being applied to every scalar: (1) fixes all coefficients in R. It sends the coordinate Y to X^N; it does not send the scalar q to q^N. Applying a polynomial B to powered scalar clock values is not an R-algebra automorphism of R. Interpolating the inverse on both roots and an entire clock block would require unit-separated cross differences, which are the unknown collision factors themselves, or a new collision-safe joint interpolation method.

## 4. What the actual skein Adams operation says

Primary: Morton–Manchon, *Geometrical relations and plethysms in the Homfly skein of the annulus*, JLMS78 (2008),305–328, https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.2851 , §7/Theorem 13 and Corollary 14 (numbering in the inspected arXiv version). Power-sum plethysm sends an alphabet to its m-th powers; their torus-cable operator is a plethysm followed by its framing/twist operator. These are annular skein identities, with satellite insertion a linear evaluation, not a multiplicative scalar norm theorem.

In particular three operations must not be conflated:

* the acquired root-alphabet Adams transform F_N(Y)=∏(Y−x_j^N), still degree J;
* substituting T↦T^N in K(T), which changes the clock argument and has expanded breadth 2LN;
* taking a full cyclic character norm, which adjoins a complete root-of-unity orbit and its normalization.

Ordinary Alexander cabling has the second behavior: Delta_(C_(n,r)(K))(T)=Delta_(T_(n,r))(T)*Delta_K(T^n). For an (n,1) cable its torus factor is 1, but it still produces one powered argument, not F_N or a partial-clock product. A primary statement of this classical formula is Ohtsuki, *A cabling formula for the 2-loop polynomial of knots*, §4, https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/40872 . The cable and its framing data must also be acquired if its explicit diagram or larger color is used.

A complete cyclic norm has the old full-cycle character hypothesis; injectivity of N on the native group does not supply a known cycle length or convert the interval into a full cycle. The prior cyclic-resultant and periodicity exclusions are not repeated as new conclusions here.

## 5. Test the skew-product algorithm hypothesis precisely

Primary: Caruso–Le Borgne, *Fast multiplication for skew polynomials*, arXiv:1702.01665, §1.1 and Proposition 1.6, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.01665 . Its algorithms accept an étale algebra with an actual finite-order **algebra automorphism**, its normal/working bases and the associated conversion data. They charge the algebra rank and skew degree. The setting includes split algebras, not just fields, so the issue is not merely that R is composite.

The map x↦x^N on the native multiplicative subgroup is not automatically additive. Modulo P it is x↦x^Q; there is no theorem making it a field automorphism on the full residue field unless it happens to be identity there. Likewise (1) is an isomorphism between two differently presented root algebras, not the required endomorphism taking the entire original root-and-clock transfer to its next index. Identifying the two split algebras by their known labels gives an identity on coordinates, not an additive N-power operation on their scalar values. Thus the supplied finite-alphabet inverse is not the normal-basis/automorphism input required by that source.

A genuine next interface would supply a small-rank algebra automorphism or a short skew cocycle representing the **whole** geometric product, together with its norm and zero-safe readout. Public exponent N gives neither its order nor its basis data. A known small cycle or a proved native orbit cover could make norm transitivity useful, but those are extra arithmetic hypotheses, not consequences of the failed screen. No smooth-order average-case substitution is made.

## 6. Cost and outcome

The continuing finite-alphabet transport (1), including all powered root coefficients, costs softly O(J); including factor-returning separation branches, the uniform bound is softly O(J+k)=softly O(k) on the native family. It preserves labels and local unit differences, but it does not shorten the initialized long query. Continuing with K_N, eta^N and q^N therefore retains the already paid softly O(L+sqrt(Lm)) scalar bound; it is explicitly not counted as advancement.

The unresolved acquisition target is a composable small algebra/skew transfer or another shorter contraction for the WHOLE clock orbit, rather than transport of the root alphabet alone. The Frobenius-deflated correction (3)–(4) is optional numerical information and is not required for zero/nonunit equivalence or original-label recovery. Plethysm, cyclic norm transitivity and known skew-polynomial algorithms do not currently supply the shorter initialized full-clock scalar on every remaining native instance. The guaranteed softly O(L+sqrt m) whole-family oracle remains open.
