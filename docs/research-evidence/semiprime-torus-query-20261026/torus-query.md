## 3. Four exact cross-domain forms, with their operational scope

### Cyclic-group support and a safe parity operation

Every exponent E in (2) is EVEN. Indeed ell=-2aj+Kt is even, K is even, and a j²=bN modulo K implies a=b modulo 2, so a+bN is even. Put d=E/2 and sigma=W^(H/2). No root extraction is needed. The finite factorization

    1−W^E=(1−W^d)(1+W^d)                       (3)

has its second factor a certified unit: W^d lies in an odd-order local subgroup at every component and cannot equal −1. Thus the query can use exponents d and base sigma with exactly the same local zeros. This halves integer labels, not the number of clock positions. All integer exponents remain O(log N) bits.

### Quadratic norm and Chebyshev/Lucas dynamics

Set t=W+W^-1, a base scalar. Define C_0(t)=2,C_1(t)=t,C_(m+1)=t C_m−C_(m−1), extended by C_-m=C_m. Directly C_m(t)=W^m+W^-m and

    Norm(W^E−1)=2−C_E(t).                       (4)

Let Delta=t²−4=(W−W^-1)². It is a certified scalar unit: Norm(W²−1)=4−t² is nonzero at both primes by the original order bound. Put

    L_m=(W^m−W^-m)/(W−W^-1), L_0=0,L_1=1.

The denominator is an actual unit in B; the quotient is fixed by conjugation, hence scalar. It satisfies L_(m+1)=t L_m−L_(m−1), L_-m=−L_m, and

    2−C_(2m)(t)=−Delta*L_m².                    (5)

Therefore the whole native norm query has exactly the same nonunit support as the product of the scalar Lucas values L_(E/2). This is numerical norm equality with the displayed fixed unit and square, not equality of the unsquared scalar products.

### SL2 character and skein trace form

For M=[[t,-1],[1,0]], det(M)=1 and Tr(M^m)=C_m(t). Thus det(M^E−I)=2−C_E(t). Its commuting-character product-to-sum rule is

    C_a(t)C_b(t)=C_(a+b)(t)+C_(a−b)(t).          (6)

This is the elementary one-generator trace/skein identity, verified by W powers. It is related to the classical trace specialization of [Frohman–Gelca's torus skein machinery](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9806107); no quantum knot theorem or specialization is needed for its proof here. Every matrix has size two and is acquired. A product of Jh such factors is still a long product; calling it one 2-by-2 determinant does not acquire it.

### Norm/resultant form

The quadratic polynomial P(T)=T²−tT+1 has roots W,W^-1 and unit discriminant. For an explicit integer E,

    Res_T(P(T),T^E−1)=2−C_E(t)                  (7)

for E>=0; negative E differs by the known norm-one unit and has the same expression. Also the row/clock product is the usual resultant of acquired row-root polynomial F with the finite geometric polynomial G_h. These are the same norm operation in two bases. The constant-degree resultant (7) computes one binary-labelled leaf in polylog N time, not all implicit exponents at once.

These forms are not four independent fast algorithms. Equations (3)–(7) share one two-dimensional torus representation. Their newly useful feature is that arbitrary actual zeros now have the paid factor decoder of §2.

### Inversion-folded literal support

The scalar norm obeys Norm(W^(-E)−1)=Norm(W^E−1), and W^(-E)−1=−W^(-E)(W^E−1). Thus exact integer support may also be quotiented by E~−E for the zero/nonunit query. No hit tag or scalar product equality is needed.

For E=s+Hu, 0<=s<H, negation gives (H−s)+H*(−u−1) when s!=0, and H*(−u) when s=0. After ordinary interval union, pair residues s,H−s and transport every partner interval [A,B] to [−B−1,−A−1] in the chosen representative phase 0<s<H/2. For the self-phase s=0 fold u to |u| (zero is excluded); for s=H/2 fold u to max(u,−u−1), splitting at u=0 if necessary. These interval operations and another endpoint sort cost soft K. All remaining labels are nonzero O(log N)-bit integers and still have the actual annihilator decoder.

Let S_inv be the resulting support size. Each inversion orbit has at most two elements, so

    S_union/2<=S_inv<=S_union.

The query cost becomes soft(K+sqrt(S_inv)), with the same all-bin cheap-first scheduling using S_inv. Once ordinary exact duplicate union has been performed, inversion alone gives at most a factor-two support reduction (sqrt(2) in this square-root term). This is a counting statement for this particular support quotient, not a lower bound on another method.

The original ±j partners do not generally furnish inverses. They use the SAME (a,b); their actual centered phases satisfy ell_j+ell_(-j)=H*tau, |tau|<=1. Opposite leaf exponents would require

    H*(i+i'−tau)=−2*(a+bN).

For nonaxis b!=0, the right side has absolute value at least 2(N−K), while |i+i'−tau|<=h<16H and N>256H²; this is impossible. For horizontal axes a=±K,b=0, H does not divide 2a; for vertical axes a=0,b=±K, H does not divide 2bN since gcd(N,K)=1 and K>2. Thus this native same-coefficient colour pairing does not create extra literal inversion matches in the supplied short windows.

More generally, inverse support from different rows requires b'=-b: otherwise the N term dominates the two centered phases, bounded a terms and clock terms. Explicitly adding the negated coefficient/phase rows would force inversion closure, but would only add inverse copies that this quotient immediately removes; it is not a reduction of the original support. Existing distinct signed rows may benefit from the quotient, always within the factor-two bound above.
