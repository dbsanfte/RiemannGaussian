## Weighted Bass matrix: signs, poles and tensor identity

With the stated head/tail conventions, KL weights the NEXT arc. Subtracting Jrev removes precisely backtracking. In a forward/reverse pair I+Jrev=[[1,u_i],[T_j,1]], determinant 1−T_j u_i. Sylvester reduction therefore gives the displayed det(I−B)=P det(H). Direct 2-by-2 inversion gives H_jj=1+sum_i w/(1−w), H_ii likewise, and off-diagonals −T_j/(1−w), −u_i/(1−w), with the report's signs.

The regulated reverse weight u_i/(1+delta) gives reversal determinant P(delta)/(1+delta)^(Jh). Its left diagonal is exactly

    1+sum_i w/(1+delta−w)
      =1−h+(1+delta)*sum_i 1/(1+delta−w).

The simple-pole residue is consequently the unweighted row hit count; adding the canonical row tag gives its tagged partner. Restricting to a proven singleton block supplies the intended noncancelling amplitude. No actual matrix entry has thereby been evaluated: it is the original finite singular Cauchy sum. The report correctly refuses to replace P by det(I−B), whose vertex correction can cancel all P zeros. In particular for J=1 the graph is a tree, the nonbacktracking determinant is 1, and these cancellations are essential.

Two allowed nonbacktracking steps from forward (j,i) to (k,l) have k!=j,l!=i and weight u_i*T_k. This is precisely A_J tensor A_h with the two stated rank-one-perturbed diagonal factors. Bipartite block determinant elimination gives det(I−B)=det(I−A_J tensor A_h), without an extraneous square. For F_+(z)=product(z+T_j), the characteristic polynomial is zF_+'−(J−1)F_+; the clock version is identical. These follow after clearing the rank-one determinant lemma, so no specialization at a zero denominator is used. Acquiring the row polynomial costs soft J; emitting the clock polynomial still costs soft h. The tensor identity does not acquire its norm or its singular correction.
