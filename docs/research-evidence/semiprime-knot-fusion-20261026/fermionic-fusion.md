## 1. A phase-repaired, root-free domain-wall encoding

Let d=2L, and write K(T)=c T^(-L)∏_(j=1)^d(T−x_j), where c is its known leading scalar unit. In the existing normalized trace construction c is a known inverse evaluation unit. Put A=R[iota]/(iota²+1). This is a free rank-two faithfully flat étale R-algebra, since 2 and iota are units. It need not be a field. Set n=m+d and concatenate

    alpha = (iota*t_1,...,iota*t_m, −iota/x_1,...,−iota/x_d).

The normalized homogeneous-rapidity, zero-vertical-field Felderhof DW partition function is exactly

    D_n(alpha) = ∏_(r<s)(1−alpha_r alpha_s).                 (1)

To obtain a literal polynomial vertex model, take all u=v=0, all beta_j=0 in the source weights, and use the horizontal-line diagonal gauge that replaces the two c-weights sqrt(1−alpha_r²),sqrt(1−alpha_r²) by 1,1−alpha_r². The other weights are a_1=a_2=1, b_1=alpha_r, b_2=−alpha_r. Choose the source c-boundary orientation so that its forced boundary c-weight is the one normalized to 1; reversing that gauge instead introduces ∏_r(1−alpha_r²), also a unit here. In either convention, the gauge cancels on internal edges and its boundary contribution removes exactly the source factor ∏_r sqrt(1−alpha_r²). Equation (33) therefore gives (1). One may first derive this over formal square roots and clear their invertible factors; the resulting polynomial identity has coefficients in Z[alpha], so specialization to R is valid. No square-root tower is acquired.

Separating the two blocks in (1) gives the finite identity

    D_n = U_clock U_root ∏_(i,j)(1−t_i/x_j),
    U_clock = ∏_(i<h)(1+t_i t_h),
    U_root  = ∏_(j<l)(1+(x_j x_l)^(-1)).                   (2)

Every factor of U_clock, U_root, and every 1−alpha_r² is a certified unit: an odd-order element cannot equal −1 in either residue field. This remains true for repeated or reciprocal native roots. Thus there is no accidental vanishing background.

More explicitly,

    ∏_(i,j)(1−t_i/x_j)
      = (∏_j x_j)^(-m) c^(-m) (∏_i t_i)^L P_m,          (3)
    ∏_i t_i = eta^[m(m+2)].

All factors explicitly shown in (3) are units acquired in softly O(L+log m) ring operations from the native root list and binary exponentiation. The two U products need not be acquired to prove the zero/nonunit equivalence. Equations (2)–(3), as polynomial identities, retain collision zeros and never divide by K(t_i). Since D_n is fixed by iota↦−iota it belongs to R; A can also be represented as pairs at constant arithmetic overhead. Therefore this gives a genuine native exact DW zero/nonunit encoding rather than merely a knot analogy.

**Cost.** The n line labels can be expanded in O(L+m) ring operations. A literal square lattice has n² vertices; its determinant solution has order n. The product formula (1) still contains the cross product (3), exactly the unpaid prefix. Unit certification is cheap reasoning but does not itself evaluate D_n. A descriptor for the clock block has size O(log m), yet none of the cited identities contracts that descriptor in softly O(L+sqrt m). The encoding therefore does not improve the existing softly O(L+sqrt(Lm)) bound. Bit costs multiply these ring bounds by softly O(log N); the quadratic étale algebra has constant overhead.

## 2. Explicit Yang–Baxter crossing and actual rank loss

Algebraize source 2's R-matrix using x=exp(2(u_1−u_2)), t=exp(2 gamma), h²=t. Its nontrivial entries are

    a=h(x−1)/(tx−1),  b_plus=x(t−1)/(tx−1),
    b_minus=(t−1)/(tx−1),  c=(x−t)/(tx−1),
    R=diag(c, [[a,b_plus],[b_minus,a]], 1).              (4)

Direct multiplication gives det[[a,b_plus],[b_minus,a]]=c and det R=c². This computes where actual spectral fusion can occur.

Taking the auxiliary t=−1 makes both tx−1 and x−t units whenever x is a native odd-order ratio: they are signed 1+x. Hence all different-clock crossings in this specialization are invertible and Yang–Baxter moves legitimately reorder them. But these moves do not reduce their number. The auxiliary square root h=iota is already available at constant rank. This is a safe specialization for crossing, not rank-reducing fusion.

To force fusion between adjacent native clocks, instead take t=q, x=q. The denominator q²−1 is a certified unit, h=eta is native, c=0, and the central block has rank one:

    [[a,b_plus],[b_minus,a]] = 1/(q+1) [[eta,q],[1,eta]].

Together with the final diagonal 1, R has rank two instead of four, over R as well as either residue field. Thus geometric parameters really do offer an acquired constant-cost rank-deficient map. It is not an invertible crossing: one must prove that the discarded channel contributes a certified unit, or retain it in a direct-sum/sum-of-channels identity. Neither the Yang–Baxter equation nor source 2's determinant formula proves this for the acquired knot K. Repeating the rank-two projection alone is not equality of the original partition function and may remove a collision channel. No cabling aggregate-color collapse is counted as progress here.

## 3. Test the smaller rectangular determinant: where its boundary goes

The trigonometric partial-DW identity in source 3, equation (47), has an n-by-n determinant with entries

    exp(2 z_i(j−1)) [1 − exp(M gamma)
      ∏_(k=1)^M sinh(z_i−y_k)/sinh(z_i−y_k+gamma)
      * exp(−2 gamma(n−j))],                           (5)

and prefactor exp(−(n−1)Σz_i)/Delta(exp(2z_i)). Here z_i are horizontal spectral parameters; they are not the native roots x_j above. Equation (5) genuinely reduces determinant dimension when n is small. However, its entries include every vertical parameter through the displayed ratio. Clearing those denominators is part of any finite-ring adaptation, not optional normalization.

For a geometric clock one can choose y_k in an additive gamma-string, so the ratio telescopes. In multiplicative variables the exact elementary mechanism is

    ∏_(i=0)^(m−1) (1−zq^i)/(1−zq^(i+1))
       = (1−z)/(1−zq^m).                              (6)

This is cheap as a rational expression, but at an interior collision 1−zq^r=0, 1≤r<m, both an original numerator and an original denominator vanish. The right side loses that interior zero completely. Consequently the small determinant of ratios is not a zero-preserving replacement for the original cross product. In the polynomial, denominator-cleared DW partition function the full product of denominator factors returns; acquiring it recreates a long orbit product.

The safe alternative of rotating every denominator by a nontrivial 2-primary phase makes denominators units, as in §1 and the prior Graeffe audit. But such a phase is not a nonzero native q-step: the two primary components are disjoint. Thus this safe denominator choice does not give the telescoping string (6). This is a focused failure of the explicit published rectangular specialization, not a general impossibility statement about all fusion constructions.

Confluent/Vandermonde normalizations also require care. Native target roots can repeat or be equal locally. The polynomial vertex definition and identities (1)–(3) remain valid without division. A direct numerical use of a Cauchy/Izergin determinant formula with those Vandermonde denominators is not automatically legitimate over R. No nonunit root difference is silently inverted here.

## Remaining constructive interface

The positive acquisition is the polynomial DW transfer (1)–(3), with a rank-two auxiliary algebra, explicit local weights, and only unit extra factors. Actual adjacent-clock fusion (4) is also available cheaply. What remains missing is a **zero-preserving closed fusion rule for its discarded channel**, or a polynomial rectangular identity whose entire remaining boundary factor is certified unit without being another long native product. Such a rule would need to accept the acquired O(L)-entry Seifert/root data and a binary geometric block, and produce an acquired scalar in softly O(L+sqrt m), with original labels retained for localization. None of the three inspected primary identities supplies that rule. The exact full-query rate is unchanged.

## Follow-up: retain the fusion amplitude explicitly

The rank-two fusion map in §2 has a concrete rank-one central image. Let

    v=(eta,1)^T, w=(1,eta),
    Pi = v w/(2 eta),  Q=I−Pi.

Since wv=2eta is a unit, these are acquired complementary projectors over R. The original central fusion block is (2eta/(q+1))Pi, so its proportionality amplitude is itself a certified unit. This closes that local normalization; it does not certify the channel which is removed.

Here is an exact two-line fermionic transfer test with the amplitude retained. For adjacent clock colors a,b=qa and a variable native root x, put

    f=1−a x, g=1−b x, D(x)=diag(f,g).

The determinant of D is the desired two-line cross factor. In the acquired image/kernel basis v=(eta,1), u=(−eta,1), whose determinant is 2eta, one computes exactly

    B^(-1)D B = [[s,d],[d,s]],
    s=(f+g)/2=1−(a+b)x/2,
    d=(g−f)/2=(a−b)x/2,
    det D = s²−d² = f g.                              (7)

Thus the projected channel has scalar s, while retaining the eliminated channel via a Schur complement gives

    f g = s * [s−d²/s]                                (8)

on the branch where s is a unit. The correction is not a harmless boundary unit. Conditional on a collision x=a^(-1), f=0 and g=1−q is a certified unit. Hence s=(1−q)/2 is a unit, d=s, and the **correction in (8) is zero**. A projection onto the image channel would miss this collision. The same occurs at x=b^(-1). This specialization is compatible with the native odd-order subgroup. It is a conditional algebraic test, not a proof that the particular value a^(-1) occurs in a prepared catalogue for a specified semiprime. At other inputs s itself can be nonunit; this branch must first perform its gcd/zero test and must not invert it unconditionally.

Equation (7) is an exact canonical one-particle/free-fermion channel calculation, not a claim that every Alexander tangle intertwiner equals D. It tests the proposed replacement of the two independent cross factors by the small fusion image. Any full graphical fusion identity must reproduce its determinant, including the off-diagonal coupling or its Schur correction, for the crossing-product transfer used in (2). The Yang–Baxter relation alone does not remove that coupling. Importantly, this is stronger than simply counting lines: even when the chosen image normalization and the Schur pivot are units, the retained correction contains precisely a desired collision zero.

An odd-order-safe phase can change this calculation only in the already useful special pairing b=−a: then (1−ax)(1+ax)=1−a²x², and the added phase factor is a unit for native x. For adjacent native colors b=qa, b/a=q is not −1. Adding both safe phase partners yields

    (1−ax)(1−bx)(1+ax)(1+bx)
      = (1−a²x²)(1−b²x²),

which still has two distinct clock colors a²,b². It does not turn (8)'s zero correction into a unit.

One may instead carry the entire scalar amplitude through repeated fusion. Its value for a root x then contains ∏_i(1−t_i/x) (up to certified units), a degree-m polynomial in x^(-1) even though its displayed auxiliary module can remain small. Acquiring that scalar at every acquired root recreates the original long norm; acquiring all coefficients costs at least m outputs in that representation. This is not a proof against another structured evaluator, but explicitly charges the amplitude left unpaid by the small-module claim. No correction computed here is both unit-safe and sufficient to shorten the native clock.
