# The exact high digit obstructs small progression carriers

2026-10-16. **A new actual-source arithmetic obstruction to a proposed cheap acquisition route.** On a majority-size subfamily of the existing qualified semiprime supply, every cover of the actual targets by k geometric progressions of total listed length T satisfies

    k^2 T >= m^1.96/32.

The starts and ratios may depend on the entire input or private advice. Holes, overlaps, repeated terms, ratios outside the gamma subgroup and cyclic wraparound are allowed. Thus polylogarithmically many progressions cannot supply a nearlinear-size carrier. This closes a concrete escape left by the [uniform-boundary packet](../semiprime-uniform-boundary-20261016/README.md), while leaving acquisition on other representations open.

The proof is classical and has two independent final-byte reviews. Three authorized Sol 6.1 agents investigated the carrier, scalar norm and direct q-resultant routes in parallel. No whole-family softly O(m) collision query, improved factoring exponent or new Lean theorem is claimed. The general query bound remains softly O(m^(3/2)).

## Why the lifted arithmetic forces the obstruction

The [high-digit proof](reports/high-digit-carrier-obstruction.md) fixes the odd prime m and low digit n=N mod m, then varies h in N=n+mh modulo m^2. The exact native centered lift satisfies

    delta_j(h)=delta_j(0)-m*j^(-1)*h  (mod m^2).

The literal offsets depend only on these two input digits. Their low image is exactly n(Squares-1), with zero included, so the deduplicated set Delta has J>m/2 elements. Let E(Delta) count ordered quadruples with delta_1+delta_2=delta_3+delta_4.

If the four inverse tags have nonzero signed sum, their high-digit equality holds for at most one h. If their signed sum is zero, the low quadratic identity also fixes the sum of squares; the two unordered tag pairs must then be identical. Counting these cases gives the uniform estimate

    sum_h E(Delta_h) <= 4m^3.

Consequently at most 4m^1.96 coprime residues modulo m^2 have E(Delta)>m^2.04. The existing varying-menu selector strips transfer this residue count to an absolute O(M^5.980) bad-pair count against the SAME original qualified supply, of size Omega(M^6/log^2 M). It can be unioned with the earlier losses. On the remaining actual majority family, E(Delta)<=m^2.04 at EVERY current candidate m. No equidistribution of semiprimes modulo m^2 is assumed.

The span [-3m^2,m^2] and gamma order greater than 8m^2 identify this integer energy with the multiplicative energy of the actual target set Z. The inherited orders satisfy this in both hidden fields and the global unit group. No order or discrete-log acquisition is supplied to an algorithm.

For any progression cover, partition the distinct targets among its progressions. A subset of a length-T_i progression has at most 2T_i-1 distinct pair products, even with cyclic wraparound. Cauchy--Schwarz and Holder give

    E_times(Z) >= J^4/(2k^2T),

which proves the displayed tradeoff simultaneously for ALL advised covers. Total length O(m) requires Omega(m^.48) progressions; polylogarithmically many require length at least m^1.96/polylog(m). This polynomial gap uses the exact high digit, beyond the previous low-digit boundary theorem.

## A more general carrier restriction

Every finite ambient carrier C containing Z must have

    |CC| >= m^1.96/16.

Thus a nearlinear-size carrier must have a nearly quadratic number of distinct pair products. A rank-r multiplicative progression box of listed size T has |CC|<=2^r T, even with repeated elements and cyclic relations; it therefore needs 2^r T>=m^1.96/16. Nearlinear boxes of fixed rank, or rank o(log m), are excluded. Arbitrary polylogarithmic rank is not excluded by that inequality.

These are representation bounds, not arithmetic-operation lower bounds. Many progressions with shared computations, high-productset carriers, fixed-F cancellations and different factor-selective observables remain possible. The theorem does not assert that a carrier description is necessary for every fast query algorithm.

## Scalar norm processing still needs a new evaluator

The [direct q-resultant audit](reports/direct-q-resultant.md) checks the scalar-only target rather than assuming all row values must be acquired. For D(t)=prod_j(t-z_j), F=P_L(t;q), and G(X)=X^J D(1/X), the exact composite-safe identity is

    Res(D,F)=prod_(i<L) G(q^i).

Baby/giant evaluation forms a degree-Jb block polynomial and costs softly O(Jb+L/b+J), hence softly O(J+sqrt(JL)). This implementation is already scalar-only; its degree charge does not arise from returning a full row list. It is an upper bound for that implementation, not an impossibility theorem for scalar norms.

The inspected velusqrt theorem pays one scalar q-product over a composite ring, while the special-resultants theorem constructs a full degree-JL composed product. Neither inspected interface supplies the desired compressed scalar evaluator. Primary PDFs/texts and the root's independent applicability review are preserved. A hypothetical improved norm oracle must also support subset/common-interval restrictions, or another paid method of resolving saturated gcds. The existing native leaf recovery is inherited and pinned.
