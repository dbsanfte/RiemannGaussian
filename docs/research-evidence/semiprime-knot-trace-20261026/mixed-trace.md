## 1. The common orientation and its finite guards

Put

    gamma=beta^((N+1)/2), A=beta^((N−1)/2),

The actual identities are

    gamma=beta^((P+Q)/2) modN,
    delta=A^-1 modP, delta=A modQ,
    delta+delta^-1=t=A+A^-1.                         (1)

For gamma the exponent difference is (P−1)(Q−1)/2, a multiple of each local field order. For delta the relevant differences are (P−1)(Q+1)/2 and −(P+1)(Q−1)/2. ## 4. Execute a nonabelian Fricke candidate

Primary trace reference: Goldman, *Trace Coordinates on Fricke spaces of some simple hyperbolic surfaces*, https://arxiv.org/abs/0901.1404. The standard SL2 identity is tr[U,V]=x²+y²+z²−xyz−2, where x=trU,y=trV,z=trUV. It is an integral matrix identity and may be checked directly over R; a generic complex-character parametrization does not acquire z from x,y.

Use the favorable framed model U=diag(delta,delta^-1), V=[[1,1],[1,2]], detV=1. Then

    x=t,y=3,z=delta+2delta^-1,
    tr[U,V]=6−t².

Both x,y and the commutator trace are cheap known scalars. Substitution into Fricke gives

    z²−3tz+2t²+1=0.                                (5)

Its two public roots are A+2A^-1 and A^-1+2A, separated by the unit A−A^-1. The actual z is the SAME mixed orientation as delta. Equation(5) is therefore an acquired but NONSELECTING second quadratic; it does not provide an acquired linear condition on delta. Choosing unequal diagonal entries in V makes z orientation-sensitive but does not compute its value.

One can instead choose a known companion U with trace t and a known V, then compute their traces outright. That creates a lawful pair of matrices but also chooses its relative eigenbasis/framing. It is not proof that the resulting trUV is the unknown mixed-framing value above. This is the precise missing relative coordinate. Fricke/skein relations can propagate acquired trace coordinates; this calculation supplies no new coordinate from the native arithmetic.
