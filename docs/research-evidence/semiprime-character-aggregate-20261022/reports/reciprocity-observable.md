## Exact collision-sufficient symbol outputs

Let J_N(a) be the ordinary Jacobi symbol, including zero on nonunits. It is exactly multiplicative even on nonunits, since N is odd and squarefree. Thus

    b_j=J_N(H_(sign_j)(y_j))
       =product_(r∈R_(sign_j),n<K)J_N(y_j−q^(r+mn))

belongs to{−1,0,1}, and b_j=0 EXACTLY when the assigned H value is a nonunit. A hypothetical simultaneous algorithm returning these J ternary symbols in soft O(m+J) would suffice, even without computing any H value or a gcd of H. Select a zero-symbol row, which exists by the own-tag collision after paid boundary screens. Its original UNMASKED scalar row product is nonunit. The inherited one-row geometric interval product algorithm localizes one nonunit leaf in soft O(sqrt L); the original positive gamma exponent gap makes saturated recovery useful. This is the same already proved recovery mechanism, with “zero symbol” replacing “nonunit H value.” No restriction-stable character oracle is needed after the row is selected.

Let S_N be the full Gaussian quartic Jacobi symbol with rational ideal denominator(N), extended by zero whenever the Gaussian norm is nonunit. For a RATIONAL residue a,

    S_N(a)=1 if gcd(a,N)=1, and0 otherwise.        (1)

The unit equality follows at a split hidden prime because the two conjugate Gaussian prime factors contribute inverse fourth-root phases; at an inert prime p=3mod4 the Euler exponent(p²−1)/4 is divisible by p−1, hence is1 on every rational unit. Norm(a)=a² gives the stated zero behavior. Therefore

    S_N(H_(sign_j)(y_j))=|b_j|²

is a Boolean UNIT mask. It is neither a new quartic phase nor a factor for free; evaluating it directly at the implicit product would already solve row detection. A selected Gaussian prime above a hidden split factor would give a different symbol, but that selected ideal is not public input and is not substituted for(N).

## Why the whole known-zero symbol does not select a row

The symbol of the product over ALL assigned rows is known to be0 from coverage. This does not specify a zero row. Multiplicativity has an absorbing zero and cannot encode the location in the surviving phase. By contrast an adaptive zero-symbol evaluator for products over public row subsets would suffice by balanced row bisection using O(log J) queries, followed by the paid scalar one-row locator. This is a weaker alternative algorithmic target than all J outputs, but no such subset evaluator has been acquired here. Each subset query has to be charged; it cannot be answered by the known root-level promise.

Likewise computing a unit-only phase formula, with no defined behavior or localization guarantee on nonunits, does not implement this sufficient symbol output. The zero convention is the substantive part of the required character evaluation.

## Audit of the concrete reciprocity route

For each acquired O(log N)-bit integer a, the Jacobi symbol J_N(a) is computable with logarithmic overhead by binary/half-gcd reciprocity. Brent--Zimmermann's primary algorithm is O(M(B)log B) for EXPLICIT B-bit operands. Applying it after acquiring all H residues adds only soft O(J) native work. This proves that symbols are cheaper outputs AFTER the H evaluation; it does not acquire their implicit numerators sooner.

To apply ordinary integer reciprocity directly to an unreduced exact product A_j=product(y_j−q^i), one must supply its actual integer representation or a new compressed-reciprocity algorithm. Using least-residue q,y_j as integer lifts, their powers q^i may have Theta(m² log N) bits each; even the small integer lift of each modular difference is an unacquired member of a J-by-D family. The H product is not an explicit O(m)-term sparse binary integer. A factored expression is not automatically a sparse integer in binary, and its expansion is not paid. Reducing the whole numerator modulo N first is exactly the missing modular product evaluation.

At the formal-polynomial level, reciprocity exchanges explicit polynomial arguments and their Euclidean remainders. Here H has degree D~m² with unacquired coefficients; replacing a scalar numerator by a resultant or a polynomial-Jacobi expression does not provide the explicit remainder. A fast gcd algorithm on supplied polynomial coefficient vectors cannot be run on this implicit H representation without supplying those operations. No field-character evaluation oracle is assumed.

Shparlinski's 2000 paper “Computing Jacobi symbols modulo sparse integers and polynomials and some applications” is a relevant lead, but only its author-institution abstract was retrievable in this inspection. That abstract asserts polynomial-time algorithms for special exponentially large sparse integers and polynomial analogues; it does not state a soft-m algorithm for this factored geometric product representation. No detailed theorem or complexity claim from the inaccessible full paper is used. Thus the paper is NOT dismissed by an invented hypothesis, and is not cited as an implemented compressed-product oracle either. Its full theorem would require a separate exact contract check before application.

## Coupled Gaussian phase retains the same unpaid product

A source-preserving nonlinear kernel already identified in scratch is

    alpha(x,y)=(1+i)(x−y)(x−i y).

For prepared odd-subgroup x,y, x−i y has unit norm x²+y². Thus

    S_N(alpha(x,y))=S_N(1+i) S_N(x−i y) 1_unit(x−y).

This genuinely retains a coupled unit phase, unlike norm-one chords. Its full phase product requires the crossproduct product(x−i y), which is not silently called cheap. Relative shifted-window phases can be paid by boundary polynomial evaluations, but their unknown initial phase and absorbing collision zeros remain. No reciprocity identity inspected here computes the masked sum or locates the original collision from those relative phases. This observation is retained only to distinguish the direct rational symbol(1) from the genuinely nonlinear phase; it is not repackaged as acquisition.

## Precise theorem still needed

A successful reciprocity construction can target ONLY one of the following smaller outputs: the J zero-symbol indicators above, or enough adaptively restricted product symbols to select a nonunit original row. It need not output the large H polynomials, all leaf phases, or a primitive quartic phase. It must accept the PUBLIC character-period root descriptors and native scalar target list, handle zero symbols exactly, and pay all construction and symbol queries in soft O(m+J), including logarithmic bit costs. No existing theorem checked here supplies that algorithm.
