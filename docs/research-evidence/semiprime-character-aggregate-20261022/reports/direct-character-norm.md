## A concrete relative-norm carrier and its actual action

A tempting character carrier is A=R[T]/(T^m-Q), Q=q^m, with the supplied scalar q an actual root. Put V=T/q; then V^m=1. For a unit a modulo m the map

    T -> q^(1-a) T^a

IS a genuine automorphism of this carrier: its mth power remains Q. This repairs the otherwise invalid substitution T->T^a when Q^a!=Q. Its coefficient action is a permutation with explicit powers of q and costs soft O(m). The square-residue subgroup has a rank-three fixed algebra here (the exponent orbits are zero, squares, and nonsquares); removing the trivial component by the primitive cyclotomic quotient leaves rank two. Relative quadratic periods are therefore legitimate proof-side objects, rather than merely formal residue masks.

However its orbit of q*V is q*V^a, not q^a. Evaluation at the distinguished scalar component V=1 makes the whole orbit q. Conversely the polynomial encoding the desired block roots has coefficient phases q^r: product_(r in R_e)(X-q^r V^r). Acting by V->V^a permutes V exponents but retains q^r, and rewriting r'=ar modm introduces the exact factors q^(r-r'). Those factors are not a common scalar and include the original Q carries. Therefore this acquired automorphism does not identify the actual scalar block with a relative norm of one orbit element. The row-dependent carried exponent descriptions of the x_j do not supply such an orbit identity either.

This is an exact account of the candidate carrier, not a general character-norm obstruction. A different element, enlarged carrier, or an acquired correction contraction could still be useful. Enlarging it requires charging the resulting degree and every downstream multiplication; the small fixed subalgebra alone does not make all original-q coefficient phases lie there.

## Direct transition contraction remains the missing operation

In the actual degree-J target algebra B=R[X]/F_e, let

    B_e(Z)=product_(r in R_e)(Z-q^r),
    U_n=q^(mn*|R_e|) B_e(X q^(-mn)) mod F_e.

Then exactly R_e=product_(n<K) Norm_B/R(U_n). Multiplicativity allows the norm before or after the product, and all q inverses are certified units. The individual scalar norm is

    Norm(U_n)=product_(j,r)(x_j-q^r Q^n).

Thus the desired contraction is one scalar product of K samples of a bivariate split resultant. Acquiring the degree-J|R_e| scalar transition polynomial would cost about m² output coefficients. Acquiring all U_n as quotient vectors costs JK about m² residues. Neither output is necessary in principle for the final scalar; neither is acquired in soft m by multiplicativity alone.
