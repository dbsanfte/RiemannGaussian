## 1. Elliptic shifted products: legitimate finite degeneration, no acquired collapse

Krotkov–Spiridonov, *Infinite elliptic hypergeometric series: convergence and difference equations*, Sb. Math. 214(12) (2023), 1751–1778, equations (2.1)–(2.5), defines theta(z;p)=(z,p/z;p)_infinity, |p|<1, its quasiperiodicity theta(pz;p)=-z^(-1)theta(z;p), and the FINITE elliptic shifted factorial product theta(tq^n;p), 0≤n<K. At p=0, theta(z;0)=1-z and this finite object becomes the ordinary finite q-Pochhammer. Primary page: https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=sm&option_lang=eng&paperid=9874&wshow=paper .

The p=0 degeneration is exact and useful: it does not require an analytic limit to interpret a finite polynomial. It is nevertheless the SAME length-K product, with no complexity theorem claiming J changing-parameter values for soft(K+J). Elliptic summation identities add balancing/termination conditions; simply naming our product an elliptic shifted factorial supplies no such parameter specialization.

There is an apparent special geometry worth making explicit. In a COMPLEX Tate description with nome Q=q^m, q represents m-torsion modulo the period Q. This permits theta/isogeny multiplication identities on COMPLETE torsion products. It does not mean q^m=1 as a scalar modulo N. The finite H uses K actual representatives of each scalar orbit, whereas theta identifies representatives up to Q with quasiperiodic factors. Replacing that finite product by a Tate norm requires a proved finite endpoint correction acquired at the native unit inputs. No such cheaper correction was identified: the uncanceled factors are precisely finite shifted products or their weighted ratios. Taking theta endpoint ratios whose quasiperiodicity becomes a monomial can cancel the desired collision factors as well; such a ratio is not an OR-of-zeros detector at a zero-over-zero specialization.

The source gives analytic difference equations and convergence statements, not an exact finite-semiprime acquisition algorithm with an explicit bit/precision/tail certificate. No separate theta value or Tate-curve point is treated as free public input here.

## 2. Modular equations and deformation: quasi-linear in a larger output

Andreas Enge, *Computing modular polynomials in quasi-linear time*, arXiv:0704.3177v2, 23 July 2008, computes the classical prime-level modular polynomial in O(ell² log³ell M(ell)) bit time, within soft O(ell³). The pinned version labels its main floating-point correctness/precision result heuristic. Its input is a level and modular-function setup; its output is a full modular equation. It does not claim soft O(ell) construction for an implicit endpoint correction or evaluation of J finite character products. Primary: https://arxiv.org/pdf/0704.3177 .

Sabrina Kunzweiler–Damien Robert, *Computing modular polynomials by deformation*, author-hosted 28-page paper, gives an UNCONDITIONAL CRT algorithm quasi-linear in the integral modular polynomial's output size, and reduction modulo a supplied prime in soft O(ell²*(log p+log ell)^O(1)). The introduction states degree ell+1 in each variable and integral size O(ell³ log ell). Its small-prime CRT stages construct suitable curves/torsion and deform isogenies; these are charged algorithm inputs/stages, not hidden semiprime Frobenius data. Primary: https://www.normalesup.org/~robert/pro/publications/articles/modular_deformation.pdf .

The newer theorem repairs the older heuristic status for modular-polynomial construction; it does not make the output ell-linear. At ell~m, explicit two-variable modular-equation construction remains at least quadratic coefficient volume even modulo a small prime. More importantly, no equation in those papers is instantiated as an exact finite H endpoint formula selecting its correct branch over unfactored N. An algebraic relation without the correct root/value selection would not acquire a norm or locator. Thus neither old analytic nor newer deformation cost supplies the target interface.

## 3. Recent square-root-isogeny work: evaluation targets stay charged

*On the Parallelization of Square-Root Vélu’s Formulas*, Mathematical and Computational Applications 29(1) (2024), article 14, describes algorithms for kernel-point generation and image-curve/POINT evaluation with explicit independent-work partitioning. Its authors' primary article is https://www.mdpi.com/2297-8747/29/1/14 . The inspected primary abstract/algorithm description concerns parallel latency and fixed-kernel isogeny evaluation; it does not state total soft(sqrt ell+J) work for arbitrary J evaluation points. The full article fetch was rate limited during this pass, so no stronger theorem is attributed to it.

The original square-root Vélu primitive was already audited in the repository: https://velusqrt.isogeny.org/velusqrt-20200616.pdf . Its scalar geometric-product example is composite-ring safe and costs soft sqrt L for ONE supplied argument. Its isogeny inputs include a supplied kernel generator of a specified odd prime order, and its fast resultant/index-system construction pays its explicit polynomial inputs. Our prepared q has no public period m, and a quadratic-residue subset of actual high-order scalar orbit representatives is not automatically that complete kernel. Reusing kernel preprocessing does not by itself remove J evaluation work; no new amortized theorem was found in the recent source search.

Fast division-polynomial/elliptic-divisibility evaluation was considered as a possible positive exception: a complete multiplication-map divisor can have logarithmic-length doubling formulas. Applying that exception would require an explicit curve/point identity equating the finite Legendre-selected scalar divisor with such a multiplication-map divisor, including its endpoint factors. None was found; calling H a division polynomial would omit this necessary construction. No logarithmic evaluation theorem is imported for H on that analogy.

## Result and precise residual

The finite elliptic degeneration is mathematically valid but leaves the same finite product. Complete theta multiplication uses a period identification absent from the actual scalar product and leaves an unacquired endpoint/branch problem. Modern modular-equation computation has an explicit quadratic/cubic level-dependent output charge. Recent parallel square-root-isogeny work does not provide a total-work J-target acquisition theorem for our supplied inputs.

The exact positive theorem still needed is a finite, source-preserving character-product identity whose right side consists of soft O(m) ACQUIRED scalar/ring operations at all J actual targets, including every level-m coefficient, finite endpoint and correct algebraic branch; or a direct shared norm/locator algorithm with that cost. None of the inspected primary sources supplies it. This is an applicability outcome, not exclusion of theta/isogeny methods in general, and no improved factoring exponent is claimed.
