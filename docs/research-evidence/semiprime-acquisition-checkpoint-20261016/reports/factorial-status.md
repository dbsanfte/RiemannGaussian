# Factorial cancellation pass

No all-input O~(m)-bit annihilator acquisition was obtained. No obstruction theorem is claimed.

The exact original coverage identity is

  N+delta_j-m^2 I=(P-1)(Q-a_j),  0<=I<=2m^2+1.

Thus a correct term contains a WHOLE local order, even when its prime-power factors could alternatively be distributed over several terms.

An exact public integer cancellation exists. Let T=2m^2+2 and U_j=product_{i=0}^{T-1}(N+delta_j-m^2 i). For prime m, set D=T!/m^{v_m(T!)}. Then D divides each U_j: for each prime ell!=m and k>=1, the AP step is invertible modulo ell^k, so it contains at least floor(T/ell^k) multiples of ell^k. Summing over k proves v_ell(U_j)>=v_ell(T!) (with a zero term making divisibility trivial).

On the inherited PRIME-local-order family R_P,R_Q>16m^2>T, division by D preserves their numerator valuations. This produces exact generalized-binomial row ratios with no hidden-factor advice. On general odd local orders>B it is NOT preservation-safe, because denominator prime powers may remove necessary parts of a smooth/composite local order. The whole-order promise alone does not license cancellation.

Even in the prime-order family the quotient has no proved O~(m)-bit bound; generic numerator and quotient sizes remain O~(T) per row and O~(mT) over all rows. Computing these quotients therefore does not meet the requested acquisition bound. No additional paired-semiprime identity proving a small integer ratio was found in this pass.
