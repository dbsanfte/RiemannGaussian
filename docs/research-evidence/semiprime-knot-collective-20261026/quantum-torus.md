## 1. Native data and the permissible auxiliary parameter

Let ell=k/2=3^e, S=ell²=H/4. The actual unit-b conic phase is

    mu²=abN modS, lambda=2mu, 0<=mu<2S,

    E=bN+c_b mu²+2mu-S*tau_b(mu),
    c_b=(bN)^-1 modS,
    tau_b(mu)=(c_b mu²-a_b(mu))/S.

This is an acquired sparse phase/carry list of total size J=soft k, not a dense S-element input. Put rho=beta^S, so rho²=eta and rho⁴=q. The native beta/eta/q orders are odd and their relevant short orders exceed16H. In particular rho has the same local order as q, since raising an odd-order element to4 does not change its order.

An auxiliary primitive ell-th root zeta may lawfully be adjoined: 3 and ell are units modulo N, so the cyclotomic algebra is etale of rank phi(ell)=Theta(k). A whole cyclotomic coefficient vector for EACH of J unrelated entries would cost J*phi(ell), not soft J.

## 2. Execute the honest cyclic quantum-torus determinant

Over that auxiliary extension, take

    U e_r=a*zeta^r e_r,
    V e_r=e_(r+1) (r<ell-1), V e_(ell-1)=c e_0,
    U^ell=a^ell I, V^ell=cI,
    UV=zeta VU.

For the claimed base-ring scalar cost, a,c are acquired units of R; zeta alone lives in the auxiliary extension. Extension-valued central inputs would require their actual coordinate cost. These are genuine finite cyclic matrices with nontrivial central characters, not the unauthorized relation q^ell=1. For any polynomial G,

    det(G(U)-V)=product_(r=0)^(ell-1)G(a*zeta^r)-c.   (1)

Only the identity permutation and the full cyclic permutation contribute to this diagonal-minus-cycle determinant. In particular det(I-U-V)=1-a^ell-c. This is the elementary scalar quantum-Frobenius/Chambers phenomenon; no large determinant algorithm remains unpaid for (1).

Apply it to the acquired split native polynomial F(X)=product_j(X-v_j). Its auxiliary cyclic norm is

    det F(U)=(-1)^(ell J) product_j(v_j^ell-a^ell).     (2)

All v_j powers, differences and products are acquired in O(J log ell) ring operations, soft J at the bit scale. For the native reciprocal knot Laurent normalization K(X)=c_0 X^-L F(X), multiply by the known unit c_0^ell a^(-ell L); ell is odd so det U=a^ell. Thus the entire scalar norm, including its units and zeros, is actually acquired. This is prior cyclic-norm content in a quantum-torus realization, not a new q-factorial algorithm.

The extra subtraction by c in (1) is an ADDITIVE scalar modification. A proper gcd of its value factors N, but a saturated additive zero is not automatically an original annihilator. The unshifted norm (2) instead factors into genuine powered root differences and can use retained labels when they meet the original gap contract. ## 3. Actual clock central characters do not coalesce

The following optional factor-or-interface test uses only paid routines and does not change the clock. Screen q^ell through cap16H. A proper gcd factors. If the separator instead finds a small exact common projected order, coprimality of the two original q-orders forces that common order to1; q^ell=1 globally then supplies the short nonzero annihilator ell on the OLD large odd q base, and the licensed extractor factors in soft k. Otherwise

    ord_P(q^ell),ord_Q(q^ell)>16H.                   (3)

This costs soft k and uses no hidden order or inverse. It is a known short-label order-screen composition, not counted as a new factoring advance.

For the actual physical clock z_i=eta*q^i, 1<=i<=m, with m<16H, equation(3) gives

    z_i^ell-z_j^ell is a unit for i!=j.              (4)

Consequently a single standard auxiliary cyclic orbit {a*zeta^r} can contain at most ONE physical clock point in either local field: two of them would have equal ell-th powers, contradicting(4). The central characters z_i^ell vary with i. This statement uses the native unit guard, not a generic dense Fourier calculation.

Thus the direct proposal “replace the whole short geometric interval by one root-of-unity orbit with a central character” fails even when the character a^ell is nontrivial. A direct UNION of standard cyclic blocks covering all m points requires at least m blocks per component; at dimension ell per block it is not a rank-O(k) encoding. This is a scoped architectural statement, not a lower bound on a different determinant identity, cancellations between channels, or an implicit contraction algorithm. It does not prohibit a new joint conic identity.

Even the two legitimate conic phase lifts mu and mu+S have native exponents differing by2S, so their roots differ by eta; their auxiliary ell-th central characters differ by eta^ell, which is not1 under the short guard. Treating those two lifts as one auxiliary orbit would lose the earlier exact eta-interlacing boundary rather than reproduce it.

## 4. Replace the auxiliary rotation honestly: the rank-one boundary is nonzero

Let D=diag(1,q,...,q^(r-1)) and let C be a cyclic shift with wrap c, for any r=O(k) in the guarded range. Directly on basis vectors,

    D C-q C D=c*(1-q^r) e_0 e_(r-1)^t.              (5)

The right-hand coefficient is a UNIT; it is not a small analytic error, zero central phase or ignorable determinant multiplier. Thus the actual geometric alphabet is a rank-one-defective cyclic Weyl pair, not a representation of the root-of-unity torus used in(1). More strongly, if two invertible free r-by-r matrices exactly satisfied DC=qCD, determinants would imply q^r=1, impossible in the native range. This only excludes that exact finite Weyl representation; rank-one-defective models are allowed, but need their explicit boundary contraction.

The finite cyclization of a scalar dilogarithm recurrence exposes that contraction exactly. Starting from

    g_(j+1)=y/(1-x*q^(j+1))*g_j,

a length-r periodic closure would require, after clearing,

    (xq;q)_r*g_r=y^r*g_0.

If g_r=g_0 is invertible then y^r=(xq;q)_r. At auxiliary zeta this is the cheap Fermat expression1-x^r (up to a supplied start convention). At arithmetic q it is the ACTUAL block q-product. Choosing a “central coefficient” y therefore does not pay the normalization; it names an r-th root of the required block scalar. If a block contains an original collision, that scalar is nonunit and y cannot be a unit in an invertible cyclic-dilogarithm operator. Keeping the equality cleared preserves the zeros but retains the block product.

For all J native row starts and about m/r clock blocks, these central normalizations are Q_r evaluated at J*m/r actual shifted points, with Q_r the degree-r acquired q-factorial polynomial. The standard initialized implementation costs

    soft(r+J*m/r+J),                                (6)

apart from scalar unit powers, and requires no collision inverse. At r=Theta(k), J~k,m~k^(3/2), this is of order m, above the target. Optimizing r restores the old soft(J+sqrt(Jm)) bound. The optimizer r about sqrt(Jm) can exceed O(k); that fallback is not a rank-O(k) construction. A formula that divides away all y's would divide away precisely the desired native product; inverting them at collision is illegal. Numerical values of unrelated proved UNIT phase corrections are optional for a zero-only query, but these block normalizers are not certified units and carry the desired zeros themselves.

This is the explicit missing correction for this candidate. No new invariant matrix description is counted as its scalar readout.

## 5. The metaplectic quadratic phase and the low-a cutoff

One can instead use the honest conductor-S quadratic Weil kernel, with auxiliary primitive S-th root Zeta. For a unit c modulo S, its quadratic convolution matrix is

    G_(mu,nu)=Zeta^[c*(mu-nu)^2]
             =Zeta^(c mu²)*Zeta^(-2c mu nu)*Zeta^(c nu²).

The middle matrix is a permuted Fourier matrix because2c is a unit. Its inverse exists after division by S, a certified unit. Hence this exact Gaussian OPERATOR is invertible of rank S, even though a particular complete Gauss SUM can evaluate to a square-root-sized scalar. That scalar evaluation does not give a rank-ell operator compression. Likewise an invertible r-dimensional representation of UV=Zeta VU would force S|r after reduction to each residue field, by taking determinants. A conductor-S Weyl representation of rank O(ell) is therefore unavailable within this standard architecture.

The actual sparse native phase mask can legitimately be projected down to O(k) selected coordinates. But it is not an invariant quantum-torus subspace. For the diagonal indicator P_I and unit shift C on Z/S,

    (P_I C-C P_I)e_mu=(1_I(mu+1)-1_I(mu))*e_(mu+1).  (7)

For any proper nonempty low-a mask this has a nonzero unit-coefficient boundary. The actual primitive pair a=7,b=1 or a=5,b=1 from the preceding packet supplies a concrete retained two-root mask +/-r, avoiding both diagonals and public zero deletion; it is proper and nonempty. Thus already on ACTUAL native support there is no free restriction of the translation/Weil identities. The full b=1 low-a mask is proper too: its unit-square image modulo S has S/3 distinct residues, all in one nonzero class modulo3, while the signed band |a|<k contains at most ceil(2k/3) representatives in that class; S/3>ceil(2k/3) for the actual k>=18.
