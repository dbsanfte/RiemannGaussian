COMPLETE PRIME BANKS: PAID BRANCHES, CUTOFF TRANSPORT, AND THE OPEN COVARIANCE

Scope and outcome
-----------------
This is a new pure analytical review of candidate10_prime_bank_obligation.json.
It preserves all frozen code, results and previous analytical files. I read
OBSTRUCTION_AUXILIARY_ANALYTIC_REVIEW_001.txt and
prime_bank_uniform_attempt_001.txt to avoid repeating a norm-only or past-only
argument. No arithmetic run, numerical response evaluation, test, hash, or
parameter search was performed. The source is actual mu throughout.

No closed all-scale signed recurrence is obtained. Several concrete reductions
are proved, however:
  * the existing Chebyshev trace removes the logarithm from the full weighted
    branch envelope, with explicit constants and an empty small-x range;
  * theta=3/4 makes the entire v branch bounded dyadic-energy padding;
    theta>3/4 gives finite critical norm;
  * all changing-cutoff terms cancel jointly except a paid v-weighted term;
  * an entire small-index component of K is paid independently of RH;
  * the companion difference has a stronger no-log-cofactor bound, yielding
    finite critical norm for its v-bank even at theta=3/4;
  * the complete integer covariance kernel restricts K/bank overlap to a
    new prime strictly greater than every prime of the smooth coefficient.
The remaining obligation is an explicit high-smooth/cofactor source correlation,
with full bank-bank and mainterm covariances retained. Finite Abelian zeros at
one do not supply the needed finite-prefix rate. No RH theorem is claimed.

1. Fixed arithmetic, domains, and coefficient envelopes
------------------------------------------------------
Keep u=1/3, v=2/3, beta=9/2, and
  a_b=mu*(n^(-b)), h_b=(a_b-delta)/b,
  p=h_u*h_v, a=p-d,
where d is the COMPLETE same-prime prime-power overlap. Prefixes use inclusive
real endpoints and vanish below one. The reduced original target is
  R(x)=sum_(n<=x)a(n)-m_u x^(2/3).
The frozen centered candidate equals R+D-m_v x^(1/3); those two complete
components already have paid critical-norm costs. They are not removed from
the frozen program or its output.

Let d_2(n) be the divisor count. Actual local a_b prime-power coefficients are
nonpositive with complete absolute mass one, so |a_b(n)|<=1. Therefore
  |p(n)|<=beta*d_2(n).
Every coefficient at a prime power belongs to the same-prime branch, hence
  a(1)=a(p^e)=0 for every prime p and e>=1.
Away from prime powers a=p. Thus |a|<=beta*d_2 is valid; the previous, more
conservative |a|<=2*beta*d_2 also remains valid and is used in the component
constants below. No pointwise divisor bound is imported from data.

For k>=2 define
  z_u(k)=p(k)+beta*a_v(k),
  z_v(k)=p(k)+beta*a_u(k),
  z_0(k)=z_u(k)+z_v(k),
  Delta(k)=z_u(k)-z_v(k)=beta*[a_v(k)-a_u(k)].       (1.1)
All these transfer sequences are ZERO at k=1. The naive displayed formulas
involving a_b(1)=1 must not reinstate a unit bank term. Safely,
  |z_u|,|z_v|<=2*beta*d_2,
  |z_0|<=4*beta*d_2,
  |Delta|<=2*beta.

For a prime q not dividing k>1, actual multiplicativity gives
  a(qk)=-z_0(k)+q^(-u)z_u(k)+q^(-v)z_v(k).         (1.2)
It holds because qk has at least two distinct primes, so d(qk)=0. It must
not be applied to k=1: a(q)=0, and the unit corrections have already been
removed by zero extension of the transfer sequences.

Let theta be rational with 1/2<=theta<1, Y=x^theta and M=floor(x/Y).
For x>=1 define
  Pi_b(x,k)=sum_(Y<q<=x/k, q prime)q^(-b),
  L_b(x)=sum_(2<=k<=M)z_b(k)Pi_b(x,k), b=0,u,v,
  K_theta(x)=sum_(2<=h<=x,Pplus(h)<=Y)a(h).
The roots here are positive REAL powers and the prime cutoff is strict on
the bank side. For every large prime q>Y, q^2>x and k<=x/q<q; the cofactor
is coprime to q automatically. Every coefficient is partitioned exactly:
  R=K_theta-L_0+L_u+L_v-m_u x^(2/3),
  R_new,theta=K_theta-L_0+L_u-m_u x^(2/3),
  R=R_new,theta+L_v.                              (1.3)
Every repeated-prime and smooth-number term remains in K. At theta=1/2 the
strict q>sqrt(x) condition still makes this argument valid. At theta>=1
all banks are empty and K is the full coefficient prefix; that case is an
exact empty-bank representation, not a better signed estimate.

2. Exact unconditional prime input, with a standalone fallback
-------------------------------------------------------------
The existing all-real estimate is
  psi(t)=sum_(p^e<=t)log p <= C_psi*t, t>=1.
Its repository proof is prime_discrepancy_proof.txt, section 1, equations
(1)--(4) and the finite-head paragraph immediately after (4). It derives
the estimate from the factorial trace and a universal periodic-floor
certificate. prime_twist_proof.txt, Scope and section 2, inherits and uses
this same proved C_psi. No PNT, RH, or unproved prime-error estimate is used
below. C_psi can be the existing certified all-scale constant.

For complete self-contained validity, C_psi=4*log 2 is also an elementary
fallback without computing any source values. For an integer N>=1,
  psi(2N)-psi(N)<=log binom(2N,N)<=2N log 2.
Indeed each prime-power index in (N,2N] contributes one to its prime's
valuation of the central binomial coefficient, and all other valuation
summands are nonnegative. Sum this at N=2^(j-1) to obtain
  psi(2^j)<=(2^(j+1)-2)log 2.
For 2^(j-1)<t<=2^j, monotonicity gives psi(t)<=4(log 2)t. The t=1 head
is zero. This is a symbolic factorial proof, not an arithmetic experiment.

Write Theta_prime(t)=sum_(q<=t,q prime)log q<=psi(t). Partial summation gives,
for 0<b<1 and T>=1,
  sum_(q<=T)log(q)q^(-b)
    =Theta_prime(T)T^(-b)
       +b integral_1^T Theta_prime(t)t^(-b-1)dt
    <=[C_psi/(1-b)]T^(1-b).                       (2.1)
For x>1 every bank prime has log q>theta log x, hence
  Pi_b(x,k)<=[C_psi/(theta*(1-b)*log x)](x/k)^(1-b).              (2.2)
This is the source of the logarithmic prime-count saving. It applies to
the whole declared bank, not only its newest members.

3. Safe general branch power rule and all endpoints
---------------------------------------------------
Consider any transfer sequence f, zero at one, satisfying |f(k)|<=C*d_2(k),
and its full b-weighted bank B_f,b(x)=sum_(2<=k<=M)f(k)Pi_b(x,k).
Ordered divisor counting and the decreasing-integral bound give
  sum_(k<=M)d_2(k)k^(b-1)
    <=(M^b/b)sum_(r<=M)1/r
    <=M^b(1+log M)/b.                             (3.1)
Combining (2.2), (3.1), M<=x^(1-theta), and log M<=(1-theta)log x gives
  |B_f,b(x)|
    <=[C*C_psi/(b*(1-b)*theta)]x^(1-b*theta)
                                      *[1/log x+(1-theta)].     (3.2)
The bank is EMPTY for
  1<=x<=x_0(theta):=2^(1/(1-theta)).
Below that value M<2. At equality M=2, but its only possible interval
requires x^theta<q<=x/2=x^theta, so it is empty too. Thus for every
nonempty bank log x>log 2/(1-theta), and uniformly for ALL x>=1,
  |B_f,b(x)|<=A_b,theta*x^(1-b*theta),
  A_b,theta=
    C*C_psi*(1-theta)*(1+1/log 2)/[b*(1-b)*theta].                 (3.3)
For empty banks the bound is automatic, including x=1 where (3.2) is
not used. It is safe for every 0<b<1 and 1/2<=theta<1. At theta>=1 use
the exact zero bank rule instead of the expression in (3.3).

The older integer-envelope rule also remains valid independently of the
Chebyshev saving:
  |B_f,b(x)|<=C/[b*(1-b)]*x^(1-b*theta)
                                  *[1+(1-theta)log x].          (3.4)
The compiler may keep both derivations. The no-log rule is an optional
proved improvement, not a numerical fit. There is no b=0 limit to (3.3);
the unweighted bank needs its own rule, and no critical envelope for it
is supplied here.

Put alpha=1-b*theta. For every integer dyadic X>=1, (3.3) implies
  E_(B_f,b)(X)<=2^(2alpha)*A_b,theta^2*X^(2alpha-1).               (3.5)
For b*theta>1/2 the critical integral is finite, with the safe cost
  ||B_f,b||_crit^2
    :=integral_1^infinity |B_f,b(x)|^2/x^2 dx
    <=A_b,theta^2/(2b*theta-1).                   (3.6)
At b*theta=1/2 the block energy is at most 2*A_b,theta^2, hence is cofinal
padding with NO logarithmic loss. Equation (3.3) alone does not prove a
finite critical integral at this endpoint. For b*theta<1/2, it leaves the
positive energy exponent 1-2b*theta; that is not cofinal padding.

For the full L_v use C=2*beta, b=2/3. At theta=3/4,
  A_v,3/4=3*beta*C_psi*(1+1/log 2),
  |L_v(x)|<=A_v,3/4*sqrt(x),
  E_Lv(X)<=2*A_v,3/4^2.                            (3.7)
The bank is empty through x=16. With the standalone C_psi=4 log 2,
A_v,3/4=12*beta*(1+log 2). These are exact analytic constants.
At theta=5/6,
  A_v,5/6=(9/5)*beta*C_psi*(1+1/log 2),
  |L_v(x)|<=A_v,5/6*x^(4/9),
  ||L_v||_crit^2<=9*A_v,5/6^2.                    (3.8)
This strengthens the older log-containing envelope but leaves its existing
certificate valid. All mixed terms with L_v are kept in the exact square;
both-direction Young/triangle bounds pay them with these costs.

4. Joint channel identities and one stronger companion component
---------------------------------------------------------------
For k>=2, z_0=z_u+z_v and Delta=z_u-z_v. Therefore the FULL bank combination
can also be written
  -L_0+L_u+L_v
    =-(1/2)sum_k z_0(k)[2Pi_0-Pi_u-Pi_v]
       +(1/2)sum_k Delta(k)[Pi_u-Pi_v].            (4.1)
The common and difference channels have different arithmetic content.
The positive weights in (4.1) do not give a sign to z_0 or Delta.
After the v peel the exact remaining expression is
  R_new=K_theta-sum_k z_0(k)[Pi_0-(1/2)Pi_u]
                  +(1/2)sum_k Delta(k)Pi_u-m_u x^(2/3).         (4.2)
Neither channel can be discarded. In particular eliminating p from Delta
does not eliminate the unresolved common-channel bank.

There is a genuine companion improvement without any missing cancellation.
Let g(n)=n^(-u)-n^(-v)>=0, g(1)=0. Then
  Delta=-beta*(mu*g),
  G:=sum_n g(n)/n=zeta(1+u)-zeta(1+v)>0.
This series converges absolutely. For every 0<b<1,
  sum_(k<=M)|Delta(k)|k^(b-1)
    <=beta sum_(d<=M)g(d)d^(b-1)
                             sum_(r<=M/d)r^(b-1)
    <=(beta/b)M^b G.                              (4.3)
This removes the divisor-count logarithm before the prime saving. Thus
  |B_Delta,b(x)|<=D_b,theta*x^(1-b*theta)/log x,
  D_b,theta=beta*C_psi*G/[b*(1-b)*theta],          (4.4)
for x>x_0(theta), and the bank is zero before then. It gives a logarithmic
gain beyond the single-branch no-log envelope. At b=2/3, theta=3/4,
  ||B_Delta,v||_crit^2
       <=(6*beta*C_psi*G)^2/log 16.               (4.5)
So the DIFFERENCE part of the endpoint v bank has finite critical norm;
the entire endpoint L_v has only the bounded-block conclusion (3.7)
from the proved bounds. The remaining u-weighted difference still has
pointwise exponent 1-theta/3>1/2 for theta<1. The gain does not by itself
peel that term or establish the full target.

A finite signed harmonic bound is also legitimate here. Actual divisor
inversion gives sum_(d<=y)mu(d)floor(y/d)=1. Hence
  |sum_(d<=y)mu(d)/d|<=1+1/y<=2,
and the finite convolution for Delta yields
  |sum_(2<=k<=M)Delta(k)/k|<=2*beta*G.             (4.6)
This is a proved finite-prefix statement. It is a bounded harmonic
component, not a decay rate, square-root prefix bound, or a proof that
its weighted bank mean vanishes at a useful rate.

5. Paid smooth-sector reduction and its precise limitation
---------------------------------------------------------
For any 0<r<=1/2 and theta>=1/2, all h<=x^r automatically satisfy
Pplus(h)<=h<=x^theta. Therefore K contains the ENTIRE component
  K_small,r(x)=sum_(2<=h<=x^r)a(h).
Using the conservative envelope |a|<=2*beta*d_2 and divisor counting,
  |K_small,r(x)|<=2*beta*x^r(1+r log x).           (5.1)
For r<1/2 it has the explicit finite critical cost
  ||K_small,r||_crit^2
    <=(2*beta)^2*[1/(1-2r)+2r/(1-2r)^2
                                      +2r^2/(1-2r)^3].         (5.2)
For r=1/2 its integer block energy is at most
  8*beta^2*(1+(1/2)log(2X))^2,
which is cofinal log padding, not a finite-critical conclusion.

Choosing r=1-theta is natural because it includes every possible bank
cofactor. For theta=5/6 its critical cost in (5.2) is 39*beta^2/4;
for theta=3/4 it is 20*beta^2. These are exact symbolic evaluations of
the integral, not actual-source calculations. Thus K can be replaced
in the proof obligation by its complete high-index smooth complement,
with K_small paid in both directions and its mixed terms retained.

Choosing r=1/2 exposes a sharper pairing obstruction: every retained
smooth coefficient h>sqrt(x) and every bank prime q>x^theta>=sqrt(x)
satisfy qh>x. A naive prime-multiplication pairing of these coefficients
therefore leaves the original pointwise coefficient prefix. An argument
may use a larger source domain, but must retain its whole outside-domain
complement and its endpoint costs. The new component bound is not a
norm-only proof of the high smooth sector; that sector still has large
indices and cannot be controlled by small-cofactor moments alone.

6. Exact changing-cutoff cancellation, with every old/fresh boundary
-------------------------------------------------------------------
First fix x and two cutoffs sqrt(x)<=Y_1<Y_2. Define K(x;Y), L_b(x;Y)
with that cutoff, and
  J_b(x;Y_1,Y_2)
    =sum_(Y_1<q<=Y_2,q prime)q^(-b)
                               sum_(2<=k<=x/q)z_b(k).           (6.1)
Empty cofactor ranges are zero. Every newly smooth coefficient has unique
largest prime q in this interval, to exponent one. Thus the full source
law, including all cofactors, proves
  K(x;Y_2)-K(x;Y_1)=-J_0+J_u+J_v,
  L_b(x;Y_1)-L_b(x;Y_2)=J_b.                     (6.2)
Consequently, with the main unchanged,
  R_new(x;Y_2)-R_new(x;Y_1)=J_v.                 (6.3)
The large unweighted and u-weighted entry corrections cancel JOINTLY.
They were not dropped or independently declared small. At cutoffs
Y_1=x^(theta_1), theta_1>=3/4, the absolute bound for J_v is at most
the full absolute envelope proved for L_v with that lower cutoff.
For theta_1>3/4 it is finite-critical; at theta_1=3/4 it is bounded-block
padding. Thus admitted theta choices in this range are equivalent proof
representations modulo a paid component. Increasing theta shifts burden
into K; it is not a new cancellation mechanism by itself.

For dyadic transport x->2x, let Y=x^theta, Y'=(2x)^theta and retain the
old coefficient prefix h<=x separately. Its gained smooth terms are
  G_old=sum_(h<=x,Y<Pplus(h)<=Y')a(h)
       =-J_0,old+J_u,old+J_v,old,
where J_b,old is (6.1) at the parent x. This is valid already from
Y>=sqrt(x), including theta=1/2. The new bank difference is EXACTLY
  L_b(2x;Y')-L_b(x;Y)
    =F_b,new-J_b,old,
  F_b,new=sum_(q>Y',x<qk<=2x,k>=2)q^(-b)z_b(k).   (6.4)
The child cutoff guarantees uniqueness in F_b,new. Let
  K_fresh=sum_(x<h<=2x,Pplus(h)<=Y')a(h).
Then the complete reduced transport is
  R_new(2x)-R_new(x)
    =K_fresh-F_0,new+F_u,new
       -m_u[(2x)^(2/3)-x^(2/3)]+J_v,old.          (6.5)
For the full R, F_v,new is added and J_v,old cancels against the change
of L_v, leaving the exact full fresh coefficient sum. Equations (6.4)-(6.5)
retain old-bank gains, newly born domains, unit exclusion and every main
increment. J_v,old is paid by the parent branch envelope; the analogous
absolute bound for F_v,new follows from the complete child envelope.

What remains unresolved in (6.5) is the joint F_0/F_u/K_fresh signed
transport, including its covariance with the parent when squared. The
old-cutoff cancellation supplies no bound for that fresh low-frequency
mode. The previous within-pair and smoothing lemmas still do not imply
the missing negative credit.

7. Full integer covariance kernels and the coprime support reduction
-------------------------------------------------------------------
For rational theta>0 define the exact integer threshold
  B_theta(q)=ceil(q^(1/theta)),
equivalently the least integer N with N^theta>=q. At rational theta=A/B,
the comparison is the exact integer N^A>=q^B; floating root rounding is
unnecessary. A prime q is a bank prime at integer n exactly when
  n<B_theta(q).
The equality boundary belongs to the smooth side. For a coefficient
h>=2 set
  A_theta(h)=max(h,B_theta(Pplus(h))).
Then its K contribution is active exactly for integer n>=A_theta(h).

For a dyadic integer X, coefficient h>=2, cofactor k>=2 and prime q,
the COMPLETE K/bank intersection count is
  I_(X,theta)(h;q,k)
    =max(0,min(2X,B_theta(q))-max(X,A_theta(h),qk)).                (7.1)
Every endpoint is integer. It counts n in [X,2X) satisfying simultaneously
h<=n, Pplus(h)<=n^theta, qk<=n, and q>n^theta. It includes moving-cutoff
entry boundaries; there is no unsupported common-domain replacement.
Thus exactly
  <K,L_b>_X
    =X^(-2)sum_(h>=2,k>=2,q prime)
                   a(h)z_b(k)q^(-b)I_(X,theta)(h;q,k).           (7.2)
The finite kernel automatically kills indices outside the block horizon.
No k=1 term is admitted. If the small-index K peel is used, add its exact
activation restriction rather than dropping its covariance by sign.

If I is positive, some counted integer n satisfies
  Pplus(h)<=n^theta<q.
Therefore q>Pplus(h), and q does NOT divide h. This proves the coprime
support reduction from the actual positive intersection, rather than
assuming coprimality from a formal label. The same-largest-prime diagonal
q=Pplus(h), and every q<Pplus(h), is exactly zero. Also
  k<=n/q<n^(1-theta)<=n^theta<q,
so q does not divide k. The source pair retains every possible overlap
between h and k; gcd(h,k)=1 is NOT implied. A generator may partition
that overlap, but must retain its other sector.

The complete bank/bank count is
  J_(X,theta)(q,k;r,l)
    =max(0,min(2X,B_theta(q),B_theta(r))
                                      -max(X,qk,rl)),            (7.3)
and
  <L_b,L_c>_X
    =X^(-2)sum_(q,r prime,k,l>=2)
          z_b(k)z_c(l)q^(-b)r^(-c)J_(X,theta)(q,k;r,l).          (7.4)
For a positive count BOTH large primes exceed BOTH cofactors: k,l<=
n^(1-theta)<=n^theta<min(q,r). Hence both primes are coprime to both
cofactors. The q=r common-prime sector and q!=r sector are both retained;
there is no independence or cancellation assertion for either.

For completeness the K/K count is
  max(0,2X-max(X,A_theta(h),A_theta(g))),
and the K/main or bank/main kernels replace the count on their exact
intersection interval by the sum of n^(2/3) on that interval. The main
square is m_u^2*X^(-2)sum_(X<=n<2X)n^(4/3). These retain the main as a
separate atom; putting it inside K must not conceal its mixed costs.

The root's five-atom representation
  (K,-L_0,L_u,L_v,-m_u*n^(2/3))
therefore has a complete explicit Gram interpretation. After the paid
L_v peel, four atoms remain. Equations (7.1)-(7.4) are a substantive
source-domain reduction of the cross kernels, not a proof of their sign
or size. They expose a distinct-next-prime/smooth-coefficient correlation
with source overlap h,k still present; this can be the next named AST
obligation without merely copying the total target square.

8. Finite Abelian zeros versus actual finite-prefix remainders
-------------------------------------------------------------
Let D_b(s)=zeta(s+b)/zeta(s) and P(s)=H_u(s)H_v(s). Over k>=2,
  Z_u(s)=beta*D_u(s)[D_v(s)-1],
  Z_v(s)=beta*D_v(s)[D_u(s)-1],
  Z_0=Z_u+Z_v,
  Z_Delta=beta*(D_v-D_u).                         (8.1)
They are first Dirichlet identities on Re(s)>1, then analytic continuations.
All have analytic value zero at s=1 because D_u(1)=D_v(1)=0. Their
derivatives need not be zero: this is not an unlimited vanishing-moment
certificate. In particular (8.1) alone neither proves ordinary convergence
of sum z(k)/k nor a rate for its finite prefixes.

There is a safe exact way to expose the missing terms. For any eta>0,
  H_z(M):=sum_(2<=k<=M)z(k)/k
    =Z(1+eta)
       +sum_(2<=k<=M)z(k)/k*[1-k^(-eta)]
       -sum_(k>M)z(k)/k^(1+eta).                  (8.2)
The last series is absolutely convergent. If |z|<=C*d_2, elementary bounds
give
  |finite correction|<=C*eta*(1+log M)^3,
  |last tail|<=C*(1+eta)*M^(-eta)
                         *[(1+log M)/eta+1/eta^2].              (8.3)
The tail bound follows by partial summation of
sum_(k<=t)d_2(k)<=t(1+log t). Taking eta to zero removes Z(1+eta), but
does not justify removing either remainder. No useful signed rate follows
by replacing these finite prefixes with the analytic value zero.
The bounded finite Delta harmonic statement (4.6) is separately proved
and can be used; it still supplies no decay.

The exact smaller-cofactor representation is
  L_b(x)=sum_(q>x^theta,q prime)q^(-b)
                                     F_zb(floor(x/q)),
  F_zb(t)=sum_(2<=k<=t)z_b(k).                    (8.4)
Its finite Abel expansion retains
  F_zb(M)Pi_b(x,M)
    +sum_(k=2)^(M-1)F_zb(k)[Pi_b(x,k)-Pi_b(x,k+1)].              (8.5)
The first endpoint need not vanish when M is floored. A proposed cofactor
moment must control the weights in (8.5), not just an unweighted norm of
F_zb at one sampled scale. The complete K sector and kernels (7.2)-(7.4)
still remain after that control.

Even a separate square-root cofactor prefix envelope, if hypothetically
supplied, would not make the unweighted bank or u bank subcritical by
absolute summation: the upper end q of order x yields powers x and
x^(2/3), respectively, up to prime-count logarithms. This failure is
visible without a conjecture in actual fixed columns. At k=2,
  z_0(2)=-beta*[(1-2^(-u))+(1-2^(-v))],
  z_u(2)=-beta*(1-2^(-v)),
both are nonzero. The elementary prime-count lower bound already proved
in the earlier candidate review gives, for theta<1, their separate
Pi_0(x,2) and Pi_u(x,2) columns of orders at least x/log x and
x^(2/3)/log x eventually. This is a statement about separate columns,
not a lower bound for the full signed banks. The other cofactor columns
and K must cancel them. Treating each small column independently cannot
close the target, despite the zero in (8.1).

9. Exact Mellin boundary compensation, not a missing-tail shortcut
-----------------------------------------------------------------
The moving cutoff has an analytic boundary too. In any initial region
where the sums are absolutely convergent, for a generic coefficient
bank B_f,b,
  s integral_1^infinity B_f,b(x)x^(-s-1)dx
    =sum_(q prime,k>=2,qk<q^(1/theta))
         f(k)q^(-b)[(qk)^(-s)-q^(-s/theta)].       (9.1)
The strict product condition is equivalent to a nonempty bank interval.
For K,
  s integral_1^infinity K_theta(x)x^(-s-1)dx
     =sum_(h>=2)a(h)*max(h,Pplus(h)^(1/theta))^(-s).              (9.2)
The delayed smooth admission and the negative bank endpoint terms match
coefficientwise through (1.2). Their sum recovers the original coefficient
Dirichlet series and all complete main fractions. Replacing a truncated
Z by Z(1)=0 while omitting q^(-s/theta) in (9.1) removes precisely this
boundary compensation. Equations (9.1)-(9.2) are complete identities,
not a new holomorphic bound for the original target.

10. Exact next signed input and limits of the present reduction
--------------------------------------------------------------
At the input obligation's theta=5/6, the obligation remains, for every epsilon>0,
  exists C_epsilon<infinity, independent of dyadic X>=1, such that
  X^(-2)sum_(X<=n<2X)
       |K_theta(n)-L_0(n)+L_u(n)-m_u*n^(2/3)|^2
       <=C_epsilon*X^epsilon.                    (10.1)
