GLOBAL MOMENT DISCOVERY: INEQUALITIES WITH QUANTIFIERS AND SCALE COSTS
7 October 2026. Written universal proofs; independent exact/native audit.
Known-framework calibrations. No new prime-distribution bound or RH proof.

1. THE SEARCH TARGET IS AN INEQUALITY FOR AN ARITHMETIC FAMILY

The detector now represents row-indexed coefficient families, normalized
circle moments, mixed energy states, signed scale transfers, positive
functionals and forced recurrences. The input has a complete declared
coefficient range. Every proposed bound must declare its scale variables,
row domain, coefficient assumptions, recurrence, initial cost, and the
quantifiers covered by its certificate. A fitted exponent is discovery
data, not a uniform estimate. The finite search is restricted to its
declared matrices, states, shifts and weight grid.

The benchmarks intentionally use unrelated families: additive twists of
the zeta inverse, generic polynomial pairs, signed recursive polynomials,
and positive lacunary products. The QRH exponents, sextic characters and
cubic theta function are not training targets or primitives in this search.
These calibrations do not verify the QRH manuscript's global bounds.

2. ADDITIVE-TWIST SECOND MOMENTS: ALL INTEGER SCALES

For any complex coefficients a(1),...,a(D), and integers D,H>=1, define
A_u=sum_(n<=D)a(n)e_H(u*n), 0<=u<H, and M2=sum_u|A_u|^2.
Finite additive-character orthogonality gives exactly

 M2=H sum_(r mod H)|sum_(n<=D,n=r mod H)a(n)|^2.

Each bucket contains at most ceil(D/H) indices. Cauchy's inequality gives
M2<=H*ceil(D/H)*sum_n|a(n)|^2. The squared l2 operator norm is exactly
H*ceil(D/H): choose a(n)=1 on a largest bucket and zero elsewhere.
The proof includes composite H and the zero residue. Numerical training
only at D<=H cannot identify the bucket factor; a changed scale ratio
rejects its omission. Actual mu coefficients are obtained by inversion of
u(n)=1, then audited independently by complete integer factorization.
The result is a generic bound, not new cancellation of mu.

For e_p(u*n^d), with PRIME p and any integer d>=1, the same identity groups
indices by n^d mod p. A nonzero polynomial X^d-r has at most d roots over
a field. Each root residue contributes at most ceil(D/p) indices, hence
M2<=p*d*ceil(D/p)*sum|a(n)|^2 for all D and all prime p. This retains r=0.
Over composite moduli, that root bound can fail. At H=D=16,d=2 the zero
bucket has four indices 4,8,12,16. Coefficients 1 only there have energy
256, exceeding the incorrectly extended prime-field bound 128.
An operator/modulus holdout is essential, in addition to scale holdouts.

3. LEARNING A CLOSED FOURTH-MOMENT STATE

Let P,R be polynomials with real rational coefficients supported on
0,...,N-1. Integrals use normalized Haar measure on the whole unit circle.
Define E=integral(|P|^4+|R|^4), C=integral|P R|^2; both are nonnegative.
The grammar searches signed matrices with entries +/-1 and phase shifts
L=k*N, k=0,1,2. There are 16*3=48 programs. It fits an UNKNOWN two-by-two
transfer on generic constant inputs, then changes coefficient inputs and
spans. Forty finite fits yield 24 universal transfers and 16 rejections.

For opposite signed rows, U=P+z^L R, V=P-z^L R, signs/swaps permitted,
odd phase terms cancel. In E_next and C_next the only remaining
nonconstant phase terms contain z^(+/-2L) P^2 conjugate(R)^2 or its
conjugate. Their unshifted frequency lies in [-2(N-1),2(N-1)]. When
L>=N, their integral is zero, giving the LEARNED recurrence

 [E_next]   [2 8] [E]
 [C_next] = [1 0] [C].                                    (1)

For proportional signed rows, both outputs are equal up to a sign.
When L>=2N, every nonconstant phase term vanishes by the same frequency
support bound, now also for shifts +/-L. Its different learned recurrence is

 [E_next]   [2 8] [E]
 [C_next] = [1 4] [C].                                    (2)

For L=N, (2) generally fails because odd phase terms may have a constant
coefficient. For L=0, a recurrence fitted on constant inputs need not
hold on polynomials. Fresh dense inputs independently reject all 16
bad fits. A failed two-state closure need not reject the operator:
adding a missing energy state or finding a valid partial functional is
an allowed future extension. No recurrence is promoted from a fit alone.

4. FROM ONE STEP TO EVERY LEVEL

The positive-functional grammar searches weights (1,c) and a rate lambda.
For (1) it discovers (E+2C)_next=4(E+2C); for (2) it discovers
(E+4C)_next=6(E+4C). These are exact positive left eigenfunctionals.
Induction gives the bounds 4^j(E_0+2C_0) and 6^j(E_0+4C_0) for every
level j, retaining the initial cost. Positive right eigenvectors at
rates 4 and 6 also prove that a smaller positive-functional rate is
impossible for these particular transfers. This does not exclude
stronger bounds for a smaller arithmetic subclass by other arguments.

Repeat the opposite-row transfer with L=N, starting from P_0=R_0=1.
Its pointwise pair energy is |P_j|^2+|R_j|^2=2^(j+1), and N=2^j.
The difference of the two next fourth moments is a constant multiple
of integral Re(z^N R_j conjugate(P_j)), which vanishes by frequency
support [-N+1,N-1]. Thus their fourth moments are equal at every level.
Writing m_j=integral|P_j|^4, (1) gives

 m_(j+2)=2*m_(j+1)+8*m_j,  m_0=1, m_1=6,
 m_j=(4*4^j-(-2)^j)/3 <= (3/2)*N^2, EVERY j>=0.            (3)

The scalar recurrence is also discovered from the finite observations;
its universal proof comes from (1), rather than those observations.
For odd j>=1 the ratio is at most (4+1/2)/3=3/2; for even j it is at
most 4/3, and at j=0 it is 1. Equality at j=1 makes 3/2 sharp.
This is the established Rudin-Shapiro polynomial family, a calibration.
It is not a bound for mu, primes or zeta in the critical strip.
Primary prior art for (3) and automatic mixed-moment recurrences:
Ekhad and Zeilberger, arXiv:1605.06679v2, pages 2-4.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.06679
The source attributes the formula to Littlewood; neither the recovered
formula nor the general computer-algebra strategy is claimed novel here.

Repeat proportional rows with L=2N and P_0=R_0=1. Now N=3^j and
P_j has coefficient 1 precisely when the ternary digits of its index
are all 0 or 2. There are 2^j coefficients. Since E=2C at each step,
(2) gives integral|P_j|^4=C_j=6^j for every j. This is a known
lacunary/Riesz-product framework, another unrelated calibration.

5. PERSISTENT ERRORS AND CRITICAL RESONANCE

A homogeneous recurrence may have a valid bound at its critical rate.
With persistent forcing the conclusion changes. For
U_(j+1)<=lambda U_j+q^(beta*(j+1))*B, normalize by q^(beta*j).
The accumulated cost is a geometric series with ratio r=lambda/q^beta.
If r<1, every horizon is bounded by B/(1-r), plus the initial cost.
If r=1, persistent positive forcing yields j*B: a logarithm of the
scale q^j. If r>1 the normalized cost grows geometrically. These are
sharp for equality recurrences. Missing a strict contraction margin
cannot be repaired by fitting a constant over finitely many levels.
The source retains a fit through level 31 that fails at level 257.

6. CONVERTING A FAMILY MOMENT INTO AN ARITHMETIC TARGET

This gate abstracts the role of repeated or approximate target rows,
without prescribing a character order, sixth powers or a number field.
Let a row family have q-moment at most B, q an even positive integer.
Suppose at least m rows satisfy |A_u-T|<=E for the same arithmetic target
T. The triangle inequality and positivity give

 B>=sum_(admissible u)|A_u|^q>=m*max(|T|-E,0)^q,
 |T|<=E+(B/m)^(1/q).                                      (4)

The source learns the moment and row-count exponents 1/2 and -1/2 from
saturating second-moment replication cases, without QRH-specific data.
Exact integer power comparisons produce a rational upper rounding of
the root, rather than an uncertified floating-point upper bound. The
independent audit changes to complex rows and moments q=2,4,6.
Discarding the approximation error is explicitly rejected at T=1,
five zero rows, E=1: the valid bound is 1, the incorrect bound is 0.

For (4) to be global, THREE separate premises must be global: the
family moment B, the admissible actual-prime/arithmetic row count m,
and the approximation error E. Finite row counts or a finite-scale
copying relation cannot supply those quantified premises. Applying
certificates from a different coefficient family is disallowed.

For ordinary zeta, one possible target is
A_W(D)=sum_n mu(n)W(n/D) with smooth compactly supported W on (0,infinity).
Initially Re(s)>1, direct Mellin transformation gives

 integral_0^infinity A_W(D)D^(-s-1)dD=hatW(s)/zeta(s),
 hatW(s)=integral_0^infinity W(y)y^(s-1)dy.                 (5)

A proved all-D bound A_W(D)=O_W(D^theta) gives a holomorphic left side
on Re(s)>theta. At a zeta zero rho there, the right side has a pole if
hatW(rho)!=0. A bound for one W whose transform vanishes at rho may
erase that zero information. A target-appropriate nonvanishing family
of Mellin numerators is therefore mandatory before any zero-exclusion
consequence. For example allowing W(y)=y^(-rho)phi(y) with fixed positive
smooth phi yields hatW(rho)=integral phi(y)/y dy>0, but the global bound
must actually cover such test weights. This is a conditional bridge,
not a newly proved Mobius estimate or an RH proof.

7. INDEPENDENT AUDIT AND ORDINARY-ZETA SCOPE

The checker uses exact Laurent expansion and support conditions for
every certified transfer; autocorrelations and binary/ternary coefficient
formulas instead of the source's recursive polynomial implementation;
and native 384-bit complex circle evaluations at changed row grids.
A too-short grid is an explicit negative control. Native additive
families include every row and independently factor every coefficient.
Fresh forcing parameters, recurrence bases, positive metrics and public
domain/missing-data controls complete the audit. These are written
proofs and implementation audits, not theorem-prover formalization.

The generic arithmetic bounds quantify all scales but supply no new
mu or prime-distribution estimate. The recursive families have genuine
global moment certificates, but they are different coefficient families.
Applying their bounds to zeta requires a separately certified transfer
that retains ordinary-zero information and controls all scale costs.
Global Hecke-family bounds and a new prime-specific critical-scale
cancellation estimate remain open detector targets. No new historically
verified operator, QRH proof verification or RH proof is claimed.
