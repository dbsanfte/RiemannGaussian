COUNT-CONTROL RESIDUAL GRAMMAR POSTREVIEW076
=========================================

Scope
-----
This is a new postreview; all076 pre-run sources and reviews are frozen
and unchanged. It makes no arithmetic source query, scientific rerun,

1. Same-column signed count envelopes
------------------------------------
At each parent, combine atoms on column i BEFORE interpreting prediction:

 b_i(N)=sum_{t:i(t)=i}a_t N^(-gamma_t), N=max(1,n_i),
 L=sum_i b_i(N_i)B_i.

This signed envelope is the actual source-column coefficient. The
structural A_i=sum|a_t| certificate safely discards its cancellation;
multiple nonzero atoms do not by themselves demonstrate useful signed
prediction. For a column with rational exponents gamma_t, choose the
LCM D of their reduced denominators and write x=N^(-1/D). Then
b_i(N)=P_i(x)=sum_t a_t x^(D gamma_t), an exact rational polynomial.
For a prime column with min gamma>=1/2 one can instead factor
b_i(N)=N^(-1/2)P_i(N^(-1/D)), choosing D to include denominator2.
The remaining exponents D(gamma_t-1/2) are nonnegative integers.
Thus sqrt(N)b_i(N) is exactly a rational polynomial on0<x<=1.

An executable structural envelope rule can certify |P_i(x)|<=k_i on
the whole closed interval[0,1], for example using exact Bernstein
coefficients: convert P to degree m Bernstein basis and use that the
basis weights binomial(m,r)x^r(1-x)^(m-r) are nonnegative and sum1.
Then min c_r<=P(x)<=max c_r, so k_i=max|c_r| is a rational universal
bound. Degree elevation or a declared finite rational subdivision can
sharpen the bound, with every segment and endpoint retained. Resource
gates on D, degree, coefficients and subdivision count are essential:
arbitrary rational exponents can generate an impractically large degree.

For prime columns this yields |b_i(N)B_i|<=k_i sqrt(Q_i), because
|B_i|<=sqrt(N Q_i); direct columns have exact coefficient sum_t a_t
and use its absolute value as k_i. Empty blocks vanish. Replacing A_i
by these certified k_i in the h-budget/beta construction pays all
correlation cross terms and may reward actual same-column cancellation.
It is a sound NEW certificate, not a smaller observed-count maximum.
An active gamma<1/2 produces a negative exponent after factoring; unless
the corresponding coefficients cancel exactly, no finite polynomial
bound at x=0 follows. Keep such a branch arithmetic/unpaid, or require
a separately verified rational-function envelope on its full count domain.

This rule alone can still move the whole problem into the residual.
The next construction below targets that residual exactly.

2. Residual ordinary-Mobius coefficient stencil
---------------------------------------------
For any integer v>=0 define the finite prefix stencil

 s_v(n)=1[n<=v//8]-1[n<=v//10]-1[n<=v//4]+1[n<=v//5], n>=1.

Then G(v)=sum_n mu(n)s_v(n), including mu(1)=1. At parent u merge
literal prime quotient multiplicities m_ij and direct collisions into

 v_j(u)=sum_i b_i(N_i(u))m_ij(u)+sum_d a_d 1[j=u//d].

The residual has the EXACT identity

 R(u)=G(u)-L(u)=sum_n mu(n)c_u(n),
 c_u(n)=s_u(n)-sum_j v_j(u)s_j(n).

The parent and all prime/direct interactions use ONE shared mu sequence.
Expanding the square therefore retains both diagonal c_n^2 mu(n)^2
and2 sum_{n<m}c_n c_m mu(n)mu(m). Neither term family may be discarded.
Count-power coefficients can be algebraic; keep their symbolic rational
power expressions or full certified intervals, rather than replacing
them by floats. Merged equal endpoints can cancel symbolically before
interval evaluation, improving both the grammar and numerical enclosure.

Every child j<=u//2. Thus every child prefix ends at most at u//8.
For every integer u//5<n<=u//4, the child stencil vanishes and the
parent stencil is -1. Consequently c_u(n)=-1 on this complete top
source band for EVERY operator of the present prime/direct grammar.
No count normalization or extra coefficient can annihilate these high
ordinary-Mobius coefficients pointwise. A successful residual estimate
must bound this signed band and/or its correlation with lower terms.
This support fact is not an asymptotic lower bound on R, nor an
impossibility theorem: sums at different n can cancel arithmetically.

3. Recommended executable construction: exact endpoint-event residual
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Represent a prefix M(e) as an endpoint event(e,coefficient). Build the
parent events(e,+1) for e=u//8,u//5 and(e,-1) for e=u//10,u//4.
For every merged child j subtract v_j times its SAME four events.
Drop events at e=0 because M(0)=0, and combine all equal endpoints
symbolically, preserving coefficient signs and direct/prime collisions.
Sort remaining endpoints0=e0<e1<...<em. If d_r is the combined
coefficient of M(e_r), then

 R=sum_r d_r M(e_r)
  =sum_{r=1}^m c_r [M(e_r)-M(e_(r-1))],
 c_r=sum_{h=r}^m d_h.

Proof: an ordinary mu(n) in(e_(r-1),e_r] occurs in exactly the
prefixes with endpoints e_h>=e_r, giving the displayed suffix sum.
This is finite telescoping with the M(0)=0 boundary paid exactly.
Empty segments contribute zero; coefficient-zero segments may be
removed or joined. There is no dropped terminal event or interpolation.
The construction is executable and can be independently tested on
arbitrary synthetic mu vectors, rational coefficients and quotient-count
maps, without any Mobius scientific query or candidate success.

Return endpoints, signed symbolic coefficients, their conserved prefix
expansion, all count variables, and the untouched top-band segment.
The desired new arithmetic leaf is the UNIFORM signed interval estimate

 [sum_r c_r(u)(M(e_r(u))-M(e_(r-1)(u)))]^2<=C u^q
 for every integer u>U0.

It is precisely the residual target, now expressed as shared arithmetic
intervals rather than an opaque fitted maximum. A proof may regroup
these intervals into signed pairings or establish paid cross-correlations;
it must not assume this target or the parent G bound to price them.
The generic unconditional fallback |mu(n)|<=1 pays
|R|<=sum_r |c_r|(e_r-e_(r-1)), including all endpoint costs. It is
safe but may be far too large. A finite small interval sum is not its
uniform price, and an imported Mertens/RH-strength bound would itself
be a separate arithmetic premise, not a free theorem of this stencil.

4. Exact count-jump residual evolution
------------------------------------
Let P_i=P_i(u), P'_i=P_i(u+1), and retained/entering/exiting sets be
I=P_i intersect P'_i, E=P'_i minus P_i, F=P_i minus P'_i. Then

 B'_i-B_i=sum_{p in I}1[p divides u+1] Delta G(u//p)
           +sum_{p in E}G((u+1)//p)-sum_{p in F}G(u//p),
 n'_i-n_i=#E-#F,
 Delta G(j)=G(j+1)-G(j).

This uses the exact integer floor jump, including child units and the
open lower/closed upper cutoff membership. Both cutoff boundaries can
move; an exited prime cannot be treated as retained. For direct d,
B'_d-B_d=1[d divides u+1]Delta G(u//d). Coefficients a_d are fixed.
Write b_i=b_i(N_i), b'_i=b_i(N'_i). Product subtraction gives

 R(u+1)-R(u)=Delta G(u)
 -sum_i [b'_i(B'_i-B_i)+(b'_i-b_i)B_i]
 -sum_d a_d 1[d divides u+1]Delta G(u//d).

The normalization-change term(b'_i-b_i)B_i is essential. Freezing
counts between anchors omits it. Its exact signed size can be enclosed
from rational power bounds at both integer counts. For gamma>=0,
mean-value gives |(N')^-gamma-N^-gamma|
<=gamma |N'-N| min(N,N')^(-gamma-1), with all min counts>=1;
this is a safe auxiliary bound, not a claim of small all-scale cost.
Count0 to1 has N=N'=1 and zero normalization change despite membership
changes. At any child Delta G is the four exact divisibility/mu jumps
recorded in the075 postreview, with no lost boundary correction.

Telescoping R over parents must retain R(U0+1), every floor crossing,
cutoff event and normalization event. A weighted difference-energy
bound can pay the resulting square via Cauchy, but its complete number
of increments/weights and anchor cost must enter C. Merely observing
small residual increments or adding a free count-jump feature is not
such a proof. This offers a second independently testable conservation
rule alongside the endpoint-event stencil.

5. Prime-toggle folding: an exact arithmetic residual construction
----------------------------------------------------------------
Let c(n) be any finitely supported coefficient stencil (real, algebraic
or formal symbolic coefficients are allowed), extended by zero outside
its support. For a prime p, split the sum into v_p(n)=0,1,>=2. The
last part has mu(n)=0. Write n=pm in the middle part; p does not divide
m and mu(pm)=-mu(m), including squareful m where both sides vanish.
Therefore the EXACT identity is

 sum_n c(n)mu(n)=sum_{m:p does not divide m}[c(m)-c(pm)]mu(m).

No positivity, independence, large-scale hypothesis or cutoff approximation
is involved. c(pm) means the complete SAME residual stencil, including
upper/lower parent bands and all direct/prime/count-power interactions.
Removing one band before folding would change the residual. Extend c by
zero so support endpoints are paid exactly even when pm exits a band.

Iterate over a finite set P of DISTINCT primes and Q=product_{p in P}p.
The transformations commute and give

 R=sum_{m:gcd(m,Q)=1} c_P(m)mu(m),
 c_P(m)=sum_{d|Q}mu(d)c(dm).

Proof by induction: after a previously folded set, split its restricted
sum by the next p. Multiplication by p preserves coprimality with the
previous product, and the two coefficient pieces combine with a minus
sign. The divisors of squarefree Q have signs mu(d)=(-1)^omega(d),
which exactly enumerate the combined subset signs. Repeating a prime
adds no new identity and should be canonicalized away. The unit m=1
is retained, with mu(1)=1 and complete folded coefficient c_P(1).

A sparse implementation need not enumerate all2^|P| divisors: for each
original supported n, if any p in P has p^2|n drop the row because its
actual mu value is zero; otherwise divide by every selected p dividing
n, multiply its coefficient by(-1) for each division, and merge equal
remaining m. Every retained m is coprime to Q. This implements the
same divisor formula. It can be independently checked by formal
coefficient conservation using these Mobius relations, or by synthetic
squarefree-factor sign models; arbitrary unconstrained mu vectors do
NOT satisfy the fold identity. Canonical symbolic expressions should
be combined before numerical interval enclosure, to preserve cancellation.

The new support is within the old horizon; it need not be strictly
smaller because original n coprime to all P are retained. The complete
arithmetic leaf is now

 [sum_{m:gcd(m,Q)=1}c_P,u(m)mu(m)]^2<=C u^q, every integer u>U0.

This is equivalent to the residual estimate, with explicit local prime
relations applied. It does not assume a bound on the parent G. A
coefficient-only universal fallback is
|R|<=sum_{m:gcd(m,Q)=1}|c_P,u(m)|,
since |mu(m)|<=1, or one may retain a paid squarefree indicator.
Any stronger cancellation/energy bound needs an independent proof on
this restricted ordinary-Mobius sum. Removing the gcd restriction or
assuming restricted mu samples independent changes the target.

Folding has a useful genuinely universal coefficient guarantee:

 sum_{p does not divide m}|c(m)-c(pm)|
 <=sum_{p does not divide m}|c(m)|+|c(pm)|<=sum_n|c(n)|.

Iterated sparse coefficient l1 therefore never increases. This does
NOT guarantee strict reduction or a power-saving uniform envelope.
Coefficient square energy can INCREASE: take c(1)=1,c(p)=-1 and zero
otherwise; initial sum c(n)^2=2, while folded coefficient at1 is2,
with energy4. A proposed weighted-energy compression rule must pay its
collision factor or prove its exact quadratic comparison separately.

For the raw parent G stencil, p=2 maps its negative upper band
(u/5,u/4] onto the positive band(u/10,u/8]. The subtraction c(m)-c(2m)
can reinforce their signs, rather than cancel them. This explains why
simply choosing the smallest prime is no general residual improvement
theorem. The full residual may contain lower interaction coefficients
that change that conclusion; schedule selection should inspect its
symbolic coefficients, never observed mu signs to choose a fit.

Recommended executable extension: generate a bounded finite list of
prime-toggle schedules, fold the residual endpoint/sparse coefficient
stencil exactly, and retain a coefficient-only l1 certificate plus any
separately verified weighted-energy certificate. Export original-to-folded
conservation, vanished prime-square rows, gcd restriction, unit coefficient,
support horizon, symbolic count dependence and an all-integer residual
proof leaf. Selection may use provable coefficient costs, not observed
Mobius residuals. Uniform improvement still requires a compression/jump
lemma valid for every u with fixed or explicitly controlled schedule.

Q and its divisor vocabulary can grow exponentially with schedule length;
sparse implementation work instead depends on stencil support and schedule
length, but support expansion from prefix intervals can be source-sized.
Budget product bit length, factor/divisibility work, symbolic-expression
growth and live memory before construction. A moving P(u) needs an exact
per-parent schedule rule, uniform complexity/price and paid changes in
the folded stencil between parents. One successful finite schedule or
coefficient reduction is not an all-scale arithmetic theorem, whole-head
bound or RH result.

Recommendation
--------------
Use the endpoint-event residual construction and prime-toggle folding
as the next executable detector rule. Search bounded schedules by
symbolic coefficient costs, with exact conservation and optional
Bernstein count-envelope certificates, rather than enlarging the same
coefficient dictionary or refitting C. Propose a specific uniform folded
residual/compression leaf, including gcd restriction, untouched high-band
interaction, count transitions, complete base and implementation cost.
The observed076 prediction is useful finite evidence for diagnostics,
while its whole-head/core/RH objective remains open.
