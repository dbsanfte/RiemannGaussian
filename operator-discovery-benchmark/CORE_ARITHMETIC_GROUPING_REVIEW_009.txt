ARITHMETIC GROUPING: EXACT CHAINS, FRONTIER OBSTRUCTIONS AND TRANSPORT RULES

Scope and results
-----------------
Only this new memo is written. Inputs are CORE_SIGNED_WORK_HANDOFF.txt,
candidate10_signed_work_obligation.json and the complete source/kernel
identities in007/008. No numerical source evaluation, experiment, test,
hash, subagent or prior/frozen edit is performed.

Two proof-route countercertificates are established:
1. Any fixed finite-prime adic orbit grouping, followed by absolute values
   on its groups, still loses at least c_P*X*log^2 X for this full source.
2. Even growing prime-strip sets contained in primes<=2X leave a singleton
   fresh-prime frontier and lose at least c*X^(1/3)/log^2 X. This lower
   bound is independent of how the small-prime strip grows.
The second uses the imported, proved prime number theorem; it does NOT
assume RH or any unknown signed prefix estimate.

These refute specified ABSOLUTE GROUPING proof routes, not the actual
signed-work conjecture. Cross-component grouping, scale-dependent large
prime/cofactor transport and signed boundary compensation are different
rules and must receive separate proof gates. Exact one-/two-leg chain
identities and a detector-enumerable transport certificate grammar are
given below. No all-scale closing source estimate is claimed.

1. Precise complete target and grouping scope
--------------------------------------------
Use f=beta*nu0-beta*nu1-R_u*nu2-beta*mu, beta=9/2, and the unchanged
actual-source states of008. Let a be any fixed generated delay coefficient,
g_a(n)=f(n)-a*1_(2 divides n)*f(n/2). The complete step kernel is
 Phi_eta,X(h,k)=K_eta,2X(h,k)-K_eta,X(h,k)/2,
 K_eta,X=(1-eta)*d_X(max(h,k))
                   +(eta/2)*log(h*k)*ell_X(max(h,k)),
with the exact full-cell d,ell from008. All ordered pairs1<=h,k<=4X-1
are retained; repeated valuations, diagonals, units and the old-head
product are part of this same kernel. Eta lies in[-2,2].

Given a finite prime set P, strip every P-adic valuation from each index:
 h=h0*r, k=k0*s, r,s P-smooth and gcd(h0*k0,product P)=1.
The unique complete orbit at cofactor pair(h0,k0) contains all such r,s
within the finite source horizon. Group all actual terms in each orbit,
then consider the majorant
 A_P(X)=sum_(h0,k0) abs(sum_(r,s admissible)
                g_a(h0*r)g_a(k0*s)Phi_eta,X(h0*r,k0*s)).   (1.1)
All16 mode crosses may be combined INSIDE each term or orbit before
this absolute value. This definition already gives that method its best
possible within-orbit cancellation; taking more separate absolutes only
makes its bound larger.

The theorem also covers refinements of these orbits and exchange of the
two legs, since paired symmetric terms have the same value and sign.
It does NOT cover arbitrary unions of distinct stripped cofactor pairs
before taking absolute values. Such a union adds a new cross-orbit
arithmetic pairing and cannot claim the theorem's hypothesis. If every
orbit is placed into one group, its absolute is just the complete signed
target and its bound is still the original open problem.

2. Comparable fresh support and singleton orbit theorem
-------------------------------------------------------
Take h,k in I_X=[2X,5X/2], with integer endpoints understood. The parent
kernel vanishes there and the child kernel is K_eta,2X. Its integration
domain lies between max(h,k) and4X. The factor multiplying x^-2 is
 1-eta+eta*log(h*k)/(2log x).
For X>=4, log(h*k)/(2log x) is between3/4 and1. Thus for every|eta|<=2
this factor is at least1/2. Since max(h,k)<=5X/2,
                  Phi_eta,X(h,k)>=3/(40X).               (2.1)

If h,k are P-free, this truncated orbit is a SINGLETON. Removing a
prime in P is impossible. Inserting any prime>=2 into either leg exits
the horizon4X-1, including at the lower endpoint2X. All longer paths also
preserve the stripped cofactors, so there is no indirect in-domain path
to a different term. This uses the actual finite boundary rather than
assuming that an infinite geometric chain remains present.
Consequently
 A_P(X)>=(3/(40X))*[sum_(h in I_X,P-free)|g_a(h)|]^2.       (2.2)
The diagonal and ordered off-diagonal pairs are both included. Combining
the four states at a singleton leaves the complete g_a(h), so joint-mode
cancellation has already been granted and cannot evade(2.2).

For fixed P, the complete absolute mode Euler factor at prime q, t=q^-1/3,
z=q^-s, is
 A_q(s)=1+(2-t)z+(1-t)^2*z^2/(1-tz).
Multiplying by(1-z)^2 gives1+O(q^(-Re(s)-1/3)+q^(-2Re(s))). Hence the
absolute nu0 coefficients have zeta(s)^2 times an absolutely convergent
Dirichlet series for Re(s)>2/3, with positive value at1. The fixed delay
changes only the prime2 factor. Restricting to P-free coefficients changes
every prime in P to local factor1, another FINITE change with positive
value at1. Elementary d2 summation and the same absolutely convergent
convolution proof as008 therefore give, for fixed P,
 sum_(n<=x,P-free)|g_a(n)|=d_(a,P)*x*log x+O_(a,P)(x),
                         d_(a,P)>0.                     (2.3)
The bounded other coefficient modes cost only O(x); every source term
was combined before absolute mass was counted. Thus the interval mass
in(2.2) is(d_(a,P)/2)*X*log X+O_(a,P)(X), and
           A_P(X)>=c_(a,P)*X*log^2 X, sufficiently large X. (2.4)
It cannot supply ANY fixed-polylog or all-epsilon cofinal bound. The
constants in(2.3) depend on P; this fixed-P argument is not silently
made uniform when P grows. A separate growing-P proof follows.

3. Growing strip sets <=2X: actual prime attenuation still leaves a power
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
At a prime q, the actual full four-mode coefficient is exactly
 f(q)=-R_u*q^(-1/3)+(beta+R_u)*q^(-2/3).                  (3.1)
The constant terms of the leading modes DO cancel; treating the full
prime coefficient as -2beta would be an incorrect source substitution.
For every odd prime, g_a(q)=f(q), whatever fixed delay a is used.

The exact residue is
 R_u=beta*[zeta(4/3)/zeta(2/3)^2-1/zeta(2/3)]>0.
Here zeta(4/3)>0. For0<s<1, the alternating paired Dirichlet eta series
is positive and zeta(s)=eta(s)/(1-2^(1-s))<0. Therefore positivity and
nonzero R_u do not rely on a floating-point sample. Once
 q^(-1/3)<=R_u/[2(beta+R_u)], f(q) is strictly negative and
                   |f(q)|>=R_u*q^(-1/3)/2.              (3.2)

Let P(X) be ANY set of primes contained in[2,2X], including sets whose
cardinality grows with X. Every prime q in(2X,5X/2] is P(X)-free. It is
a singleton in the pair orbit exactly as in section2. The imported PNT
pi(x)~x/log x and partial summation give
 sum_(q prime in I_X)|g_a(q)|
       ~R_u*I*X^(2/3)/log X,
 I=integral_2^(5/2)t^(-1/3)dt
        =(3/2)*[(5/2)^(2/3)-2^(2/3)]>0.                  (3.3)
The q^-2/3 term is of smaller order X^(1/3)/log X. All primes in this
range are odd; both delayed cases retain exactly the same prime mass.

The only imported global theorem in this section is the classical PNT,
not any conjectured RH-scale remainder. A primary proof-formalization
reference is Mario Carneiro, "Formalization of the prime number theorem
and Dirichlet's theorem" (2016), https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.02029.
Its PNT input suffices for the fixed-ratio interval and weighted partial
summation above; no prime table, fitted count or new source calculation
is used here. Without importing PNT, the same guard can be stated with
the explicit weighted-prime-mass lower bound as its independent premise.

Combining(2.1),(3.3) uniformly over ALL choices of such P(X),
 A_(P(X))(X)>=[(3/40)*R_u^2*I^2+o(1)]
                                  *X^(1/3)/log^2 X.     (3.4)
This defeats even a subpower cofinal proof by orbit absolutes. In fact
these isolated prime-pair terms are positive already, since f(q)<0 for
both legs and their kernel is positive. Signed composite/center sectors
outside these singleton orbits must compensate; absolute values on the
separate orbits prevent that compensation.

This statement includes all smooth cutoffs Y(X)<=2X, not only power
cutoffs X^delta. It does not refute the full signed target, nor a route
that crosses these prime-frontier components before taking absolutes.
If one retains the old-head product signed while majorizing the fresh
sum absolutely, that product is not independently paid or sign-known;
it needs a NEW cancellation estimate. The present theorem does not
declare such an unproved hybrid credit impossible.

4. Generic detector growth guard and necessary domain crossing
--------------------------------------------------------------
The previous argument is a reusable structural certificate. If the FULL
source prime coefficient, after combining every mode, has first nonzero
asymptote A*q^-lambda with A!=0, lambda>=0, and the delay does not alter
odd prime coefficients, PNT gives singleton frontier mass of order
X^(1-lambda)/log X. A positive comparable-pair kernel of order1/X then
forces grouped absolute cost of order
                         X^(1-2lambda)/log^2 X.          (4.1)
Lambda<1/2 rejects this cofinal proof route. Lambda=1/2 or larger merely
passes this ONE obstruction; it does not prove a target bound. Kernel
weights with additional powers must be included in the exponent, with
their full inverse/recovery cost recorded separately.

The detector can compile the first nonzero exponent from exact local
source expressions in q^-1/L, including all cross-mode cancellations,
then combine it with the orbit-support singleton witness and the kernel
lower certificate. Here the constant exponent0 cancels, but lambda1/3
survives. This is an actual-source proof-route rejection, not numerical
ranking of a prime strip or a claim that prime attenuation proves RH.

A useful next grammar must cross different P-free cofactors or include
the large prime itself in a scale-dependent chain. Merely adding more
primes below2X cannot do so. Including all primes<=4X-1, however, makes
every index reducible to the unit and the entire pair support one orbit.
Taking its absolute only renames the complete signed sum. Between these
extremes, a growing-prime construction needs a proved signed correlation
or boundary estimate, not only an orbit-connectivity certificate.

5. Exact complete one-/two-leg chain transport compiler
-------------------------------------------------------
For a prime p not dividing a cofactor m, multiplicativity gives
nu_i(p^e*m)=nu_i(m)*v_i(e), with v_i(0)=1. Let t=p^-1/3 and
S_i(E)=sum_(e=0)^E v_i(e). For E>=1 the exact partial sums are
 S0(E)=-(1-t)*t^(E-1),
 S1(E)=(1-t)*t^E,
 S2(E)=1+t-t^(E+1),
 S_mu(E)=0; all S_i(0)=1.                                (5.1)
These include every valuation and have no analytic continuation premise.
For any kernel values Phi_e, exact finite Abel summation is
 sum_(e=0)^E v_i(e)Phi_e
     =S_i(E)Phi_E+sum_(e=0)^(E-1)S_i(e)(Phi_e-Phi_(e+1)). (5.2)
Here E=floor(log_p((4X-1)/m)); the kernel may already contain the fresh
support mask, source delay and full signed step. Its upper exit is the
explicit S_i(E)Phi_E term. E0 is a one-point chain and is NOT stable or
canceled merely because its infinite local series has a zero at1.

For two p-free cofactors m,n and valuation rectangle0<=e<=E,0<=f<=F,
apply(5.2) twice. The result is EXACTLY the sum of
 corner: S_i(E)S_j(F)Phi_(E,F),
 first edge: sum_(e<E)S_i(e)S_j(F)(Phi_(e,F)-Phi_(e+1,F)),
 second edge: sum_(f<F)S_i(E)S_j(f)(Phi_(E,f)-Phi_(E,f+1)),
 interior: sum_(e<E,f<F)S_i(e)S_j(f)
       [Phi_(e,f)-Phi_(e+1,f)-Phi_(e,f+1)+Phi_(e+1,f+1)].  (5.3)
Multiply by c_i*c_j*nu_i(m)*nu_j(n) and sum ALL16 i,j. A fresh/domain
mask belongs inside Phi; it is not removed by using a rectangular chain.
This automatically retains common primes on both legs and both exits.
Distinct-prime chains compose sequentially; their new endpoints and
support intersections must remain in the certificate.

For the fixed prime2 delay, replace the local sequence by
v_i,a(e)=v_i(e)-a*v_i(e-1), with v_i(-1)=0. Thus its partial sum is
S_i,a(E)=S_i(E)-a*S_i(E-1), with S_i(-1)=0. Other primes retain(5.1).
This is an exact modewise construction of g_a, not a divisor assumption
that excludes even cofactors or prime-square channels.

The infinite UNWEIGHTED local chain is not zero for the full source:
sum_(e>=0) f(p^e*m)=-R_u*nu2(m)*(1+t), p does not divide m. (5.4)
The other three local sums vanish, while S2 tends1+t. For a prime2 delay
this limiting expression is multiplied by1-a. Infinite local sums here
are convergent geometric sums, but the subsequent cofactor series cannot
be asserted to converge at the real pole or at critical norm weights.
The nu2 complement has a genuine macroscopic prefix/pole channel; the
full target cancels it jointly with boundary/kernel-variation terms.
Dropping(5.4), either edge in(5.3), or truncated one-point chains is an
unsound zero-sum proof. Positivity of a local geometric tail does not pay
its transported prefix correlation.

6. Growing/adaptive alternative with explicit source obligations
----------------------------------------------------------------
There is a finite enumerable transport grammar beyond fixed strip orbits:
choose a source mode row or complete cross-row; a prime-adjunction/removal
rule on one or both legs; valuation depth from the exact finite support;
an ordering such as largest prime; and a rational kernel-difference rule
from(5.2)/(5.3). It must output every edge/corner and every unassigned term.
Using a largest-prime parent gives cofactor<=index/2, hence a genuinely
smaller arithmetic argument, but it does not on its own contract the
complete signed prefix norm or remove the common-mode forcing.

The exact adjunction polynomial for p not dividing m is
 f(pm)=A0(m)+p^-1/3*A1(m)+p^-2/3*A2(m),
 A0(m)=beta*[-2nu0(m)+nu1(m)+mu(m)],
 A1(m)=beta*[nu0(m)-nu1(m)]-R_u*nu2(m),
 A2(m)=beta*nu1(m)+R_u*nu2(m).                            (6.1)
At m1, A0=0, A1=-R_u, A2=beta+R_u: the isolated prime formula. For
m2, with t2=2^-1/3, A0(2)=beta*(2-t2-t2^2)>0. Thus actual opposite
coefficient signs can arise at different cofactors. But the same-prime
composite2p is OUTSIDE4X-1 for every fresh prime p>2X. A useful pairing
of that frontier must change the prime/cofactor support or keep an exact
out-of-domain exit; pretending2p is present is not a cancellation.
Unequal coefficient sizes and the changed kernel must also be paid.

This suggests enumerating cross-cofactor/prime transports, not merely
enlarging a fixed strip. A proof certificate can use an exact permutation
or disjoint matching of term vertices (including the16 mode labels), or
a rational nonnegative flow whose total assignment at each original
vertex is exactly1. Kernel/source defects and unmatched boundary vertices
are then explicit. Shared unit terms cannot be reused once for each
different large prime without an assignment budget. Combining all large
prime chains into one connected component is allowed, but its unproved
whole-sum estimate must stay OPEN rather than count as local contraction.

For such a generated transport, a closing gate requires an independent
all-scale bound on the TOTAL signed residual plus exits, with smaller
arguments and the full joint center retained. Fixed-polylog residuals
of any degree are useful for the cofinal goal; the degree2 branch also
needs008's negative cubic diagonal compensation. The source-specific
claim that these transported residuals have a polylog upper bound is
not implied by(5.3) or by the PNT. In particular bounded coefficients
do not pay the companion prefix correlations in A_i.

A detector can therefore soundly synthesize identities, verify finite
support/assignment budgets, apply the singleton growth guard and compile
conditional recovery. It still needs a proved arithmetic residual lemma
to close a surviving growing-prime transport. No such lemma is proved
here. The useful progress is exclusion of a broad apparently promising
absolute-grouping family and identification of the exact support-changing
transition and boundary terms that the next family must admit.

Final status: fixed-P and growing-cutoff<=2X absolute-route obstructions
proved; complete chain/transport identities proved; full signed work,
cofinal target and RH remain OPEN. Only this new memo was written.
