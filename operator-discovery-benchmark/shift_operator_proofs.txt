PROOFS FOR THE COEFFICIENT-SHIFT OPERATOR RECOGNIZER

Let b_alpha=P_alpha(u), alpha in Q, u(n)=1, using the coefficient-local
power program proved in root_proofs.txt. It is multiplicative, with
  b_alpha(p^e)=c_alpha(e)=alpha(alpha+1)...(alpha+e-1)/e!, c_alpha(0)=1.
This formula is used ONLY by the proof audit, not by the learner.

For any e>=0 and k>=0, direct cancellation of finite products gives
  [product_{j=1}^k(e+j)]*c_alpha(e+k)
    =[product_{j=0}^{k-1}(e+alpha+j)]*c_alpha(e).
This is a polynomial identity in alpha, so it holds even if a coefficient
vanishes. The proof never divides by c_alpha(e).

For m=product_p p^k_p define nonzero integer A_m(n) and polynomial B_m:
  A_m(n)=product_{p^k||m} product_{j=1}^k(v_p(n)+j),
  B_m(alpha,n)=product_{p^k||m} product_{j=0}^{k-1}(v_p(n)+alpha+j).
Multiplicativity and the local polynomial identity prove, for ALL n>=1,
ALL m>=1 and ALL rational alpha,
  A_m(n)*b_alpha(m*n)=B_m(alpha,n)*b_alpha(n).
No convergence assumption, cutoff, nonzero-coefficient assumption or
restriction to the training parameters is involved.

The proof may contain counts for composite bases. Counts that merely multiply
a known law can be left as arbitrary scalar indeterminates: no primality
hypothesis on that multiplier is necessary. For a prime-power base p^k,
the bounded count equals floor(v_p(n)/k). For each involved prime let L be
the least common multiple of its count exponents k. Write v_p(n)=L*u+r,
0<=r<L. Every count for p^k then equals (L/k)*u+floor(r/k). Substituting
these expressions into the candidate cross-polynomial and checking it is
identically zero for EVERY finite remainder r proves it for every integer
u>=0, hence every n. This is a universal quotient/remainder proof, not
extrapolation from tested exponents. Counts for other composite bases may
be treated as free scalar values only when the stronger polynomial identity
already holds irrespective of them.

The discovered candidate is a relation
  L(alpha,n,v)*b_alpha(m*n)+R(alpha,n,v)*b_alpha(n)=0.
When each count base in v is prime, its bounded count is exactly v_p(n).
The proof recognizer treats these valuations as independent indeterminates
and expands L*B_m+R*A_m. If this polynomial is identically zero, substitution
at the actual n proves the candidate. Since A_m(n)>0, cancellation of this
integer is legitimate. A candidate failing this symbolic criterion remains
unproved even if its finite numerical validations passed. Nonmultiplicative
index maps are also left unproved by this particular recognizer.

For prime p the simplest result is
  (v_p(n)+1)*b_alpha(p*n)-(v_p(n)+alpha)*b_alpha(n)=0.
Define the prime-basic derivation D_p f(n)=(v_p(n)+1)*f(p*n), and the
valuation multiplication operator N_p f(n)=v_p(n)*f(n). This becomes
  (D_p-N_p)b_alpha=alpha*b_alpha.
D_p is a classical derivation of the Dirichlet-convolution algebra; it is
not a historically new operator. The standard interpretation identifies
arithmetic functions with formal series in the prime variables, where
D_p is partial differentiation and N_p is x_p*d/dx_p. The equation is
the familiar local differential equation (1-x_p)F'=alpha F for the factor
(1-x_p)^(-alpha).

The algorithm may recover scalar/polynomial multiples of this equation.
Such variants are duplicate laws, not distinct mathematical discoveries.
Additive shifts and floor/remainder maps are genuinely included in discovery;
their rejection provides evidence about the searched finite grammar, not
a theorem that no richer mixed arithmetic identity exists.

VALIDITY LIMITS AND ZETA BRIDGE

The shift operator has explicit lookahead: output coefficients through N
may need inputs through m*N. It is an operator on infinite arithmetic
functions, not a cutoff-consistent map requiring only the first N inputs.
The output identity implies an absolutely convergent series identity in a
right half-plane for b_alpha. For the recovered positive roots alpha=1/q,
|b_alpha(n)|<=1, and powers of valuations grow at most powers of log n;
the sums converge for Re(s)>1. This does not establish a holomorphic
global qth root through zeta's zeros or a new RH implication.

PRIOR ART

Wai Yan Pong, Applications of differential algebra to algebraic independence
of arithmetic functions, Acta Arithmetica 172.2 (2016), section 2, defines
D_p f(n)=f(np)*v_p(np), describes its correspondence to prime-variable
partial differentiation, and discusses valuation multiplication derivations.
https://www.impan.pl/shop/publication/transaction/download/product/91275

The proof criterion is an exact polynomial identity checked by SymPy.
It is supported by this universal argument, but is not a theorem-prover
certificate. Independent large-cutoff/new-parameter checks audit its
implementation rather than serving as substitutes for the proof.
