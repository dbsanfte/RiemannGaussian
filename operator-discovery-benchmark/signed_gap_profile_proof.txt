ACTUAL SIGNED NULLSPACE GAPS WITH INDEPENDENT CONSTANT AND EXPONENT LAWS

The target is the full actual defect Delta(X)=A(2X)-4A(X), not an unsigned
proxy or a selected part. Q(N)=sum_(n<=N)mu(n)^2 and N=4X. The fitted family
is D(X,q)=C(q)*X*ceil(X^epsilon(q))*ceil(ell(X)^L), ell=1+floor(log_2 X).
Ceilings use the original integer/fraction split for powers above one.
L is a nonnegative rational. Numerical fits propose the HALF-GAP
Delta(X)<=D(X,q)*Q(4X)/2 at every dyadic X and every sufficiently large q.
It is stronger than merely positive nullspace gap; it is not proved.
Q<=4X makes this an RH-sufficient full signed defect bound when
epsilon(q)>0 tends to zero. Every finite C(q) may depend on q but never X.
No positivity assertion for a complete dual matrix is made from this scalar
test. It isolates the missing arithmetic premise before a Schur comparison.

1. CONSTANTS AND EXPONENTS ARE INDEPENDENT.
Exponent schedules use 1/ceil(q^a), 1/(2^ceil(q^a)), and 1/q!, with positive
rational a; fixed rational epsilons are separate noncofinal calibrations.
Constant programs include 1, 1+ceil(q^r), exponentials, factorials and
powers of the exponent denominator. There is also the fully quantified
family: every sufficiently large q has SOME finite C_q working at all X.
Only finitely sampled constants are fitted for that family. A new q has
no prediction; its required finite constant can be reported as a diagnostic
but must not be turned into a successful extrapolation.

For each fixed q, factorial and exponential constants remain finite and
scale-independent. Their growth as q increases changes the candidate claim,
not the ordinary X exponent. No collection of finite growth templates is
claimed to cover all RH-compatible constant laws. The existential per-level
family removes that unnecessary uniform growth restriction but still has
an unproved all-scale arithmetic premise.

2. SLOWLY GROWING CONSTANTS CAN STILL FORCE ZERO SIMPLICITY.
Suppose a full signed-defect family satisfies, for every small positive
epsilon and every dyadic X,
Delta(X)<=B_epsilon*X^(2+epsilon)*ell(X)^L,
where B_epsilon=O(epsilon^(-kappa)) as epsilon decreases to zero. Dyadic
induction, summing a geometric series and bounding the earlier log weights
by the final one, gives
A(X)<=K*epsilon^(-kappa-1)*X^(2+epsilon)*ell(X)^L.
Here K is independent of epsilon for epsilon in (0,1], after adding A(1).

Set sigma=1/2+h and choose epsilon comparable to h, with epsilon<=h.
Cauchy on each block and a convergent geometric moment sum yield
|1/((sigma+it)*zeta(sigma+it))|
 <=K_t*h^(-(kappa+L+3)/2), 0<h<=h0.
Indeed the energy coefficient contributes h^(-(kappa+1)/2), and summing
ell(2^j)^(L/2)*2^(-j*h/2) contributes h^(-L/2-1). This uses the exact
unfiltered Mertens Mellin identity, already proved in the source stage.
A critical-line zero of multiplicity m has reciprocal magnitude comparable
to h^(-m) at its fixed height; hence m<=(kappa+L+3)/2.
If kappa+L<1, this forces m=1. Unbounded constants alone do NOT remove
that restriction. For epsilon=1/ceil(q^a) and C(q)=O(q^r), kappa=r/a.
Power ceiling schedules have bounded relative gaps near zero, so q can be
chosen with epsilon between two fixed positive multiples of h. The declared
exponential schedules have this property too. For factorial schedules the
nearest smaller epsilon can lose a factor q+1. This factor is subpolynomial
in 1/h, so the same LIMITING pole exponent and integer multiplicity bound
hold; a plain Big-O with that exact exponent is not asserted there. A
subpolynomial constant law similarly uses every positive kappa and then
takes the limiting exponent. Reports label this limiting-power scope.
For constant laws that are powers of the reciprocal epsilon, kappa is that
exact power along the schedule, with the above gap qualification.
This is a conditional CONSEQUENCE of the proposed global bound, not a
claimed fact about actual critical zeros or a disproof of a candidate.

For example, the previous detector's positive powers r=1/6,1/3,1/2,
epsilon=1/q and L=0 STILL impose critical-zero simplicity if proved.
Its positive r allowed unbounded constants, but did not necessarily avoid
the stronger simplicity consequence. The original unrestricted cofinal
B_epsilon criterion has no such imposed constant-growth premise.

3. ANONYMOUS GLOBAL BOUND INFERENCE IN A SEPARATE PROOF QUEUE.
The detector receives integer responses: the minimum prefix-square energy
over binary vectors of length m with mass J. Its declared dictionary is
monomials in J and m, with sparse exact rational fitting. It is not given
an expected polynomial, antiderivative or known coefficient vector. The
selected polynomial is frozen before fresh response dimensions and before
its analytical proof attempt.

For any binary vector with J ones, the ith occupied position is at most
m-J+i. Therefore every prefix through t has at least
max(0,t-(m-J)) ones. Prefix-square energy, summed for t=0,...,m-1,
is at least sum_(k=0)^(J-1) k^2. Equality is attained by placing all ones
in the last J positions. The generic admission backend checks whether the
DETECTED polynomial has boundary P(0,m)=0 and symbolic difference
P(J+1,m)-P(J,m)=J^2. If so induction proves it equals that sum for all
integer J>=0. The exchange argument then proves the global minimum bound.
This is an inferred universal estimate, not an RH-strength prime estimate.

Applied to any boolean divisor-row mask at N=4X, the indicator of omitted
rows in (2X,4X] obeys C*z=z and G_w*z=0. If there are J omitted rows,
z^T H z>=P(J) and z^T S z<=D*J-P(J). This quantitative obstruction
does not assume that omissions are periodic or distributed in either half
of the tail. Any positive tail density fails when D=o(X^2). More generally
J of power alpha fails when 2alpha exceeds the ordinary power of D.
For cofinal epsilon approaching zero and D of power 1+epsilon, omitting
substantially more than square-root many tail rows obstructs this universal
PSD route. Other representations of an arithmetic operator are not excluded.

4. CALIBRATION AND COUNTERFEIT CONTROLS.
The elementary H row-norm is <=8X^2. Thus D=16X^2 gives
Delta<=D*Q/2 at ALL integer X for the actual inverse. Its energy exponent
is THREE. It is the unconditional generic calibration, not the RH bound.
The exact full signed defects and Q counts at new large and irregular
scales are checked against independently factor-derived arithmetic.

A counterfeit vector that agrees with actual mu through every development
coefficient and is one thereafter has cumulative sum n+c eventually.
Its exact full defect is (28X^3-X)/3-2(c^2-c)X once X is beyond the shared
prefix. Thus it fails every cofinal fixed-q family of exponent <3, whatever
finite constant law or logarithmic factor is used. It is not the actual
inverse; complete divisor constraints distinguish it. A finite successful
fit can still miss this future tail, so numeric success is not global proof.

All exact candidate formulas and learned constants are frozen. Analytical
dispositions are written afterwards; no final-case correction of constants
is made. The guided grammar, generic proof methods and inherited identities
are explicit. No global critical arithmetic gap or RH conclusion is claimed.
