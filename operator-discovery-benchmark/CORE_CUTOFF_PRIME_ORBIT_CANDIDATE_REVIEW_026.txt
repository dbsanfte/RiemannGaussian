3. These block groups are antichains, not insertion pairs

If B<q_1<q_2<=2B and q_1 divides q_2, then q_2>=2q_1>2B, a contradiction.
Thus distinct members of EVERY selected block group form an antichain under
divisibility. In particular no interior edge q_2=pq_1 with p prime exists.
The fixed-single-prime2,3,5 calibration groups are necessarily singletons;
that is a structural proof, not a finite empirical result.

Let Q_B be the product of selected primes and
 D_r(B,V)={d|Q_B : B<rd<=V}.
For squarefree r coprime to Q_B the exact group, before its weight/mask, is
 mu(r) sum_(d in D_r)mu(d)C_1(rd,t)
 +mu(r)^2 sum_(d in D_r)C_2(rd,t).                       (3.1)
The set D_r is a truncated antichain of selected-prime products. Distinct
d,d' are incomparable; the ratio d'/d can be less than2 even though a direct
prime insertion leaves the block. Equal-cardinality prime exchanges preserve
mu(d), hence preserve the sign of the odd source factor. Only a difference in
the PARITY of the number of selected primes gives opposite odd factors.
Common cofactor is not the same as opposite signs. For example an early
selected P={2,3} slice can group2r and3r with the same odd factor.

For program4, residual cofactors have only primes>B^(1/3); for program9,
their primes are either<=B^(1/6) or>B^(1/2). These descriptions do not remove
the actual old-source state or imply any sign for C_j. The selected slices
can be compared through PRIME EXCHANGES or multi-prime/cofactor transfers,
not by claiming complete product_(p in P)(I-T_p) cubes inside the block.
Complete cube identities on [1,V] remain valid, but their lower and upper
faces must be restored when forming (B,V].

6. Where the absolute grouping removes potentially essential cancellation

For fixed V and height define T(V,t)=sum_r G_r(V,t). Exactly
 integral sum_r|G_r|-|integral T|
 =integral[sum_r|G_r|-|T|]
  +[integral|T|-|integral T|],                          (6.1)
with measure dt/(2pi) throughout. Both terms are nonnegative. The first
measures SAME-height cross-group cancellation; the second measures signed
height cancellation. Split G_r=O_r+E_r using its odd/even parts. Then
 integral sum_r(|O_r|+|E_r|-|O_r+E_r|)>=0                (6.2)
measures within-group odd/even cancellation. The target retains (6.2) before
absolute values, but discards both cancellations in (6.1).

The saved aggregate odd/even totals establish finite cancellation in the
aggregate. They do not locate it uniquely among (6.1),(6.2), do not describe
unobserved heights, and do not prove that any one mechanism dominates at
all scales. Selected finite peak ratios show no certified factor2 over all
declared blocks. No continuous sign or prime-specific theorem follows.

8. Global telescoping does not pay orbit-resolved absolute variation

At the common window, the exact scalar event identity is
 kappa j_q=(|F'_q|^2-|F'_(q-1)|^2)/|s|^2-W_0(q,t).
Here W_0(q,t) means the complete kappa-weighted ZERO-SECTOR integrand on this
same window, not its unweighted polynomial or its already integrated value.
Summing ALL events telescopes the two endpoint energies. An orbit subset
does not telescope: discrete summation by parts produces energy values at
EVERY change of its membership indicator. Those intermediate full states,
and their head corrections, must remain. Inserting mu(q)=0 events with zero
joined work does not create cancellation; their energy and W_0 changes cancel
exactly before omission.

A bounded endpoint energy never generically bounds partition-resolved total
variation: a bounded nonnegative sequence can oscillate while separate rise
and fall classes accumulate large absolute sums. Therefore current all-prefix
energy/cumulative-work recovery cannot be inverted into (2.1) without a new
variation/correlation premise. Exact divisor-zero rows can annihilate complete
constraints, but partial shell rows require their missing divisors; the earlier
dual-Gram nullspace warning still applies.
