PRIME INSERTION EULER BRIDGE: INDEPENDENT ANALYTICAL AUDIT 056

Disposition and scope
=====================
The conditional bridge is sound for the fixed ordinary-mu concave kernel,
fixed nonempty prime insertion, and fixed nonzero finite scale polynomial
specified below. A whole square-root-plus-fixed-log estimate for that exact
filtered insertion prefix implies ordinary RH. The estimate itself remains
OPEN. No recovery of the original K prefix, converse from RH, numerical bound,
source-table admission, or executable 056 proof checker follows from this
review. All existing files were preserved. No scientific data, tests, source
atoms, or norm observations were evaluated.

The principal new requirement is sharper than the old divergent-branch test:
the insertion makes the branch tend to zero. One must retain an analytic
polynomial plus an O(delta^k) remainder, rather than an undifferentiated O(1).
An analytic amplitude correction is shown below to have bounded kth right
derivative. This supplies precisely the remainder needed for the implication.

Imported facts are the standard meromorphic continuation, Euler product,
functional equation and zero-location properties of ordinary zeta, and the
Euler beta integral and gamma recurrence. Primary references are NIST DLMF
https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.2 , https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.4 ,
https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.10 , https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.12.E3 ,
https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.2 and https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.5.E1 .
The insertion transform, regularized singular-integral calculation and
conditional implication are derived here, not imported upper-bound theorems.

1. Exact source and the unit
===========================
Fix 0<alpha<1 and a fixed squarefree integer R>1, with k=omega(R)>=1.
Let ordinary mu apply to every actual integer divisor, and define

 K(n)=-sum_(d|n)mu(d)*(log d)^alpha, K(1)=0,
 I_alpha=int_0^infinity (1-exp(-u))*u^(-alpha-1)du>0,
 a_R(n)=K(Rn), n>=2,
 A_R(x)=sum_(2<=n<=x)a_R(n), x>=1.

These are unrounded mathematical expressions. Rounded archived coefficients
are finite numerical enclosures, not the analytic source of this bridge.
The source integral is

 K(n)=I_alpha^-1 int_0^infinity
                   product_(p|n)(1-p^-t)*t^(-alpha-1)dt, n>=2.    (1)

Repeated valuations remain in the arithmetic argument but do not repeat
factors. In particular for every integer n>=2,

 0<K(Rn)<=K(n)<=(log n)^alpha.                                  (2)

The upper inequality follows by multiplying the integrand in (1) by the
additional factors for primes dividing R but not n; these lie in (0,1).
This insertion is E_R K(n)=K(Rn), not the defect D_R K. It has NO shared-prime
zero or external coprime mask. Those features of D_R must not be transferred.

If extended to n=1, E_R K(1)=K(R)>0. The n>=2 transform therefore removes
K(R), not the negative -(log R)^alpha unit of the nonempty defect D_R.
Here K(R)=I_alpha^-1 int product_(p|R)(1-p^-t)t^(-alpha-1)dt.

2. Initial full Euler transform
==============================
For Re(s)>1, absolute convergence, positive integral domination and the
prime-power Euler factors give

 F_R(s)=sum_(n>=2)K(Rn)n^-s
       =I_alpha^-1 int_0^infinity
          [U_R(s,t)*zeta(s)/zeta(s+t)-G_R(t)]t^(-alpha-1)dt,      (3)
 U_R(s,t)=product_(p|R)(1-p^-t)/(1-p^(-(s+t))),
 G_R(t)=product_(p|R)(1-p^-t).

For clarity, summing the integral integrand over ALL n>=1 gives
zeta(s)/zeta(s+t)*U_R(s,t). At each p|R its Euler factor is
(1-p^-t)/(1-p^-s); at p not dividing R it is
(1-p^(-(s+t)))/(1-p^-s). Removing n=1 subtracts G_R(t).
Near t=0 both terms in (3) are O(t^k), locally uniformly for Re(s)>1.
At infinity the bracket is bounded and t^(-alpha-1) is integrable.
No reciprocal-zeta Dirichlet series on the critical line is used.

Equivalently, where the integral exists,

 F_R(s)=zeta(s)*H_R(s)-K(R),
 H_R(s)=I_alpha^-1 int_0^infinity
                    U_R(s,t)/zeta(s+t)*t^(-alpha-1)dt.           (4)

At s=1 this H_R is holomorphic in a sufficiently small disk. Reciprocal zeta
has a removable zero at 1, the ray 1+t is zero-free, and the finite prime
denominators do not vanish. Near t=0 the integrand and its locally bounded
s derivatives are dominated by a constant times t^(k-alpha-1); at large t
Euler-product separation gives uniform bounds. The compact middle interval
has a common zero-free neighborhood. Thus the real pole is simple, with

 kappa_R=H_R(1)
 =I_alpha^-1 int U_R(1,t)/zeta(1+t)*t^(-alpha-1)dt >0,            (5)
 F_R(s)=kappa_R/(s-1)+holomorphic near 1.

3. Fixed-height continuation; no globally rightmost zero
=======================================================
If RH is false, reflection supplies a nontrivial zero with real part>1/2.
Choose its nonzero imaginary height gamma. The real interval 0<s<1 has no
zero by the alternating-series identity; Euler-product and line-1 zero
freedom put the chosen zero inside the strip. Isolated zeros are finite on
the compact horizontal segment at that height. Choose a RIGHTMOST zero
rho=b+i gamma at that fixed height, 1/2<b<1, with multiplicity d>=1.
This does not presume any globally rightmost zeta zero.

For s=sigma+i gamma, sigma>b, and t>=0, zeta(s+t) is nonzero. The finite
prime denominators are also nonzero, because Re(s+t)>0 implies
|p^(-(s+t))|<1. Every compact part of this horizontal path has a complex
tube on which (3) is holomorphic: bounded t gives compact separation, large
t gives uniform Euler separation, and small t is uniformly O(t^k).
This continues the initial transform from Re(s)>1 toward rho. Zeros at
other heights require no zero-free half-plane assertion.

For any sufficiently small fixed epsilon>0, the t>=epsilon contribution in
(3) is holomorphic in a full disk about rho. On bounded t this follows from
rightmostness and compactness; at large t from Euler separation. The
-G_R contribution is independent of s and finite, so it is holomorphic too.

4. Elementary principal singular integral for every d and k
==========================================================
Put beta=k-alpha, so k-1<beta<k. Factor zeta(rho+z)=z^d g(z), with g analytic
and g(0)!=0. Take delta>0 small and 0<=t<=epsilon within the factorization
disk. Because the finite Euler denominators are nonzero there,

 U_R(rho+delta,t)=t^k u(delta,t),
 zeta(rho+delta)/zeta(rho+delta+t)
       =[delta/(delta+t)]^d*g(delta)/g(delta+t),
 u(0,0)=u_0=product_(p|R)log(p)/(1-p^-rho)!=0.                 (6)

The principal model is

 J_(beta,d)(delta)=delta^d int_0^epsilon
                         t^(beta-1)/(delta+t)^d dt.

It has the exact asymptotic

 J_(beta,d)(delta)=Q_(k-1)(delta)+B_(beta,d)*delta^beta
                       +O(delta^k),                          (7)
 B_(beta,d)=Gamma(beta)*Gamma(d-beta)/Gamma(d)!=0,

where Q has degree<=k-1. In particular Q=0 when d>=k.

For d>=k, substitute t=delta*v. The integral extended to infinity is
Euler's beta integral, since beta>0 and d-beta>0. Its omitted tail produces
O(delta^d), hence O(delta^k). This proves (7) in that case.

For d<k let r=k-d>=1 and c_l=(-1)^l binom(d+l-1,l). Subtract the first r
large-v terms. The finite coefficient is the absolutely convergent integral

 B_(beta,d)=int_0^infinity v^(beta-1)
                [(1+v)^(-d)-sum_(l=0)^(r-1)c_l*v^(-d-l)]dv.    (8)

At zero, the strongest subtracted power has exponent -alpha>-1; at infinity
the Taylor remainder has exponent -alpha-1<-1. Integrating the subtractions
up to epsilon/delta creates exactly the analytic terms

 sum_(l=0)^(r-1) c_l*epsilon^(beta-d-l)/(beta-d-l)*delta^(d+l).

All their degrees are<=k-1. The remaining tail is O(delta^k), proving (7).
The integrand in brackets in (8) has strict sign (-1)^r for all v>0:
apply the integral Taylor remainder of (1+z)^(-d) at z=1/v. Thus the
coefficient is nonzero even without any gamma identity.

For completeness its gamma value is not a formal use of a divergent beta
integral. Regularize the tail at v=1 by subtracting the r powers and adding
sum c_l/(d+l-beta). The result is analytic for 0<Re(beta)<d+r, excluding
the integer poles d,...,d+r-1, and equals the ordinary beta integral when
0<Re(beta)<d. The Euler beta recurrence therefore continues to this
regularized expression. Iterating it from d to k gives

 B_(beta,d)=product_(j=0)^(r-1)(d+j)/(d+j-beta)*B(beta,k)
           =Gamma(beta)*Gamma(d-beta)/Gamma(d).

For beta=k-alpha the regularized tail equals (8), including the integrable
subtractions on (0,1). No denominator is zero. Each recurrence factor is
negative in the d<k case, agreeing with the strict sign above.

5. Why the variable amplitude pays the sharper remainder
=======================================================
In the small-t integral of (3), write

 A(delta,t)=u(delta,t)*g(delta)/g(delta+t)
          =u_0+delta*B(delta,t)+t*C(delta,t),                   (9)

where B,C are analytic with all derivatives bounded on a smaller closed
bidisk. Its constant term contributes u_0*J_(beta,d). The remaining integral
has bounded kth derivative along delta>0, uniformly as delta decreases to0.
Here is an explicit bound that avoids an unsupported O(1) remainder.

After k differentiations and the product rule, the delta*B term is a finite
sum bounded by constants times

 int_0^epsilon t^(beta-1)
          delta^(d+1-i)/(delta+t)^(d+j-i) dt,
 0<=j<=k, 0<=i<=min(j,d+1),

and the t*C term by the same expression with t^beta and delta^(d-i),
0<=i<=min(j,d). Derivatives falling on B,C only reduce j. Substitute
 t=delta*v. The respective scale factors are delta^(beta+1-j), positive
powers because beta+1-k=1-alpha>0. If the v integral grows at its upper
endpoint, its growth instead leaves delta^(d+1-i) or delta^(d-i), both
nonnegative powers. The borderline logarithmic exponent cannot occur:
beta is noninteger and all other exponents are integers. Hence each term
is uniformly bounded. Differentiation is legitimate for each delta>0 by
compact domination. A bounded kth derivative yields finite lower right
derivative limits and a Taylor polynomial of degree<=k-1 with remainder
O(delta^k), by repeated integration along the real segment.

Combining (7), (9), the analytic large-t part and the unit contribution gives

 F_R(rho+delta)=Q_(k-1)(delta)+C_R*delta^(k-alpha)+O(delta^k),
 C_R=(u_0/I_alpha)*Gamma(k-alpha)*Gamma(d-k+alpha)/Gamma(d)!=0.   (10)

Q here is a generally complex polynomial, not a known zero polynomial.
The nonzero coefficient includes every finite multiplicity d, including
d<k. Its sign for d<k is accounted for by Gamma(d-k+alpha); there is no
extra (-1)^d factor. Formula (10) is a positive-real approach asymptotic,
which is sufficient for the holomorphy contradiction below.

6. Whole finite scale filters and the head terms
===============================================
Fix an integer q>=2 and a NONZERO finite polynomial P(z)=sum_(j=0)^J c_j z^j,
with fixed real coefficients, and define

 T_P(x)=sum_(j=0)^J c_j A_R(q^j*x), x>=1.

A fixed division by alpha is harmless. The mean-neutral compatibility
condition is P(q)=0; P(1)=0 is optional. For Re(s)>1 exact partial summation
and changes of variables give

 int_1^infinity T_P(x)x^(-s-1)dx
  =P(q^s)*F_R(s)/s-E_P(s),                                   (11)
 E_P(s)=sum_j c_j*q^(js)*int_1^(q^j) A_R(y)y^(-s-1)dy.

E_P is entire. This retains the n>=2 convention and every finite head.
Equation (5) shows P(q)=0 cancels the actual real pole; if P(q)!=0, an
all-epsilon square-root premise would already contradict that positive
pole. There is no inferred or fitted mean. No condition on P(1) is needed
for the off-critical argument.

Suppose P(q^s)/s has a zero of order h>=0 at nonreal rho. This order is finite:
P is nonzero, q^s is locally invertible, and rho!=0. Write its leading
coefficient a_h!=0. Multiplying (10), and subtracting the analytic product
with Q and the entire head E_P, gives a nonzero term

 a_h*C_R*delta^(k+h-alpha)+O(delta^(k+h)).                    (12)

Higher multiplier terms give delta^(k+h+1-alpha)=o(delta^(k+h)). If the
transform in (11) were holomorphic at rho, its Taylor coefficients through
degree k+h-1, after that analytic subtraction, would have to vanish by
successive division along delta>0. It would then be O(delta^(k+h)), which
contradicts (12). A finite polynomial zero cannot remove this fractional
branch. Annulus-root exclusions and simple-zero assumptions are unnecessary.

7. Integer-to-real extension and the conditional conclusion
==========================================================
Assume, as the unpaid whole-source premise, that for fixed finite C and
fixed B>=0,

 |T_P(N)|<=C*sqrt(N)*(log(e*N))^B for every integer N>=2.       (13)

For x in [N,N+1), A_R(q^j*x)-A_R(q^j*N) contains at most q^j-1 terms.
Equation (2) bounds each by (log(q^j*x))^alpha. Thus

 |T_P(x)-T_P(N)|<=sum_j |c_j|*(q^j-1)*(log(q^j*x))^alpha,

with the j=0 contribution zero. This is a fixed O((log(e*x))^alpha) error.
Consequently (13) supplies an all-epsilon bound on the whole real T_P:
for every epsilon>0, |T_P(x)|<=C_epsilon*x^(1/2+epsilon).
The finite initial interval is harmless. Its Mellin integral converges
absolutely and locally uniformly, with all local s derivatives, throughout
Re(s)>1/2. It is therefore holomorphic there. Uniqueness of continuation from
Re(s)>1 along the tubes in section3 makes it the same transform as (11).
The contradiction (12) excludes every off-critical right-hand zero. Zeta's
reflection then yields ordinary RH.

Paid result: (13) for one fixed exact nonempty inserted source and one fixed
nonzero mean-neutral scale polynomial implies RH. It does not require a
bound on the original K prefix. It does require the exact ordinary-mu kernel
on all integers and the full signed filtered prefix. Positivity, finite
sample success, jet nullspaces, and small-t suppression pay none of (13).
The assertion remains valid for any fixed finite logarithmic power; no
uniformity over R, alpha, q, P, or their changing complexities is claimed.

8. Audit caveats for detector and publication
============================================
- E_R and D_R are different. The new E_R words suppress the transform's
  small-t numerator by k factors but remain nonzero on shared-prime n.
- The removed unit is K(R), not the defect's -(log R)^alpha. Finite heads
  in (11) and optional P(1) constants remain in the complete transform.
- The branch tends to zero for k>=1. O(1) control alone cannot exclude a
  holomorphic continuation; the polynomial and O(delta^k) argument above
  is essential, including arbitrary zero multiplicity.
- Repeated insertion of the same prime is idempotent on this radical source;
  it does not increase k. R is fixed and squarefree in this theorem.
- The real pole is an exact positive kappa_R. A mean-neutral grammar must
  enforce P(q)=0, or else its proposed square-root premise is impossible.
- Neither first-jet nullspace membership nor source small-t suppression
  establishes the whole signed bound. No quantitative inversion from this
  filtered insertion to the old uninserted source is supplied.
- The theorem is separately reviewed analytic mathematics. It is not an
  executable 056 admission, formal proof, actual all-scale estimate, RH
  proof, or claimed RH converse. No scientific data or old tests were used.
