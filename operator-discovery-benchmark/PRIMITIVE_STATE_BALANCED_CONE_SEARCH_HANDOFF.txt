Purpose and method

Publication020 reduced the sum of cone defects by about 99%, but its matrix
penalty increased. This experiment instead minimizes the worst of three
normalized penalties: the scalar nonnegativity defect, the PSD defect of C,
and the PSD defect of H=P+4C. Each penalty is half the squared distance to
its cone. Its fixed normalizer is the maximum of its initial numerical value
and 2^-40. The common objective is max(p_scalar/b_scalar, p_C/b_C, p_H/b_H).

Positive normalizers preserve the zero set of the exact cone-distance problem.
A decreasing common objective does not imply each raw penalty decreases at
every step. Every component is retained in the trace and publication report.
Numerical penalties and gradients are proposal data, never exact cone proofs.

The proximal model minimizes max_i(r_i+g_i dot d)+||d||^2/(2alpha).
Its three-variable simplex dual is searched over three vertices, three edges
and the interior. Numerical weights are quantized to a feasible 2^-240 dyadic
simplex grid and re-scored. Each halved step recomputes the model. Exported
directions, face decisions, model gaps and KKT residuals are independently
checked as finite-record calculations. No true-gradient or QP certificate is
claimed. The mandatory numerical gap tolerance is 1e-40 times a declared scale
with unit floor. Numerical stationarity is not an infeasibility certificate.

The initial step is the reciprocal of an eight-step weighted curvature power
estimate, clamped to 2^-900..2^900. Up to 96 iterations use at most three trials
and the Armijo condition F_new<=F_old-||d||^2/(8alpha). Accepted steps can only
retain or shrink the step size. This may be conservative when one block
dominates the curvature estimate. There is no convergence guarantee from a
finite trial or iteration limit. Near-parallel Gram subtraction can return
UNKNOWN under the declared numerical consistency policy.

Scientific status

Even a successful finite endpoint would leave the universal nonconstant
arithmetic identity for that fixed candidate unresolved. Publication019's
symbolic source identities do not prove that aggregate identity. Uniform
arithmetic bounds and the ordinary-zeta recovery premises also require proofs.
Finite numerical evidence alone cannot establish signed control at every
scale or RH. No global-bound, RH or infeasibility flag is promoted here.

The accompanying primitive_state_uniform_identity_probe_design_v1.txt specifies
a future way to reject a fixed candidate's nonconstant formal identity at a
fresh scale, using an exact evaluation or modular witness without assembling
another full SDP. It subtracts the all-zero formal evaluation to remove the
scalar budget, retains every source family and the signed parent storage term,
and requires independent replay. A nonzero witness refutes only that candidate's
formal identity; zero means only that the probe did not refute it. This is a
design, with no real candidate evaluated and no measured cost claim. It does
not discharge the universal identity or scalar inequality obligations.
