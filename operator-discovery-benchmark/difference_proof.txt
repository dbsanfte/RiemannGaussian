ORDINARY-ROOT-SENSITIVE QUADRATIC INDEX DIFFERENCES

Status: written universal proofs with independent exact and Arb witnesses;
not machine formalization. This is a mathematically verified candidate
application, not a verified historically new primitive or an RH proof.

1. Correct type and the ordinary Dirichlet root relation

Let D be ordinary Dirichlet convolution, with unit delta_1. LOG_D and
EXP_D are its coefficient-local normalized logarithm and exponential.
For an augmentation sequence h, h(1)=0, define the pushforward

  (E_a h)(N)=sum_(n>=2,n(n+a)=N) h(n),  a=0,1,... .

The map n -> n(n+a) is strictly increasing and exceeds n for n>=2.
E_a acts only on the augmentation ideal; it does not send the unit
to the index 1+a. All sums and all subsequent coefficient computations
are finite. Define the m-th forward-difference combination

  A_m=sum_(a=0)^m (-1)^(m-a) binomial(m,a) E_a,  m>=1,
  P_(m,k,beta)(f)=EXP_D(beta*(I+A_m^k)*LOG_D(f)), k>=1, f(1)=1.

These are ordinary D roots, not roots in a transported product:

  P_alpha(f) D P_beta(f)=P_(alpha+beta)(f),
  P_1(f D g)=P_1(f) D P_1(g),
  P_beta(f)=P_1(f^(D beta))=P_1(f)^(D beta).

The equations follow by taking LOG_D and using linearity of I+A_m^k.
Each A_m application sends support n>=b to indices >=b^2. Hence A_m^k
sends it to indices >=b^(2^k). The formal inverse is coefficient local:

  P_1^-1 = EXP_D * [sum_(j>=0)(-1)^j A_m^(k*j)] * LOG_D.

The stars in this displayed composition mean composition of maps, not
convolution of their scalar values. At a fixed cutoff all sufficiently
late terms vanish. Generic exact checks use unrelated signed rational
inputs, ordinary divisor products and fractional roots. A separate sparse
control through 10^8 requires two nonzero inverse correction powers;
stopping after the first is explicitly rejected. The independent checker
also solves the inverse by increasing-index triangular elimination.

E_0 is the classical square-index pushforward: its scalar Dirichlet
action is F(s)->F(2s), and its log-zeta input becomes LOG(zeta(2s)).
E_a for a>0 need not be multiplicative as an index map. We do not call
it a D algebra homomorphism merely because the conjugated P_1 is a
normalized unit-group homomorphism.

For zeta input at m=2,k=2,beta=1, the coefficients at 20 and 45 are
-1 and 1. Swapping prime labels 2 and 3 sends 20 to 45 but fixes u, so
even this specific zeta output is not prime-relabel invariant. The first
correction support begins at 16; its coefficient at 20 is -2 and none of
its supported divisors contributes at 45. The ordinary correction EXP
therefore gives these values exactly. The coefficients at 4 and 5 are
both 1 but the coefficient at 20 is -1, rejecting multiplicativity
despite the ordinary unit-group laws.

2. Known-operator calibration and the recovered compound law

The integer-weight grammar fixes four quadratic nodes a=0,1,2,3 and
primitive weights with a positive last nonzero entry. It scores 1120
vectors. Vanishing zeroth and first moments gives six training fits;
the fresh second-moment gate leaves (-1,3,-3,1), the classical third
forward difference. Binomial weights are prior art, not a new primitive.
The identities

  sum_a (-1)^(m-a) binomial(m,a) a^j=0 for j<m,
  sum_a (-1)^(m-a) binomial(m,a) a^m=m!

follow by taking m differences of a polynomial. They also follow from
the classical Stirling formula in NIST DLMF 26.8.6.

The compound search forms quadratic image words of lengths k. It does
not identify a cancellation order just by naming a finite difference.
Exact rational Taylor profiles at thirteen (m,k) pairs and three fresh
positive parameters recover the order and leading coefficient. The
1890-program order grammar gives three training fits and one survivor;
the 80-program leading recurrence grammar gives four fits and one
survivor. Independent interpolation and separate LOG/EXP recursions
recompute every decision and every coefficient.

3. Universal cancellation order under nonlinear index iteration

For a scalar test kernel define

  B_m w(n)=sum_(a=0)^m (-1)^(m-a)binomial(m,a) w(n(n+a)),
  W_(m,k)(n,s)=B_m^k[n^-s].

This is the dual of A_m^k; the full word set is closed under reversal,
so the scalar kernel agrees with the coefficient pushforward. Set d=2^k.
We prove

  W_(m,k)(n,s)=n^(-d*s-r_k) G_(m,k)(1/n,s),
  r_k=m*(2^k-1),
  c_(m,k)(s)=G_(m,k)(0,s),
  c_(m,0)=1,
  c_(m,k+1)=(-1)^m*(2^k*s+r_k)_m*c_(m,k).

Here (x)_m=x(x+1)...(x+m-1), and G is jointly analytic near u=0
and for every complex s. Start with G_0=1 and r_0=0. If the statement
holds at k, put t=2^k*s+r_k and u=1/n. Then the next raw bracket is

  sum_a w_a (1+a*u)^(-t) G_k(u^2/(1+a*u),s).

Expand G_k in powers u^(2j)(1+a*u)^(-j). Every coefficient of total
degree ell<m is a polynomial in a of degree at most ell, so the
moment identities annihilate it. At degree m only j=0 contributes:
all j>0 require an a-power below m and also vanish. The coefficient
is (-1)^m*(t)_m*G_k(0,s). Division of the bracket by u^m gives an
analytic G_(k+1), with r_(k+1)=2*r_k+m. This proves both laws.

The order is at least r_k for every s, and exactly r_k except at the
finitely many roots of c_(m,k). It is not exact at all complex s:
s=0 annihilates the full kernel identically. The verifier retains that
negative control. No extrapolation from finite profiles is needed.

4. A rigorous grouped continuation into negative half-planes

For zeta input u(n)=1 put g=LOG_D(u), so g(n)=1/r for n=p^r and
zero otherwise. Initially define

  Delta_(m,k)(s)=sum_(n>=2) g(n) W_(m,k)(n,s).

For Re(s)>1/d every word's series converges absolutely. The cancellation
law extends the grouped sum further. On compact sets of s, G is bounded
near u=0. Therefore |W(n,s)|<=C*n^(-d*Re(s)-r_k) for large n.
Since 0<=g(n)<=1, absolute/local-uniform convergence after grouping holds
whenever d*Re(s)+r_k>1, or

  Re(s)>eta_(m,k)=-m+(m+1)/2^k.

Thus Delta is holomorphic on this half-plane. This is a sufficient domain,
not a claim of its maximal possible continuation. The selected instance
m=2,k=2 has d=4,r=6,eta=-5/4. The trivial-zero control m=6,k=1 has
d=2,r=6,eta=-5/2. In particular both domains cover the entire critical strip.

Grouping is essential. The ordinary coefficient series of A_m^k(g)
has coefficient (-1)^(m*k)/r at p^(d*r) for every prime p>m. Indeed a
positive shift a<=m cannot keep an intermediate power of such a prime
a prime power, whereas the all-zero word maps p^r to p^(d*r). Therefore
its individual terms do not tend to zero on Re(s)<=0. Negative-half-plane
continuation above is not absolute convergence, or even ordinary natural-
order convergence, of that coefficient Dirichlet series. This distinction
remains an explicit rejection gate in the detector.

5. Quantitative bounds and a native-zeta scalar representation

For a word let Phi_w(n) be its quadratic image, and write
Phi_w(n)=n^d H_w(1/n), H_w(0)=1. Let

  h(u,s)=sum_w weight(w)*H_w(u)^(-s)=sum_(j>=r_k) c_j(s) u^j.

Choose R=1/[2(m+1)]. Starting at |u|<=R, each reciprocal update is
u->u^2/(1+a*u), with a<=m; it stays inside this disk. The logarithm of
the normalized image is a sum with powers 2 of logs(1+a*u), so its
magnitude is at most (d-1)log 2. The word weights have total absolute
mass 2^(m*k). Consequently

  |c_j(s)|<=M(s)*R^-j, M(s)=2^(m*k+|s|*(d-1)).

After treating a finite input prefix n<=N0=4(m+1), the Taylor expansion
converges absolutely at every remaining 1/n. Interchange of the two
absolutely convergent sums gives the scalar identity

  Delta(s)=sum_(n=2)^N0 g(n)W(n,s)
    +sum_(j>=r_k)c_j(s) [LOG(zeta(d*s+j))
                          -sum_(n=2)^N0 g(n)n^(-d*s-j)].

LOG here is the normalized Euler logarithm on Re(d*s+j)>1. This entire
formula is valid on Re(s)>eta. A finite prefix must be removed when
the closest normalized polynomial zero prevents direct expansion at n=2.
The implemented arguments have minimum real part near two or larger,
where the native principal log equals the normalized Euler log because
|LOG(zeta)|<=log(zeta(Re z))<pi.

For an s compact set with minimum real part sigma, truncation after j=J
has tail at most

  M*(N0+1)^(-d*sigma) * q^(J+1)/(1-q)
       * (1+(N0+1)/(d*sigma+J)), q=1/[R*(N0+1)]<1.

The independent version uses R=1/[3(m+1)], a different finite prefix and
a sharper bound 2^(m*k)*(1-m*R)^(-|s|*(d-1)). It computes exact polynomial
logarithms by Newton interpolation, then a separate exponential recurrence.
The production uses native polynomial LOG/EXP. Both require the known
leading coefficient as well as all vanished lower coefficients.

For a direct grouped input tail after N, sum the first Taylor degrees
r<=j<=J using

  |c_j|*N^(1-d*sigma-j)/(d*sigma+j-1),

then bound the remainder by

  M*R^(-J-1)/(1-1/(R*N))
    *N^(-d*sigma-J)/(d*sigma+J).

These bounds certify two negative-real-part grouped comparisons. Their
ordinary coefficient series is not labelled convergent there.

6. Zero-free correction, original zeta zeros and ordinary roots

On Re(s)>1 the formal Dirichlet identity is

  H_(m,k,beta)(s)=zeta(s)^beta * C_(m,k,beta)(s),
  C_(m,k,beta)=exp(beta*Delta_(m,k)).

Delta is holomorphic on Re(s)>eta, so C is holomorphic and never zero
throughout that half-plane, for every complex beta. At beta=1 this
continues H meromorphically there and preserves every zeta zero and
its multiplicity exactly; its only inherited pole is zeta's pole at 1.
Fractional beta retains ordinary-zeta root monodromy, unlike the earlier
factorial-normalized subset transfer. For a zero of multiplicity ell,
the phase is exp(2*pi*i*ell*beta); a nonzero holomorphic C cannot remove it.

For every m,k the correction domain includes Re(s)>0. Thus RH is
equivalent to single-valued holomorphic continuation to Re(s)>1/2 of
every pole-cancelled dyadic root (s-1)^(1/2^j) H_(m,k,1/2^j), j>=1.
The usual reflection symmetry converts no zeros to the right of the
critical line into RH. This is a root reformulation, not a proof of the
required continuation or a new zero-free estimate for zeta.

7. Positive-domain full-series bounds and actual comparisons

For real beta in [0,1], the ordinary zeta-power coefficients d_beta(n)
lie between zero and one. Write the correction's formal coefficients
as r_beta. If L bounds sum_n |A_m^k(g)(n)|/n, then
sum_n |r_beta(n)|/n<=exp(beta*L) by the convolution exponential majorant.
For sigma>1 the output tail after N is therefore at most

  (1+1/(sigma-1))*N^(1-sigma)*exp(beta*L).

To see this, expand H=d_beta D r_beta and bound the inner tail
sum_(a>N/b) a^-sigma by (1+1/(sigma-1))*(N/b)^(1-sigma).
The bound is also valid when N/b<1. The source computes L from a finite
word-weighted prime-power sum plus an integral bound; the independent
version uses a longer prefix and a normalized log(zeta(4)) tail.

Six fresh complex cases compare exact rational arithmetic output through
4096 against the native shifted-log-zeta identity with explicit infinite
tails. The independent output reaches 6144 and uses a distinct reference:
one native zeta root times the direct grouped prime-power correction,
whose remainder uses exact leading Taylor coefficients and Cauchy bounds.
It rejects a smaller coefficient-2 perturbation at every point.

8. Two actual nontrivial-zero disks and one actual trivial-zero disk

The existing authoritative certificates isolate simple critical-line
zeros near heights 14.134725 and 21.022040. New tests certify C nonzero
across both full disks for beta=1/2,1/4,1. Fractional cases have nontrivial
monodromy, and integer cases correctly have trivial phase. Source and
independent root controls reuse those unchanged zero certificates.
The recorded phase bounds apply to |(exp(2*pi*i*beta)-1)*C_beta|.
The full H jump also includes its zeta^beta factor and therefore has no
strictly positive uniform modulus bound at the zero itself.

For a stable source enclosure, native Delta is evaluated at the centre
and extended over the rectangle by a derivative bound. For m=2,k=2,
Phi_w(n)>=n^4 and log Phi_w(n)<=4log n+3log 2. Hence on real part >=sigma,

  |Delta'|<=16*[4*(-zeta'(4sigma)/zeta(4sigma))
                        +3log 2*log(zeta(4sigma))], sigma>1/4.

Multiply this by sqrt(2) times the disk radius to enclose the rectangle.
The independent method instead sums grouped prime-power kernels directly
on the rectangle, with changed input cutoffs and exact Taylor tails.

For the additional zero at s=-2, use m=6,k=1. Native zeta(-2)=0, and
its derivative is nonzero. A Taylor/Rouche bound counts one simple zero
in the radius-1/10000 disk. Independently, the functional equation gives
zeta'(-2)=-zeta(3)/(4*pi^2), and a larger-circle Cauchy bound repeats the
count. This is a classical trivial zero used as a new calibration.

At any negative integer s=-ell with m>ell, the m-th difference of
(n(n+a))^ell, as a polynomial in a, is exactly zero. Thus Delta(-ell)=0
and C_beta(-ell)=1 when that point lies in the proved continuation domain.
For m=6,k=1 this applies at -2. At beta=1 the transformed function
therefore also has derivative zeta'(-2), as well as the same simple zero.

An exact repeated-integral identity provides a stable whole-disk bound:

  W_(m,1)(n,s)=(-1)^m (s)_m
       *integral_[0,1]^m n^-s (n+t_1+...+t_m)^(-s-m) dt.

If sigma+m>0 and 2sigma+m>1, it implies
|Delta|<=(|(s)_m|)*log(zeta(2sigma+m)).
The rising factor is small near -2 for m=6. The independent verifier
uses explicit distances from the six factors and the looser bound
zeta(2sigma+m)-1. Both certify nonzero C over the entire crossing disk;
neither infers this from a finite raw sum with catastrophic cancellation.

9. Scope, historical status and reproducibility

Finite differences, square-index dilation, linear maps of augmentation
sequences and formal arithmetic LOG/EXP are known building blocks.
No exact publication match for this particular quadratic-iteration
application, cancellation/domain hierarchy and negative-integer behavior
has been established in the reviewed sources. This leaves originality
unresolved; it does not prove that the operator was previously unknown.
The candidate has a substantive verified arithmetic/domain consequence
and an ordinary-zero bridge, but no new estimate establishing RH.
