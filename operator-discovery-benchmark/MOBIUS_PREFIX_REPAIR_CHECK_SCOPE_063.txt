The eight fill rules
--------------------
Let R=product(primes) and E(d) be each supplied residual row on1..H. EVERY
coprime row is retained, including d=1 and squareful coprime rows whose ordinary
mu is zero. At a noncoprime row d:
 raw: retain the supplied original E(d);
 zero: use0;
 left/right: use E(a) or E(b) at nearest coprime nodes a<d<b;
 linear: use (b-d)E(a)/(b-a)+(d-a)E(b)/(b-a);
 reciprocal_linear: use a(b-d)E(a)/(d(b-a))+b(d-a)E(b)/(d(b-a));
 core: strip ALL registered-prime factors from d, obtaining d0, and use E(d0);
 core_inverse: use E(d0)/(d/d0).
Node1 is always coprime and available on the left. The ONLY virtual node is
H+1 on the right, with value0. It is used whether or not H+1 is coprime as an
ordinary integer; its role is the boundary of the finite vector. The reciprocal
weights are exactly interpolation in1/d, not interpolation in d. All operators
are linear in the input row and preserve its unit value. The filled arrays are
reconstructed independently and must match every supplied entry exactly.

Independent forward floor-matrix verification
---------------------------------------------
For supplied coefficients c_j, j1..H, calculate suffix sums
 A_j=sum_(l=j..H)c_l, A_(H+1)=0.
Then the exact forward floor action is
 sum_(j=1..H)c_j floor(j/d)
 =sum_(m<=H/d)sum_(j>=md)c_j
 =sum_(m<=H/d)A_(md).
The checker replays this at EVERY d<=H, not just coprime or squarefree d.
It uses no ordinary-Mobius inverse. Every d>H is automatically zero because
all floor horizons are<=H. This costs O(H log H) bounded rational additions.
The complete coefficient L1 at both rows is recomputed and matched exactly.

The matrix has diagonal1 and is upper triangular under descending indices,
so a full filled vector has a unique coefficient vector. To understand this
inverse analytically (not as a verification oracle), ordinary finite divisor
inversion gives
 A_j=sum_(m<=H/j)mu(m)*filled(jm), c_j=A_j-A_(j+1).
Indeed sum_(m<=H/d)A_(md)=filled(d) by sum_(l|n)mu(l)=1[n=1]. All sums are
finite; no Dirichlet-series convergence is presumed. The unit row proves
 sum_j j*c_j=filled(1)=E(1).
Thus repairing a source with nonzero unit generally changes a long zero first
moment. A zero-moment label cannot be retained without pricing this drift.

Safe universal norm bound, and its limited benefit
--------------------------------------------------
The exact inverse formula yields
 ||c||_1<=2*sum_j|A_j|
  <=2*sum_(d<=H)tau(d)*|filled(d)|
  <=2*H*(1+log H)*||filled||_infinity.
The last step is the elementary identity sum_(d<=H)tau(d)=sum_(a<=H)floor(H/a)
and the harmonic upper bound sum_(a<=H)1/a<=1+log H. This is an unconditional
generic finite envelope, not a proved sup norm for the actual source residual.
The numerical L1 cost is exact; no unknown analytical sup bound is fitted.
For each positive interpolation rule the noncoprime fill is a convex combination
of retained values (or0), and core/core_inverse only sample retained values
with a factor<=1. Those elementary facts do not pay the residual as N grows.

Masked-source invariance and full recovery
----------------------------------------
By complete mu_M*One convolution,
 sum_(j<=H)c_j Psi_M(j)=sum_(d<=H)mu_M(d)*filled(d)
                    =sum_(d<=H,gcd(d,R)=1)mu(d)*E(d).
Every noncoprime fill is free for this masked-source identity, but it can change
coefficient cancellation and cost. The original unit and all coprime residual
rows are fixed. Noncoprime freedom does NOT change the arithmetic signed
remainder, establish a lower bound for all possible fills, or prove optimality.

When merged into a long completion, the short repair is zero on d>H and cancels
the original short residual on all coprime d<=H. Merging equal horizons can
cause signed coefficient cancellation. The root must recompute the ENTIRE
coefficient vector/cost, then use062 to verify the ORIGINAL source at each new
N,y, including every original and added tail horizon. This checker intentionally
does not infer that binding from an arbitrary supplied residual array.

Uniform N growth, complete joined prefix growth,061 core recovery and RH all
remain OPEN. Restoring a generic short inverse can restore a current-scale
Mertens interval coefficient; a small horizon does not imply a small arithmetic
coefficient. Repair can also cost more than leaving the residual priced in062.
Neither the finite array identity nor the positive envelope proves a uniform
square-root/polylog repair rate. Such a theorem remains a separate all-N,
all-prefix, whole-joined-source obligation.
