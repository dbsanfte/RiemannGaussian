RIGHT-LEG PRIME PREFIX: EXACT RECOVERY KERNELS AND LOCAL INVERSE COST

Scope and conclusion
--------------------
This new analytical memo reviews the exact selected prime-slide word 13391:
sum_{e=0}^6 T_(q^e)^right. I read its fixed program and algebraic/bound
metadata in prime-slide-results/pilot-001/selected.json, the ordered-prime
obligation, the prior pairing review, and the new post-analysis module
prime_slide_recovery.py. No source arithmetic, numerical
experiment, tests, hashes, new search or frozen edits were performed.

Its proved complete normalized bilinear auxiliary is
O(X^(-1/4)*(1+log(2X))^2) at theta=3/4. This statement concerns the
complete selected auxiliary; it does not recover the original covariance.

The exact integrated recovery has a useful new distinction. The e=1 right
lift is inside the coefficient prefix and identifies its nonsmooth,
non-prime-power sector. Every e>=2 lift is outside that prefix. The e=2
B component tends to +1 locally and supplies an order-one outside term.
Consequently the outside complement is not a negligible prime-power tail.
Reexpressing it by local Euler laws gives an exact signed bank decomposition,
but substituting this into recovery cancels the small filtered expression
and returns the original largest-prime relation. It supplies no new bound.

The local Euler rows have a two-root recurrence, verified below. Any finite
family of filters whose every channel multiplier has positive valuation at
t=0 cannot uniformly recover an unfiltered nonzero channel projection by
bounded local linear coefficients. Quantitative recovery loses inverse
prime powers. This is a local class obstruction, not an impossibility of
global signed compensation, different source identities, or a closed
recurrence with additional paid terms.

1. Complete original source and overlap domain
----------------------------------------------
Use actual mu, u=1/3, v=2/3 and beta=9/2. Set
  U=a_u=mu*(n^(-u)), V=a_v=mu*(n^(-v)), B=U*V,
  p=beta*(U-delta)*(V-delta), a=p-d,
where d is the COMPLETE same-prime prime-power channel. Thus a(1)=0 and
a(p^r)=0 for all primes p and r>=1. For h>1 which is not a prime power,
  a(h)=beta*(B(h)-U(h)-V(h)).                         (1.1)
This non-prime-power mask is mandatory when expanding a into channels.
Every bank cofactor is k>=2. Its transfer sequences are
  z0(k)=beta*(2B(k)-U(k)-V(k)),
  zu(k)=beta*(B(k)-U(k)), zv(k)=beta*(B(k)-V(k)),
with all three zero at k=1.

Let 1/2<=theta<1 and tau(q)=ceil(q^(1/theta)). Put
  alpha(h)=max(h,tau(Pplus(h))).
For integer dyadic X>=1 the complete ordered overlap is
  I_X(h;q,k)=max(0,min(2X,tau(q))-max(X,alpha(h),qk)). (1.2)
The high-smooth version also places h^2 in the upper minimum. This is
the exact integer count of X<=n<2X with h<=n, Pplus(h)<=n^theta<q,
qk<=n, and, in the high-smooth version, n<h^2. All powers in these
domain statements denote exact inequalities, not rounded diagnostics.

A positive count proves k<q and q>Pplus(h); hence q is coprime to both
h and k. It does not prove gcd(h,k)=1. Every shared-prime sector remains.
Use either full or high-smooth I consistently throughout what follows.
Write H(n) for the corresponding smooth a-prefix (with h>sqrt(n) in
the high-smooth case), and define
  Q_e(X)=X^(-2)*sum_{h,q,k} a(h) z0(q^e k) I_X(h;q,k),
  Q_C6(X)=sum_{e=0}^6 Q_e(X).                        (1.3)
All sums are finite on their original domain h,k>=2, q prime. The
baseline is Q_0=X^(-2)*sum_{X<=n<2X} H(n)L0(n).
This is one signed bilinear covariance, not the square of a prefix.
The full K,-L0,Lu,-main energy and all other mixed terms stay required.

2. Exact selected local filter and its proved auxiliary bound
-------------------------------------------------------------
Set t=q^(-1/3). The local series in an exponent variable z are
  U_q(z)=(1-z)/(1-tz),
  V_q(z)=(1-z)/(1-t^2 z),
  B_q(z)=(1-z)^2/((1-tz)(1-t^2 z)).
For a fresh q the cumulative local rows through exponent m>=1 are
  C_m^U=t^m, C_m^V=t^(2m),
  C_m^B=-t^(m-1)+t^(2m-1)+t^(2m).                    (2.1)
At m=0 all three rows are one. Thus selected m=6 has
  F_B=-t^5+t^11+t^12,
  F_U=t^6, F_V=t^12.                                 (2.2)
In particular for k>=2 on the original coprime domain,
  sum_{e=0}^6 z0(q^e k)
       =beta*(2F_B B(k)-t^6 U(k)-t^12 V(k)).           (2.3)
The common positive valuation is five, i.e. prime weight 5/3. The
local polynomial l1 norms are 3,1,1. Together with the fixed original
tensor projection, the archived constant is C=486. The earlier complete
support proof gives explicitly
  |Q_C6(X)| <=486*(1+3/5)*2^(3/4)
                       *X^(-1/4)*(1+log(2X))^2       (2.4)
at theta=3/4. For general theta the exponent is 1-5theta/3 and the
factor 2^max(2-5theta/3,0) replaces 2^(3/4).
This proof includes every ordered h,k,q term and all nine channel
components. No assumption about random Mobius signs is used.

3. Complete right-leg pullback, including valuations and units
-------------------------------------------------------------
For a fixed original q and e put m=q^e k. Since q does not divide k,
  v_q(m)=e, k=m/q^e>=2.                              (3.1)
For e=0 interpret v_q(m)=0. Define the pullback count
  J_e(h;q,m)=1_{v_q(m)=e, m/q^e>=2}
       *max(0,min(2X,tau(q))
                -max(X,alpha(h),q*(m/q^e))).          (3.2)
In the high-smooth case the same h^2 upper endpoint is present.
Every quantity in the maximum is integer on its displayed valuation
mask. Then the exact integrated recovery terms are
  Q_e(X)=X^(-2)*sum_{h>=2,q prime,m>=1}
                         a(h) z0(m) J_e(h;q,m).       (3.3)
This is a bijective substitution, not an enlargement. In particular
  Q_0=Q_C6-sum_{e=1}^6 Q_e.                           (3.4)
In a channel expansion include the h non-prime-power mask from (1.1).
In (3.2) exclude m=q^e, which would be the missing k=1 cofactor.
It is incorrect to replace v_q(m)=e by v_q(m)>=e, to admit q|k, to
drop the lower k>=2 endpoint, or to replace the original cutoff by a
new cutoff depending on m. All h/k gcd overlap is preserved:
gcd(h,m)=gcd(h,k) since q is coprime to h.

For each positive pointwise term at integer n:
  e=0: m=k<=n/q<q;
  e=1: 2q<=m=qk<=n, v_q(m)=1 and Pplus(m)=q>n^theta;
  e>=2: m=q^e k>=2q^2>2n, and m<=q^(e-1)n.             (3.5)
The strict inequality q^2>n uses theta>=1/2, including its endpoint.
Because k<q, Pplus(m)=q in every e>=1 case. It is not a prime power:
k>=2 has a prime factor distinct from q. Thus (3.5) gives exact
interior and exterior sectors, without assuming a hidden source zero.

The e=1 sector is exactly the full nonsmooth non-prime-power coefficient
prefix. Indeed a nonsmooth non-prime-power m<=n has a unique prime
q=Pplus(m)>n^theta; q^2>n makes its exponent one, and its cofactor
k=m/q is >=2 and <q. No repeated-prime or prime-unit exception survives
in this equivalence. All higher lifts instead live outside the original
prefix. No source entry outside n may be deleted from (3.3).

4. Interior relation and the full order-one exterior complement
--------------------------------------------------------------
At a fixed n write N(n)=sum_{m<=n,Pplus(m)>n^theta}a(m). It has no
prime-power terms, so the exact first-lift bank is
  sum_{q>n^theta,2<=k<=n/q} z0(qk)
       =2N(n)+beta*(U_out(n)+V_out(n)),               (4.1)
where U_out and V_out are the same nonsmooth, non-prime-power prefix
with coefficient U or V. This follows from z0(m)=2a(m)+beta(U(m)+V(m))
on that domain. In particular Q_1 has a real connection to the original
smooth/nonsmooth covariance, but also carries both companion terms.
There is no available domination which discards them.

For a transparent complete signed decomposition define, always with
k>=2 and the ORIGINAL q cutoff and k<=n/q,
  B_b(n)=sum q^(-b) B(k),
  U_b(n)=sum q^(-b) U(k), V_b(n)=sum q^(-b) V(k).
Here b=0 means an unweighted bank. The selected filtered bank is
  F(n)=beta*(-2B_(5/3)+2B_(11/3)+2B_4-U_2-V_4).        (4.2)
The baseline and first-lift banks are respectively
  L0=beta*(2B_0-U_0-V_0),
  Z1=beta*(2B_u+2B_v-4B_0-U_u+U_0-V_v+V_0).          (4.3)
The COMPLETE outside sum from e=2 through 6 is
  Z_ext=beta*[2(B_0-B_u-B_v-B_(5/3)+B_(11/3)+B_4)
                         +(U_u-U_2)+(V_v-V_4)]
       =T(n)+F(n),
  T(n)=beta*(2B_0-2B_u-2B_v+U_u+V_v).                 (4.4)
Consequently
  L0+Z1+T=0, L0+Z1+Z_ext=F.                          (4.5)
After multiplying by H(n), summing the complete dyadic block, and
dividing by X^2, these are the exact integrated recovery identities.

The outside B term has no common small prime factor. Already
  B(q^2)=1-2t-t^2+t^3+t^4 ->1
as q grows. Its e=2 z0 term therefore tends to 2beta*B(k). The complete
e>=2 B multiplier is 2*(1-t-t^2+F_B), also order one. The higher
e>=3 B tail starts at degree one, not degree five. The strong selected
zero was produced by cancellation ACROSS the baseline, first lift and
outside complement, not by making each recovered term separately small.

The weighted F term is paid by (2.4). But inserting Z_ext=T+F into
(3.4) cancels this paid F on both sides and leaves the exact (4.5)
baseline relation. It does not bound H*T. Bounding the outside sum by
the same local cancellation which proved F is therefore not an
independent recovery argument. This diagnoses a specific route; it
does not exclude a different genuinely signed estimate for H*T together
with its interior and companion terms.

All banks above exclude k=1. If they are rewritten with cofactor units,
their omitted prime-count terms must be restored individually. In the
whole filtered bank the unit correction is
  sum_{q>n^theta,q<=n/2} beta*(-2t^5-t^6+2t^11+t^12).
Its absolute local coefficient is at most 8beta*q^(-5/3). Since
|H(n)|<=beta*n*(1+log n), its integrated correction is at most
  20beta^2*2^(1-2theta/3)*X^(-2theta/3)*(1+log(2X)).   (4.6)
For completeness, |U(k)|,|V(k)|<=1 follows from their local geometric
Euler rows; the proper-divisor convolution for p gives |a(h)|<=beta*d2(h)
on non-prime-powers, while the other a entries vanish. The divisor sum
bound gives the displayed H envelope, and the elementary integer tail
sum with exponent 5/3 proves (4.6).
It is harmless padding for the WHOLE filter. Individual e=1 and e=2
unit extensions have order-one local terms, so (4.6) must not be used
to drop their units independently. No unit extension is needed for
the exact formulas (3.2)-(4.5).

5. Local two-root identity and exact recovery coefficients
----------------------------------------------------------
There is a useful sharper Euler identity, valid for every e>=0:
  t*B(q^e)+U(q^e)-(1+t)*V(q^e)=0.                    (5.1)
To prove it put the three local rational series over their common
denominator. The numerator becomes
  (1-z)*[t*(1-z)+(1-t^2 z)-(1+t)*(1-tz)]=0.
This includes e=0. It is an identity among the LOCAL prime-power rows,
not a global identity tB(k)+U(k)-(1+t)V(k)=0 for arbitrary k.

Every cumulative row in (2.1) obeys
  C_m=(t+t^2)C_(m-1)-t^3 C_(m-2), m>=2.             (5.2)
It follows either directly from (2.1), or because each component is
a linear combination of t^m and t^(2m), including m=0 when interpreted
by that combination. For a fixed k, take any fixed weighted channel
projection W_m=w_B B(k)C_m^B+w_U U(k)C_m^U+w_V V(k)C_m^V.
The weights can also depend on the original q, provided they stay fixed
across m. This includes the z0 projection and the original -z0+t*zu
bank projection. Such W_m has the exact form
  W_m=P*t^m+Q*t^(2m),                               (5.3)
and obeys (5.2) on the original cofactor/prime domain.
Thus
  W_0=((t+t^2)W_1-W_2)/t^3.                         (5.4)
Since t^3=q^(-1), this low-row recovery already includes a factor q;
its W_1 and W_2 rows are not the cofinal selected rows.

For the selected depth there is also the exact two-filter formula
  W_0=t^(-10)*(1-t^6)/(1-t)*W_5
                -t^(-11)*(1-t^5)/(1-t)*W_6.          (5.5)
Solve the two equations W_5=P t^5+Q t^10 and
W_6=P t^6+Q t^12 to obtain this identity. More generally for m>=2,
  W_0=t^(-2m+2)*(1-t^m)/(1-t)*W_(m-1)
                -t^(-2m+1)*(1-t^(m-1))/(1-t)*W_m.   (5.6)
At depth six the coefficients grow like q^(10/3) and q^(11/3).
Even though both depth-five and depth-six COMPLETE auxiliaries are
cofinal at theta=3/4, (5.5) is a PRIME-DEPENDENT identity inside their
kernel sums. A q-independent bound for each integrated auxiliary cannot
be multiplied by the variable coefficients after integration. Inside
the sum their inverse powers undo the positive-valuation envelope.
Moving them outside the sum by a supremum costs growing powers of X.

The same issue applies to all exact recurrences among these cumulative
depths. A larger depth changes local suppression and inverse cost; the
two-root relation does not itself create a closed scale induction.

6. Scope-limited impossibility of bounded local linear recovery
--------------------------------------------------------------
Here is a precise statement, independent of the actual numerical source.
Let v denote a finite formal channel-state vector. Fix finitely many
local filters y_l=M_l(t)v. Suppose every entry of every matrix M_l is
a polynomial (or continuous bounded function) which vanishes at t=0.
Let L be a fixed nonzero target linear map on these formal states.
There do not exist uniformly bounded local linear maps R_l(t), for
t=q^(-1/3) over arbitrarily large primes q, such that
  L=sum_l R_l(t)M_l(t).                              (6.1)
Indeed every right-hand summand tends to zero in any finite-dimensional
matrix norm, while L does not. A finite family is essential here.
If these matrices obey ||M_l(t)||<=C_l t^m for m>0, then any recovery must
satisfy the quantitative lower bound
  sum_l ||R_l(t)|| C_l >=||L||*t^(-m).                (6.2)
For individual exponents use instead
||L||<=sum_l ||R_l(t)||C_l*t^(m_l).
In the polynomial ring this says the ideal of positive-valuation
entries has zero constant term; bounded linear combinations cannot
produce an unfiltered constant-term identity.

This formal-state theorem applies to the current finite all-channel
positive-valuation stencil class. It does not assert that the actual
values B(k),U(k),V(k) are arbitrary or independent random variables.
Using an additional actual-source restriction, cross-prime identity,
integrated compensation or different support may evade its hypotheses.
A number of filters growing with q, or unbounded recovery coefficients,
also falls outside the bounded finite-class assertion. None is silently
licensed by a cofinal auxiliary bound.

For the SELECTED diagonal channel filter itself,
  |F_B|=t^5*(1-t^6-t^7),
  1-t^6-t^7>=c0:=1-2^(-2)-2^(-7/3)>1/2.
Thus separate B recovery costs between q^(5/3) and c0^(-1)q^(5/3);
U recovery costs q^2, and V recovery costs q^4. Full diagonal-channel
inversion has norm q^4 in a coordinate supremum norm: t^12 is its
smallest absolute multiplier, since c0*t^5>t^12 for q>=2.
For any fixed channel with selected multiplier positive valuation,
uniformly bounded local inversion is already excluded.

One observed scalar W_6 does not in general determine W_0: in (5.3)
take Q=1,P=-t^6 to obtain W_6=0 but W_0=1-t^6!=0. Two observations can
recover it by (5.5), at the stated growing cost. This formal example is
not a counterfeit actual-mu source and is not a global impossibility
proof. The exact recurrence shows why requiring recovery of all three
separate channels can overstate the cost for a single target projection;
the correct projection-level two-filter cost is still unbounded.

7. Smaller cofactors are real; a smaller-scale target is not yet obtained
------------------------------------------------------------------------
All original cofactors satisfy k<=n/q<n^(1-theta). This produces true
smaller-scale source prefixes inside each bank. It does not turn the
whole recovery term into the same obligation at that smaller scale:
the other coordinate remains H(n), with h potentially close to n and,
in the high-smooth sector, h>sqrt(n). Replacing H(n) by H(floor(n/q))
discards the entire fresh smooth complement and is not an inequality.

Explicitly write n=qj+r, 0<=r<q. Then k<=j, but the smooth coordinate
still has h<=qj+r, Pplus(h)<q, the original n^theta cutoff, and the
original high-smooth lower boundary. The dyadic condition is
X<=qj+r<2X, not a single common dyadic interval independent of q and r.
All q,r-dependent entry endpoints and fresh h terms survive. This is
an exact smaller-cofactor representation, not a decreasing-scale
recurrence with an already bounded forcing.

Conversely reindexing by the lifted m in (3.3) moves e>=2 terms to
larger source indices, with m<=q^(e-1)n and m>n. A future-scale recurrence
would require new estimates or a proven contraction/compensation and
cannot be declared well founded merely because m/q^e is small.

The remaining usable proof input is therefore quite explicit: bound
or cancel the full signed H*T/interior-companion covariance in (4.1)-
(4.5), with its exact original prime window and all gcd sectors, or
give a closed decreasing-scale relation which includes the fresh H
complement. The other K,bank-bank,Lu,main covariances and initial moment
cost must also be retained. Assuming an RH-strength bound for a B or
U companion, or feeding the original complete moment back as its own
premise, would not close this obligation noncircularly.

8. Exact low-action reduction and source-only post-analysis audit
----------------------------------------------------------------
The selected cumulative operator also has an exact two-action reduction:
  C6=A(t)*I+D(t)*T_q^right,
  A=t^5+t^6-t^11,
  D=t^5+t^6+t^7+t^8+t^9+t^10.                       (8.1)
Substitution of the local rows U(q)=t-1, V(q)=t^2-1 and
B(q)=t+t^2-2 recovers all three rows (2.2). Equivalently solve for
the two coefficients with the U,V rows and then use (5.1) to verify B.
Here I means the baseline action; T means the actual one-prime action.
Their q-dependent weights stay INSIDE the original kernel sum.

Thus the COMPLETE selected auxiliary can be represented using only
weighted original and first-lift interior terms. No explicit future
source indices are needed for that alternative representation: (8.1)
has already compensated all higher prime-power terms by exact source
identities. The recovery issue is not a failure to perform that valid
simplification. It is recovery of the unweighted original term.

For a fixed q let V0(q),V1(q) denote the corresponding integrated kernel
sums without summing q, and let VC6(q) be the selected one. Then
  VC6(q)=A(q)V0(q)+D(q)V1(q),
  V0(q)=A(q)^(-1)VC6(q)-(D(q)/A(q))*V1(q).           (8.2)
Since A=t^5*(1+t-t^6)>0, its inverse is comparable to q^(5/3);
D/A tends to one. Multiplying the paid auxiliary by A(q)^(-1) loses
its proved prime suppression, and the remaining unweighted interior
V1 carries the companions in (4.1). The integrated bound on sum_q VC6(q)
does not bound sum_q VC6(q)/A(q). This is a genuine exact reduction to
an interior weighted pair, but no independent signed bound for that
unweighted pair has been established.

I reviewed prime_slide_recovery.py without executing it or its tests.
Its prefix recurrence and difference-to-raw-row conversion implement
(5.2) on exponents 0..6. Tensoring the two per-coordinate low-action
representations reduces a general same-prime stencil to I/I, I/T,
T/I and T/T with polynomial weights, all under the SAME original
coprime masks. The verifier reconstructs all nine tensor multipliers
using independently expanded checker low rows and compares the saved
exact polynomials. Its mathematical admission premise is that the saved
rows belong to the already independently audited frozen study. This
post-analysis verifies source identities, not a new source moment.

The emitted pullback descriptors preserve v_q(H)=e,v_q(K)=f, the
inverse-image h>=2 non-prime-power mask, and k>=2. Their kernel uses
H/q^e and q*K/q^f, not the lifted smooth endpoint. The separately stated
high-smooth upper limit (H/q^e)^2 must be included whenever that variant
is used. A left lift is outside n on the high-smooth base; the descriptor
does not claim it is always outside n on every small smooth base.
These scopes agree with the exact inverse-image calculus in section 3.

The local recovery routine uses B(h)B(k) as a witness. If its nonzero
multiplier polynomials have minimum degree m>0 and total l1 norm L,
then an all-channel scalar recovery identity forces
  max_j |c_j(q)|>=q^(m/3)/L.
This follows by taking just the B,B entry of the identity. Other
components need not also vanish for that witness conclusion, although
the current cofinal all-channel bank has positive common valuation.
If every B,B entry is zero, the routine conservatively declines this
particular positive-order certificate rather than issuing an unsound
claim. Its stated exclusion is finite, bounded, same-prime LOCAL
linear recovery; global signed/domain-compensated proofs stay open.

The feedback payload also records a conditional centered shared-prime

Its premise is a coupled W0=2J+beta*(M_u+M_v) cumulative moment bound

The payload explicitly leaves that induction, distinct-prime and
