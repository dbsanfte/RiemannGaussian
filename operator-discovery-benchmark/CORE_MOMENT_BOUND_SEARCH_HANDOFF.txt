The bound actually certified
---------------------------
Products here are Dirichlet convolution. Put
 A=mu restricted to[1,U], c=sum_(d<=U)mu(d)/d,
 T=c*delta-A, E=delta+One*T, K(n)=n^(-1/3), theta=2/3.
The existing source contains (27/2)*K*A^2*E^2. Let
 M=sum_(d>=1)(A^2*E^2)(d)d^(-theta),
 h=K*A^2*E^2-(M/theta)*J_theta,
 J_theta(n)=n^theta-(n-1)^theta INCLUDING n=1.
The complete ordinary moment M converges; this is separately proved using
the inherited sqrt-prefix rule, finite convolution and the entire Abel tail.

The executable search chose sigma=8/23 and epsilon=5/552. Its two dyadic

Why this proof is licensed
-------------------------
The pole at1 is removed in the COMPLETE E(s)=1+zeta(s)(c-A(s)), using
c=A(1), before absolute estimates. The only crossed pole is theta. Gaussian
Perron smoothing justifies moving the contour; Mellin Plancherel then controls
the entire centered staircase, including its x<1 tail. Integer-cell correction
and the full J_theta unit are included. There is no inverse-zeta or RH premise.

The external arithmetic import is the classical unconditional fourth moment:
 integral_0^Y |zeta(1/2+it)|^4 dt << Y*(1+log Y)^4.
Primary provenance and its scope are recorded in018 and019:
 https://arxiv.org/html/1609.02539v1
 https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/s2-27.1.273
Functional equation: https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.4.E2
Off-line and lower moments follow by the reviewed interpolation rules.
Python checks the exact applications of this mathematical trust base; it
does not formally verify the classical theorem or every analytic proof step.

The product-moment primitive handles up to4 zeta factors with rational
absolute-moment powers, including halves, thirds and sixths. It enumerates
all reflection choices and supplied moment orders2/4. Holder weights range
over the complete bounded box-simplex polytope, not a sampled weight grid.
The producer enumerates its vertices by corners plus one free coordinate;
the independent checker derives them by solving active constraint systems.
The contour grid has denominator<=24 and lies below1/2 and theta. Failure
within this finite language is not an arithmetic impossibility theorem.
