ASYMPTOTIC BULK MODES OBSTRUCT UNIFORM PSD STORAGE DRIFT 073
==========================================================

Scope and proved conclusion
----------------------------
This is a new analytical follow-up, written before scientific073
outcomes were supplied. It does not alter the initial prime-block review,
read candidate outcomes, rerun a study, use hashes, or write another file.
The root's proposed states use the actual source

 w_k(n)=1_(8k,10k](n)-1_(16k,20k](n), X=20k,
 Z(k)=sum_n mu(n)w_k(n), k>=1 integer.             (1)

For EVERY fixed theta>0 the signed smooth source state with cutoff
y=(20k)^theta is o(k/log k). This includes all the proposed rational
theta values, including theta below1/2 and theta=1/2. The proof below
uses quantitative unconditional Mertens cancellation and a primary
rough-count theorem; it does not assume RH or independent signs.

Consequently, for any FIXED finite positive-semidefinite storage energy
of the proposed form, and any FIXED q<2, a uniform constant drift is
impossible if any positively weighted direction has unequal coefficients
on one of omega=1,2,3 and omega>=4. The necessary exact constraints are

             v_U1=v_Uge4, v_U2=v_Uge4, v_U3=v_Uge4. (2)

They are independent of the alternating signs of the asymptotic modes.
They are necessary, not sufficient. Directions satisfying (2) still have
unpaid Z/smooth correlations. This excludes specified storage formulas,
not actual Mobius cancellation, the original head goal, or RH.

1. Imported primary theorems and their limited roles
-----------------------------------------------------
Only the following unconditional imported facts are used. They are
analytical inputs, not detector discoveries.

(i) Fixed-r squarefree almost-prime asymptotics:

 Q_r(t):=#{n<=t:n squarefree, omega(n)=r}
 ~ t (log log t)^(r-1)/((r-1)! log t),             (3)

for every fixed positive integer r. The primary source is E. Landau,
"Sur quelques problemes relatifs a la distribution des nombres premiers,"
Bulletin de la Societe Mathematique de France28 (1900),25-38. Section4
specifies distinct prime factors; equation(8), printed page32, states
the asymptotic, with its induction proof continuing through page35.
[Landau's original paper](https://www.numdam.org/article/BSMF_1900__28__25_1.pdf)

(ii) A quantitative unconditional Mertens estimate. It suffices that,
for every fixed A>0,

 |M(t)|<=C_A t/(log t)^A for all sufficiently large t. (4)

For a primary modern proof use E. S. Lee and N. Leong, "New explicit
bounds for Mertens function and the reciprocal of the Riemann zeta-
function," Theorem1.1, equation(9), and Theorem2.2. Their unconditional
bound of order t log(t) exp(-c sqrt(log t)) implies (4). Only that
asymptotic consequence is used; no source-scale computation is borrowed.
[Lee and Leong's paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.06141)

(iii) Let Phi(t,y) count positive y-rough integers <=t, including1,
where rough means every prime factor is STRICTLY greater than y. Put
u=log t/log y. Kai Fan, "Numerically explicit estimates for the
distribution of rough numbers," Journal of Number Theory260 (2024),
120-150, Corollary1.2, proves a uniform unconditional approximation

 Phi(t,y)=t integral_(1..u) y^(s-u) omega(s) ds
          +O(t (log y)^(-3/4) exp(-c sqrt(log y))), (5)

for t>=y>=2, with an absolute c>0. Here omega is Buchstab's continuous
function: omega(s)=1/s for1<=s<=2 and
(s omega(s))'=omega(s-1) for s>2. Only the unconditional statement is
used, not the RH-conditional estimate printed next to it.
[Fan's primary paper, Corollary1.2](https://stvfan.github.io/files/papers/Numerically%20explicit%20estimates%20for%20the%20distribution%20of%20rough%20numbers.pdf)

The consequences and storage obstruction below are directly proved here.
No stronger smooth-sum asymptotic is needed or claimed.

2. A compact-ratio rough-count consequence
------------------------------------------
Fix eta>0 and U<infinity. Uniformly when

 1+eta<=log t/log y<=U, y->infinity,

(5) implies

 Phi(t,y)=t omega(log t/log y)/log y
                    +O_(eta,U)(t/(log y)^2).     (6)

Here is the elementary deduction, including the endpoint issue.
On any compact interval [1,U], omega is bounded and Lipschitz. On [1,2]
this follows from1/s. On each successive interval it follows from the
delay equation and continuity: away from a joining point,
omega'(s)=(omega(s-1)-omega(s))/s is bounded. A derivative can jump at2,
but the function is still Lipschitz there. No derivative value at2 is
needed. Put ell=log y. Subtract omega(u) inside the integral in (5).
The Lipschitz error is at most

 K_U integral_(0..infinity) v exp(-ell v) dv=K_U/ell^2.

The constant part integrates to
omega(u)(1-exp(-ell(u-1)))/ell. Since u-1>=eta, its omitted lower-boundary
tail is O(exp(-eta ell)/ell). The error in (5) is also O(t/ell^2) as
y->infinity. This proves (6), uniformly on the stated compact ratios.
Thus the following smooth proof is valid even when the ratio approaches2.

3. Exact convolution for the signed smooth prefix
--------------------------------------------------
Define

 A_mu(x,y)=sum_(n<=x, P+(n)<=y)mu(n), P+(1)=1,
 M(t)=sum_(n<=t)mu(n),

with real x,y and every upper integer boundary interpreted through floor.
For y>=1, let r_y(d)=1[P-(d)>y], with P-(1)=infinity. The exact identity
of arithmetic functions is

 mu(n)1[P+(n)<=y]=(mu * r_y)(n).                 (7)

Proof on prime powers: for p<=y the local convolution is mu(p^e),
because r_y has only its unit at this prime. For p>y the coefficient
is sum_(j=0..e)mu(p^j), which is0 for e>=1. The unit coefficient is1.
Multiplying the local identities proves (7) for every integer, including
squareful zeros. In particular r_y counts ALL rough integers, including
rough prime powers; replacing it by squarefree rough integers changes
(7) and would invalidate the proof.

Summing (7) gives two exact finite forms:

 A_mu(x,y)=sum_(a<=x)mu(a)Phi(x/a,y)
         =sum_(d<=x, P-(d)>y)M(x/d).             (8)

For any real1<=T<=x, split the first sum at a<=T. The tail equals

 sum_(a>T)mu(a)Phi(x/a,y)
 =sum_(d<=x/T, P-(d)>y)[M(x/d)-M(T)].            (9)

If x/d=T, the bracket is zero, so including that equality causes no
boundary error. The strict lower cutoff a>T and the floors in M(T)
are literal, not an approximation.

Let epsilon(T)=sup_(t>=T)|M(t)|/t. By (4), for every fixed A and large T,
epsilon(T)<=C_A/(log T)^A. The unrestricted harmonic bound in (9) gives

 |tail|<=epsilon(T)[x sum_(d<=x/T)1/d+T Phi(x/T,y)]
       <=epsilon(T) x[2+log x].                  (10)

This deliberately includes all rough d and bounds their count by x/T.
It requires no rough-count estimate in the possibly small argument
x/T of the tail. Thus there is no unlicensed uniformity near t=y.

4. Signed smooth sums are smaller than k/log k at all fixed powers
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Fix theta in(0,1), and take

 x=a k, y=(20k)^theta,
 a in {8,10,16,20},
 L=log x, T=exp(L^(1/4)).                        (11)

For sufficiently large k, T<x/y. For every a_cofactor<=T, the ratio

 log(x/a_cofactor)/log y
 =1/theta+O_theta((L^(1/4)+1)/L)

stays in a fixed compact subset of(1,infinity), uniformly over the FOUR
source endpoints. This is the needed comparison for y defined from20k,
rather than redefining y separately at each endpoint. At theta=1/2,
the compact interval may include2, which is allowed by section2.

Apply (6) to each term of the truncated sum in (8), writing
u=log x/log y and ell=log y. Its leading part is

 (x/ell) sum_(a_cofactor<=T)mu(a_cofactor)/a_cofactor
                           omega(u-log(a_cofactor)/ell). (12)

Lipschitz continuity gives the following bound for replacing omega by
omega(u), WITHOUT assuming cancellation in an error term:

 absolute error in the inner sum
 <=K sum_(a<=T)log(a)/(a ell)
 =O_theta((log T)^2/ell)=O_theta(L^(-1/2)).       (13)

The error from (6), summed absolutely over the same cofactors, is

 O_theta(x (1+log T)/ell^2)=O_theta(x/L^(7/4)).    (14)

For completeness, the ordinary reciprocal sum converges and equals0:

 sum_(a>=1)mu(a)/a=0.                            (15)

Convergence follows by partial summation from (4) with A>1. For s>1
its Dirichlet series is1/zeta(s), by the absolutely convergent Euler
product. Partial summation makes its tail uniformly small as s decreases
to1, so its value at1 is the limit1/zeta(s)=0. The elementary simple pole
of zeta at1 suffices here; no zero-free theorem is being inferred from
finite sums. The same partial summation gives

 |sum_(a<=T)mu(a)/a|=O_A((log T)^(1-A)).          (16)

Use A=16 in (10),(16). The tail in (10) is O(x/L^3). The constant-omega
part of (12) is smaller still. Multiplying (13) by x/ell and adding
(14) proves the explicit sufficient bound

 A_mu(a k,(20k)^theta)=O_theta(k/(log k)^(3/2)),  (17)

uniformly over a=8,10,16,20. Constants and the sufficiently large base
may depend on the FIXED theta. This proof covers every fixed theta in
(0,1), however small. It is not a statement uniform over theta(k)->0,
theta(k)->1, a growing list of theta values, or subpower y(k).

If theta>=1, y>=20k, so every source endpoint is fully smooth and
A_mu(a k,y)=M(a k); (4) is more than enough. Taking the SAME y in the
four prefixes gives, for every fixed theta>0,

 S_theta(k)=A_mu(10k,y)-A_mu(8k,y)
            -A_mu(20k,y)+A_mu(16k,y)
           =o(k/log k).                         (18)

For a fixed finite list of theta values the bounds may be combined by
taking the largest of their constants and bases. No root-denominator
restriction is required. At theta=1, S_theta=Z exactly. For theta=0 the
unit is the only smooth squarefree integer and w_k(1)=0, so S_0=0;
that special case also has no bulk mode.

The stronger suggestion A_mu(x,x^theta)~-x/log^2x for theta>1/2 is
unnecessary for this obstruction and is NOT established by (17).
An exact second-order smooth expansion would require additional signed
moment and boundary work. In particular a uniform expansion across the
theta=1/2 transition should not be inferred by differentiating (6).
The present result deliberately uses only the proved small-o statement.

5. The fixed-sector bulk hierarchy on the actual source
-------------------------------------------------------
For r=1,2,3 define the complete signed squarefree sector

 U_r(k)=sum_(n squarefree, omega(n)=r)mu(n)w_k(n).

Because mu(n)=(-1)^r in this fixed sector, (3) gives

 U_r(k)=(-1)^r[Q_r(10k)-Q_r(8k)-Q_r(20k)+Q_r(16k)]
 ~ a_r k (log log k)^(r-1)/log k,
 a_r=(-1)^(r+1)2/(r-1)!.                        (19)

Indeed log(c k)/log k->1 and loglog(c k)/loglog k->1 for each fixed
c>0. The leading signed band length is10-8-20+16=-2, which is nonzero.
Each of the four asymptotic errors is little-o of the displayed scale,
so their subtraction is licensed. No short-interval estimate is used.

The complete >=4 sector has the exact identity

 U_ge4=Z-U_1-U_2-U_3.                            (20)

It retains actual signs across all squarefree omega>=4 integers; it is
not granted one sign. Quantitative Mertens cancellation at the same four
endpoints gives

 Z(k)=O_A(k/(log k)^A) for every fixed A>0.        (21)

Thus U_ge4 has the large deterministic compensating modes needed to
cancel the three displayed sector contributions. Storing its square as
an independent positive term retains those modes instead of canceling
them. This observation concerns storage energy, not a new Z lower bound.

6. Exact necessary constraints for every positively weighted ray
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Let the exact state vector be

 x(k)=(Z,U_1,U_2,U_3,U_ge4,S_theta1,...,S_thetas).

The list of theta values is FIXED and finite. Let v be a fixed real or
rational coefficient vector and put delta_r=v_Ur-v_Uge4. By (20),

 v.x=(v_Z+v_Uge4)Z+sum_(r=1..3)delta_r U_r
                         +sum_i v_Si S_thetai.  (22)

Suppose at least one delta_r is nonzero, and let R be its largest index.
Equations (18),(19),(21) imply

 v.x(k)~delta_R a_R
                 k (loglog k)^(R-1)/log k.      (23)

The higher-r terms in (22) are EXACTLY absent because their coefficients
are zero; no higher-order asymptotic for them is needed. The lower-r
terms have a smaller power of loglog. Even when R=1, every smooth term
is little-o of its scale by (18). This is why proving that smooth
small-o for ALL proposed theta values was necessary.

Now fix finitely many v_j, lambda_j>=0 and q<2, all independent of k,
and define the proposed energy

 P(k)=Z(k)^2+sum_j lambda_j(v_j.x(k))^2,
 F(k)=P(k)/k^q>=0.                              (24)

If some lambda_j>0 has a nonzero delta_r, (23) and positivity give a
constant c>0 such that for all sufficiently large k,

 F(k)>=c k^(2-q) (loglog k)^(2R-2)/(log k)^2.    (25)

No cancellation with another added square can remove this lower bound.
This is the point where positive semidefiniteness is essential.

If there were a finite uniform drift constant C with

 F(k)-F(floor(k/2))<=C for EVERY integer k>=2,    (26)

telescoping would give F(k)<=F(1)+max(C,0)floor(log_2 k). But (25)
divided by1+log k tends to infinity for q<2: a positive power of k
dominates all fixed logarithmic factors. This is a contradiction. It
already holds along k=2^h, so an eventual recurrence with a paid finite
base set is also excluded. Uniform negative C is itself impossible for
a nonnegative F along that chain.

Therefore EVERY active ray must satisfy (2). A zero-weight ray may have
arbitrary coefficients because it contributes nothing. An arbitrarily
small but FIXED positive lambda does not evade the theorem: it only
changes c and the eventual contradiction scale. The result excludes
the proposed q=1,18/17,258/257 equally; all are strictly below2.

The theorem does not apply without modification to scale-dependent
weights tending to zero, scale-dependent directions, a growing number
of directions or theta values, variable q, or indefinite storage.
Those would be different proof grammars and must charge their domains
and their own recurrence. No finite score determines the asymptotic
contradiction threshold.

7. PSD matrix form, exact nullspace and what survives
-----------------------------------------------------
Write e_a for coordinate vectors and put

 b_r=e_Ur-e_Uge4, r=1,2,3,
 H=e_Z e_Z^T+sum_j lambda_j v_j v_j^T.

These three b_r are independent rational bulk-mode vectors. Their
alternating amplitudes a_r do not affect their nullspace. Because
e_Z.b_r=0, positivity gives

 H b_r=0 for all r
 iff v_j.b_r=0 for all r and all lambda_j>0.      (27)

For example, b_r^T H b_r=sum_j lambda_j(v_j.b_r)^2. Thus (27) is an
exact rational necessary matrix condition. More generally any fixed
PSD H with target domination has the same necessary annihilation of
these bulk vectors. To see this, factor H=B^T B and use the highest
sector whose vector B b_r is nonzero; the norm-square argument leading
to (25) applies. If H kills the higher sectors, their full values,
including subleading errors, are exactly annihilated.

A generic rational-nullspace projection enforcing v.b_r=0 is therefore
mathematically justified BEFORE candidate scoring for this state family.
It is not a claim that the original source lacks the forbidden sector
contributions. Keep every sector in exact state reconstruction and
certificate checking; restrict only the positive quadratic directions.

There is an important exact simplification after projection. By (22),
every surviving ray becomes

           v.x=(v_Z+v_Uge4)Z+sum_i v_Si S_thetai. (28)

The independently stored sectors no longer add independent arithmetic
features to an admissible ray. With eight smooth coordinates the ambient
state has13 coordinates and the three constraints leave a10-dimensional
coefficient space. The additional exact null direction
Z-U_1-U_2-U_3-U_ge4 leaves at most9 effective arithmetic features.
It should be marked as an identity or quotiented out, not presented as
a learned cancellation mechanism. Additional dependencies among chosen
smooth states could lower the effective dimension further.

Passing the nullspace filter does NOT prove (26). Equation (18) still
allows signed smooth coordinates as large as k times inverse powers of
log k, which can be much larger than k^(q/2)sqrt(log k) for q<2.
It merely says these coordinates cannot cancel a surviving fixed-sector
k/log k mode. There may be additional smooth bulk modes and additional
necessary annihilation conditions; this review does not assert their
coefficients or completeness. Proving a uniform recurrence for (28)
still requires actual Z/smooth cross-scale correlations, paid bases and
constants. The full original-head and RH transfer obligations remain
unchanged.

8. Exact centering is a next grammar, not an automatic remedy
------------------------------------------------------------
The obstruction suggests replacing positive storage of raw density
sectors by source-independent centered states whose deterministic modes
are removed before squares are formed. Merely subtracting the leading
equivalent in (19) is insufficient: an o(k/log k) remainder can still
have k/log^2 k growth and obstruct q<2. Any finite asymptotic truncation
can leave a power-of-k term divided by further logarithms. A legitimate
center requires its exact definition, all endpoint/boundary conventions,
and a proved residual price or another complete signed recurrence.

An exact prime-count stencil, a named smooth main/composite complement,
or a common-prime/cell correlation from the initial073 review can expose
that residual. Taking a fitted target-valued constant as its center
just reconstructs the finite target. Imported asymptotics justify the
three specific raw-sector exclusions proved here; they do not turn any
remaining centered direction into a square-root theorem.

Disposition
------------
The fixed-r bulk hypothesis is correct, with the signs and factorials
in (19). The required smooth small-o hypothesis is also correct for
every fixed theta>0 in the proposed source states, including all small
pilot theta values, by the complete direct argument (7)-(18).
Therefore the three rational nullspace constraints (2) are theorem-
certified necessary conditions for constant drift of ANY fixed energy
(24) at q<2. This is stronger than finite candidate rejection and may
be applied before the frozen search. The surviving Z/smooth recurrence,
additional smooth-mode centering and every full-head/RH growth claim
remain open.
