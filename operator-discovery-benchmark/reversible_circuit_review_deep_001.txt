Double-interleaved reversible circuits53 and59: exact kernels and open gate
2026-10-09. New analytical review; frozen checker/sources/results unchanged.

The only study artifact read is reversible-circuit-results/pilot-001/
proof_queue.json, using its programs, certificates and obligations. No numerical

These are the new circuit IDs53 and59, not identically numbered old potentials.

Notation and the actual two programs
------------------------------------
M(n)=sum_{j<=n}mu(j), M(0)=0, mu the ACTUAL complete Mobius sequence. Write
 rho(mu(m))=+1 unless mu(m)<0, in which case it is-1, including rho(mu(0))=1.
On the actual three-valued source, rho(mu(m))=1+mu(m)-mu(m)^2. This is a unit
sign, not mu itself and not a squarefree mask: squareful arguments give+1.

For a prime assignment chi(p) in{+1,-1}, define its multiplicative Euler kernel
 k_{alpha,chi}(p^e)=chi(p)^e*(alpha)_e/e!, k(1)=1,
 (C_{alpha,chi}f)(n)=sum_{d|n}k_{alpha,chi}(d)f(n/d).
Let S_s multiply a whole state by s(n). Prime assignments are extended through
the Euler kernel; e.g chi(p^e) in the kernel means chi(p)^e, not reevaluation
of a root formula at p^e. A whole-argument sign stage is a different operation.

Define, for all primes p and positive integers n,
 q(p)=(-1)^floor(sqrt(p)),
 u(p)=rho(mu(floor(sqrt(p)))),
 v5(p)=-1 if p mod5 is1 or4, otherwise+1,
 s(n)=(-1)^floor(n^(2/3)),
 r7(n)=-1 if n mod7 is1,2 or4, otherwise+1,
 s3(n)=rho(mu(n-floor(n^(1/3)))),
 s2(n)=rho(mu(n-floor(sqrt(n)))).
The exact selected outputs are
 F53=C_{-1/2,u} S_r7 C_{1/3,1} S_s C_{1/6,q} M,
 F59=C_{-1/3,u} S_s2 C_{-1/6,v5} S_s3 C_{-1/3,v5} M.
All phases use the same fixed source throughout the circuit AND inverse.
Changing source therefore changes source-dependent phases; a diagnostic control
cannot silently retain actual-Mobius phase data or be promoted to actual mu.

The objective is the FULL D(X)=X^-2 sum_{X<=n<2X}F(n)^2. It is not a norm
of convolving mu and then taking an additive prefix, and is not a sum of
separate path squares. Neither operation is licensed by the circuit theorem.

Exact full path kernel and an unavoidable tail
---------------------------------------------
For any circuit C_c S_t C_b S_s C_a, put A=k_a,B=k_b,C=k_c. Direct expansion
of all three divisor sums gives
 F(n)=sum_{d1*d2*d3*k=n} A(d1)B(d2)C(d3)
                  *t(d1*d2*k)*s(d1*k)*M(k).
Every ordered multiplicative path, including every prime-power allocation,
is present. Equivalently
 P_n(k)=sum_{d1*d2*d3=n/k}A(d1)B(d2)C(d3)
                           *t(n/d3)*s(n/(d3*d2)),
 F(n)=sum_{k|n}P_n(k)M(k).
The coefficient P_n(n)=t(n)s(n) is always+1 or-1. No other path contributes
to that coefficient, so a vanishing sum of convolution orders alone cannot
cancel the direct M(n) channel.

Expanding the additive prefix inside this identity gives another exact form:
 F(n)=sum_{j=1}^n mu(j)W_n(j),
 W_n(j)=sum_{k|n,k>=j}P_n(k).
For EVERY j>floor(n/2), all proper divisors have disappeared and
 W_n(j)=s(n)t(n).
Thus with Dtail(n)=M(n)-M(floor(n/2)),
 F(n)=s(n)t(n)*Dtail(n)+Bhead(n),
 Bhead(n)=s(n)t(n)M(floor(n/2))+sum_{k|n,k<n}P_n(k)M(k).
The complete moment contains ||Dtail||^2, ||Bhead||^2 and
2*X^-2 sum s(n)t(n)Dtail(n)Bhead(n). Any successful estimate must bound this
actual signed correlation or find another valid closing identity. Magnitude
kernel bounds do not attenuate the input's final half-interval.

At a prime output there are only the three single-prime allocations and k=p.
Consequently, exactly for every prime p,
 F53(p)=s(p)r7(p)M(p)+(1/6)q(p)s(p)r7(p)-(1/3)r7(p)-(1/2)u(p),
 F59(p)=s3(p)s2(p)M(p)-(1/3)v5(p)s3(p)s2(p)
                              -(1/6)v5(p)s2(p)-(1/3)u(p).
The difference from the unit-signed M(p) is at most1 for53 and5/6 for59.
This is an exact all-prime lemma, not a numerical fit. A cofinal bound for
the selected output would in particular bound the prime-output M energy:
 X^-2 sum_{X<=p<2X}M(p)^2<=2D(X)+2/X
for53, with a smaller constant for59. That necessary condition is not the full
target energy, and no replacement of general n by primes is justified here.

Full squaring retains the Gram correlation
 D(X)=X^-2 sum_{X<=n<2X}sum_{k,l|n}P_n(k)P_n(l)M(k)M(l).
Its diagonals have no independently variable negative sign. If source or
phase arguments coincide, their unit squares reduce to1; their off-diagonal
correlations cannot be assumed independent. This full quadratic form, or the
equivalent tail/head correlation above, is a concrete remaining proof gate.

Which arithmetic information the phases actually use
-----------------------------------------------------
The input M uses every actual mu(j). ID59's s2,s3 also query actual mu at
near-full shifted arguments. The terminal u queries actual mu at floor(sqrt(p))
for EVERY prime p, not just a finite small-prime bank. It does NOT query mu(p),
which is simply-1 at every prime. The sign u is+1 at squareful root indices.
Its distribution among square-length prime intervals is not supplied by its
syntax or by the inverse theorem.

The q assignment is entirely geometric parity on those same root intervals.
The v5 assignment uses fixed residue classes, and the middle assignment1 in53
is constant. The r7 stage is a fixed residue sign on whole output arguments.
There is no justification to call any of these an independently varying
arithmetic prime sign. In particular q and u have different defining laws;
neither may be identified with the other through a finite response alias.

The source-dependent shifted phases in59 are NOT root-boundary impulses.
For g_k(n)=n-floor(n^(1/k)), k=2 or3, its exact positive-index increments are
 g_k(n)-g_k(n-1)=1-1_{n=m^k}, n>=2.
It visits all nonnegative integers in order, with a stutter at each perfect
power. On a root plateau, its argument advances at every n. Consequently
s_k(n)=rho(mu(g_k(n))) can vary throughout the plateau through the actual
source sequence. A bound counting only perfect-power events misses these
source variations. Indeed the root event itself keeps the argument unchanged.

Character structure and exact defects
-------------------------------------
Let psi5 be the real quadratic character mod5, zero on multiples of5.
For p!=5, v5(p)=-psi5(p); v5(5)=+1. Its completely multiplicative prime lift
is a Liouville factor times a unit extension of psi5 whose value at5 is-1.
This uses the constant prime law mu(p)=-1 algebraically, not fresh source data.
In the absolutely convergent half-plane Re(z)>1 its kernel Euler product is
 A_{alpha,v5}(z)=zeta(2z)^alpha*(1+5^(-z))^alpha*L(z,psi5)^(-alpha).
To check the identity, at p!=5 use
 1+psi5(p)p^(-z)=(1-p^(-2z))/(1-psi5(p)p^(-z));
the exceptional local factor at5 contributes the displayed (1+5^(-z))^alpha.
Fractional powers here mean the branch specified by the convergent Euler
product. This formula is not a continuation across unknown zeros. Sign stages
do not in general preserve such Euler-product factorizations of the output.

Similarly let eta7 be the completely multiplicative unit extension of the
quadratic character mod7 with eta7(7)=+1. On7-coprime n,
 r7(n)=-eta7(n).
On multiples of7, r7(n)=+1. With P7 the projection onto multiples of7,
 S_r7=-S_eta7+P7*(I+S_eta7).
The defect has support at7-multiples, not a subpower sparse set. This nevertheless
is a structured finite-prime defect, rather than an arbitrary unknown mask.
Since S_eta C_{alpha,chi}=C_{alpha,chi*eta}S_eta for a unit multiplicative eta,
the outer stage of53 separates EXACTLY as
 C_{-1/2,u}S_r7 g=-S_eta7 C_{-1/2,u*eta7}g
                       +C_{-1/2,u}P7*(I+S_eta7)g,
 g=C_{1/3,1}S_s C_{1/6,q}M.
The terminal eta7 sign does not alter the bulk moment, but the defect and
bulk/defect cross term do. Output of the defect remains supported at7-multiples.

There is an exact7-adic version retaining every local factor. For7-coprime m,
n=7^j*m, and C7(e)=k_{-1/2,u}(7^e), the outer output equals
 sum_{e=0}^{j-1} C7(e)
       sum_{d|m}k_{-1/2,u}(d)*g(7^(j-e)*m/d)
 -C7(j)*eta7(m)*sum_{d|m}k_{-1/2,u*eta7}(d)*g(m/d).
The first sum is empty for j0. These e terms coexist at the same n, so all
their mixed products remain. Local7-power dilations have a convergent geometric
norm majorant even at the critical boundary, since their dilation weight is
at most7^(-e) and each local coefficient has magnitude at most1. This permits
uniform finite-prime bookkeeping, but does not estimate the unresolved g core.

Commutator reductions and the failure of boundary sparsity
----------------------------------------------------------
For any unit whole-argument phase sigma define the exact commutator
 [C_{alpha,chi},S_sigma]f(n)
 =sum_{d|n}k_{alpha,chi}(d)[sigma(n/d)-sigma(n)]f(n/d).
It has no d1 term, but generally has many other terms. The magnitude estimate
is at most twice the convolution norm; it gains no critical exponent by itself.
Relative to a completely multiplicative unit candidate eta, the exact defect is
 E_sigma,eta(n,d)=sigma(n/d)-sigma(n)eta(d),
 C_{alpha,chi}S_sigma=S_sigma C_{alpha,chi*eta}+R_sigma,eta,
where R is the divisor sum with that E coefficient. An EXACT multiplicative
phase (including a global minus sign) makes Rzero. Root pseudophases require
their actual defect, not an inferred multiplicative law.

ID59 has an immediate exact simplification with its defect retained. Put
a=-1/3,b=-1/6,c=-1/3 and tau=s2*s3. Then
 F59=S_tau C_{c,u}C_{-1/2,v5}M
       +[C_{c,u},S_tau]C_{-1/2,v5}M
       +C_{c,u}S_s2[C_{b,v5},S_s3]C_{a,v5}M.
This uses the true same-phase group law C_b C_a=C_{a+b}; it is not a claim
that the middle arithmetic sign can be erased. The first core has a simpler
two-kernel Euler product, while the other two terms are the precisely required
arithmetic correlation defects. Their mutual mixed terms belong to D(X).
The shifted-source argument law above supplies no subpower support bound for
either defect; actual source-sign correlations are needed.

ID53's purely geometric s already gives a rigorous obstruction to a generic
"only root boundaries matter" argument. Consider d2 and n=2m, and put
u=m^(2/3), kappa=2^(2/3), which lies strictly between1 and2. The relative
phase is (-1)^(floor(kappa*u)-floor(u)). On every full unit interval
u in[j,j+1), floor(u) is fixed while kappa*u spans length kappa>1. Each
parity of floor(kappa*u) occupies at least kappa-1 of that span. Thus for
E_sigma,eta with EITHER possible eta(2), the defect occupies at least
(kappa-1)/kappa of each u interval before endpoint rounding.
The map m=u^(3/2) has derivative comparable to sqrt(j) on that interval.
Rounding its finitely many interval endpoints costs O(1) integers per cell.
Summing the cells in a large m block loses only O(X^(2/3)) endpoints, versus
an order-X occupied measure. Hence for some fixed positive constant c and
all sufficiently large blocks the divisor2 defect is nonzero on at least
cX even outputs. Its nonzero coefficient in C_{1/3,1} is1/3.
The same conclusion holds whether eta(2) is+1 or-1: no multiplicative twist
turns this root-parity commutator into only sparse boundary impulses.

This proof counts geometric intervals only; it evaluates no actual mu or
output. It also explains why an additive first-difference root-localization
lemma from the old potential family cannot be transplanted into divisor
commutators, whose arguments n and n/d are multiplicatively separated.
It concerns one divisor coefficient, not a lower bound on the whole
commutator output; other divisor terms can still cancel through real arithmetic.

No generic compensating path swap is hidden here. Swapping d1 and d2 changes
A(d1)B(d2)s(d1*k) to A(d2)B(d1)s(d2*k), with the same outer t factor. For
a single prime allocation the two magnitudes are1/6 and1/3 in53, and1/3 and
1/6 in59. Unit signs cannot cancel unequal magnitudes. Other paths must be
kept; a universal pair-cancellation involution based on this swap is unavailable.
ID53's formal sum of orders1/6+1/3-1/2=0 likewise does not make an identity:
its prime phases differ and the two intervening signs have nonzero defects.

Recovery, finite head, and the precise generic obstruction
---------------------------------------------------------
Both certificates record orders[1,1,1] and recovery_power3. Each stage kernel
and inverse is dominated by d1=1 because its absolute alpha is at most1.
At sigma=1/2+epsilon, a divisor dilation has unsquared norm d^(-1-epsilon),
so forward and inverse have bound at most(1+1/epsilon)^3 uniformly in finite T.
The corresponding squared-energy recovery cost is(1+1/epsilon)^6. Neither
an algebraic sum of signed alphas nor a phase resemblance removes that cost.
Exact reductions would need new justified certificates, not reuse of a smaller
cost for the present double-interleaved programs.

There is, however, a rigorously sharper GENERAL majorant worth recording for
a future certificate version. Put beta=|alpha|. Term by term,
 |(alpha)_e|<= (beta)_e,
because |alpha+j|<=beta+j for every j>=0. The positive multiplicative
generalized-divisor kernel d_beta has Euler sum
 sum_d d_beta(d)/d^(1+epsilon)=zeta(1+epsilon)^beta.
Thus BOTH a convolution and its inverse have finite-horizon unsquared norm
at most zeta(1+epsilon)^|alpha|, with no source assumption. Interleaved unit
signs do not change this estimate. The full circuit's unsquared inverse bound
can therefore be sharpened mathematically to
 (1+1/epsilon)^Gamma, Gamma=sum_stages |alpha|.
Here Gamma=1 for53 and5/6 for59; squared recovery uses2Gamma. This is a
new derived coefficient-majorant lemma, not cancellation of signed orders or
erasure of a stage. The actual frozen certificates and their recorded power3
remain untouched and correct. A future restricted checker could certify the
fractional Gamma rule explicitly; current finite observations do not supply it.
The sharper epsilon cost improves recovery constants, but still does not
provide ANY bound for the transformed actual moments themselves.

The queued condition is ONE fixed circuit and an unbounded set of integers q:
for each such q a finite Cq independent of every dyadic X>=32 with
D(X)<=Cq*ceil(X^(1/q)). For any epsilon>0 choose an available q with1/q<2epsilon.
Its weighted dyadic tail is bounded by a convergent sum of
O_q(X^(1/q-2epsilon)). The finite n1..31 head must also be retained. The
inverse theorem then gives the full weighted L2 bound for actual M. This is
the conditional recovery bridge, not a proof of its premise. Both circuits
have F(1)=1 and D(1)=1 by their exact signs and k(1)=M(1)=1.

A precise generic obstruction limits what the existing primitives can prove.
Hold EVERY phase and kernel fixed, even the actual-Mobius-dependent ones,
and consider the linear circuit on an arbitrary formal input f(n)=n. It
satisfies the same envelope |f(n)|<=n. At, for example, epsilon=1/4 its
weighted squared norm grows as sum n^(-1/2), so is not finite as T grows.
Uniform inverse recovery forces its transformed weighted norm also to diverge.
If magnitude envelopes, unit signs and the existing recovery bound alone
proved cofinal subpower transformed moments for all such inputs, the dyadic
argument above would make that weighted norm finite, a contradiction.

This input is NOT the actual Mobius prefix and is NOT a numerical control
evaluation with re-bound source phases. It disproves a generic argument from
only those functional hypotheses; it neither refutes nor establishes the
actual-Mobius moment estimate. A valid closure must use the relation
M(n)-M(n-1)=mu(n), together with actual signed correlations beyond unit-phase
grammar. For59 those include shifted source signs at intermediate divisor
arguments and root-Mobius prime assignments. For53 they include the geometric
root phase, residue-character twists/local7 defect, and root-Mobius prime lift,
all correlated with the complete additive prefix. None is bounded by the
certificate or the derived sparse/character bookkeeping alone.

Concrete proof/search consequences
----------------------------------
An exact structural normalization rule can move completely multiplicative
signs through convolutions by twisting prime phases, delete a terminal unit
sign from the moment observer, and merge adjacent same-phase convolutions.
It must keep root/source commutator defects and finite bad-prime corrections.
This separates genuine interleaving from reducible sign/convolution syntax
without reading responses or choosing a favorite circuit.

For the present programs the useful next proof objects are the full P_n(k)
Gram form, its explicit tail/head correlation, the two59 commutator residuals,
and the53 character bulk plus local7-adic correction. The geometric positive-
density lemma rules out treating53's root commutator as bounded padding from
boundary counts. The prime-output lemma retains a necessary target component.
These are concrete reductions and obstructions; none closes the required
cofinal moment inequality for actual mu. All full mixed terms, all primes,
the finite head and recovery cost remain. No RH/global moment theorem is
asserted and no frozen source is changed.
