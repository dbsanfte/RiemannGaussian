SIGNED SPARSE RAW HEAD: DIAGONAL TRANSFER, SOURCE CLOSURES, AND LOCAL BARRIERS 067
============================================================================

Scope and disposition
---------------------
This is a source-only mathematical review. It reads the current
MOBIUS_SPARSE_PREFIX_HANDOFF.txt and MOBIUS_COMPLEMENTARY_CONVOLUTION_REVIEW_066.txt,
the source mobius_sparse_prefix_066.py, and the older source-only reviews
MOBIUS_PREFIX_COMPLETION_REVIEW_062.txt, MOBIUS_PREFIX_REPAIR_REVIEW_063.txt,
MOBIUS_QUADRATIC_CONTRACTION_REVIEW_064.txt,
MOBIUS_GROWING_CONDITIONING_REVIEW_065.txt, and
MOBIUS_WALSH_UNIFORM_PREFACTOR_REVIEW_065.txt. The older divisor-language
handoff was consulted to distinguish its finite cone obstruction from
the present raw source and positive-price obstructions.

No scientific result array, optimizer, numerical Mobius study, archived
candidate, artifact hash, or old test was read or executed. Only this
new review is written. No subagent is used. All arithmetic below is
ordinary mu; every channel, window, row, and lag shares the same sequence.
No RH assumption, Mertens bound, prime-distribution estimate, random-sign
model, or outside-prime deletion is invoked. The elementary estimates are
proved here, so no external arithmetic fact is needed.

The central new paid component is a uniform diagonal bound for dyadic
moments of the actual joined sparse RAW head. Its amplitude does not grow
with the number of tail dictionary entries. The resulting diagonal
budget is O(sqrt(N)*log(2N)^(3/2)) for every prefix. The unclosed leaf is
the aggregate positive part of the SIGNED off-diagonal correlations.
This leaf is sufficient, not necessary, for the requested raw-head bound.

A second paid component is an exact full-floor-closure bound controlled
by exceptional source support. It pays both endpoint prefix strips and
allows a detector to synthesize other source closures. Concrete barriers
show that a divisor auxiliary confined to small horizons, or a fixed
finite prime pairing followed by triangle, cannot pay the remaining
interior. Those are scoped barriers for positive proof prices, not lower
bounds for the signed raw head and not a refutation of RH.

1. Exact source, raw inverse, and cumulative tail kernel
-------------------------------------------------------
Fix p=3, q=5, integer N>=2, D=floor(4N/3), 1<=y<=D, and
r in[3/5,1]. The registered primes are still 2,3,5 in the old bridge;
the raw inverse in this review is UNMASKED ordinary mu, as in066.
Put

 W(x)=floor(4x)-3floor(2x)+2floor(x),
 F(d;r)=W(N/(3d))-r*W(N/(5d)),
 f_y(d;r)=1[d<=y]*F(d;r),                         d>=1.

Writing x=k+t, 0<=t<1, gives W(x)=floor(4t)-3floor(2t).
Its values on the four successive quarter intervals are 0,1,-1,0.
Consequently |F(d;r)|<=1+r<=2. F and f_y vanish when d>D.
All floors and every support inequality use these inclusive integer
conventions; empty intervals contribute zero.

Let the six full atoms be

 Z_(b,v)=floor(vN/b),
 lambda_(3,v)=a_v, lambda_(5,v)=-r*a_v,
 (v,a_v)=(4,1),(2,-3),(1,2).

Drop Z=0 and coalesce coincident Z before taking norms. Thus
F(d;r)=sum_Z lambda_Z(r)*floor(Z/d), and

 F_atom:=sum_Z |lambda_Z(r)|<=6*(1+r)<=12.          (1.1)

Let a_T(r) be the SIGNED COALESCED tail-step dictionary from066, so

 f_y(d;r)=F(d;r)+sum_T a_T(r)*1[d<=T].             (1.2)

The dictionary retains the common y threshold and all compensating tail
thresholds. Define its step function

 g(d;r):=sum_T a_T(r)*1[d<=T]
        =f_y(d;r)-F(d;r)
        =-1[d>y]*F(d;r).                         (1.3)

This is valid for every positive integer d, including d>D, where all
terms vanish. Therefore |g(d;r)|<=1+r<=2. This signed cumulative bound
is stronger than a bound on sum_T |a_T|, which can grow with N/y.

For j>=1 and n>=1 define the exact joined kernel

 kappa_j(n;r):=sum_T a_T(r)*1[floor(T/n)=j]
             =g(jn;r)-g((j+1)n;r).               (1.4)

The equality follows because
1[jn<=T]-1[(j+1)n<=T]=1[floor(T/n)=j]. The same assertion in interval
notation is floor(T/(j+1))<n<=floor(T/j); neither endpoint may be changed.
It follows from (1.3), BEFORE evaluating any ordinary-mu sign, that

 |kappa_j(n;r)|<=2*(1+r)<=4.                      (1.5)

The kernel is zero for n>floor(D/j), and is zero for all n when j>D.
In particular, no correlation below introduces an arithmetic radius
beyond the D already queried by066.

The full raw inverse coefficients are exactly

 c_j=d_j+z_j,
 d_j=sum_(Z=j)lambda_Z,
 z_j=sum_(n>=1)mu(n)*kappa_j(n;r),                (1.6)

with finite sums. The full-floor convolution mu*One=delta_1 contracts
each full atom to its coefficient at j=Z; the step inverse gives (1.4).
This is the SAME raw vector as066, not the core_inverse fill.
For any set of rows, sum |d_j|<=F_atom. The unit mu(1)=1 is included in
z_j; squareful n have their ordinary value zero.

2. A uniformly paid diagonal for the joined head
------------------------------------------------
Let I=[L,U] be any nonempty integer interval, L>=1. Define

 E_I=sum_(j in I) z_j^2,
 D_I=sum_(n>=1)mu(n)^2*sum_(j in I)kappa_j(n)^2.   (2.1)

D_I is the true arithmetic diagonal, not a free-sign expected moment.
Using (1.5), |mu|<=1, and coalescing before optional triangle gives

 D_I<=2*(1+r)*sum_(j in I,n>=1)|kappa_j(n)|
     <=2*(1+r)*sum_T |a_T|*
                      [floor(T/L)-floor(T/(U+1))]
     <=4*M1/L,                                  (2.2)

where M1=sum_T |a_T|*T is the coalesced dictionary's first moment.
The middle line is exact interval telescoping: a fixed T's consecutive
j windows are disjoint and their lengths sum to the displayed floors.
It also covers T<L and windows containing n=1. A finer finite paid
diagonal certificate is 2*(1+r)*sum_(j,n)|kappa_j(n)|; an actual squarefree
diagonal evaluation can be still smaller.

The066 source theorem, with all channels joined, proves

 M1<=12*(1/3+r/5)*N*
             [2+log max(1,D/(y+1))]
    <=(32/5)*N*[2+log max(1,D/(y+1))].            (2.3)

Equations (2.2)-(2.3) prove D_I=O(N*log(2N)/L), uniformly in EVERY
prefix and every r in[3/5,1]. No bound for E_I has been assumed here.

The distinction is important. A crude estimate
kappa^2 <= (sum|a_T|)*sum |a_T|*1_window
would introduce an unnecessary growing dictionary mass. Formula (1.3)
provides the independent source amplitude control that removes it.
The diagonal argument uses actual shared mu, and does not require
independence among different windows or channels.

3. Exact lag correlations and complete dyadic transfer
------------------------------------------------------
For h>=1 define the SIGNED lag correlation

 R_I(h)=sum_(j in I,n>=1)mu(n)*mu(n+h)*
                       kappa_j(n)*kappa_j(n+h),
 C_I=2*sum_(h>=1)R_I(h).                          (3.1)

Every sum is finite: for I starting at L, n and n+h need not exceed
floor(D/L). Expanding the actual squares in (2.1) gives exactly

 E_I=D_I+C_I>=0.                                 (3.2)

The factor two counts both ordered off-diagonal pairs. No row-specific
signs, no independent copies of mu, and no diagonal alias are introduced.
Equivalently the pair coefficient is

 Q_I(m,n)=sum_(j in I)kappa_j(m)*kappa_j(n),
 C_I=sum_(m!=n)mu(m)*mu(n)*Q_I(m,n).               (3.3)

The quotient form (1.4) shows the arithmetic geometry: individual terms
link m,n for which floor(T/m)=floor(U/n) belongs to the same block,
with ALL signed T,U terms summed. Taking absolute values separately at
T,U, individual lags, or independent rows loses legitimate cancellation.

Set H=floor(sqrt N), J=max(1,ceil(H/2)). Partition the entire head
[1,J-1] into dyadic intervals

 I_L=[L,min(2L-1,J-1)], L=1,2,4,..., L<J.

Let B be the number of nonempty intervals, B=0 when J=1. Each interval
has |I_L|<=L, and B<=1+floor(log_2(max(1,J-1))). Write C_I^+=max(C_I,0).
By Cauchy-Schwarz and sqrt(u+v)<=sqrt u+sqrt v for nonnegative u,v,

 sum_(1<=j<J)|c_j|
 <=F_head+sum_I sqrt(|I|*E_I)
 <=F_head+sum_I sqrt(|I|*D_I)
           +sum_I sqrt(|I|*C_I^+)
 <=F_head+2*B*sqrt(M1)+Risk_head,                  (3.4)

 F_head=sum_(1<=Z<J)|lambda_Z|<=12,
 Risk_head=sum_I sqrt(|I|*C_I^+).                 (3.5)

When C_I<0, (3.2) gives E_I<=D_I directly, so the use of C_I^+ in
(3.4) is valid. It does not assert that individual R_I(h) are positive.
The deterministic part 2*B*sqrt(M1) is uniformly
O(sqrt N*log(2N)^(3/2)). It is therefore O_epsilon(N^(1/2+epsilon))
for every positive epsilon. The direct floor atoms add at most12,
including any small-N atom lying in the head.

Thus the precise sufficient UNPAID leaf is

 Risk_head=O_epsilon(N^(1/2+epsilon))              (3.6)

uniformly in all required N,y at r=3/5 and r=1. This is an aggregate
signed-correlation risk bound. It does NOT require an assumed Chowla
estimate for each lag, or a sum of absolute individual correlations.
A stronger sufficient premise would bound each C_I^+ by
O_epsilon(N^(1+2epsilon)/L), but that stronger premise is not required.

The leaf is sufficient, not necessary. Dyadic Cauchy can overprice a
concentrated coefficient; a small head L1 does not imply this particular
aggregate block-energy risk bound. It must not be advertised as an
equivalent RH formulation or an already proved arithmetic theorem.

For the whole continuum of ratios one does not need to prove convexity
of C_I or Risk_head, which need not be convex. The complete c_j vector
is affine in r, so its head L1 is convex. Applying (3.4)-(3.6) at BOTH
endpoints with uniform constants bounds the head L1 for every r between
them. Endpoint moment certificates must correspond to the same source
and declared block rule. Convexity of the actual norm is the transfer.

Combining a genuine proof of (3.6) with066's terminal bound and the old
long coefficient bound would pay the complete raw prefix budget.
The existing conditional bridge would then need its usual source and
parameter admission. This review proves the diagonal component and
the conditional transfer, and DOES NOT prove (3.6) or RH.

If a compiler instead squares c_j=d_j+z_j, its energy is
E_I+2*sum_(j in I)d_j*z_j+sum_(j in I)d_j^2. Those terms must be kept
explicitly. Formula (3.4) avoids an unpriced direct-atom cross term by
separating the already paid floor coefficients with triangle. For finite
moment replay, compiling z_j from kappa and adding F_head is the clean
source-bound implementation of this theorem.

4. What a detector can synthesize from this transfer
---------------------------------------------------
This review supplies exact finite convolution and norm rules, not a
preselected actual-Mobius off-diagonal theorem. A useful search grammar
can have the following independently checkable construction objects:

 * A coalesced step dictionary and its cumulative source function g,
   with the identity (1.3) and the bound |g|<=1+r proved from W's profile.
 * Integer or rational row kernels from (1.4), with all windows and
   units retained. At endpoint r=3/5, a common denominator5 can be used
   for exact integer polynomial autocorrelation; it must be undone in
   every moment and price. Other rational sources need their actual
   common denominator rather than an assumed fixed scale.
 * Source-declared row blocks and source-declared lag groups. A grouping
   identity is signed: sum_h R_I(h) equals the sum of the group totals.
   Upper-bounding separate positive parts is valid but can be weaker
   than the single positive part in (3.5).
 * Candidate auxiliary controls on the signed group totals, with their
   own amplitude, support, initial-value, recurrence, or divisor-boundary
   certificates. The search should be able to propose these controls,
   rather than receiving a black box that assumes (3.6).
 * Exact independent energy replay, either from row squares or from
   direct shifted products, that verifies (3.2), the diagonal, the lag
   partition, and direct-atom handling.

All-scale admission of a control must prove its dependence on N,y,
not merely evaluate it at saved scales. The moment compiler can expose
the missing correlations and verify finite identities; an exact finite
C_I value is not an all-scale upper theorem. Rational lag partitions
chosen before arithmetic evaluation are legitimate detector templates;
post-evaluation choices need an explicitly declared selection rule.

The paid diagonal suggests where arithmetic effort belongs: the grammar
should seek an upper control of the complete signed C_I or its aggregate
risk. An unsigned triangle price for every pair can be much larger than
the already paid diagonal, so it is not an automatic closure.

4a. A paid band of nearby lags, with a cofinal cutoff parameter
--------------------------------------------------------------
There is a further noncircular partial correlation theorem. For every
integer h>=1, (1.5) gives

 |R_I(h)|<=sum_(j in I,n>=1)|kappa_j(n)*kappa_j(n+h)|
          <=4*sum_(j in I,n>=1)|kappa_j(n)|
          <=4*M1/L.                              (4a.1)

The first line is an independently computable KERNEL-OVERLAP envelope
and can be smaller than the following uniform bounds. Ordinary-mu
squareful factors only contract it. No signed fixed-lag estimate is
assumed. This argument retains the complete coalesced kernel at both
arguments; independently splitting its T channels would be weaker.

Choose any declared integer lag cutoff K>=0. Let

 C_I^far(K)=2*sum_(h>K)R_I(h).

The exact near/far splitting, (2.2), and (4a.1) prove

 E_I<=((4+8K)*M1/L)+C_I^far(K)
     <=((4+8K)*M1/L)+max(C_I^far(K),0).           (4a.2)

If the near signed sum is negative, replacing it by its positive
envelope is still valid. If the far signed sum is negative, its
positive part vanishes and the same bound remains valid. Hence the
ENTIRE raw head has the complete transfer

 sum_(1<=j<J)|c_j|
 <=F_head+B*sqrt((4+8K)*M1)+Risk_far(K),
 Risk_far(K)=sum_I sqrt(|I|*max(C_I^far(K),0)).    (4a.3)

Here B still counts dyadic ROW blocks; K counts near LAGS. They must not
be confused. The case K=0 is exactly the unsplit theorem (3.4).
The cutoff may exceed a block's maximum possible lag; absent lags are
zero, and (4a.2) is still valid though needlessly coarse.

For any positive rational delta choose K=floor(N^delta). By (2.3),
the PAID part of (4a.3) is

 O_delta(N^((1+delta)/2)*log(2N)^(3/2)).           (4a.4)

The exponent cost delta/2 is explicit. A single fixed positive delta
does not provide every epsilon. A cofinal declared sequence of positive
rational deltas tending to0 permits the correct quantifier transfer:
if for each delta in that sequence and every eta>0 one independently
proves

 Risk_far(floor(N^delta))
   =O_(delta,eta)(N^((1+delta)/2+eta))             (4a.5)

uniformly in N,y at both r endpoints, then for any requested epsilon
choose delta<epsilon and eta<epsilon-delta/2. Absorb the paid logarithms
using the remaining positive exponent gap. Formula (4a.3) then pays the
requested O_epsilon(N^(1/2+epsilon)) head. Constants may depend on the
chosen delta and eta, which is allowed; no uniform constant as delta->0
has been assumed.

Thus a detector can treat arbitrary rational delta as a construction
parameter, with exact floors and charged bit/arithmetic capacities.
There is no mathematical denominator whitelist. A finite pilot list of
deltas is only a search sample; it does not by itself establish the
cofinal family or (4a.5). The far risk remains OPEN. The merit of this
partial theorem is that a whole small band of lags is already paid by
source geometry, without demanding a separate Chowla-type statement
for any fixed lag. It also allows future controls to be searched on
larger signed lag groups, with their complete boundaries retained.

5. Full-floor closure with an exceptional source: exact theorem
--------------------------------------------------------------
There is another reusable search rule independent of moment estimates.
Let v(d) be any finite source supported on [1,X], and define its ordinary
full inverse

 A_v(j)=sum_(n<=X/j)mu(n)*v(jn),
 c_v(j)=A_v(j)-A_v(j+1), A_v(X+1)=0.              (5.1)

Every row, including the virtual final row, is present. Finite triangle
and divisor counting give

 ||c_v||_1<=2*sum_(j<=X)|A_v(j)|
          <=2*sum_(d<=X)|v(d)|*tau(d).           (5.2)

A slightly sharper positive envelope, if useful to an exact detector, is

 ||c_v||_1<=sum_(d<=X)|v(d)|*
                     [2*2^omega(d)-mu(d)^2].    (5.3)

Indeed A_v(1) occurs only once in the full variation triangle, every
A_v(j), j>=2, occurs twice, and sum_(n|d)|mu(n)|=2^omega(d).
All the values in this refinement are unsigned factorization data.
The simpler tau rule is sufficient for the all-scale conclusions below.

Now let a detector construct an arbitrary finite full-floor closure

 Q(d;r)=sum_X zeta_X(r)*floor(X/d),
 e(d;r)=f_y(d;r)-Q(d;r).                          (5.4)

Use D'=max(D,max used X); f_y is zero beyond D, but e need NOT be
zero there if a larger horizon is introduced. Coalesce zeta and retain
all d<=D'. Ordinary full convolution makes Q's inverse the coefficients
zeta_X at j=X. Linearity and (5.2) prove

 ||raw(f_y)||_1
 <=sum_X |zeta_X|+2*sum_(d<=D')tau(d)*|e(d)|.     (5.5)

This is an exact complete RAW source bound. It does not change the fill,
assume an inverse norm, omit a boundary, or inherit a bound from a
different source. It bounds the head as a subset as well as the whole
raw coefficient norm. A candidate Q whose exceptional source is bounded
and sparse provides a noncircular uniform certificate.

The detector can search zero, the six-full-floor closure, signed
subcombinations of those atoms, and other declared finite full-floor
rules. The cost of e stays inside (5.5); a partial floor subcombination
can have a large value near d=1 and must not inherit |e|<=2 without proof.
For an affine zeta vector, the full RHS of (5.5) is convex in r when e
is taken from the same affine source. Checking both endpoint costs bounds
every r in[3/5,1]. This uses the complete vectors and common support.

6. Exact paid lower and upper prefix strips
-------------------------------------------
For Q=0, |e(d)|<=1+r and support d<=y. Since

 sum_(d<=y)tau(d)=sum_(a<=y)floor(y/a)
                <=y*(1+log y),                 (6.1)

the zero closure gives the universal complete raw bound

 ||raw(f_y)||_1<=2*(1+r)*y*(1+log y)
               <=4*y*(1+log y).                 (6.2)

It pays all y<=sqrt N*log(2N)^A for any fixed A, with the usual
O_epsilon(N^(1/2+epsilon)) rate. This extends the already known y<=H
empty-long branch by a legitimate polylogarithmic prefix strip.

For Q=F, e=-1[d>y]*F, so (1.1),(5.5) give

 ||raw(f_y)||_1<=6*(1+r)
                   +2*(1+r)*sum_(y<d<=D)tau(d).
                                                        (6.3)

There is an exact upper-exit chamber. Put

 A_exit=max(floor(2N/3),floor(4N/5))=floor(4N/5).

For every integer A_exit<d<=D,
floor(4N/(3d))=1, floor(2N/(3d))=floor(N/(3d))=0,
and all three q-channel floors are zero. Hence F(d;r)=1 independently
of r. For any y>=A_exit, the complete exceptional source is exactly
-1 on (y,D] and zero elsewhere. The stronger bound is

 ||raw(f_y)||_1<=6*(1+r)+2*sum_(y<d<=D)tau(d).      (6.4)

It includes y=D, when there is no exception. The inclusive floor
definition of A_exit avoids losing a row when a real exit is an integer.

For completeness, an elementary all-scale divisor bound is as follows.
For delta>0 let B_delta=ceil(2^(1/delta)) and

 C_delta=product_(prime ell<B_delta)(1-ell^(-delta))^(-2).

This is a finite explicit constant. For ell>=B_delta, e+1<=2^e
<=ell^(delta*e). For each smaller prime,
(e+1)*ell^(-delta*e)<=sum_(a>=0)(a+1)*ell^(-delta*a)
=(1-ell^(-delta))^(-2). Multiplying over prime powers proves

 tau(d)<=C_delta*d^delta                         (6.5)

for every positive d, without a prime-distribution theorem. Thus (6.3)
and (6.4) pay every upper prefix strip

 D-y<=sqrt N*log(2N)^A,

at O_epsilon(N^(1/2+epsilon)), for every fixed A. Choose delta smaller
than epsilon and absorb the fixed logarithmic power in the remaining
exponent. The finitely many small N require no missing asymptotic branch;
(6.3) remains an exact valid inequality there.

More generally, (5.5),(6.5) pay any independently proved exceptional
weighted support sum sum_d |e(d)|=O(sqrt N*polylog N), for D'=O(N).
Support cardinality alone is enough when |e| has a fixed uniform bound.
This is a reusable auxiliary control a detector can invent: construct
a full-floor closure, then prove the size and amplitude of its exceptions.

7. Source chambers and exact divisor costs without enumerating each row
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
For one horizon Z and h>=1, floor(Z/d)=h precisely on the integer interval

 [floor(Z/(h+1))+1,floor(Z/h)].                    (7.1)

The h=0 interval starts at Z+1. Group equal quotient intervals, and
intersect their endpoints over the source and candidate horizons.
Also include the source cut y+1, the exit D+1, and D'+1. Each resulting
common chamber [a,b] has an exact constant value

 e(d;r)=u_[a,b]-r*v_[a,b]                        (7.2)

when the closure has the same affine channel convention; a general
affine zeta gives an equivalent affine constant. The exceptional support
is the union of chambers on which that constant is nonzero. Its exact
cardinality is the sum of b-a+1 over those chambers, with no per-d
source evaluation and no ordinary-mu sign table.

Define T_tau(x)=sum_(d<=x)tau(d), T_tau(0)=0. The exceptional cost in
(5.5) is exactly

 2*sum_chambers |e_[a,b](r)|*[T_tau(b)-T_tau(a-1)]. (7.3)

The identity T_tau(x)=sum_(a<=x)floor(x/a) permits independent quotient
grouping. A single floor has at most 2*floor(sqrt Z)+1 distinct positive
quotient breakpoints, by splitting denominator indices at sqrt Z.
Thus finitely many declared horizons yield a sparse chamber description;
there is no obligation to enumerate all D' integers merely to certify
source support or a tau-weighted cost. Endpoint costs remain exact
rationals, because all chamber values at rational r are rational.

Constructing the chambers and exact divisor summatory values is finite
verification. To claim a uniform rate the detector must also prove a
symbolic size or weighted support bound, as in the two strips above.
Observing a small chamber cost at a few N does not supply that theorem.

8. A concrete joined interior source for barrier certificates
------------------------------------------------------------
Take N=15k, k>=1, so D=20k. Choose y=16k=4D/5. The q-channel exit
is 4N/q=12k; y is STRICTLY ABOVE it. Also 2N/p=10k<y.
Thus the full source tail is exactly1 on (16k,20k], independently of r.
The sparse dictionary is exactly

 a_(16k)=+1, a_(20k)=-1,                         (8.1)

at BOTH r endpoints and throughout the entire interval. This does not
delete a q term; the q source is already zero on this exact tail.
The six full horizons are 20k,10k,5k,12k,6k,3k, so d_1=0.
The first head kernel, whenever J>1, is

 kappa_1(n)=1[8k<n<=10k]-1[16k<n<=20k].           (8.2)

The overlap on (10k,16k] has cancelled BEFORE norms. The two remaining
intervals are disjoint and have integer lengths2k and4k. For all
sufficiently large k, row1 belongs to the required head with H=floor
sqrt(15k), J=ceil(H/2). The example is not an isolated indicator
substitute: it is the actual joined truncated W source.

9. Divisor-annihilator auxiliary: support barrier and full-span limit
--------------------------------------------------------------------
For any finite signed b(m), the full ordinary divisor identity gives

 sum_n mu(n)*sum_(m:n|m)b(m)=b(1).

For a kernel w(n), set

 residual(n)=w(n)-sum_(m:n|m)b(m).

Then exactly

 sum_n mu(n)w(n)=b(1)+sum_n mu(n)residual(n),
 |sum_n mu(n)w(n)|<=|b(1)|+sum_n mu(n)^2*|residual(n)|.  (9.1)

This is a valid actual-Mobius search primitive. An unweighted residual
price sum|residual| is also valid but weaker. It is essential that each
divisor sum is complete; clipping it at small n destroys the contraction
and leaves an additional boundary obligation.

If b is supported on m<=L, then residual(n)=w(n) for every n>L.
For (8.2), if L<=16k the residual price includes all squarefree n in
(16k,20k]. An elementary lower estimate is enough. There are4k integers
in this interval. A non-squarefree integer has a prime-square divisor;
the number divisible by a^2 in an interval of length4k is at most
4k/a^2+1. Summing over primes<=sqrt(20k), and bounding their sum by
all integers a>=2, gives

 #squarefree n in(16k,20k]
 >=4k*(1-sum_(a>=2)a^(-2))-floor(sqrt(20k))
 >=k-floor(sqrt(20k)).                            (9.2)

The last inequality uses sum_(a>=2)a^(-2)<=1/4+integral_2^infty t^(-2)dt
=3/4. Consequently this restricted auxiliary grammar has a linear
positive-price lower bound at the actual source. In particular a
horizon L=O(sqrt N*polylog N) cannot pay the whole head uniformly.
This remains true even though the source kernels and channels were
joined exactly before the residual norm.

The barrier is scoped to the stated support cap and residual triangle
price. It does not obstruct auxiliaries with current-scale horizons,
signed residual moment controls, or a different admitted source closure.
It is NOT a lower bound for |sum mu*w|: the signed ordinary-mu interval
may cancel strongly.

There is also an exact warning at the opposite extreme. Restrict to
squarefree n<=X and allow every squarefree divisor column m<=X.
The columns 1[n|m] form an invertible triangular zeta matrix. Its inverse
has coefficients mu(m/n) on comparable squarefree indices. Therefore
there is a representation with zero residual and

 b(1)=sum_(n<=X)mu(n)w(n).

The optimum in (9.1), with unrestricted coefficients and all those
columns, is exactly the absolute target: the lower inequality follows
from (9.1), and the full zeta inverse attains it. A primal/dual search
over the complete current-scale span can certify finite bounds, but
does not itself provide an independent all-scale estimate.

The dual explanation is equally exact. With all m>1 columns present,
the dual equations sum_(n|m)z_n=0 force z_n=mu(n)*z_1 recursively.
With only m<=L columns, every coordinate n>L is unconstrained by those
equations; placing z_n=sign(w(n)) there and zero below L gives the
support-frontier lower certificate. Squarefree residual coordinates
use the same reasoning with their exact squarefree support mask.

For the vector head, direct d_j can be incorporated into w_j at n=1,
because mu(1)=1. Independent full-span row programs then reproduce the
exact head norm. An all-scale theorem still requires a constrained,
source-bound auxiliary rule and an independently paid residual control.

10. Exact fixed-prime pairing and a positive-price barrier
--------------------------------------------------------
Let P be a FIXED finite set of ordinary primes, R=product_(p in P)p,
and E_p w(m)=w(pm). Splitting the ordinary mu sum by p valuation gives

 sum_n mu(n)w(n)=sum_(p not dividing m)mu(m)*[w(m)-w(pm)].  (10.1)

Terms of valuation>=2 have mu=0. Repeating this for distinct primes,
with the rough mask retained, proves

 sum_n mu(n)w(n)
 =sum_(gcd(m,R)=1)mu(m)*Delta_P w(m),
 Delta_P=product_(p in P)(I-E_p).                  (10.2)

Every dilation is finite because w has finite support. The unit and
all outside primes are retained. This is an actual ordinary-mu identity,
not a free-sign relaxation. A natural paid positive price is

 Price_P(w)=sum_(gcd(m,R)=1)mu(m)^2*|Delta_P w(m)|. (10.3)

For (8.2), every m in(16k,20k] has w(m)=-1, and every nontrivial
dilation in Delta_P has argument>=2m>20k, hence value0. Thus
Delta_P w(m)=-1 on that entire interval for any fixed P.

The number of integers in an interval of length B that are coprime
to fixed R is at least B*phi(R)/R-phi(R). For a prime ell not dividing
R, CRT shows that the number which are coprime to R and divisible by
ell^2 is at most B*phi(R)/(R*ell^2)+phi(R). A non-squarefree R-rough
integer has such a prime-square divisor. With B=4k and upper endpoint
20k, the same union bound as (9.2) therefore proves

 # {16k<m<=20k: gcd(m,R)=1, mu(m)^2=1}
 >=k*phi(R)/R-phi(R)*(1+floor(sqrt(20k))).          (10.4)

For FIXED P this is Omega_P(k). Consequently the exact fixed-prime
pairing followed by the positive price (10.3) cannot prove the desired
square-root head rate uniformly. The calculation preserves the joined
source, uses its actual squarefree mask, and assumes no prime-counting
asymptotic.

Combining the same fixed-prime pairing with low-horizon divisor
annihilators does not remove this survivor: a divisor column supported
below L has no value at a rough m>L, and its prime-dilated values cannot
gain support there. In the grammar consisting of (10.2), auxiliaries
supported at m<=L<=16k, and residual triangle, the same lower bound
survives. This statement does not cover arbitrary unrestricted arithmetic
controls or new signed correlation certificates.

The constants in (10.4) depend on the ENTIRE fixed R. Replacing P by all
primes through a growing cutoff and reusing this lower bound would be
invalid. Growing conditioning can change the arithmetic and resource
regime, and is outside this scoped no-go theorem. Fully conditioning all
needed factors can simply compute the exact target, as the full-span
annihilator limit does; it supplies no growth theorem by itself.

This is different from064's fixed-sign obstruction for a surrogate
indicator and from065's growing-conditioning core_inverse analysis.
It is a positive-price barrier for the actual joined raw source. It
also does NOT obstruct the diagonal route: for row1 a diagonal of
order N already has the desired square-root price after taking a square
root. The missing work there is precisely the signed off-diagonal upper
control, not a positive interval-length bound.
