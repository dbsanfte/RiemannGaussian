1. A SHARP ARITHMETIC SCORE ENVELOPE

The following statements concern the zeta input f(n)=1, not generic
normalized units. Fix 0<alpha<1 and n>=2. Put p=P^-(n), the actual
smallest prime factor of n, and g=gcd of its prime exponents.
The binomial/Jordan expansion in fractional_prime_proof.txt gives

 |B_alpha(n)| <= (1/g)*|R_alpha(n^(1/g))|.                  (1)

Indeed, every binomial coefficient b_j and finite-difference term T_j
is nonnegative, |J_(j-1)(g)|<=g^(j-1), and T_j is homogeneous of degree j.
The triangle inequality and rescaling give (1), including prime powers.
All terms have absolutely convergent expansions, even at the endpoint.

The Bernstein integral for |Lambda_alpha(m)| has an exponential factor
bounded by one and a product of positive factors 1-q^(-t), each <=1.
Retaining only the factor belonging to P^-(m) therefore gives
|Lambda_alpha(m)|<=(log P^-(m))^alpha. For m=n^(1/g), the smallest prime
is still p. Divide by (log m)^alpha and apply (1):

 |B_alpha(n)| <= g^(alpha-1)*(log p/log n)^alpha
                <= (log P^-(n)/log n)^alpha.               (2)

This holds for every integer and every alpha in the stated interval.
It supplies actual prime information at all scales, although computing
the coefficients still contains arithmetic work. It is a coefficient
envelope, not a primality-speed claim or a zeta zero-free theorem.

The exponent alpha is optimal for this envelope. At n=2*q with actual
primes q tending to infinity, set t=log 2/log(2*q). Then
|B_alpha(2*q)|=t^alpha+(1-t)^alpha-1=t^alpha-alpha*t+O(t^2).
Its ratio to t^alpha tends to one. Every exponent theta>alpha would
therefore fail eventually along actual primes. Only their elementary
unboundedness is used; no new quantitative prime-distribution theorem.

3. THE IMPORTED PRIME-DISTRIBUTION THEOREM AND ITS DOMAIN

For x>=y>=2 let Phi(x,y) count positive integers <=x with no prime factor
<=y, including 1. We use the published unconditional statement

 Phi(x,y)<x/log y.                                        (3)

Source: Kai (Steve) Fan and Carl Pomerance, An inequality related to the
sieve of Eratosthenes, author version dated 9 August 2023, Theorem A,
page 1. That theorem is attributed there to Fan's earlier work.
https://math.dartmouth.edu/~carlp/erat060523rev.pdf
The primary PDF is cached in literature/roughness/fan_pomerance_rough_numbers.pdf.
Fan's later primary paper also explicitly states its uniform range:
https://stvfan.github.io/files/papers/Numerically%20explicit%20estimates%20for%20the%20distribution%20of%20rough%20numbers.pdf

We import the theorem as existing mathematics. Its full sieve proof was
not independently reverified or theorem-prover formalized here. We use
the constant one, not the sharper constant with small-case exceptions.
For 1<y<2, (3) also holds by the trivial count Phi(x,y)<=x and log y<1.
No RH hypothesis is imported.

4. A GENUINE ALL-SCALE GLOBAL MOMENT BOUND

For integer X>=2, integer k>=1 and gamma=2*k*alpha, define

 M_(2k)(X)=sum_(X<=n<=2X)|B_alpha(n)|^(2k).

Let v_n=min(1,log P^-(n)/log X). Since log n>=log X, (2) implies
|B_alpha(n)|^(2k)<=v_n^gamma. The layer-cake identity for a finite sum is

 sum v_n^gamma = integral_0^1 gamma*t^(gamma-1)*#{v_n>t} dt.

For 0<t<1, v_n>t implies P^-(n)>X^t. The count is therefore bounded by
Phi(2X,X^t)<2X/(t*log X), using (3) and its trivial extension. If gamma>1,
the resulting integrable envelope yields

 M_(2k)(X) <= [2*gamma/(gamma-1)]*X/log X, EVERY X>=2.      (4)

In particular, for alpha=1/2 and k=2,

 sum_(X<=n<=2X)|B_(1/2)(n)|^4 <= 4*X/log X.               (5)

This is an all-scale upper bound for the actual arithmetic coefficient
family, not an extrapolation from a finite fit. It uses existing
prime-distribution information through (3), so it does not supply a new
independent prime estimate. For example, prime counts bounded by (5)
are a consequence of an existing sieve theorem, not a new density result.

5. THE CRITICAL COST AND THE WEAKER REGIME

For X>=3 put L=log X>1 and split the envelope at t0=1/L. Use the trivial
count <=2X below t0 and (3) above it. This gives

 M_(2k)(X) <= 2X*L^(-gamma)
               +(2*gamma*X/L)*integral_(1/L)^1 t^(gamma-2)dt.

At gamma=1 this is 2X*(1+log log X)/log X. For 0<gamma<1 it is at most
2X/[(1-gamma)*(log X)^gamma]. The X=2 case is covered explicitly by
the trivial bound 2X. The program retains these scale conditions and
does not substitute the divergent gamma>1 formula at the endpoint.

The loglog cost belongs to this positive-envelope proof. It does NOT
prove that the actual critical moment cannot satisfy a stronger bound
through additional arithmetic structure. A finite constant fit cannot
justify discarding the envelope's logarithmic loss.

6. FOURIER FAMILY, WEIGHTS AND ZERO INFORMATION

For weights 0<=w_n<=1 on a subset of [X,2X], the weighted moment is at
most the unweighted moment in (4). Define
A_u=sum sqrt(w_n)*B_alpha(n)^k*exp(2*pi*i*u*n/H).
With distinct support residues modulo H, complete orthogonality gives
sum_u|A_u|^2=H*sum w_n|B_alpha(n)|^(2k). The simple sufficient condition
H>=X+1 covers the complete integer doubling window, including both ends.
Hence the alpha=1/2, k=2 family satisfies

 sum_(u=0)^(H-1)|A_u|^2 <=4*H*X/log X.                     (6)

Composite moduli are allowed. Repeated residues require a separate
collision bound; the public scale compiler rejects H<X+1 for the
complete window. The arbitrary-weight count transfer has a broader
domain than this bounded-weight energy certificate; they must not be
silently identified.

These coefficientwise powers and Fourier energies have no certified
ordinary-zero bridge. The two retained branches for the original B_half
do not automatically survive taking Hadamard powers. The positive moment
bound gives no RH error term or exclusion of off-line ordinary zeros.
