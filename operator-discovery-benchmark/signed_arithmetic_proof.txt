SIGNED ARITHMETIC KERNEL CERTIFICATES: SUPPLIED LEMMAS AND UNKNOWN REMAINDER

Status and provenance.
This new backend supplies universal algebra and elementary actual-arithmetic
lemmas. A numerical learner selects frozen rational kernel parameters; these
lemmas do not infer those parameters or supply a nominated winning vector.
Numerical tests verify implementations, not global estimates. No unsmoothed
critical Mobius bound or RH proof is claimed. Previous frozen stages are
unchanged. The coprime-squarefree theorem and its usual dyadic defect instance
were already available in the critical-defect stage; the reusable arbitrary
kernel certificate and explicit obligations generalize that component method.

1. Kernel syntax, exact moments, and radical certificates.
A kernel w is a continuous polygon with rational knots (a_i,y_i),
0=a_0<a_1<...<a_m=L, y_m=0, and w(t)=0 for t>=L. Its domain is t>=0.
The left value y_0 can be nonzero; this creates no discrepancy boundary cost
because E_k(0)=0. The certificate records whether BOTH endpoint values vanish,
but only the compact-support/right endpoint must vanish. Zero extension to
negative t is not part of this kernel's declared domain.

Integral w = sum_i (a_(i+1)-a_i)(y_i+y_(i+1))/2, exactly rational.
For homogeneous W_X(t)=X w(t/X), every real X>0 is allowed.
Define V(w)=Integral_0^L sqrt(t)|dw|(t). For slope m_i on [a,b], its
contribution is |m_i|(2/3)(b sqrt(b)-a sqrt(a)). The backend encloses each
radical using integer squares, never floating comparisons: for v=p/q>=0 in
lowest terms and B=2^bits, j=isqrt(p q B^2),
 j/(qB) <= sqrt(v) <= (j+1)/(qB),
with identical bounds if j^2=p q B^2. Consequently substituting the upper
radical at b and the lower radical at a is a rigorous rational upper bound
U(w)>=V(w). Precision bits belongs to the certificate; it changes the valid
upper constant, not the theorem. AST and all segment evidence are recomputed
on verification, so altering a moment, slope or cost cannot be admitted.

Dilate syntax is arbitrary rational positive s and rational c:
 w(t)=sum_s c_s K_s(t), K_s(t)=(2s-max(s,t))_+.
Knots are the union of 0,s,2s. Its exact integral is
 (3/2)sum_s c_s s^2.
Learned rational zero-moment combinations are admitted without sign restrictions.
No particular vector is required for the component theorem. General polygons
can be admitted by their rational integral even when they have no dilate form.
Nonzero-integral kernels have a density main term and are not admitted as
zero-moment cancellation components. Identity/zero kernels remain valid but
provide no information about the dense arithmetic remainder.

2. Actual coprime-squarefree discrepancy, uniform with an explicit charge.
Mu is the COMPLETE Dirichlet inverse of One on positive integers, so
sum_(d|n) mu(d)=1_(n=1). Finite induction or factorization gives its usual
squarefree values and multiplicativity. A matching finite array cannot
establish this all-scale provenance. The backend's finite evaluations verify
complete divisor constraints through the entire required endpoint.
For integer k>=1 and real y>=0, define
 Q_k(y)=sum_(1<=g<=y,(g,k)=1) mu(g)^2,
 c_k=(1/zeta(2))*product_(p|k) p/(p+1), A_k=2^omega(k)+2.
Then |Q_k(y)-c_k y|<=A_k sqrt(y).
Proof: mu(g)^2=sum_(d^2|g)mu(d). Only (d,k)=1 contributes. Inclusion-exclusion
counts integers j<=y/d^2 coprime to k as (phi(k)/k)y/d^2 with error at most
2^omega(k). There are at most sqrt(y) d. Extending the main sum to infinity
costs at most 2sqrt(y), since for y>=1 the absolute d^-2 tail beyond floor
sqrt(y) is at most 2/sqrt(y). The absolutely convergent Euler product gives
c_k above. For 0<=y<1, Q_k=0 and c_k<=1 complete the claim. No prime number
theorem, random signs or zero-free region is used. Distinct-prime count and
trial-factorization operation count are exposed; growth of k never disappears
into a fixed uniform constant.

For a continuous BV f on [0,Y], E_k=Q_k-c_k t obeys
 sum_(g<=Y,(g,k)=1)mu(g)^2 f(g)-c_k Integral_0^Y f(t)dt
 =f(Y)E_k(Y)-Integral_0^Y E_k(t)df(t).
This uses right-value integer atoms, continuous f, and E_k(0)=0, so there are
no unspecified jump corrections. Polygon weights satisfy all these conditions.
Put Y=L X/r and f(g)=X w(rg/X), integer r>=1. If Integral w=0, f(Y)=0,
the density term vanishes exactly. The substitution t=rg/X gives
 Integral_0^Y sqrt(g)|df(g)| = X^(3/2) V(w)/sqrt(r).
Therefore for EVERY real X>0, EVERY positive integer k,r, actual Mu,
 |sum_(g>=1,(g,k)=1)mu(g)^2 X w(rg/X)|
 <= A_k U(w) X^(3/2)/sqrt(r).
This is the global component theorem. The backend also supplies an entirely
rational upper coefficient by enclosing sqrt(1/r). It does NOT bound the
signed combination over all primitive pairs. Its global flag is conditional
only on the checked kernel conditions and declared actual Mu provenance;
the always-false full-signed-bound flag has a different scope.

3. Exact full coupled Mobius energy and partition.
Write M(n)=sum_(j<=n)mu(j), M(0)=0 and
 A(N)=sum_(n=N)^(2N-1)M(n)^2, integer N>=1.
K_N(m) counts exactly the n in [N,2N) with n>=m, at every integer m>=1;
its value at 2N is zero. Expanding the COMPLETE square gives
 A(N)=sum_(a,b>=1)mu(a)mu(b)K_N(max(a,b)).
Thus if sX are integers,
 F(X)=sum_s c_s A(sX)
     =sum_(a,b>=1)mu(a)mu(b) X w(max(a,b)/X).
All cross terms are present; these expressions do not treat the signs as
independent. If scales are arbitrary rationals and sX is not integer, the
quadratic polygon expression remains well-defined but must not be relabeled
as an integer block energy A(sX). The learner/root must declare this domain.

For arbitrary polygon w, X a positive integer, let T=floor(LX) and
W(n)=X w(n/X). Then an O(T) independent evaluation is
 F_w(X)=sum_(n=1)^T M(n)^2 [W(n)-W(n+1)].
This follows by telescoping the increments M(n)^2-M(n-1)^2; W(T+1)=0.
It is valid even when T is a zero-weight endpoint and when slopes change at
noninteger locations. The arithmetic evaluation cost is not a cancellation
estimate.

For every a<b, the UNIQUE substitution a=g u,b=g r has gcd(u,r)=1,u<r.
Squarefreeness gives exactly
 mu(gu)mu(gr)=mu(u)mu(r)mu(g)^2 1_((g,ur)=1).
Hence for any ONE shared integer cutoff B>=0,
 F_w(X)=D_w(X)+P_(w,B)(X)+R_(w,B)(X),
 D=sum_n mu(n)^2 W(n),
 P=2sum_(u<r<=B,(u,r)=1)mu(u)mu(r)
                  sum_(g>=1,(g,ur)=1)mu(g)^2 W(gr),
 R=2sum_(u<r,(u,r)=1,r>B)mu(u)mu(r)
                  sum_(g>=1,(g,ur)=1)mu(g)^2 W(gr).
All sums have gr<=LX; larger terms vanish. No pair is lost, no independent
cutoff is applied per dilate, and no sign is asserted for R. Finite R can be
computed EXACTLY as F-D-P; this avoids dense pair enumeration and does not
replace its mathematical obligation by a presumed sign. Tests independently
enumerate dense pairs and compare every residual at small endpoints.

For a zero-moment kernel the already-proved component theorem yields
 |D|<=3 U(w) X^(3/2),
 |P|<=2 U(w) X^(3/2) S_B,
 S_B=sum_(u<r<=B,(u,r)=1)|mu(u)mu(r)|[2^omega(ur)+2]/sqrt(r)
     <=3 B^(3/2)(1+log B)^2 for B>=1.
Indeed 2^omega(ur)+2<=3 tau(u)tau(r),
sum_(u<r)tau(u)<=r(1+log r), and
sum_(r<=B)tau(r)<=B(1+log B) prove this bound. B=0 or 1 has empty P.
B=floor(X^(1/3)) gives O_w(X^2(1+log X)^2); every fixed positive epsilon
absorbs its logarithm with a finite epsilon-dependent constant. If w or its
term count/coefficients vary with X or q, U(w) and all costs must be charged.
The dense signed R remains entirely unbounded at critical strength here.

4. Conditional positive dyadic stencil bridge; not an admitted inequality.
After merging equal scales and removing zero coefficients, check scales are
integer powers of two, S=max s>1, c_S=1, lower c_s<=0 and sum c_s s^2=0.
Then p_s=(-c_s)s^2/S^2>=0 and sum_(s<S)p_s=1. IF there is a finite C>=0
independent of X such that for EVERY dyadic integer X>=1,
 sum c_s A(sX)<=C X^(2+epsilon), epsilon>0,
then E(n)=A(n)/n^2 obeys, at n=SX,
 E(n)<=sum p_s E(sn/S)+(C/S^(2+epsilon))n^epsilon.
Every argument sn/S is a smaller dyadic integer. Keeping
H=max_(dyadic 1<=n<S) E(n), a running-maximum induction gives the safe bound
 E(n)<=H+C n^epsilon/[S^2(1-2^(-epsilon))]
for every dyadic n>=1. Initial scales are finite and cannot be dropped.
A ceil RHS C X^2 ceil(X^epsilon) is covered by replacing C with 2C.
Mixed lower signs or non-dyadic scales do not receive THIS bridge; they still
receive their exact kernel and valid component certificates. The backend
labels this bridge conditional and never marks its premise proved.

An RH-strength conclusion requires a positive schedule epsilon(q)->0,
EVERY integer q>=q0, finite C_q independent of X, and ALL dyadic X. A finite
q table does not supply this premise or its cofinal tail. These energy bounds
would give the usual zero-exclusion bridge: for any sigma>1/2 choose epsilon
small enough that sum over dyadic blocks of M(n)^2 n^(-2sigma'-1) converges
with 1/2<sigma'<sigma. Cauchy then gives absolute convergence of the M-Mellin
integral in Re(s)>1/2, representing 1/zeta(s) from its original Re(s)>1
identity. Holomorphy excludes zeros there, and the functional equation gives
RH. This conditional reasoning never fills the missing actual F or R bound.

5. Filtered actual Liouville/squarefloor calibration.
Declare IndicatorSquares(n)=1 if n is a positive square, else 0, and
lambda=Mu*IndicatorSquares. Complete Mu*One=Unit gives exactly
 lambda*One=IndicatorSquares.
Prime-power coefficients of lambda are (-1)^j, so this is the actual
Liouville sequence, not an anonymous unrelated generator. For EVERY integer
x>=0, including x=0,
 sum_(d<=x)lambda(d)floor(x/d)=Prefix(lambda*One,x)=floor(sqrt(x)).
Consequently the FILTERED signed sum lies between 0 and sqrt(x). This is a
useful exact all-scale signed arithmetic calibration: the unsigned sum is
sum_(d<=x)floor(x/d)>=x, so its square-root bound uses actual signs/constraints.
It is NOT a bound for the ordinary unsmoothed prefix of lambda or Mu and is
not an RH estimate. The sparse root-feature law is learned from responses
first; a separate analytical admission recognizes its frozen canonical
floor_root(q=2,offset=0) coefficient one via the supplied convolution theorem.
Other polynomial/divisor/root features can be present in the learner's broad
dictionary. This backend only admits laws it proves; unrecognized exact-looking
fits remain unresolved, not refuted or globally accepted. A supplied theorem
recognizer is proof-method bias and is not a winning vector given to discovery.

6. Independently reviewed coupled formula and actionable residual route.
The full energy expansion and primitive partition above are mathematically
exact with all endpoints. Mu*One=Unit constrains each complete coefficient,
but neither the convolution null relation nor the squarefree density alone
controls the signs of mu(u)mu(r) across the dense residual. Positivity of an
auxiliary Gram only helps after its complete cross terms and unit forcing cost
are proved. Dropping the residual or applying a diagonal-only Gram estimate
would be a gap. The backend preserves these boundaries explicitly.

A tractable NEW residual feature route, not a proved bound, is to regroup the
EXACT same R by the remaining shared factor rather than primitive arity:
 R=2sum_(g>=1)mu(g)^2
       sum_(r>B,(r,g)=1)mu(r)W(gr) T_(gr)(r-1),
 T_k(y)=sum_(1<=u<=y,(u,k)=1)mu(u).
Here g<=floor(LX/(B+1)); every equality/coprimality constraint is retained.
T_k has exact complete divisor/inclusion-exclusion rewrites, but no supplied
square-root estimate. This exposes signed coupled prefix/bilinear features at
small shared g, including g=1 (which must not be omitted), and reciprocal
cutoffs. A learner can search frozen rational combinations of dyadic r-shells,
shared-g shells, floor differences and nonnegative coupled correlation energies.
Both displayed R expressions and every shell complement are exact identities.
Candidate upper bounds must apply to the TOTAL R, possibly with a telescoping
nonnegative potential; fitting favorable shells separately is insufficient.
Any Cauchy expansion retains ALL off-diagonal correlations. This route makes
the missing signed estimate measurable and typed, without assuming it.

For the dyadic energy stencil, D/P are already subcritical up to a fixed
logarithm. A genuinely useful new all-scale proof would bound R from above
by a cofinal C_q X^2 ceil(X^epsilon(q)) family, with any potential telescoped
and nonnegative and all initial scales/cutoff changes charged. Present kernels,
finite fits, identities and filtered squarefloor calibration do not provide
that premise. Higher arities retain full collision patterns, modulus factors,
coefficient norms and operation/bit costs; no compression is counted as RH gain.

7. Exact energy-only bridge obstruction: more moments can erase the target.
For positive scales and rational coefficients define P(t)=sum_s c_s s^t.
If an integer t>2 has P(t)<=0, the nonnegative abstract energy A(X)=X^t
satisfies sum c_s A(sX)<=0 for EVERY X>0. Nevertheless it does not satisfy
A(X)=O(X^(2+epsilon)) for any epsilon<t-2. Thus even a zero-constant all-scale
one-sided stencil inequality cannot imply critical energy from the energy
recurrence alone. This is a structural bridge obstruction, NOT a counterexample
to any conjecture requiring complete actual Mu*One=Unit.

If at least the first two kernel moments vanish, P(2)=P(3)=0: the cubic
countermodel is annihilated exactly. More generally P(3)<=0 already supplies
that obstruction, without demanding more moments. The backend tests exact
rational P(3),P(4) by default and records which monomial models qualify.
The t=4 model only tests an unconstrained energy recurrence: unlike t=3 it is
incompatible with the supplied actual trivial cubic upper energy bound.
Additional arithmetic/upper-growth premises can therefore remove some abstract
countermodels; they must be stated and proved rather than inferred numerically.

Cubic growth is genuinely realized by the noncancelling coefficient sequence
a(n)=1, whose prefix is n and whose exact block energy is
 A_block(X)=(7/3)X^3-(3/2)X^2+(1/6)X.
For the legal zero-quadratic/cubic stencil c_1=32,c_2=-12,c_4=1 this gives
sum c_s A_block(sX)=(1/6)(32-24+4)X=2X. The abstract A(X)=X^3 model gives
exactly zero for this same stencil. Both are energy-only obstructions, while
a(n)=1 fails the actual inverse condition at n=2. Neither uses sampled Mu or
refutes the actual arithmetic inequality. The example underscores why signed
arithmetic coupling, rather than adding vanishing moments, is the next needed
mechanism. Candidates with this obstruction require a new arithmetic bridge;
the existing nonnegative lower-coefficient recursion must not be attached.

8. Exact factorization and stronger conditional positive-energy transform.
For dyadic integer scales 2^j, merge coefficients and insert missing zero
entries: P(T)=sum_(j=0)^m c_j T^j. Division by T-4 is exact:
 b_(m-1)=c_m; b_(j-1)=c_j+4b_j (j=m-1,...,1),
 P(T)=(T-4)B(T)+P(4), B(T)=sum_(j=0)^(m-1)b_j T^j.
For m=0, B=0 and the same identity remains valid. All rational coefficients
are explicitly retained. P(4)=sum c_j 4^j is precisely the quadratic moment
up to the nonzero kernel normalization. This factorization is certified for
ANY signs; the positive bridge has additional conditions. The symbolic order
budget caps array/representation work and returns missing, never a theorem
refutation or a zero coefficient vector. No growing-order uniformity is assumed.

At P(4)=0, define H(X)=sum b_j A(2^j X). The FULL stencil is exactly
 F(X)=H(2X)-4H(X)=sum b_j Delta(2^j X).
If every b_j>=0 and at least one is positive, H is a nonnegative energy
transform and H(X)>=b_j A(2^j X) for any positive b_j. Thus this condition
admits a stronger conditional bridge even when a lower c_j is positive.

If F(X)<=C X^(2+epsilon) at EVERY dyadic X>=1, epsilon>0, finite C>=0,
then E_H(2X)<=E_H(X)+(C/4)X^epsilon. Summing from X=1 through n/2 gives
 H(n)/n^2 <= H(1)+(C/4)(n^epsilon-1)/(2^epsilon-1), n dyadic>=1.
For any fixed positive b_j, at n>=2^j,
 A(n)<=H(n/2^j)/b_j=O_(C,epsilon,B)(n^(2+epsilon)).
The finitely many smaller dyadic n remain initial data, and H(1) uses the
finitely many actual energies A(2^i) appearing in B. These data and b_j^-1
are charged. Cofinal epsilon(q)->0 and all-scale constants would recover the
conditional RH bridge in section4. NO full signed inequality is proved here.

Existing lower-nonpositive normalized stencils always have b_j>=0. The converse
need not hold: c=[-32,8,-4,1] gives b=[8,0,1], so H=8A(X)+A(4X) and the
same conditional positive-transform proof works. This does not change the
frozen numerical candidate or its original prebackend selection tag.

The transformation also states the limitation concretely: positive b combines
ordinary Delta inequalities at finitely many dilates. If ordinary Delta(Y)
<=C_q Y^2 ceil(Y^(1/q)) globally, then
 F(X)<=C_q X^2 ceil(X^(1/q))*sum_j b_j 4^j ceil(2^(j/q)).
The finite scale/coefficient charge follows from ceil(ab)<=ceil(a)ceil(b).
Thus these stencils smooth an existing unknown arithmetic problem; operator
novelty alone supplies no new signed estimate. For mixed b, an ordinary
one-sided Delta upper bound cannot be multiplied by negative b: lower bounds
or a complete signed estimate are additional obligations. An absolute expansion
charges sum |b_j| times the dilated costs but itself does not establish either
side of the unknown signed defect.
For section8, a premise C X^2 ceil(X^epsilon) is covered by replacing C with
2C, since ceil(X^epsilon)<=2X^epsilon for X>=1. The positive-energy transform
certificate records this conversion explicitly; finite fitted constants never
supply the unknown cofinal premise.
