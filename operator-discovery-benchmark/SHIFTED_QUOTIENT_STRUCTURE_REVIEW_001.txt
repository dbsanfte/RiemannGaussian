Centered fixed-shift quotients: independent structural and RH-bridge review

Scope and conclusion
Pure analytical review of the proposed quotients/products, prime_twist_proof.txt,
and the related prime_twist_grammar.py, shift_ratio_grammar.py and
compensated_shift_grammar.py source. No numerical study, source-table probe,
test, response artifact, hash or frozen edit is performed. This new memo alone
is edited. Statements below are algebraic proofs or explicitly conditional
analytic implications, not an actual signed RH-strength moment estimate.

The exact single/product pole compensation and global rightward-zero bridge
are sound. Products of distinct normalized increments expose new arithmetic
factor/overlap channels, rather than merely rescaling one shift. Fixed-b
coefficients are NOT supported on prime powers. Subcritical preconditioning,
the small-shift prime-error limit, same-prime tuple bounds and centered harmonic
tuple estimates supply useful classifications and component theorems. Their
mixed-prime/composite/full-centering remainder remains unresolved.

1. Single quotient, arithmetic coefficients and exact centering
For fixed rational 0<b<1 put r=1-b and
 D_b(s)=zeta(s+b)/zeta(s), a_b=mu*n^(-b),
 A_b(x)=sum_{n<=x}a_b(n), c_b=1/[r*zeta(r)],
 G_b(x)=[A_b(x)-1-c_b*x^r]/b.
Here * is DIRICHLET convolution; a_b is not pointwise mu(n)n^(-b).
For p prime, e>=1,
 a_b(p^e)=-(1-p^(-b))*p^(-b(e-1)).
Thus a_b is multiplicative, nonzero at all positive integers, and its sign
for n>1 is (-1)^omega(n), including nonsquarefree n. Also |a_b(n)|<=1.
The identity a_0=delta_1 explains the subtraction1 exactly.

zeta(r)<0 for0<r<1, by the positive paired alternating eta series and its
factor1-2^(1-r)<0. In particular the prescribed real constant is finite and
negative. The pole of D_b at s=r has residue1/zeta(r)=r*c_b. For Re(s)>1,
 b*s*integral_1^infinity G_b(x)x^(-s-1)dx
 =D_b(s)-1-c_b*s/(s-r).
The prescribed subtraction cancels the real pole exactly. A rounded fitted
coefficient is not the same expression; if r>1/2 its mismatch itself prevents
an RH-strength centered holomorphy/moment claim.

The constant1 is the delta-channel subtraction, not a fixed-b Perron finite
part. Indeed D_b(0)=zeta(b)/zeta(0), and the displayed centered Mellin numerator
at0 is D_b(0)-1. Additional decay-through-zero claims would require their
own exact constant compensation. For the proposed half-plane Re(s)>1/2 this
constant distinction does not invalidate the gate. Initial/head costs remain.

2. Small-shift limit and endpoint conditioning
For every FIXED finite x, as b decreases to0,
 (A_b(x)-1)/b -> -psi(x), c_b/b -> -1,
 G_b(x) -> x-psi(x).
At a prime power a_b(p^e)/b tends to -log p. At an integer with two or more
distinct primes, a_b(n)/b tends to0. All sums at fixed x are finite, so this
derives the limit directly. The 1/b normalization prevents a trivial scalar
win from shrinking the unnormalized numerator, but recovers a familiar
prime-error channel in the limit. This is not a uniform limit as x grows:
b*log n can become large, and the composite remainders are then non-negligible.
Near b=1 the prescribed c_b behaves as -2/(1-b), creating a large constant
centering/head cost; endpoint tuning must not hide that cost either.

3. Prefix preconditioning and the actual fresh term
There is an exact ordinary-quotient representation
 A_b(x)=sum_{m<=x}m^(-b)M(floor(x/m))=L_b M(x).
The m=1 term is M(x). For x in[X,2X), every m>=2 term uses a prefix below X;
the centered output still has this complete fresh prefix with coefficient1/b.
Absolute past/envelope bounds do not prove a negative correlation sufficient
to control its full square after centering.

On the continuous norm H_sigma(f)=integral_1^infinity |f(x)|^2*x^(-2sigma-1)dx,
with f=0 below1, quotient dilation f(x/d) has norm d^(-sigma). Therefore
 ||L_b||, ||L_b^-1||<=zeta(b+sigma), when b+sigma>1,
 L_b^-1 f(x)=sum_{d<=x}mu(d)d^(-b)f(x/d).
The inverse follows from n^(-b)*(mu(n)n^(-b))=delta_1. The finite sums and
their restrictions are exact. For b>1/2 both directions have a finite uniform
bound even at sigma=1/2. The known channels1 and x^(1-b) also have finite
critical H, with squared norms1 and1/(2b-1), respectively. Thus a single
G_b for b>1/2 is bounded critical-norm preconditioning of M plus explicitly
subcritical padding. It supplies no new cancellation class by itself.
At b=1/2 the critical cost diverges; below1/2 the pole channel is supercritical
and requires the exact compensation. These distinctions should be visible.

Do not confuse this prefix inverse with the positive DIRICHLET inverse of a_b:
 t_b(n)=prod_{p|n}(1-p^(-b)), t_b(1)=1,
 a_b*t_b=delta_1.
Its summatory function has the unconditional estimate
 T_b(x)=x/zeta(1+b)+O_b(x^(1-b)),
obtained from T_b(x)=sum_{d<=x}mu(d)d^(-b)floor(x/d), bounding the floor
error and the absolutely convergent tail at1+b. Thus t_b has positive mean,
and its ordinary quotient absolute sum at critical exponents diverges.
The exact positive renewal identity
 sum_{d<=x}t_b(d)A_b(x/d)=1
is not a bounded critical-H recovery theorem for A_b from this kernel.

4. Hyperbola splitting with the complete signed error
Let S_b(y)=sum_{m<=y}m^(-b), and define exactly
 e_b(y)=S_b(y)-y^r/r-zeta(b).
Integral/Euler summation gives |e_b(y)|<=C_b y^(-b) for y>=1, with a finite
constant depending on the fixed b. Consequently
 A_b(x)-c_b*x^r
 =x^r/r[sum_{d<=x}mu(d)d^(-r)-1/zeta(r)]
   +zeta(b)M(x)+sum_{d<=x}mu(d)e_b(x/d).
The weighted Mobius mismatch here is an exact finite expression. It is NOT
an ordinary convergent tail at r<1 imported from the analytic value1/zeta(r).
The final error has an absolute bound O_b(x), which is insufficient.

For positive U,V with UV=x the exact hyperbola identity is
 A_b(x)=sum_{d<=U}mu(d)S_b(x/d)
       +sum_{m<=V}m^(-b)M(floor(x/m))-M(U)S_b(V).
The intersection rectangle and all integer endpoints are retained. Expanding
only the first S_b leaves error
 |sum_{d<=U}mu(d)e_b(x/d)|<=C_b' x^(-b)U^(b+1).
At U=sqrt(x) this is O_b(x^((1-b)/2)), genuinely below square root. But the
other exact terms include the centered weighted Mobius mismatch, all quotient
prefix terms and the intersection cost. This proves a useful local component
bound, not the full remainder. Omitting those signed terms is circular.

5. Prime-power flow, positivity and exact cocycle
Define the nonnegative prime-power kernel
 ell_b(p^e)=(1-p^(-be))/e, zero off prime powers and at1.
Then D_b=exp_*(-ell_b), t_b=exp_*(ell_b), so
 h_b=(a_b-delta_1)/b
    =sum_{j>=1}(-1)^j ell_b^{*j}/(b*j!).
At each n or finite horizon only finitely many terms occur because ell_b(1)=0.
This is the precise prime-power-tuple expansion of the full finite-b increment.
All higher orders, repeated-prime overlaps and composite coefficients remain;
retaining only its first logarithmic term changes the quotient.

The exact shift cocycle is D_b(s)D_c(s+b)=D_{b+c}(s). Thus, where total shifts
are in the declared domain,
 (b+c)H_{b+c}=b H_b+c H_c(s+b)+bc H_b H_c(s+b),
 H_b=(D_b-1)/b.
The shifted second factor corresponds to multiplying its coefficients by
n^(-b). This is a genuine factor-placement identity and an alias rule;
unshifted H_b H_c does not telescope this way.
Also partial_b a_b=-a_b*(Lambda(n)n^(-b)). The positive generator explains
the alternating convolution trees, but does not make A_b or G_b positive
or provide a stable norm contraction closing the unknown centered moment.

6. Products of distinct increments: exact arithmetic and real poles
For a fixed set B={b_1,...,b_k}, distinct positive rationals below1, k<=3,
put P_B(s)=product_i H_{b_i}(s), p_B=h_{b_1}*...*h_{b_k}, and
 J_B(x)=sum_{n<=x}p_B(n)-sum_i [R_i/(1-b_i)]x^(1-b_i),
 R_i=1/[b_i*zeta(1-b_i)]
     *product_{j!=i}[(zeta(1-b_i+b_j)/zeta(1-b_i)-1)/b_j].
These are exactly its simple real-pole residues; an exact zero residue is
allowed, not replaced by an empirical coefficient. Distinct shifts avoid
coincident higher-order poles and logarithmic main terms in this catalogue.
For Re(s)>1,
 s*H_{J_B}(s)=P_B(s)-sum_i [R_i/(1-b_i)]s/[s-(1-b_i)].
There is no additional delta subtraction: every h_b(1)=0, so p_B(1)=0.

The full summatory product is a sum over ordered arithmetic FACTOR tuples
 n_1*...*n_k<=x, n_i>=2, with weight product_i h_{b_i}(n_i).
Each h_b is nonzero at composites as well as prime powers. For fixed finite
x and all b_i->0, the coefficient limit is (-1)^k Lambda^{*k}, but exchanging
this limit uniformly with x is not justified. Coalescing pole main terms
likewise requires their complete combined limit; separate large residues can
cancel. The product degrees genuinely change arithmetic overlap structure,
whereas merely sweeping b within one degree need not introduce a new mechanism.

Every factor index is <=x/2^(k-1). Thus through horizon x, these coefficients
query mu only through that smaller horizon. For k>=2 no finite-prefix inverse
recovers the fresh source coordinates at x. The correct same-degree comparator
is (mu-delta_1)^{*k}, with the same nonempty-factor lag, not mu^{*k}-delta_1.
The proof bridge below is GLOBAL holomorphy, not finite-horizon Hilbert recovery.
No quantitative all-height inverse cost for these products is established here.

7. Two proved actual-prime component estimates
First partition each h_b into its literal prime-power restriction plus its
remaining composite part. In the product of the prime-power restrictions,
separate tuples whose prime is the SAME in every factor from mixed-prime
tuples. On a prime power,
 |h_b(p^e)|=(1-p^(-b))p^(-b(e-1))/b<=log p.
For fixed p, the number of ordered positive exponent tuples with total at
most L=floor(log(x)/log(p)) is binom(L,k). Therefore the complete one-prime
k-tuple summatory D_k(x) satisfies
 |D_k(x)|<=pi(x^(1/k))*(log x)^k/k!
         <=x^(1/k)*(log x)^k/k!.
For k=2 this is an unconditional square-root-times-log bound; for k=3 it
is below square root. Its entire dyadic energy is respectively O(log^4 X)
or O(X^(-1/3)log^6 X), hence subpower for every desired positive exponent.
For k=2,3 this channel can be peeled analytically WITH its explicit cost: if
J_B=D_k+F_k, then E_{J_B}<=2E_{D_k}+2E_{F_k}, and the reverse inequality
for F_k is identical. The cofinal moment problem is therefore equivalently
the FULL remainder F_k, which includes mixed-prime tuples, every composite
factor branch and the complete real-pole normalizer. A bounded diagonal is
not a bound for that remainder or for its signed cross term.
This cofinal peeling equivalence is NOT asserted for k=1, whose one-prime
channel has psi-scale size rather than the proved square-root component bound.

Second, the already-proved actual-prime harmonic estimate from
prime_twist_proof.txt gives A(x)=sum_{n<=x}Lambda(n)/n=log x+O(1), with
an all-scale constant (changing strict to closed endpoints is bounded).
For each fixed integer k>=1, positive Stieltjes convolution proves
 sum_{n<=x}Lambda^{*k}(n)/n
 =(log x)^k/k!+O_k((1+log x)^(k-1)).
To see this, convolve the induction hypothesis against dA(t). Replacing
dA by d(log t) in its polynomial leading term costs O((1+log x)^(k-1)) by
integration by parts and the bounded A(t)-log t. The leading integral is
(log x)^k/k!. Integrating the positive induction-error envelope against dA
costs the same order, by the same integration and monotonicity. This includes
the full prime tuples and overlaps, not just a diagonal. It is genuine signed
centering at harmonic weight, derived from a supplied proved prime theorem.
Its logarithmic remainder and reciprocal weight do not imply the proposed
unweighted square-root remainder. For finite b, every further alternating
ell_b tree/composite branch still has to be controlled. No RH-strength
estimate or unproved Selberg/PNT remainder is imported to close that gap.

8. Independent rightward-zero-chain proof, including product cancellations
Suppose the prescribed centered single quotient is globally holomorphic in
Re(s)>1/2. At an ordinary zero rho in that half-plane, rho is nonreal;
the real pole compensator is analytic there. Quotient holomorphy forces
m(rho+b)>=m(rho). Repeat at that right-shifted zero. Each step adds the SAME
fixed positive b, so in finitely many steps the real part exceeds1, contradicting
Euler-product zero-freeness. Hence no such rho exists.

For a product, allow arbitrary zero/pole cancellations between its factors.
At rho of multiplicity m, let nu_i be the order of
 zeta(s+b_i)-zeta(s) at rho. Each denominator has order m. Holomorphy of
P_B forces sum_i nu_i>=k*m. Thus at least one nu_i>=m, which implies that
rho+b_i is a zeta zero of multiplicity at least m: otherwise the numerator
order is smaller than m. This argument also handles a zero H factor cancelling
another factor's pole. Some right shift of size at least min_i b_i is therefore
forced at EVERY right-half-plane zeta zero. Repeating must again reach Re>1.
No assumption that individual factors are holomorphic, no all-height scalar
nonvanishing claim and no independence of shifted zeros is needed.

A complete all-scale moment premise yielding finite continuous H_sigma(J_B)
for every sigma>1/2 makes its Mellin integral holomorphic in Re(s)>1/2 by
Cauchy-Schwarz. The exact formula then supplies the required centered-product
holomorphy, and the ordinary-zeta functional equation gives RH. Integer-to-real
transfer is legitimate: |a_b(n)|<=1; product coefficients are bounded by a
fixed constant times d_k(n), hence by C_eta n^eta for every eta>0. Smooth
normalizer increments are O_B(n^(-min b_i)). These cell errors have finite
H_sigma when eta<sigma. Finite tested exponents/scales are not that premise.

General signed LINEAR mixtures of these products do not inherit this proof:
their top pole coefficients at a zero can cancel without any shifted zeta
zero. Such mixtures need a separate all-order noncancellation certificate.
Fixed distinct products or nonnegative complete energy stacks retaining a
proved product channel are a safe small language for this qualitative bridge.
Repeated shifts would need the exact higher-order real-pole/log main terms;
the proposed distinct-shift catalogue avoids this extra schema requirement.

9. Structural language and principled adverse controls
A small useful language consists of normalized fixed-shift increments,
distinct-factor Dirichlet products of degrees1,2,3, exact pole-centering, and
complete source-bound factor/overlap annotations. Proof transformations should
include the hyperbola rectangle complement, the shift-cocycle alias, positive
logarithmic-flow trees, and the proven same-prime diagonal peel. These change
or expose arithmetic coupling instead of fitting coefficients. Arbitrary
truncation of composite/overlap branches is inadmissible unless its complete
remainder is paid for. The old prime-twist grammar's linear logarithms recover
by multiplicity recurrences; these products require the distinct quotient
argument above. They must not borrow its shifted costs without proof.

Retain the original prime-error limit and the matched-lag same-degree
(mu-delta_1) comparator. Coherent positive or finite-prime-sign controls have
a principled failure explanation. For example the squarefree source negative
only on a finite set S of primes has Dirichlet series
 F_S(s)=zeta(s)/zeta(2s)*product_{p in S}(1-p^(-s))/(1+p^(-s)),
with a positive simple pole at1. The coefficient-defined h_b control has
series [F_S(s)zeta(s+b)-1]/b; its k-fold product has a nonzero pole of order k
at1. The actual quotient's main terms at1-b_i do not remove this pole, so
its centered all-scale RH moment must fail. A finite agreement patch adds an
entire Dirichlet polynomial and cannot remove the underlying source mean.

For the protocol's one source specifically Q(s)=zeta(s), also with positive
residue1 at its simple pole1. For partial_prime_13 use the displayed F_S with
S={p prime:p<=13}. In either case the substituted atom's residue at1 is
 Res_{s=1}Q(s)*zeta(1+b)/b>0.
The degree-k product's leading pole coefficient is the product of these
positive residues, hence nonzero. Actual-zeta compensation at1-b_i<1 is
analytic at1 and cannot cancel it. A cofinal full moment premise would make
the centered Mellin function holomorphic there and contradict this pole.
Thus BOTH protocol controls fail the all-scale premise regardless of any
finite gates; no observed response score is used in this falsifier.

This is an honest obstruction control, but its failure also measures mean/
pole mismatch. It is not by itself proof that a new product exploits actual
prime-specific signed information. If a control is given its own different
pole normalizers, it becomes a different program and the comparison must
say so; source-specific normalization cannot be silently transferred. The
rightward bridge applies to the actual ordinary-zeta quotient, not to an
arbitrary control whose inverse source is no longer ordinary zeta.

Final status
Proved: exact coefficients and centering; small-shift limit; subcritical
single-shift preconditioning; complete hyperbola/cocycle/renewal identities;
simple product residues; fixed-horizon lag; same-prime tuple and full harmonic
tuple component bounds; and the conditional global product zero-chain/RH
bridge even with cross-factor cancellations. Unproved: the full centered
mixed-prime/composite remainder moment for every proposed actual candidate.
No source-specific RH-strength signed bound or quantitative product inverse
norm is claimed. Reviewer numerical/test/source-probe work is zero.
