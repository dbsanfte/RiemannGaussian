ESTIMATES AS SEARCH TARGETS, WITH EXPLICIT CERTIFICATE OBLIGATIONS
7 October 2026. This stage proves elementary and classical-theorem consequences.
No new prime-distribution estimate, RH bound or historical novelty is claimed.

1. WHAT IS SEARCHED AND WHAT COUNTS AS A PROOF

An estimate program records a target, domain, constant, growth exponent,
logarithmic cost, parameter dependence, certificate and remaining obligations.
Numerical training proposes these fields; held-out scales can falsify them.
A finite fit cannot quantify over all scales. Universal statements below
come from the sound certificate templates described here, with their exact
algebraic witnesses, not from numerical extrapolation. The independent checker
replays the algebra and changes the arithmetic and quadrature implementations.
This is a written mathematical proof and executable audit, not a Lean proof.

The producer computes the inverse q of the constant-one arithmetic function
by triangular Dirichlet inversion. q(1)=1 and q(n)=-sum_(d|n,d<n) q(d).
It uses no factorization or prime oracle. Euler factors identify q with the
classical Mobius function mu. The independent checker factors every integer
through 131072, merges repeated factor records, reconstructs the integer and
computes mu from squarefreeness. Prime tests are used only after discovery to
certify multiplier partitions and in this separate independent checker.

Twenty rational polynomial kernels W are generated before inspecting mu or
any zero, with W(0)=1 and W(1)=0. Extend W by zero beyond 1. The exact target is

 S_W(X)=sum_(n<=X) mu(n) W(n/X).

The search scores C X^theta (log X)^b for seven rational theta and five
integer log costs. C is a dyadic upward-rounded training maximum with margin
5/4. Thirteen training scales end at 1024; fifteen held-out scales include
odd scales 100003 and 131071 and end at 131072. The zero gate is applied
after fitting, and no QRH estimate supplies a training target.

2. A WEAK BUT UNIVERSAL CERTIFICATE FOR EVERY GENERATED KERNEL

For W(u)=sum_(j=0)^d c_j u^j, write its Bernstein basis expansion with

 b_k=sum_(j<=k) c_j binomial(k,j)/binomial(d,j).

The degree-d Bernstein basis is nonnegative and sums to one on [0,1].
Consequently |W(u)|<=C_W=max |b_k|, and |mu(n)|<=1 gives

 |S_W(X)|<=C_W floor(X)<=C_W X for every real X>=1.

This is a separately synthesized certificate. A smaller learned constant
does not inherit this proof. A proposed theta>=1,b>=0 bound is certified by
this backend only if its constant dominates C_W already at X=2, because
X^(theta-1)(log X)^b then increases. The stage leaves better bounds as
unproved conjectures unless another certificate applies.

3. CANCELLATION IDENTITIES LEARNED FROM ACTUAL ARITHMETIC ROWS

The row search tries mu(k*n)=lambda*mu(n) for gcd(k,n)=1 and zero otherwise,
for multiple k and lambda. Local Euler factors certify the learned prime law

 mu(p*n)=-mu(n) if p does not divide n, and mu(p*n)=0 otherwise.

Let M(X)=sum_(n<=X)mu(n). Partition the sum into p-coprime and p-divisible
rows, and apply this law in the divisible row. Terms up to X/p cancel:

 M(X)=sum_(X/p<n<=X,p does not divide n)mu(n).             (1)

For every integer X>=1 the number of terms on the right is exactly

 C_p(X)=X-2*floor(X/p)+floor(X/p^2).

Write X=p^2*a+r, r=p*b+c, 0<=b,c<p. The count density is (p-1)^2/p^2,
and the residual from that density is -b/p+(2p-1)c/p^2. Its maximum is
attained at b=0,c=p-1. Thus the count certificate supplies

 |M(X)| <= ((p-1)^2/p^2)*X + ((2p-1)(p-1)/p^2).           (2)

The detector selects the smallest slope among its learned prime rows. It
recovers p=2 and |M(X)|<=X/4+3/4. This is a classical consequence of an
Euler factor, not an RH-quality sublinear cancellation bound. The proof
retains the boundary terms; numerical cancellation is not assumed globally.
A composite k can obey a valid local mu(k*n) law without partitioning all
integers into these two rows. Integers divisible by only one prime factor
of k are missing. The certificate backend therefore requires a prime.

The same partition produces a possible future weighted rewrite
S_W(X)=sum_(p does not divide n) mu(n)[W(n/X)-W(p*n/X)].
This rewrite alone supplies no new cancellation among the retained terms.
The current estimate engine does not certify stronger bounds by assuming it.

4. AUTOMATIC POSITIVE GLOBAL-MOMENT CERTIFICATES

Let B be a previously certified unequal-shift decoder or positive finite
mixture. Its all-integer least-prime envelope, proved in shift_ratio_proof.txt,
is |B(n)|<=h(log(P^-(n))/log n), with

 h(t)=sum_i w_i*t/[v_i+(1-v_i)t],
 w_i>0, sum_i w_i=1, 0<v_i<=1.

The certificate engine constructs h from the program, differentiates its
qth power and searches for an exact rational/logarithmic antiderivative.
It verifies the derivative exactly before accepting the synthesized result.
Each atom is increasing, with derivative v_i/[v_i+(1-v_i)t]^2>0. h(0)=0,
h(1)=1 and h has a simple zero at zero. There are no poles on [0,1].

For integer X>=2 put t_n=min(1,log(P^-(n))/log X). Then |B(n)|<=h(t_n)
on [X,2X], and finite layer-cake integration gives

 sum_(X<=n<=2X)|B(n)|^q
 <= integral_0^1 (h(t)^q)' * #{n in [X,2X]:t_n>t} dt.

For t>0 the counted integers have P^-(n)>X^t. We explicitly import
Fan/Pomerance's uniform theorem Phi(x,y)<x/log y for x>=y>=2, counting 1.
For 1<y<2 its extension follows from Phi(x,y)<=x and log y<1. Hence the
count is at most 2X/(t log X). For every integer q>=2 the integral converges:

 sum_(X<=n<=2X)|B(n)|^q <= C_q X/log X,
 C_q=2*integral_0^1 (h(t)^q)'/t dt.                      (3)

The q and v_i are parameters retained in C_q, not hidden uniform constants.
Near zero the integrand is of order t^(q-2). At q=1 it diverges; this backend
reports a missing truncated integral/loglog cost instead of accepting a
fixed constant. That limitation of this envelope does not disprove a
stronger estimate for the actual arithmetic coefficients by another method.

The source computes exact antiderivatives and native Arb integral enclosures;
the independent checker replays the derivatives and changes variables to
t=u^2 for the full integral. It also checks actual arithmetic moments via
independent prime-subset coefficient formulas. Rounded rational constants
are upper bounds for (3), with the full endpoint integral retained.

For an atomic q=2 program the search recovers C_2=2(1+v)/v without that
formula as a training target. For weights 1/3,2/3 at v=2/7,7/9 it derives

 C_2=247/63+(56/31)*log(49/18) = approximately 5.7297032513.

This strictly improves the supplied Jensen constant 127/21, approximately
6.0476190476. It is an automatically derived consequence of the declared
sieve theorem, not a newly discovered theorem about prime distribution.
The full external sieve proof has not been independently verified here.
Primary theorem: https://math.dartmouth.edu/~carlp/erat060523rev.pdf

5. THE ORDINARY ZETA ROOT GATE AND FALSE LONG-SCALE FITS

For the polynomial compactly supported kernel, its Mellin transform is

 W_hat(s)=integral_0^1 W(u)u^(s-1)du = sum_j c_j/(s+j).

Termwise integration, initially Re(s)>1, gives the ordinary-zeta identity

 integral_1^infinity S_W(X) X^(-s-1)dX = W_hat(s)/zeta(s). (4)

The term at n=1 has the same lower limit, so no n=1 term is omitted.
The engine derives the rational transform. Strict Hurwitz leading-minor
positivity for its numerator is a sufficient nonvanishing certificate in
Re(s)>=0. A failed sufficient test is an unresolved gate, not a numerator
zero. The independent checker uses a Routh array as a distinct test. For
W=(1-u)^d, W_hat=d!/[s(s+1)...(s+d)], which has no zero in the critical strip.

If a bound is stated only at integer X, it still supplies the needed real-X
growth control: between X and Y=floor X, the coefficients in the sum agree
and the mean value theorem bounds |S_W(X)-S_W(Y)| by a kernel-dependent
constant using sup |W'| and sum_(n<=Y)n/(X*Y). This O(1) cost does not change
any positive growth exponent or the obstructions below.

A fresh Rouche certificate on the retained first-zero disk proves exactly
one simple zero there. Reflection s->1-conjugate(s) preserves both zeta zeros
and that disk, so uniqueness places the zero on Re(s)=1/2. Each generated
Mellin transform is certified nonzero throughout the disk. At this zero rho,
W_hat(s)/zeta(s) therefore has a nonzero simple residue. The independent
checker changes precision and evaluates the rational numerator and denominator
instead of the producer's sum of fractions. Zero reflection is documented at
https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.10 ; no new zeta zero is claimed.

An all-scale bound with theta<1/2 would make the left side of (4) holomorphic
at rho, which is impossible. For theta=1/2,b<0, set s=rho+epsilon, epsilon>0.
The bound implies epsilon times the absolute integral tends to zero: in
u=log X coordinates this follows from epsilon integral_(log 2)^infinity
exp(-epsilon*u)*u^b du ->0. Split at any fixed large u and then let that
cutoff grow. The finite initial interval and integer-interpolation O(1) term
also vanish. But epsilon*W_hat(rho+epsilon)/zeta(rho+epsilon) tends to the
nonzero residue. Thus this negative logarithmic cost is impossible too.

The d=4 kernel produces a near-constant numerical plateau: its fitted
theta=0,b=0 bound survives every held-out scale through 131072. The detector
rejects the global claim by this root certificate. This is a concrete
calibration that numerical success alone cannot prove an estimate.

If W_hat has the nonvanishing certificate and an actual all-scale
X^(1/2)*(log X)^b bound with finite b were proved, (4) would extend
W_hat/zeta holomorphically to Re(s)>1/2 and exclude all zeros there; reflection
then gives RH. This is a sufficient route, not an estimate proved by this
search and not a claim that the reverse implication has been established.
Positive coefficient moments of B have a separate target and do not provide
this signed Mobius cancellation certificate.

6. SCOPE AND WHAT REMAINS TO BE DISCOVERED

The stage learns candidate estimates, discovers usable local cancellation
rows, and synthesizes the constants of positive all-scale bounds. It records
missing uniform cancellation lemmas as search obligations. Current backends
prove classical consequences and can reject impossible conjectures; they do
not prove a new independent prime estimate or RH-quality cancellation.

There is no completeness guarantee for a finite proof grammar. Extending it
requires more certificate-producing operations: closure of scale recurrences,
signed decompositions, multiscale roughness conditioning and identities with
parameter-uniform error budgets. Numerical samples can rank such proposals;
their quantified certificates determine whether they are theorems. Historical
priority is a separate audit. The verified-historically-new list remains empty.
