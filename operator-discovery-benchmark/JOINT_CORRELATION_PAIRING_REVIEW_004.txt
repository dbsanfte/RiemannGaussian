JOINT CORRELATION PAIRINGS: SOFT DIVISOR RECOVERY AND VALUATION DEFECTS

Scope and outcome
-----------------
The input is candidate10_centered_reciprocal_obligation.json, whose primary
target is the JOINED high-product off-diagonal upper bound, not separate
sharp bands. Only this new prose file is written. No source evaluation,
experiment, test, hash, parameter search, subagent or prior/frozen edit is
used. No actual-source cofinal estimate or RH theorem is obtained.

A concrete finite argument family is proposed. Soft divisor-Mobius filters
have proved recovery at shifts >=1/2, with constant critical norm cost above
the boundary and polylog cost at the boundary. For the actual v=2/3 filter,
the complete source reduces, after independently paid PREFIX components,
to four multiplicative states with exact rational prime-power signatures.
The signatures give a genuinely stronger uniform bound for several mixed
shared-square sectors, and exact pair-to-cofactor-defect identities which
can be enumerated without pretending the defect vanishes.

A proper-prime-power logarithmic recurrence supplies a second finite
construction primitive. Its Volterra memory is paid and absorbable, but
its joint source forcing is not proved small. Separate pole compensation
creates macroscopic deterministic prime forcing, so that forcing must stay
coupled. None of the reversible presentations is itself an exponent gain.

2. Paid soft-divisor filters, including the critical boundary
------------------------------------------------------------
For a fixed arithmetic kernel k_alpha its prefix action is
  (K_alpha F)(x)=sum_(d<=x)d^(-alpha)*F(x/d).
Its exact inverse is
  (M_alpha F)(x)=sum_(d<=x)mu(d)d^(-alpha)*F(x/d).              (2.1)
All sums at a finite x are finite. Dilation obeys
  ||F(x/d)||_(sigma,H)<=d^(-sigma)*||F||_(sigma,H).
Therefore, if alpha+sigma>1,
  ||K_alpha||,||M_alpha||<=zeta(alpha+sigma).                  (2.2)
No convergent Mobius sum on the critical source line is assumed: the
proof uses ABSOLUTE convergence on alpha+sigma>1.

Finite prime words have the same uniform control. Their factors are
  I-q^(-alpha)S_q, S_q F(x)=F(x/q),
and each inverse has complete geometric powers. The inverse norm product
is <=prod_q(1-q^(-alpha-sigma))^(-1)<=zeta(alpha+sigma).
The forward absolute product is bounded by the same zeta. Thus passing
to all primes is paid in this region; a finite source horizon uses exactly
the primes <=H, with no omitted coefficient below H. No q-free arithmetic
projection is inserted into this LINEAR divisor operator.

More generally a fixed rational power r of each Euler factor has local
coefficients (-1)^e*binom(r,e)*q^(-e*alpha). With L=ceil(|r|),
  |binom(r,e)|<=binom(L+e-1,e),
so forward and inverse coefficients are majorized by d_L(n)n^(-alpha).
The norm cost in (2.2) is at most zeta(alpha+sigma)^L. For r=0 use the
identity and L=0. Rational powers use the branch defined by the convergent
Euler/Taylor expansion in the initial right half-plane; their finite
coefficient convolution identities hold formally as well.

At alpha=1/2,sigma=1/2 retain the boundary, rather than reject it:
  sum_(n<=H)d_L(n)/n<=(1+log H)^L.                            (2.3)
This gives a truncated polylog recovery in both directions. Equivalently
any fixed sigma>1/2 has constant norm cost, so the complete cofinal class
is still preserved. At alpha<1/2, a generic absolute critical bound instead
carries H^(1/2-alpha) times a fixed log power per application. It requires
an additional signed gain or another proved compensation. This is a cost
classification, not a proof that every such operator is impossible.

These filtered presentations must be grouped as recoverable aliases when
counting estimates. Distinct arithmetic pairing arguments AFTER filtering
can still be genuinely different constructions.

3. Exact four-state reduction of the actual source
--------------------------------------------------
Apply the full v=2/3 filter to (1.1):
  w=t_b*mu_v.
Its prefix W is M_v T, while T=K_v W exactly. At sigma=1/2 the two
norm costs are <=zeta(7/6)<=7; the final inequality follows from the
integral upper bound zeta(s)<=1+1/(s-1), with no numerical evaluation.

Introduce FOUR multiplicative states
  nu0=k_u*(mu*mu), nu1=k_u*(mu*mu_v),
  nu2=k_u*mu_v, nu3=mu,
and their prefixes A_i. Then exactly
  w=f+eta,
  f=beta*nu0-beta*nu1-R_u*nu2-beta*mu,
  eta=beta*mu_v-d*mu_v.                                    (3.1)
Every convolution and prime power is complete. In particular
  f(1)=-beta-R_u, eta(1)=beta, w(1)=-R_u.
The beta*delta term of the original source has become beta*mu_v, not zero.

The eta prefix is independently paid in the critical norm. The mu_v
prefix has absolute bound <=3x^(1/3), hence squared critical norm <=27.
Here is a SELF-CONTAINED finite critical bound for the complete d prefix,
stronger than the insufficient bound D(x)<=beta*sqrt x alone.
For t=q^(-1/3) and every e>=2 its exact same-prime coefficient is
  d(q^e)=beta*(1-t^2)*t^(e-2)*(1-t^(e-1))>=0.
The complete geometric sum, with no valuation cutoff, is
  sum_(e>=2)d(q^e)
    =beta*(1-t^2)*[1/(1-t)-t/(1-t^2)]=beta.
Only q<=sqrt x can contribute, so D(x)<=beta*pi(sqrt x), and D(x)=0
for 1<=x<4.

An explicit elementary upper bound is pi(y)<=10*y/log y for y>=2.
Indeed for integer n>=1 every prime n<q<=2n divides binom(2n,n), so
  theta(2n)-theta(n)<=log binom(2n,n)<=2n*log2.
Summing at dyadic n gives theta(2^k)<2^(k+1)*log2. Bounding a real y
by the next dyadic endpoint, which is <2y, proves theta(y)<=4y.
Splitting primes at sqrt y then gives
  pi(y)<=sqrt y+2theta(y)/log y<=sqrt y+8y/log y.
Finally log y<=2sqrt y follows by integrating 1/t<=1/sqrt t on [1,y],
so sqrt y<=2y/log y, proving the stated constant ten.
Consequently, for x>=4,
  0<=D(x)<=20beta*sqrt x/log x,
  ||D||_crit^2<=400beta^2*integral_4^infinity dx/(x*log^2 x)
                  =400beta^2/log4.
We may therefore take the explicit finite
  C_D=20beta/sqrt(log4).
This proof uses neither PNT nor RH, and includes every prime power.
Soft convolution gives
  ||prefix(d*mu_v)||_crit<=7*C_D,
  ||prefix(eta)||_crit<=beta*sqrt(27)+7*C_D.                   (3.2)
This is a full-prefix peel retaining the cross in the triangle inequality;
it is NOT permission to drop eta at independent sharp product cuts.

Consequently the original complete centered moment is cofinal if and only
if the prefix F=sum f is cofinal, up to these paid components and the
bounded divisor recovery. For all sigma>1/2 the explicit direction is
  ||T||_sigma<=zeta(v+sigma)*(||F||_sigma+||eta_prefix||_sigma),
  ||F||_sigma<=zeta(v+sigma)*||T||_sigma+||eta_prefix||_sigma.   (3.3)
No claim of cofinality is inserted into this equivalence.
Also |f(n)|<=(3beta+R_u)d2(n); therefore its OWN complete high-product
off-diagonal has the same paid low-head/diagonal recovery as in the input.
The remaining joined correlation can be studied in this four-state basis.

The Dirichlet series of f, initially Re(s)>1, is
  F_D(s)=zeta(s+u)*[beta/zeta(s)^2
                -beta/(zeta(s)zeta(s+v))-R_u/zeta(s+v)]
                            -beta/zeta(s).                  (3.4)
The bracket vanishes ANALYTICALLY at real kappa by the old exact R_u.
It is not asserted that its coefficient series converges ordinarily there,

4. Prime-power signatures and valid cofactor pairings
-----------------------------------------------------
For an actual prime q put t=q^(-u), so q^(-v)=t^2. With z marking q
valuation, the local series are
  nu0: (1-z)^2/(1-tz),
  nu1: (1-z)(1-t^2*z)/(1-tz),
  nu2: (1-t^2*z)/(1-tz),
  mu: 1-z.                                                 (4.1)
All have unit one. Their first coefficients are respectively
  t-2, t-1-t^2, t(1-t), -1.
For EVERY e>=2 the coefficients are exactly
  l0(e)=(1-t)^2*t^(e-2),
  l1(e)=-t*l0(e), l2(e)=(1-t)*t^e, l3(e)=0.                (4.2)
Their absolute full tails are <=1, <=t, <=t^2, and zero. Furthermore
  |nu0(n)|<=d2(n), |nu1(n)|<=1, |nu2(n)|<=1, |mu(n)|<=1.
These statements use all valuations and multiplicativity, not squarefree
approximations. The extra powers in (4.2) are genuine prime-size suppression.

For a q-free cofactor H, the local relation does NOT equate whole states:
  nu1(q^e H)+t*nu0(q^e H)
       =t*l0(e)*[nu0(H)-nu1(H)], e>=2.                       (4.3)
Likewise
  nu2(q^e H)-t^2/(1-t)*nu0(q^e H)
       =t^2/(1-t)*l0(e)*[nu2(H)-nu0(H)].                    (4.4)
Both right sides are exact cofactor DEFECTS. They vanish at H=1 but not
for a general source cofactor. They belong in every certificate. On a
high shared-square support H<=n/q^2 they have strictly smaller arguments;
this is a potential induction edge, not a bound for that defect.

A useful rejection test for constant scalar combinations follows. For
a*nu0+b*nu1+c*nu2+d*mu the higher-valuation coefficient, ON UNIT COFACTOR,
is
  (1-t)*t^(e-2)*[a-(a+b)t+(b+c)t^2].                        (4.5)
Retaining a nonzero nu0 coefficient prevents a higher t order. This is
even stronger with independent arbitrary cofactors: one must expand the
typed cofactor functions, not just this unit polynomial. The states are
linearly independent on q-free integers, as their higher-prime-power
signatures at infinitely many other primes give independent rational
functions of the parameter, followed by the mu unit/prime values.
Tensor certificates require the corresponding independent cofactor basis.

To cancel nu0 against nu1 by an inverse t incurs q^u and loses the very
prime-size gain. Raising formal order by discarding the common nu0 mode,
or using H=1 only, does not recover the target. q-dependent combinations
such as (4.3) remain admissible, with their exact cofactor defect and any
inverse power explicitly charged.

5. A further uniform paid piece: mixed shared-square sectors
------------------------------------------------------------
For i,j in {0,1,2} let a=i+j and consider the UNION of ordered pairs
h,k<=n sharing q^2 for some prime q>n^rho, rho>0. Count the union once;
an upper bound may sum over q, but an identity may not subtract overlapping
q sectors repeatedly. Write h=q^e H,k=q^g K, q-free H,K,e,g>=2.
From (4.2), complete geometric tails and the cofactor divisor bound give
  sum_(h,k<=n, q^2 divides both)|nu_i(h)nu_j(k)|
       <=n^2*q^(-4-a*u)*(1+log n)^2.                        (5.1)
For example, sum_e>=2 |li(e)|<=q^(-i*u), and the cofactor range is
bounded by n/q^e, so the complete first-leg sum is
<=n*q^(-2-i*u)*(1+log n). No exponent cutoff is used.

The elementary tail bound, valid for real y>=1, is
  sum_(integer r>y)r^(-4-a*u)
       <=[1+1/(3+a*u)]*y^(-3-a*u).
Thus the whole block-normalized union has the absolute envelope
  C_a*2^max(2-(3+a*u)*rho,0)
      *X^(1-(3+a*u)*rho)*(1+log(2X))^2,
  C_a=1+1/(3+a*u).                                         (5.2)
The same bound holds after restricting to the joined high-product
off-diagonal, since the bound is absolute. Multiply by the fixed scalar
state coefficients when expanding f tensor f; orientations are retained.

The thresholds for cofinal polylog payment are therefore
  nu0 tensor nu0: rho>=1/3;
  nu0 tensor nu1, either orientation: rho>=3/10;
  nu0 tensor nu2 and nu1 tensor nu1: rho>=3/11;
  nu1 tensor nu2: rho>=1/4;
  nu2 tensor nu2: rho>=3/13.
Any mu leg vanishes on a shared square. These are new actual-source mixed
valuation envelopes in the bounded-recovery four-state basis. They do
NOT pay the common nu0 tensor nu0 sector below its threshold, or all
first-valuation/common-prime sectors. They improve a real part of the
forcing ledger without assigning a whole-energy contraction.

6. Complete prime-orbit kernel defects and the remaining support problem
------------------------------------------------------------------------
For a fixed q, set s_i=sum_(e>=0)li(e). The exact sums from (4.1) are
  s0=s1=s3=0, s2=1+t.
For q-free H,K and ANY complete kernel L(h,k), finite-prefix zero
extension permits the exact orbit identity
  sum_(e,g>=0)li(e)lj(g)L(q^e H,q^g K)
    =si*sj*L(H,K)
      -sum_(e,g>=0)li(e)lj(g)[L(H,K)-L(q^e H,q^g K)].        (6.1)
The infinite coefficient sums converge absolutely at this fixed q; the
actual prefix side is finite. Apply this to
  L_X(h,k)=W_X(h,k)*1_(hk>X)*1_(h!=k).
This preserves every source term in the joined off-diagonal.

Its defects have BOTH prefix exits and high-product/diagonal ENTRY edges.
They are generally signed; a nonnegative W-exit theorem alone is not a
nonnegative theorem for L_X. Only nu2 tensor nu2 has a nonzero bulk in
(6.1), with multiplier (1+t)^2. This deterministic positive-pole channel
must stay coupled to the other exit/entry terms. No separate payment of
that uncentered channel is claimed.

The shared-square parts used in (5.2) have H,K<=n/q^2. But e=0 or g=0
keeps a q-free cofactor as large as the CURRENT scale. A prime-orbit
rewriting is not automatically a smaller-scale recurrence for the entire
target. A certificate must list that current-scale complement and either
prove its absorption or leave it open. A finite prime filtration with
partial local laws does not acquire a uniform source estimate by notation.

7. A proper-prime-power recurrence with paid Volterra memory
-----------------------------------------------------------
There is another finite construction primitive, derived directly by
differentiating the complete Dirichlet series (or formal convolution).
Let Lambda be the prime-power von Mangoldt coefficient, including ALL
prime powers. The exact logarithmic coefficient equations are
  nu_i(n)*log n=(nu_i*L_i)(n),
  L0(r)=Lambda(r)*(r^(-u)-2),
  L1(r)=Lambda(r)*(r^(-u)-1-r^(-v)),
  L2(r)=Lambda(r)*(r^(-u)-r^(-v)),
  L3(r)=-Lambda(r).                                        (7.1)
The r=1 terms vanish. Every nonzero cofactor n/r is strictly smaller.
Let c=(beta,-beta,-R_u,-beta), A_i=sum nu_i, F=sum_i c_i A_i.
Summation by parts gives, for real x>=1,
  F(x)*log x=sum_(r<=x)sum_i c_i*L_i(r)*A_i(x/r)
                                    +integral_1^x F(y)dy/y. (7.2)
Units determine the finite initial data; at x=1 the identity is 0=0.
No coefficient series at kappa was summed in deriving this formula.

For x>=L=64 define the JOINT proper-prime-power bank
  P_F(x)=1/log x*sum_(r<=x)sum_i c_i*L_i(r)*A_i(x/r).
Every arithmetic argument is <=x/2. The remaining Volterra memory splits
into finite head C_head=integral_1^64 F(y)dy/y and a tail operator.
For sigma>=1/2 its tail norm is <=1/[sigma*log64]<1/2, since the Hardy
operator integral_64^x F(y)dy/y has norm <=1/sigma. The elementary lower
bound log2>2/3 gives log64>4. Therefore, on every truncated norm,
  ||F||_(sigma,[64,H])
     <=(1-1/[sigma log64])^(-1)
          *[||P_F||_(sigma,[64,H])
             +|C_head|*64^(-sigma)/(sqrt(2sigma)*log64)].      (7.3)
The finite head is explicit and source-uniformly bounded using
|f|<=(3beta+R_u)d2:
  |C_head|<=(3beta+R_u)*64*log64.
Thus memory is a paid absorption, not an unpaid current-scale cycle.

But the arithmetic bank P_F is NOT paid. Kernel L0 has limiting negative
amplitude two; no generic positive coercive contraction for all channels
has been proved. Other channel estimates cannot be separately assumed at
RH scale. A usable intermediate lemma must bound the complete JOINT
prime-power/cofactor bank, or its signed cross-prime covariance with a
well-founded coupled state system. This is a distinct explicit source
correlation primitive; (7.3) is recovery, not the bound for it.

Separate channel centering explains a further obstruction. The kappa
main-prefix coefficients of A0,A1,A2 are respectively
  m0=1/[kappa*zeta(kappa)^2],
  m1=1/[kappa*zeta(kappa)*zeta(kappa+v)],
  m2=1/[kappa*zeta(kappa+v)],
and m3=0. Here m0,m2>0,m1<0 and sum_i c_i m_i=0 EXACTLY.
Replacing A_i by centered companions plus m_i x^kappa leaves the
deterministic bank
  D_F(x)=beta*x^kappa/log x
       *[-(2m0-m1)*sum_(r<=x)Lambda(r)r^(-kappa)
                    +m0*sum_(r<=x)Lambda(r)r^(-kappa-v)].    (7.4)
The second sum converges absolutely, since kappa+v=4/3. The first is
macroscopic: elementary Chebyshev lower theta(x)>=c*x gives magnitude
at least a constant times x^(1-kappa). Consequently |D_F(x)| is eventually
at least c'*x/log x; it has no separate cofinal critical-energy bound.
This uses neither PNT nor RH. The required Chebyshev lower bound follows
from log binom(2n,n)<=psi(2n), binom(2n,n)>=4^n/(2n+1), and the elementary
O(sqrt x*log^2 x) prime-power difference psi(x)-theta(x).

Therefore the centered companion bank and D_F must stay JOINT. The
analytic pole-balance equation does not permit separate absolute payment
of (7.4), nor convergence of a bracket coefficient tail at kappa. In the
unit-cofactor prime strip there is genuine algebraic cancellation of the
constant parts: sum_i c_i*nu_i(q)=-R_u*t+(beta+R_u)*t^2.
It removes an order-one local term but leaves a power-weighted prime
channel, which still is not independently RH-scale. Finite-cofactor
pairings must retain this remaining pole channel and its composite complement.

8. Small enumerable construction family and proof gates
-------------------------------------------------------
A bounded detector grammar can use these primitives without further
parameter tuning:
  * fixed rational soft shift alpha>=1/2 and fixed rational Euler power r;
    carry the norm/log recovery certificate (2.2)-(2.3);
  * the four typed source states, exact prime coefficients e=0,e=1 and
    closed e>=2 tails (4.1)-(4.2), all cofactor states present;
  * fixed rational linear/tensor combinations, and rational-in-t pairing
    ratios as in (4.3)-(4.4), with every cofactor defect retained;
  * high shared-square cutoff q>n^rho with rational rho and the proved
    sector envelope (5.2), counting overlapping unions once;
  * complete joined-kernel entry/exit defects (6.1), and full proper-
    prime-power banks (7.1)-(7.3) with jointly retained pole compensation.

The generic simple-tail compiler now proposed in
joint_preconditioner_discovery.py has a precise sufficient certificate.
In a local variable x=q^(-1/6), suppose a term has at most one simple
denominator 1-x^p*z and its EXACT e>=2 tail is
  l_e=C*x^a*(1-x^p)^m*x^(p*(e-2)),
with p>0,a>=0,m>=1 and rational C. Then
  sum_(e>=2)|l_e|=|C|*x^a*(1-x^p)^(m-1)<=|C|*x^a.           (8.1)
If |C|<=1 and an exact Bernstein certificate bounds the first-valuation
polynomial by two on x in [0,1], every higher local coefficient is at most
one and the unit is one. Thus the full multiplicative cofactor is bounded
by 2^omega(n)<=d2(n). This is a safe GENERIC cofactor certificate; it
does not require the sharper |nu1|<=1 of the special reduction.
For two such tails with exponents a_left,a_right, the sector prime weight
is w=(a_left+a_right)/6, and (5.2) generalizes with 3+a*u replaced by 3+w
and mass constant multiplied by |C_left*C_right|. Therefore the generic
threshold is exactly rho>=1/(3+w). Multiply again by source scalar
coefficient magnitudes when bounding an actual beta/R_u tensor combination.

The compiler must retain unresolved cases with multiple poles, rational
noninteger local powers, finite polynomial heads extending above the first
valuation, or an uncertified cofactor bound. A formal simple numerator
cannot stand in for those complete coefficients. Polynomial cancellation
and tail division are identities; Bernstein and (8.1) are sufficient
inequalities, not claims of optimality or whole signed control.
For the general preconditioned correction (beta*delta-d), shifts at 1/2
give a proved cofinal/polylog restoration, not necessarily finite critical
norm. Only above the boundary is the constant critical-norm statement used
in (3.2) justified by this general argument. Below the boundary retain
the recorded power loss; calling the correction retained does not pay it.

A small exact signature search should FIRST expand into the independent
cofactor tensor basis, THEN measure the order in t. Certificates based
only on H=K=1, or cancellations hiding an inverse t, fail this gate.
Retaining the common nu0 projection leaves an unsuppressed signature;
success on a stable/mixed projection is not full-target recovery.
Once an actual mixed sector is paid, its complement remains in the joined
word; no SF/NS or cross-band cancellation is replaced by separate bounds.

For a candidate to count as more than a reversible presentation it must
propose a source-specific intermediate lemma: for example a signed bound
for a stated cofactor-defect entry/exit correlation, or for the JOINT
proper-prime-power bank after the exact pole compensation in (7.4).
The lemma must state all scales and constants, identify smaller arguments,
and avoid assuming either the target moment or separately RH-strength
companion moments. It may be numerically tested as a finite hypothesis;
finite tests do not turn the norm certificate into that missing lemma.
