LOCAL DIVISOR AUDIT FOR RATIONAL POWERS OF ORDINARY ZETA

This is a guided analytic calibration, not an autonomous operator discovery
or a new held-out arithmetic search. It supplies a general branch rejection
rule to the rational-power detector. A proved local obstruction is sufficient
to reject a claimed holomorphic continuation over an entire half-plane.

Let a=p/d be reduced, d>0. For a meromorphic function f with local divisor
order m at s0, f(s)=(s-s0)^m g(s), g nonzero and holomorphic locally. Analytic
continuation of f^a around s0 multiplies a chosen branch by exp(2*pi*i*a*m).
It is single-valued locally only if a*m is an integer. It extends holomorphically
across s0 only if that integer is nonnegative. These are exact rational tests.

The argument principle gives the divisor order from a certified contour:
(2*pi*i)^(-1) integral f'(s)/f(s) ds=number of zeros-number of poles.
The producer encloses each boundary segment with native complex balls and
certifies f!=0 there; native rigorous integration encloses the contour integral
and isolates one integer. The independent audit uses a different larger
rectangle, finer boundary partition and higher precision. Known dependency:
ordinary zeta is meromorphic with its sole pole, simple, at s=1.

For the small rectangle around one the certified winding is -1. Adding the
one known pole gives zero enclosed zeros. The larger independent rectangle
also encloses zero zeros and contains the producer's rectangle. Thus the pole
has local order m=-1 and zeta^a has order -a. For every noninteger rational
a, including a=-1/2,-1/3,-1/6, this is a branch obstruction inside Re(s)>1/2.
For integer a>0 it is an ordinary pole; for integer a<=0 there is no local
obstruction at s=1. An integer-order single-valued holomorphic multiplier
cannot cancel a nonintegral order. Thus a finite analytic multiplier outside
the fractional power does not remove this branch obstruction.

Taking a power AFTER combining divisors is different: ((s-1)zeta(s))^a
has local order a*(1-1)=0 and admits a nonzero local holomorphic branch at
s=1. This verifies only a local pole repair. It does not prove a global branch,
signed arithmetic bounds or exclusion of ordinary zeros. The same general
valuation addition rule works at other zeros and poles; it does not assert
that all their locations have been found.

At the already independently certified simple ordinary critical zero the
order is m=1. All noninteger powers have nontrivial local monodromy there.
This is not a counterexample to an RH half-plane Re(s)>1/2: the critical zero
is on its boundary. It checks that the detector retains the original branch
obstruction rather than discarding it through a numerical branch convention.

A negative power has a singularity at EVERY ordinary zero, whatever its
positive multiplicity. A positive fractional power need not: if d divides
the zero multiplicity, the local order can be a nonnegative integer. Merely
proving a positive fractional power holomorphic is therefore insufficient
to exclude all possible ordinary zeros. Its required zero bridge must track
all multiplicities, or prove a nonzero inverse or another stronger property.

Consequently no naive zeta^(-p/d) coefficient bound may be labelled RH-sufficient
without handling its pole-induced branch at one. The current fractional
moment pilot instead takes signed powers of an already zero-faithful linear
Mobius residual and recovers its original effective moment order. That pilot's
conditional Mellin bridge is unaffected by this additional negative calibration.
No uniform signed arithmetic estimate or RH proof is established here.
