What the detector does and what analysts supply
----------------------------------------------
The user asked whether bounds were being found by the detector or mostly
supplied by analytical subagents. Both were happening: the earlier detector
searched potential coefficients and moment arguments, while analysts chose
new representations and inference mechanisms. The previous signed primitive
search found the original energy as its own unpaid endpoint, not a solution.

This extension makes the argument search explicit. Starting from the current
missing component bound, the executable proposes a divisor-prefix auxiliary,
generates its proof subgoals, explores applicable rule compositions, derives
their constants/exponents/logs, and compares arguments across window scales.
It also records a REQUIRED signed-convolution/Mellin recovery goal. That goal
has no licensed proof in this grammar and remains an explicit open leaf.

Analysts supplied the reusable Fourier, summation, inverse, Abel and spacing
rules, and an independently implemented checker. The detector chooses their
applications and combinations. It does not invent arbitrary new inference
rules or discover Fourier analysis unaided. There are no runtime model calls.
This is a finite argument grammar, not a general-purpose theorem prover.

Implementation and reusable API
-------------------------------
goal()/plan() accept typed pointwise-square, spectrum, cofactor, reciprocal,
variance and interval goals with rational decay and finite-support/mass costs.
The current source adapter handles the verified p2,m3 component; generic
auxiliary arguments are independent of that adapter. Exact rational decay
permits 1/2,1/3,1/6 and other roots. Interval costs retain both U and L.

Search nodes contain their goal, rule, earlier premise identifiers, complete
bound and arithmetic assumptions. All attempted premise combinations and
side-condition failures are reproducible. Structural memoization shares
common subgoals; node/depth limits bound the search. Exact goal cycles and
unsupported goals cannot create proofs. This is not a decision procedure
for arbitrary semantic equivalence between different proposed lemmas.

rank() chooses by exponent, logarithmic degree, arithmetic assumptions,
constant, then proof size. The frozen study compares L=U^lambda at
lambda=0,1/2,1,2,3,6; complete two-variable bounds remain in the artifact.
Changing the allowed rules, rational decay, or window changes the selected
argument. compile_sawtooth() separately produces exact rational means,
combined conductors and period variance for finite rational weight arrays,
without enumerating their enormous common period. Its scope is finite algebra.

The compiler retains the full signs; the exact period variance is

The search compares several genuinely different bound compositions:
 - pointwise triangle gives variance<=U^2/4;
 - a uniform cofactor maximum gives U(1+log U)^2/12;

Crucially U/2 is valid for ANY bounded weight sequence. The actual Mobius
provider loses this comparison; there is no arithmetic exponent advantage.
This corrects the tempting but false attribution of this gain to signed
prime cancellation. The executable confirms this by removing the arithmetic
axioms and searching the SAME auxiliary again.

Full-period variance is not simply asserted on short windows. Spacing of
the combined rational frequencies pays every off-diagonal Gram entry and
gives, for any interval of L positive integers,

The direct route wins for sufficiently short windows; the spectral argument
wins for longer ones. Rational decay produces other choices: weighted Abel
wins at decay1/6, whereas the direct cofactor route keeps only one logarithm
at the critical decay1/2. These choices are found by argument comparison.
