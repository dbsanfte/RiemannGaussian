FRACTIONAL PRIME KERNEL: INDEPENDENT EXACT REVIEW 051

Scope and status
================
This is a source-only analytical review of the new unrounded arithmetic kernel.
No actual-source values, numerical study, tests, or hashes were produced by this
review. Only this new review file was written. The pointwise claims below are
proved; a centered distribution error, the original floor-residual bound, and
RH remain OPEN. A negative witness refutes uniform nonnegativity of that kernel,
not RH or every possible signed use of the expression.

The ordinary-zeta background used here is its meromorphic continuation, sole
simple pole at 1 with residue 1, Euler product in Re(s)>1, alternating-series
identity, functional equation, and usual zero location and reflection. Primary
references are [DLMF 25.2](https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.2),
[DLMF 25.4](https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.4), and
[DLMF 25.10](https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.10). The kernel identities, bounds, and
conditional bridge are derived here; these references do not supply the new
centered error estimate.

1. Complete arithmetic definition and Boolean reduction
=======================================================
Fix a,b>=0, a+b=1, a!=b, 0<alpha<=2 and sign sigma in {+1,-1}.
For every ordinary integer n>=2 define

 D(n)=sum_{d|n} mu(d)*(a*log(d)+b*log(n/d))^alpha,
 K(n)=sigma*D(n),                 K(1)=0,  0^alpha=0.

Let P be the DISTINCT prime divisors of n, k=|P|, L=log(n), x_p=log(p),
c=a-b, and C=bL. Then

 D(n)=sum_{S subset P} (-1)^|S|*(C+c*sum_{p in S} x_p)^alpha.       (1)

Every argument in (1) is between 0 and L. Repeated prime valuations have NOT
been discarded: they enter C=b log(n), even though mu restricts the divisor
choice to squarefree d. In particular replacing log(n) by log(rad(n)) would
change the kernel on prime powers.

For c<0 write h=-c=b-a>0 and complement S inside P. With

 C0=bL-h*log(rad(n))=aL+h*(L-log(rad(n)))>=0,

equation (1) is (-1)^k times the same forward Boolean difference with positive
step h and base C0. This complement factor is why reversing a and b does not
simply preserve the forward positivity statement.

2. Fractional normalizers and exact signs
========================================
For 0<alpha<1 put

 I_alpha=int_0^infinity (1-exp(-u))*u^(-alpha-1) du.

The integrand is O(u^-alpha) at zero and O(u^-alpha-1) at infinity. Thus the
normalizer is finite and strictly positive. Substitution proves

 x^alpha=I_alpha^(-1)*int_0^infinity
                    (1-exp(-xt))*t^(-alpha-1) dt,

including x=0. One integration by parts gives
I_alpha=Gamma(1-alpha)/alpha, where Gamma is its positive defining integral.
Applying the identity to the FINITE sum (1), its constant terms cancel. If c>0,

 -D(n)=I_alpha^(-1)*int_0^infinity exp(-Ct)
             product_{p|n}(1-exp(-c*x_p*t))*t^(-alpha-1) dt >0.     (2)

At zero the product is O(t^k), with k>=1>alpha. At infinity the remaining
t^-alpha-1 is integrable even if C=0. For c<0 the complement formula gives
the sign of D as (-1)^(k+1). These are strict signs, not heuristic cancellation.

For 1<alpha<2 instead use the positive finite normalizer

 J_alpha=int_0^infinity (exp(-u)-1+u)*u^(-alpha-1) du
         =Gamma(2-alpha)/(alpha*(alpha-1)).

This follows by two integrations by parts. The corresponding representation
of x^alpha is obtained by substitution. For k>=2 the constant and linear
terms in (1) both cancel; hence, for c>0,

 D(n)=J_alpha^(-1)*int_0^infinity exp(-Ct)
             product_{p|n}(1-exp(-c*x_p*t))*t^(-alpha-1) dt >0.     (3)

The origin is integrable because k>=2>alpha. For c<0 the sign in this range
is (-1)^k when k>=2. At k=1, for either fractional range, D is the difference
of two strictly increasing powers and has sign -sign(c).

At alpha=1 direct expansion gives

 D(n)=-c*Lambda(n).                                                (4)

At alpha=2 it gives

 D(n)=-c*Lambda(n)*log(n)+c^2*(Lambda*Lambda)(n).                    (5)

Here convolution is full Dirichlet convolution. At n=p^e equation (5) equals
-c*(2be+c)*log(p)^2; at n=p^e*q^f with distinct p,q it equals
2c^2 log(p)log(q); at omega(n)>=3 it is zero. Exponents e,f remain unrestricted.

The complete uniform-nonnegativity classification is consequently:

 * 0<alpha<1: ONLY a>b and sigma=-1 is nonnegative on all n>=2.
 * alpha=1: exactly sigma*c<0 gives c_abs*Lambda; omega>=2 is zero.
 * 1<alpha<2: neither orientation/sign is uniformly nonnegative.
 * alpha=2: ONLY a<b and sigma=+1 is uniformly nonnegative.

If sigma*c>0, n=2 is a negative witness for every alpha. In the remaining
unlicensed branches: forward 1<alpha<=2 with sigma=-1 is negative at n=6;
backward alpha<1 with sigma=+1 is negative at n=6; backward 1<alpha<2 with
sigma=+1 is negative at n=30. No sign claim depends on a table of mu values.

3. Pointwise bounds, including the full omega bound
==================================================
In the forward concave branch K=-D, equation (2) and factors between 0 and 1
give the exact one-factor bound

 0<K(n)<=min_{p|n} [(bL+c log(p))^alpha-(bL)^alpha]
       <=(c log(p_min))^alpha.                                    (6)

The last inequality is the concave increment bound; it can also be read from
the same positive integral after dropping exp(-Ct). It includes b=0 and all
prime powers. Specifically

 K(p^e)=[(be+c)^alpha-(be)^alpha]*(log(p))^alpha.                    (7)

At b=0 this is (log(p))^alpha for EVERY e. At b>0 it decays in e with the
usual derivative scale, rather than being supported only at e=1.

If b>0, 1-exp(-y)<=y and a positive Gamma integral give the stronger bound

 K(n)<=alpha*product_{j=1}^{k-1}(j-alpha)
        *c^k*product_{p|n} log(p)/(bL)^(k-alpha).                   (8)

Indeed Gamma(k-alpha)/I_alpha=alpha*product(j-alpha), by k-1 integrations by
parts. Alternatively (8) is the k-fold fundamental-theorem-of-calculus bound
for the derivative of x^alpha. Its exponent alpha-k is negative, so replacing
the base bL+c*sum(u_p) by bL increases the upper bound. This step is INVALID
at b=0; (8) must be disabled there, while (6) remains valid.

AM-GM, sum log(p)=log(rad(n))<=L, yields

 K(n)/L^alpha <= alpha*product_{j=1}^{k-1}(j-alpha)
                    *c^k*b^(alpha-k)/k^k.                       (9)

There is a convenient completely elementary further bound, valid also at
k=1 with its empty product interpreted as 1:

 K(n)/L^alpha <= 2*alpha*b^alpha*(c/(2b))^k/k.                    (10)

To see it, product(j-alpha)<=(k-1)! and AM-GM on 1,...,k-1 gives
(k-1)!<=(k/2)^(k-1). This is an exponential saving in omega ONLY when c<2b,
equivalently b>1/4. At b=1/4 it only gives 1/k; at b<1/4 this particular bound
does not give exponential decay. It gives no prime-distribution or RH bound.

For the licensed quadratic backward branch, h=b-a in (0,1], equation (5) is

 K=h*Lambda*log+h^2*(Lambda*Lambda),
 0<=K(n)<=h*(log(n))^2.                                          (11)

For p^e the coefficient is h*((1+h)e-h); comparison with h e^2 follows from
(e-1)(e-h)>=0. For two prime divisors, 2h^2 log(p)log(q)<=h L^2; otherwise
the kernel is zero. This proves the advertised bound without dropping powers.

A useful generic absolute envelope, even on the unlicensed signed branches,
is |D(n)|<=(log(n))^alpha. For alpha<1 use (6) after the complement rewrite.
For 1<alpha<2 and k>=2, drop all but two factors in the positive integral (3)
and replace its nonnegative base by zero. The resulting second difference is
[h(x_p+x_q)]^alpha-(h x_p)^alpha-(h x_q)^alpha<=L^alpha, with h=|c|.
For k=1 both power arguments lie in [0,L]. The alpha=2 formulas prove the
remaining endpoint. Thus all Dirichlet series in the next section converge
absolutely in Re(s)>1, without an RH premise.

4. Exact Euler integral and justified exchanges
===============================================
For fixed t>=0, the complete local Euler factor, including every e>=1, is

 1+(1-p^(-ct))*sum_{e>=1} p^(-e(s+bt))
       =(1-p^(-(s+at)))/(1-p^(-(s+bt))).                          (12)

Consequently in Re(s)>1,

 sum_{n>=1} n^(-s-bt)*product_{p|n}(1-p^(-ct))
       =zeta(s+bt)/zeta(s+at).                                   (13)

If c<0, use the complement rewrite: the absolute coefficient is at most
n^(-at)<=1. If c>0 it is at most n^(-bt)<=1. This justifies the initial Euler
product for both orientations. Integrated K is generally NOT multiplicative;
only each fixed-t integrand in (13) is multiplicative.

For 0<alpha<1 the RAW kernel has the exact series identity

 sum_{n>=2} D(n)n^(-s)
   =-I_alpha^(-1)*int_0^infinity
             [zeta(s+bt)/zeta(s+at)-1]*t^(-alpha-1) dt.            (14)

Multiply by sigma for K. In the positive forward branch the prefactor is +.
The coefficient integrands have fixed signs by (2) and its complement, so
their integrated absolute values are |D(n)|. The generic absolute envelope
and sum (log(n))^alpha n^(-Re(s))<infinity justify absolute Fubini, including
complex s. Independently, near zero the bracket is O(t); at infinity it is
bounded and t^-alpha-1 is integrable. At b=0 the bracket tends zeta(s)-1,
not zero; this is still integrable. At a=0 it tends 1/zeta(s)-1.

For 1<alpha<2 the exact compensated identity for RAW D is

 sum D(n)n^(-s)=J_alpha^(-1)*int_0^infinity
  [zeta(s+bt)/zeta(s+at)-1-c*t*L_Lambda(s)]*t^(-alpha-1) dt,       (15)
 L_Lambda(s)=sum Lambda(n)n^(-s)=-zeta'(s)/zeta(s).

The subtracted first-order term is essential: the remaining bracket is O(t^2)
at zero. At infinity its linear growth is integrable because alpha>1. The
compensated coefficient representations and the same envelope pay absolute
Fubini. Formula (15) is an analytical deduction, not an extra licensed
nonnegativity branch in the 051 executable certificate.

For alpha=1 the raw series is c*zeta'/zeta. For the positive quadratic backward
branch it is h*(zeta'/zeta)' + h^2*(zeta'/zeta)^2. All statements here are
initially and unconditionally in Re(s)>1; continuation into the critical strip
does not follow merely from positivity or a pointwise upper bound.

5. Real main term, abscissa, and the genuine missing bound
========================================================
Now restrict to the licensed forward fractional branch a>b, sign=-1,
0<alpha<1, and write F(s)=sum K(n)n^-s. For real delta down to zero and b>0,

 F(1+delta) ~ c*b^(alpha-1)*Gamma(alpha+1)*delta^(-alpha).         (16)

For small t, factor zeta(1+z)=z^-1*h(z), h(0)=1. The quotient bracket is
c*t/(delta+bt)+O(t), uniformly for positive small delta,t. Its error integral
is O(1). Substituting u=bt/delta in the main integral gives (16); the elementary
positive integral int u^-alpha/(1+u) du equals Gamma(alpha)Gamma(1-alpha)
by a Laplace integral and Tonelli. Large t contributes a bounded term.

For b=0 (hence a=c=1), instead there is a simple pole:

 delta*F(1+delta) -> kappa>0,
 kappa=I_alpha^(-1)*int_0^infinity
             t^(-alpha-1)/zeta(1+t) dt.                          (17)

Near zero reciprocal zeta is t+O(t^2), so the integral converges; its tail
converges because alpha>0. The same local quotient factorization and bounded
large-t ratio justify domination in (17). Thus the positive series has exact
abscissa of convergence 1. Positivity plus divergence at 1 prevents convergence
at any smaller real argument. An uncentered square-root-type summatory moment
cannot bypass this real main term.

The appropriate EXACT center, rather than just its leading asymptotic, is

 m(x)=I_alpha^(-1)*int_0^infinity
             x^(-bt)/zeta(1+ct)*t^(-alpha-1) dt,  x>=1,
 M(x)=int_1^x m(u) du.                                           (18)

It is positive and finite: reciprocal zeta is ct+O(t^2) at zero and is between
0 and 1 for t>0. In particular 0<m(x)<=m(1), M(1)=0. At b=0,
M(x)=kappa*(x-1) exactly. At b>0,
m(x)~alpha*c*b^(alpha-1)*(log(x))^(alpha-1); its integral has the corresponding
leading x(log(x))^(alpha-1) scale. Lower-order real terms are not negligible
at a square-root target and must not be removed by fitting only a leading pole.

For A(x)=sum_{2<=n<=x}K(n), the all-real, all-epsilon error premise

 |A(x)-M(x)|<=C_(epsilon,a,b,alpha)*x^(1/2+epsilon)
 for every epsilon>0 and every real x>=2                         (19)

implies RH. The detailed conditional proof is in the separate new
FRACTIONAL_PRIME_RH_BRIDGE_REVIEW_052.txt. Briefly, the Mellin transform of the
full density is holomorphic at nonreal points off the real cut. At a rightmost
ordinary-zeta zero at any fixed nonzero height and Re(rho)>1/2, the quotient
integral has a nonzero negative delta^-alpha singularity along s=rho+delta.
This holds for ANY zero multiplicity. The premise (19) would make the centered
transform holomorphic on Re(s)>1/2, a contradiction. No converse is proved,
and (19) itself remains OPEN. No continuation of the uncentered F throughout
that half-plane is asserted unconditionally.

6. Degeneracy and usefulness limits for discovery
================================================
The excluded a=b case is identically zero for n>=2. Small c can also shrink a
finite kernel without creating cancellation: for fixed n as c down to zero,
a=(1+c)/2, b=(1-c)/2, the forward kernel divided by c tends

 alpha*(1/2)^(alpha-1)*Lambda(n)*(log(n))^(alpha-1).

Higher-omega terms are higher order in c at fixed n. This fixed-n limit is not
a uniform summatory limit. Likewise alpha tending to 1 gives c*Lambda at fixed
n, providing the meaningful calibration endpoint, not an RH proof. Discovery
should retain parameter/source normalization when comparing finite amplitudes.

The product estimate can control kernels with many prime divisors and provides
a genuine source-specific bound. It does not pay the prime or prime-power
distribution error; those channels remain positive and carry the real main
term. An all-scale signed target must keep the arithmetic sum and the entire
center joined. Positivity does not justify dropping that cross term, and an
absolute diagonal envelope does not bound a centered square-root remainder.

The Laplace quotient integral offers exact fractional Dirichlet operators and
local transfer identities for further argument search. These identities use
unrounded logarithms and powers. An implementation involving the previous
ceil-rounded moving profiles must add its actual rounding and truncation
compensators; multiplicativity cannot be imported from this unrounded theorem.
No norm-equivalence or quantitative recovery of the original floor target has
been proved here. The conditional implication (19)=>RH is qualitative and
has an explicit unproved arithmetic premise.
