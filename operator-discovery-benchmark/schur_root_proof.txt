PROOF OF THE SCHUR DISK AND ANALYTIC TAIL CERTIFICATES

This is a universal mathematical argument with implementation certificates
computed by Arb balls; it is not a theorem-prover certificate. Each root
correction uses the coefficients and analytic polynomial-growth tail proved
in mixed_root_proofs.txt and carry_zero_proof.txt. The first root coefficients
are rational and computed by two different, exactly agreeing algorithms:
integer-binomial valuation kernels, and transported ordinary convolution.

1. REAL POLYNOMIAL SCHUR STEP

Let P(z)=1+a_1*z+...+a_d*z^d have real coefficients and put a=a_d.
Define P*(z)=z^d*P(1/z), the reversed polynomial. If |a|<1, let
  Q(z)=(P(z)-a*P*(z))/(1-a^2).
The degree is at most d-1, and the constant is exactly 1. On |z|=1,
P*(z)=z^d*conjugate(P(z)), so |P*|=|P|. If Q has no zeros in the closed
unit disk, then P has none on the boundary: a boundary zero of P would
also be a zero of P* and Q. Since |a*P*|<|P| on the boundary, Rouche's
theorem then shows P and (1-a^2)*Q have the same number of interior zeros.
That number is zero. Starting at the final constant polynomial and working
back through this argument proves the certificate for the original P.

The same identity gives a quantitative boundary lower bound:
  (1-a^2)*|Q|=|P-a*P*|<=(1+|a|)*|P|,
hence |P|>=(1-|a|)*|Q| on the unit circle. Multiplying these inequalities
over the reduction steps yields
  min_{|z|=1}|P(z)| >= product_steps (1-|a_step|).
The implementation uses rigorously enclosed absolute upper bounds cap_step
with cap_step<1 and a ball enclosure of product_steps(1-cap_step). Its
lower endpoint is therefore a valid lower bound for the exact polynomial.

2. TRUNCATION AND THE FULL LOCAL FUNCTION

The local correction F(x) is analytic for |x|<1, by the coefficient-growth
proof. For a rational r<1, let P(z) be its exact degree-m Taylor polynomial
evaluated at x=r*z. The earlier tail theorem gives an exact rational T with
  sup_{|z|=1}|F(r*z)-P(z)|<=T.
If the certified Schur boundary lower bound exceeds T, Rouche's theorem
implies F(r*z) has the same number of zeros in |z|<1 as P, namely zero.
The strict domination also rules out boundary zeros. This certifies a
whole complex disk, not a collection of sampled angles or numerical roots.

3. ANALYTIC ZETA CONNECTION

All records use r=71/100>1/sqrt(2). If Re(s)>1/2 then for every prime p,
  |p^(-s)|<1/sqrt(p)<=1/sqrt(2)<r.
A passing disk certificate therefore rules out zeros of every local factor
of the correction Euler product on the entire half-plane Re(s)>1/2. The
compactly uniform absolute product convergence proved earlier then shows
the full correction R(s) is holomorphic and nonzero there. The corresponding
root transform H(s)=zeta(s)*R(s) consequently has the same zeros there as
zeta, including their multiplicities.

A failed bound would remain unresolved. The approximate polynomial roots
used during exploration were not promoted to analytic zeros. The certificate
uses ball arithmetic and an explicit tail for the infinite function.

4. ORIGINALITY LIMIT

Schur reduction, Rouche's theorem, weighted-convolution transports and
fractional divisor coefficients are classical. Their application here yields
proved zero-preserving root transforms, but that does not establish that
the operators or the specific analytic statements are historically new.
The registry retains the novelty question separately.
