2. A proved jump-square lemma
------------------------------
Put a(n)=p(n)-d(n), so R(x)=sum_(n<=x)a(n)-m*x^(2/3).
Because the factor increments have zero constant and |h_u|<=3, |h_v|<=3/2,
  |p(n)|<=beta*d_2(n).
The complete overlap mass per prime is beta, hence |d(n)|<=beta and
  |a(n)|<=2*beta*d_2(n).
Similarly |c(n)|=|p(n)-(l_u*l_v)(n)|<=2*beta*d_2(n), where l_b is the
literal prime-power restriction. Every bound is on the FULL coefficient.

At prime powers, (e+1)^2<=binom(e+3,3), so d_2(n)^2<=d_4(n). Ordered
factor counting gives for every real N>=1
  sum_(n<=N)d_4(n)<=N*(1+log N)^3.
Consequently for f=a or c,
  sum_(n<=N)|f(n)|^2<=4*beta^2*N*(1+log N)^3.        (2.1)
This uniform elementary estimate controls local increments, NOT prefixes.
It does not assume RH or an unproved signed correlation.

3. Exact paired energy and a subcritical within-pair component
--------------------------------------------------------------
For X<=n<2X define the complete child average and half-difference
  A(n)=[R(2n)+R(2n+1)]/2,
  B(n)=[R(2n+1)-R(2n)]/2.
All child indices together fill [2X,4X) exactly. For any fixed real lambda,
  H_lambda(n)=A(n)-lambda*R(n), rho=lambda^2/2,
  E_R(2X)=rho*E_R(X)+lambda*<R,H_lambda>_X
                         +[E_(H_lambda)(X)+E_B(X)]/2.             (3.1)
This is an identity, with EVERY mixed term. Choosing lambda=1 gives a
formal half-parent coefficient, but does not create a free contraction:
  H_1(n)=sum_(n<k<=2n)a(k)+a(2n+1)/2
     -m*[((2n)^(2/3)+(2n+1)^(2/3))/2-n^(2/3)].
It contains the entire fresh transport, not a known small error.

The whole within-pair component B IS small. Put kappa=2/3. Concavity gives
  B(n)=[a(2n+1)-m*((2n+1)^kappa-(2n)^kappa)]/2.
Using (2.1) through N<4X,
  E_B(X)<=8*beta^2*X^(-1)*(1+log(4X))^3
                +(m^2*kappa^2/2)*2^(-2/3)*X^(-5/3).              (3.2)
Its contribution to (3.1) is HALF this bound. The complete cost is summable
on dyadic scales. Thus the unresolved transport is the LOW-FREQUENCY defect
  Gamma_lambda(X)=lambda*<R,H_lambda>_X+E_(H_lambda)(X)/2,           (3.3)
with the known B cost added. Bounding H_lambda absolutely alone loses its
covariance with R. Defining H_lambda by subtracting a parent never proves
that covariance has a favorable sign.

4. Compact conditional recurrence rules, with exact consequences
----------------------------------------------------------------
Let e_j=E_R(2^j), j>=0, or another nonnegative/coercive energy with a proved
comparison to E_R. All statements here are conditional proof rules. A
certificate must supply the specified uniform DEFECT estimate, not a fit.

Rule A: strict contraction. If e_(j+1)<=rho*e_j+D_j, 0<=rho<1, then
  e_j<=rho^j*e_0+sum_(i=0)^(j-1)rho^(j-1-i)*D_i.
A bounded D gives e_j<=e_0+D/(1-rho). A polynomial (1+j)^k defect gives
polylogarithmic energy O((1+j)^k), with explicit geometric cost. A defect
C_epsilon*2^(epsilon*j) gives
  e_j<=rho^j*e_0+C_epsilon*2^(epsilon*(j-1))
                                    /[1-rho*2^(-epsilon)].
Thus arbitrarily small positive defect exponents yield the cofinal theorem.

Rule B: bounded drift. If rho=1 and D_j<=D, then e_j<=e_0+jD:
E_R(X)=O(1+log X). A (1+j)^k defect gives O((1+j)^(k+1)). An arbitrarily
small exponential-in-j defect gives the same subpower conclusion with the
geometric denominator 2^epsilon-1. Nonnegative storage W=E_R+auxiliary
squares only helps if its COMPLETE drift has such a bound, W(1) is finite,
and W>=E_R. The old handoff supplies no such credit for candidate 10.

Rule C: epsilon slack. For each epsilon>0 it suffices to prove
  e_(j+1)<=rho_epsilon*e_j+D_epsilon*2^(epsilon*(j+1)),
  rho_epsilon<2^epsilon.
Induction with K>=max(e_0,D_epsilon/[1-rho_epsilon/2^epsilon]) yields
e_j<=K*2^(epsilon*j). A fixed rho>1 instead creates an exponent floor
log_2 rho; it does NOT give cofinal epsilon. At equality an extra factor j
appears and must be paid by exponent slack, not suppressed.

Rule D: strictly smaller scales. Suppose for all sufficiently large dyadic X,
  E_R(X)<=rho*E_R(X/2)+sum_i A_i*E_i(Y_i(X))+D(X),
where 0<=rho<=1, A_i are finite, Y_i<=B_i*X^(theta_i), theta_i<1, and each
E_i has a proved comparison to the jointly controlled target/auxiliary
energy at those scales. Then for every epsilon>0 strong induction works
once
  rho*2^(-epsilon)+sum_i A_i*B_i^epsilon
                   *X^(-(1-theta_i)*epsilon)<1
and the polylogarithmic/strictly subpower D is absorbed. The finite earlier
range and all initial auxiliary energies are part of K_epsilon. This does
NOT allow E_i to be silently assumed bounded: they must be included in a
closed well-founded induction system with these comparisons.

For nondyadic Y, choose dyadic Y_0<=Y<2Y_0. Nonnegative full squares give
  E_f(Y)<=E_f(Y_0)+4*E_f(2Y_0).
The second scale is strictly below X only after its declared threshold;
otherwise it is a finite-head cost. A single parent plus constant smaller-
scale forcing with rho=1 can be subpower WITHOUT being polylogarithmic.
With rho<1, finitely many constant smaller-scale weights and polynomial
log defects, a sufficiently high logarithmic power closes because
rho+sum_i A_i*theta_i^k<1. Do not confuse these two rates.

Rule E: coupled energies. For a state vector F with first coordinate R,
choose a fixed symmetric matrix P with F^T P F>=c*R^2 for all F, c>0.
If exact arithmetic gives F(2n+b)=A_b F(n)+H_b(n), b=0,1, and
  sum_b A_b^T P A_b<=4*rho*P,
then its child energy is at most rho times its parent plus the COMPLETE
  (1/(2X^2))*sum_n F(n)^T*sum_b A_b^T P H_b(n)
          +(1/(4X^2))*sum_(n,b)H_b(n)^T P H_b(n).
Apply Rules A--D only after proving the defect and coercivity. This admits
operator+auxiliary+argument search, but arbitrary definitions of H_b by
subtraction still give identities rather than bounds. Negative cross terms
cannot be omitted merely because P or the auxiliary is positive.

5. The proposed moving-average auxiliary, with complete costs
-------------------------------------------------------------
Let H>=1 be an integer, possibly H=floor(X^a), and for X<=n<2X put
  V_bar(n)=H^(-1)*sum_(h=0)^(H-1)V(n+h),
  U(n)=T(n)+V_bar(n), D_H(n)=V(n)-V_bar(n).
Then R=U+D_H EXACTLY. Use a whole window through
2X+H-2; never clip it at 2X-1. In a future evaluation all coefficient data
through that lookahead must be available. Each degree-two factor queries
source mu only through half that coefficient horizon, but that fact is not
permission to erase the lookahead. These are auxiliaries to the original
R, not changed candidate constants or a new rescore.

For 1<=k<=H-1 set w_k=(H-k)/H and W=sum_k w_k=(H-1)/2. Exact telescoping
of EVERY composite coefficient gives
  D_H(n)=-sum_k w_k*c(n+k)+m*s_H(n),
  s_H(n)=H^(-1)*sum_(h=0)^(H-1)(n+h)^kappa-n^kappa.
Since kappa=2/3, 0<=s_H(n)<=kappa*W*n^(-1/3).
Weighted Cauchy--Schwarz and then complete offset counting give
  sum_(X<=n<2X)|sum_k w_k*c(n+k)|^2
     <=W^2*sum_(X<r<=2X+H-2)|c(r)|^2.
If H<=X, (2.1) therefore proves
  E_(D_H)(X)<=24*beta^2*W^2*X^(-1)*(1+log(3X))^3
                     +2*m^2*kappa^2*W^2*X^(-5/3)
     <=6*beta^2*H^2*X^(-1)*(1+log(3X))^3
                     +(m^2*kappa^2/2)*H^2*X^(-5/3).               (5.1)
At H=1 the exact discrepancy is zero; the W bound records that fact.

For H=floor(X^a), 0<=a<=1:
- a<1/2 gives a genuinely decaying X^(2a-1)log^3 X cost, summable dyadically;
- a=1/2 gives a polylogarithmic, cofinal-admissible cost, NOT a finite
  critical-norm conclusion from this bound;
- a>1/2 leaves a positive exponent 2a-1, so this elementary rule no longer
  closes arbitrary epsilon. Improving it needs an actual signed offset
  coefficient-correlation lemma, not the same bound with a fitted ceiling.
The smooth contribution is X^(2a-5/3), smaller throughout this threshold.
Fixed H is the a=0 situation with its fixed explicit constant.

For every eta>0, complete Young inequalities give BOTH directions
  E_R<=(1+eta)E_U+(1+1/eta)E_(D_H),
  E_U<=(1+eta)E_R+(1+1/eta)E_(D_H).                 (5.2)
Thus at a<=1/2 the cofinal theorem for U is EQUIVALENT to that for R. This
is a proof-backed change of representation and a usable feedback feature,
but NOT an automatic decrease in difficulty. At a=1/2 absorb logs into any
positive epsilon with an explicit supremum; for example
(1+log(3X))^3<=4*(1+log 3)^3*[1+(3/(e*epsilon))^3]*X^epsilon.

5a. Shifts, both channels, and nested joint-state padding
---------------------------------------------------------
The same proof covers a shift, rather than only an average, and covers
transforming either T or V. Define a finite positive offset kernel K by
  (K f)(n)=sum_h k_h*f(n+h), k_h>=0, sum_h k_h=1.
For shift(H), k_H=1. For average(H), k_h=1/H on 0<=h<H.
Its mean displacement is M_K=sum_h h*k_h, namely H for a shift and
(H-1)/2 for an average. The exact telescoping weights are
  w_j=sum_(h>=j)k_h, sum_j w_j=M_K.
Replacing W by M_K proves the single-channel discrepancy bound. If T is
transformed, D_1=T-K_1 T has NO smooth part. If V is transformed, the exact
smooth part is m*[K_1(n^kappa)-n^kappa], not a constant derivative.

Let U_1=K_T T+K_C C-m*K_C(n^kappa), with (K_T,K_C) either
(K_1,identity) or (identity,K_1), so R=U_1+D_1. Apply K_2 to the WHOLE
joint state U_1, setting U_2=K_2 U_1, D_2=U_1-K_2 U_1. Then
  R=U_2+D_2+D_1 EXACTLY.
The kernels are fixed within each base-X block; if their lengths change on
a dyadic child, the resulting kernel-change terms belong to that child's
transport identity. They cannot be silently identified with the parent.

Suppose H_1,H_2<=X, each offset support is at most its H_i, and total
lookahead is at most 2X. Every sample then lies below 4X; all coefficient
and smooth windows through 4X-1 must be retained. Put L=1+log(4X) and
M_i=M_(K_i). Uniform conservative constants valid for BOTH first channels
and for shifts/averages are
  E_(D_1)(X)<=32*beta^2*M_1^2*X^(-1)*L^3
                         +2*m^2*kappa^2*M_1^2*X^(-5/3),
  E_(D_2)(X)<=128*beta^2*M_2^2*X^(-1)*L^3
                         +2*m^2*kappa^2*M_2^2*X^(-5/3).          (5a.1)
The first smooth term is exactly zero when T is the first transformed
channel. To prove the second coefficient constant, the jump sequence of
U_1 is K_T t+K_C c, with two nonnegative coefficient stencils of total
L1 mass two. Jensen/Cauchy gives its complete window square sum at most
  2*sum_(channels,offsets)k_h*sum |f_channel(n+h)|^2
     <=64*beta^2*X*L^3.
Weighted telescoping contributes M_2^2 and splitting the coefficient and
smooth parts contributes the factor two, yielding 128. No pointwise bound
on d_2(n+h) by d_2(n) is assumed. These are full window-sum bounds.
The EXACT second smooth part is
  m*[K_2 K_C(n^kappa)-K_C(n^kappa)].
Only afterward is concavity used to bound its absolute value by
|m|*kappa*M_2*n^(-1/3). Its n dependence and first-stage offsets stay in
the identity and in every complete numerical square.

Consequently both exponents a_1,a_2<=1/2 prove polylogarithmic padding for
both stages; strict inequalities give decaying costs. An exponent 2/3
at either stage is NOT covered by a cofinal padding proof from (5a.1),
even if finite evaluations look favorable. The two-stage exact expansion
contains all three pairwise mixed terms of U_2,D_2,D_1. The coercive majorants
  F_1=2*E_(U_1)+2*E_(D_1)>=E_R,
  F_2=4*E_(U_2)+4*E_(D_2)+2*E_(D_1)>=E_R            (5a.2)
follow by two successive complete Young bounds, not by asserting favorable
signs for those cross terms. Their exact initial F_i(1) and all child
lookahead costs are retained. If the padding exponents are supported,
cofinal moments for F_i, U_i and R are equivalent with explicit enlarged
constants. This certifies the compiler's conditional padding claim; it
does not certify the unresolved U_i moment.

6. The exact offset correlations exposed by smoothing
------------------------------------------------------
Define the complete offset kernels on the declared lookahead:
  Gamma_cc(X;k,l)=X^(-2)*sum_(X<=n<2X)c(n+k)c(n+l),
  Gamma_cs(X;k)=X^(-2)*sum_n c(n+k)s_H(n).
Then the EXACT discrepancy square is
  E_(D_H)=sum_(k,l)w_k*w_l*Gamma_cc(X;k,l)
                       -2*m*sum_k w_k*Gamma_cs(X;k)+m^2*E_(s_H).  (6.1)
No stationarity in k-l, sign of off-diagonal terms, or missing-boundary
assumption is used. The elementary H^2 loss comes from bounding all these
terms absolutely. A bound of order H/X times polylogs, for example, would
be a genuinely different local coefficient-offset correlation input and
would extend the useful averaging range. It is not supplied here.

Likewise the auxiliary's FULL square is
  E_U=E_T+H^(-2)*sum_(h,k)<V(n+h),V(n+k)>_X
                    +(2/H)*sum_h<T(n),V(n+h)>_X.   (6.2)
The explicit prime/composite cross correlation can be expanded without
copying the target square. For h>=0,
  <T,C(n+h)>_X
    =X^(-2)*sum_(d<=2X-1,e<=2X+H-2)t(d)c(e)K_(X,h)(d,e),
  K_(X,h)(d,e)=max(0,2X-max(X,d,e-h)).
The smooth complement is exactly
  -m*X^(-2)*sum_(X<=n<2X)T(n)(n+h)^(2/3).
This triangular/fiber kernel identifies the actual signed divisor/prime
weight, the range, and every cutoff. A proposal to bound it must retain
both covariance and all V--V terms in (6.2). Simply asking that their
sum equal a small number renames E_U and thus the original RH-scale task.

There is a nonnegative smoothing variance, but not a free absorption credit:
  Var_V=X^(-2)*sum_n [H^(-1)sum_h V(n+h)^2-V_bar(n)^2]
       =(1/(2H^2))*sum_(h,k)E_(V(n+h)-V(n+k))>=0,
  E_U=H^(-1)*sum_h E_(T(n)+V(n+h))-Var_V.
The same jump lemma bounds Var_V by a constant multiple of the H^2/X
polylog cost. At H<=sqrt X this is cofinal padding; it cannot by itself
pay a fixed proportion of a potentially supercritical parent energy.

A precise small-padding no-go makes this clear. If ||D_H||_X<=B_X, then
  ||U||_X>=max(0,||R||_X-B_X).
Claiming E_U<=rho E_R with fixed rho<1 consequently implies
  E_R<=B_X^2/(1-sqrt rho)^2.                       (6.3)
Thus a uniform smoothing contraction with polylog B already ASSERTS the
target estimate; it does not derive it from the known padding bound.
Along any scales where E_R/B_X^2 diverges, E_U/E_R tends to one. This is an
exact obstruction to selecting an argument merely by numerical contraction.

7. Prime transfer gives a different, strictly smaller-cofactor correlation
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The actual arithmetic transfer is more informative than a norm identity.
For a prime p not dividing m>1 put r_b(p)=1-p^(-b). The established law is
  p(mp)=-(r_u(p)+r_v(p))*p(m)
                        -beta*[r_v(p)*a_u(m)+r_u(p)*a_v(m)].
Since d(mp)=0 for such m, this is also a(mp). Define for m>=2
  z_0(m)=2*p(m)+beta*[a_u(m)+a_v(m)],
  z_u(m)=p(m)+beta*a_v(m), z_v(m)=p(m)+beta*a_u(m),
so z_0=z_u+z_v and EXACTLY
  a(mp)=-z_0(m)+p^(-1/3)*z_u(m)+p^(-2/3)*z_v(m).   (7.1)
This is a full three-channel source identity. Calling p multiplicative or
erasing either a_b complement would be false.

Choose any fixed theta in (1/2,1), Y=x^theta, M=floor(x/Y). Any integer
n<=x with largest prime factor greater than Y contains exactly one such
prime, to exponent ONE. Its cofactor is m<=M<Y and automatic p does not
divide m. Since a vanishes at one-prime integers, only m>=2 contribute.
Let K_theta(x) be the COMPLETE prefix of a(n) over n<=x with largest prime
factor <=Y, and for b=0,u,v put
  Pi_b(x,m)=sum_(Y<p<=x/m)p^(-b).
Then all coefficients are partitioned exactly:
  R(x)=K_theta(x)-sum_(2<=m<=M)z_0(m)Pi_0(x,m)
           +sum_(2<=m<=M)z_u(m)Pi_u(x,m)
           +sum_(2<=m<=M)z_v(m)Pi_v(x,m)-m_u*x^(2/3).              (7.2)
Here m_u is the fixed main coefficient; summation m is a cofactor.
No prime range, repeated-prime branch, or smooth-number complement is lost.
The cutoff is real and strict on the large-prime side.

This exposes actual PRIME-BANK/SMALL-COFACTOR correlations different from
a generic bound on R itself. They are well-defined missing-lemma inputs:
weights count primes in (x^theta,x/m], and source coefficients/prefixes lie
at most at x^(1-theta). But a bound for K_theta is NOT supplied by a small
cofactor energy. It includes every smooth-number coefficient up to x.
Absolute counting only gives an x*polylog cost. It must remain in the
joint argument together with its covariance with the bank terms.

Changing the bank under transport also has an exact old/fresh boundary:
  K_theta(2x)-K_theta(x)
    =sum_(x<n<=2x,Pplus(n)<=(2x)^theta)a(n)
       +sum_(n<=x,x^theta<Pplus(n)<=(2x)^theta)a(n).
The old-bank gains in the second sum cannot be omitted when constructing
a paired defect or smaller-scale induction.

8. One entire large-prime weighted branch is rigorously subcritical
-------------------------------------------------------------------
For m>=2, |z_u(m)|,|z_v(m)|<=2*beta*d_2(m). The integer integral bound
sum_(j<=y)j^(-b)<=y^(1-b)/(1-b), 0<b<1, and ordered divisor counting give
  sum_(m<=M)d_2(m)m^(-(1-b))
                              <=M^b*(1+log M)/b.
Therefore the COMPLETE b-weighted large-prime branch has absolute bound
  |sum_(2<=m<=M)z_b(m)Pi_b(x,m)|
       <=[2*beta/(b*(1-b))]*x^(1-b*theta)
                                      *[1+(1-theta)log x].        (8.1)
Here z_b denotes the appropriately paired state of (7.1), not an omitted
selector or fitted coefficient. For b=u or v the prefactor is 9*beta.

Take theta=5/6 as a symbolic proof choice, not a numerical search. Then
M<=x^(1/6), and the ENTIRE v=2/3 branch, denoted L_v(x), obeys
  |L_v(x)|<=9*beta*x^(4/9)*(1+log x/6),
  ||L_v||_crit^2<= (9*beta)^2*(153/2).              (8.2)
Indeed integral_1^infinity x^(-10/9)(1+log x/6)^2dx=153/2.
This is strictly subcritical without RH. More generally any theta>3/4
makes the v branch subcritical. The u=1/3 branch retains exponent
1-theta/3>=2/3 even as theta approaches one, so the same absolute method
cannot peel it at the critical scale.

Consequently one new exact reduced target is
  R_new(x)=K_(5/6)(x)-sum_(2<=m<=x^(1/6))z_0(m)Pi_0(x,m)
              +sum_(2<=m<=x^(1/6))z_u(m)Pi_u(x,m)-m_u*x^(2/3),
  R(x)=R_new(x)+L_v(x).                            (8.3)
Both-direction triangle bounds with (8.2) show cofinal equivalence. The
whole cross term with L_v is kept; its finite-critical cost is paid, not
assumed negative. This eliminates a particular arithmetic term rather than
merely introducing another positive square.

The remaining bank terms can be expanded into genuinely smaller prefixes.
For any f with F(t)=sum_(2<=m<=t)f(m), finite summation by parts gives
  sum_(m=2)^M f(m)Pi_b(x,m)
    =F(M)Pi_b(x,M)+sum_(m=2)^(M-1)
                         F(m)[Pi_b(x,m)-Pi_b(x,m+1)].
All weights and weight differences are nonnegative. The boundary term is
retained: floor M does NOT generally make Pi_b(x,M) zero. This is the
precise smaller-scale weighted input a rule compiler can expose. It does
not justify replacing F by a bound already equivalent to RH.

9. Exact Abelian cancellation, and the still missing rate
--------------------------------------------------------
The new transfer states have simple Dirichlet identities over m>=2:
  Z_u(s)=P(s)+beta*(D_v(s)-1)=beta*D_u(s)*(D_v(s)-1),
  Z_v(s)=P(s)+beta*(D_u(s)-1)=beta*D_v(s)*(D_u(s)-1),
  Z_0(s)=beta*[2*D_u(s)D_v(s)-D_u(s)-D_v(s)].
Since D_u(1)=D_v(1)=0, all three have exact ANALYTIC value zero at one.
This identifies the companion cancellation that would remove the leading
large-prime mean if its finite weighted sums were controlled. It is not
an assertion that sum z_b(m)/m converges ordinarily, nor a uniform rate
for partial sums. Such an assertion requires source-specific information.
The series identities are first proved absolutely in Re(s)>1, then continued.

The small-cofactor operator has weights roughly x/(m log x), so a few
small m can create an x/log x contribution. The compensating zero at one
therefore matters; one cannot just take absolute values and call every
cofactor small. The explicit lemma candidates are bounds for these prime-
bank weighted Z prefixes and their complete covariance with K_theta, with
all endpoints and changing-bank gains retained. They are structurally
specified weighted correlations, not a claim of proved cancellation.
A full bound on their sum in (8.3) remains equivalent to the original
cofinal target. Individual estimates/comparison lemmas would be genuine
progress only if obtained without assuming that target or a square-root
M/M_2/shifted-prefix bound that already imports RH-strength information.

10. Obstructions, proof-input hygiene and ranking reductions
------------------------------------------------------------
The existing handoff's root fibers, paired polynomial-Mobius correlation,
