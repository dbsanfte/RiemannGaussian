2. Exact source and pole structure before any positive majorant

Fix integer U>=1. All products of coefficient states are Dirichlet
convolutions. Let A(n)=mu(n)1_(n<=U), c=A_hat(1), T=c delta-A,
 E=delta+One*T, D=One_[1,U], u=1/3, theta=1-u=2/3.
Thus E_hat(s)=1+zeta(s)[c-A_hat(s)]. Its pole at1 is REMOVABLE because
T_hat(1)=0 exactly; this is a harmonic identity, not a vanishing unit
coefficient. The integer unit of T is c-1 and generally is nonzero.

For arbitrary integer p>=0,m>=1 the raw function is
 F_raw(s)=zeta(s+u)A_hat(s)^p E_hat(s)^m.
For the current head-corrected component it is
 f=A^2*E^3-c^3 D,
 F_f(s)=zeta(s+u)[A_hat(s)^2 E_hat(s)^3-c^3 D_hat(s)]. (2.1)
The actual inverse identity gives A^2*E^3=c^3 One on n<=U, including n=1;
therefore f has a support gap through U. This source-specific fact must
not be claimed for arbitrary bounded coefficients in an ablation.

On a fixed strip sigma<=Re(s)<=2, 0<sigma<theta, F_raw and F_f have
only the possible SIMPLE pole at theta. Their residue coefficients are
 M_raw=A_hat(theta)^p E_hat(theta)^m,
 M_f=A_hat(theta)^2 E_hat(theta)^3-c^3 D_hat(theta).   (2.2)
Pole cancellation at1 must be applied to the WHOLE E expression before
contour shifting. Positive triangle bounds on a non-pole vertical line
do not license treating its separated summands as pole-free functions.

E(theta) itself is an ordinary moment because the actual E has bounded
prefixes. Multiplying this value three times defines an ANALYTIC residue;
it does not prove ordinary convergence of the E^3 coefficient series at
theta. Everything below uses that analytic residue explicitly. It works
without an ordinary cubic convergence premise, so the old ordinary gate
must not be relabeled proved merely by adding a moment order.

3. Uniform finite-factor bounds, including low heights and full head

Put v=1-sigma, a=1/v, d=2+a. For actual mu, every real t satisfies

head, unit and cross terms were present before applying the inequality.
For the raw p,m function the corresponding U exponent is
 b_U=2(p+m)(1-sigma).                               (3.2)

Generic bounded-weight ablation: if |a_n|<=1 and c=sum_(n<=U)a_n/n,

4. Generic executable product-moment grammar

For a factor |zeta(a_i+it)|^(b_i), 0<a_i<1,b_i>0, the detector may
reflect or not. A reflected factor has effective line a_i'=1-a_i and
height exponent b_i(1/2-a_i), by the functional equation on |t|>=2.
Choose a licensed order q_i from(1.1) at a_i', and Holder allocation
lambda_i>=0 with lambda_i q_i<=b_i and sum lambda_i<=1.
The unused Holder mass applies to the constant function. The remaining
power b_i-lambda_i q_i may use convexity on the effective line, with
height cost (b_i-lambda_i q_i)(1-a_i')/2 plus epsilon slack.
Therefore a valid dyadic PRODUCT-INTEGRAL exponent is
 d_integral=1+sum_i[reflection_i*b_i(1/2-a_i)
                  +(b_i-lambda_i q_i)(1-a_i')/2].    (4.1)
The leading1 already includes the length of the interval. Do not add a
second interval-length factor. Allocation vertices suffice for minimizing
this affine cost at fixed reflection/order choices; the full rational
simplex and all licensed orders/lines can be enumerated and replayed.

For F_raw the two complete positive branches are
 |zeta(s+u)|^2 and |zeta(s)|^(2m)|zeta(s+u)|^2.
The Perron denominator subtracts2 from EACH dyadic integral exponent.
Every resulting exponent must be strictly negative after a fixed chosen
epsilon allowance. At a zero exponent, an unspecified T^epsilon or
positive critical logarithm does not yield a summable global Mellin norm.
Convexity, reflection constants and moment constants depend on the FIXED
contour and library choices; they are independent of all source cutoffs.

The physical energy exponent is independently
 e_X=2sigma-1+lambda_cutoff*2(p+m)(1-sigma).         (4.2)
Strict height convergence and physical payment are separate gates.
The finite-source/head type, analytic centering, full unit and source
inverse bindings are additional gates. A negative exponent for one branch
alone is insufficient. No requested target power is used to prove(4.1).

6. Analytic-centered global norm: full Gaussian argument

Let P_f(x)=sum_(n<=x)(K*f)(n), extended by0 below1, with K(n)=n^(-u).
Use exp(e s^2), e>0, in Perron initially on Re(s)=2, where the coefficient
series is absolutely controlled. For each FIXED U the entire finite
polynomials and zeta have polynomial growth on the shifted strip; the
Gaussian sends horizontal edges to zero. Shift to sigma. Only theta is
crossed, giving the exact residue term
 P_e(x)-(M_f/theta)exp(e theta^2)x^theta
  =(1/(2pi i))integral_(sigma)F_f(s)exp(e s^2)x^s ds/s.
No zero-free statement or ordinary cubic moment enters this identity.

Mellin Plancherel, dominated convergence using(5.2), and the pointwise
Gaussian Perron limit away from integers identify the complete function

Thus
 integral_0^infinity |C_f(x)|^2 x^(-2sigma-1)dx
 <= C_norm(sigma) U^(10(1-sigma)).                  (6.1)
Uniformity in U comes from(5.2), not from a uniform horizontal-edge limit.
All earlier prefixes are retained. The artificial lower tail0<x<1 is
EXACTLY -(M_f/theta)x^theta and is integrable since sigma<theta. It also
gives |M_f|^2<=2theta^2(theta-sigma) times the left side of(6.1), without
assuming any ordinary coefficient moment at theta.

At integer n, define C_n=P_f(n)-(M_f/theta)n^theta. On n<=x<n+1,
 C_n=C_f(x)+(M_f/theta)(x^theta-n^theta).
For sigma<1/2 the safe integer-cell inequality of018 is

 <=8 integral_1^infinity|C_f(x)|^2x^(-2sigma-1)dx

It follows directly from the square triangle, the factor between n and

9. An optimistic-library obstruction to further absolute-envelope payment

This is a limitation of the declared proof grammar, not an arithmetic
lower bound or a claim that the corresponding actual component is large.
Even suppose every fixed moment order at every effective line>=1/2 were
available with height cost1+epsilon. On a belowhalf sigma line, reflecting
the 2m powers of zeta(sigma+it) still costs m(1-2sigma). With the native
interval-length cost1 and /s^2 cost-2, its optimistic Mellin dyadic cost
is m-1-2m sigma. Strict summation requires
 sigma>L_m=(m-1)/(2m).                              (9.1)
Ignoring all extra shifted-factor/domain costs only makes this frontier
more favorable. Leaving part of the first factor to convexity cannot
improve it in this grammar; neither can an extra shifted reflection.

Write z=lambda_cutoff*(p+m). The full finite absolute-envelope energy is
 e(sigma)=2sigma-1+2z(1-sigma).
For z<1/2 its optimistic infimum over(9.1) is
 e_inf=[-1+z(m+1)]/m.                               (9.2)
The infimum is not an admitted strict tail endpoint. For z>=1/2 the
same energy is nonnegative everywhere on0<sigma<1/2: its endpoint lower
bound is min(2z-1,z)>=0. Abovehalf contours also have e(sigma)>0
for z>0, so they do not evade this physical-cost obstruction. Thus
positive(9.2), or z>=1/2, rejects payment
by this whole absolute-envelope/native-moment language even with an
unbounded moment-order catalogue. At p=2,m=4,lambda_cutoff=1/24 one
has z=1/4 and e_inf=1/16>0; the same optimistic gate rejects m>=4.
Stronger standalone moment orders cannot fix that power cost. A next
rule must reduce the signed finite-factor U cost, exploit a genuinely
joined construction, or supply a different recovery geometry. This
obstruction never licenses rejecting an actual arithmetic bound itself.
