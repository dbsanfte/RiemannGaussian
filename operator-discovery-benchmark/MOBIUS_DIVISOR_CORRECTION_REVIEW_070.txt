LOCAL DIVISOR CORRECTIONS, QUOTIENT COST AND SCALE CLOSURE 070
===========================================================

Scope
-----
This is an independent mathematical review of the proposed correction
language, not a review of forthcoming070 numerical output. I read
MOBIUS_CONVOLUTION_SCALE_HANDOFF.txt, MOBIUS_CONVOLUTION_SCALE_REVIEW_069.txt
and the already existing mobius_convolution_scale_069.py as text. No
scientific runner, historical test, optimizer, numerical study or newly
produced070 result was executed or inspected. Only this file is written.
No hashes are made and no former frozen artifact is changed.

The purpose is a reusable bound for the actual ordinary-mu source. Local
divisor relations are sound additional arithmetic constraints. They remove
the particular scalar-prefix adversary of069 when their domain contains3.
They also have a strong degeneracy: all relations through U determine the
entire ordinary-mu table through U. Every polynomial in those prefixes
can then be corrected to its exact finite target-valued constant. That
fact alone gives no all-scale cancellation estimate.

There are two substantive outcomes here. First, a precise quotient and
defect-propagation description identifies what the correction grammar
can and cannot certify, including an obstruction for any fixed collection
of local constraints, regardless of multiplier degree. Second, a finite
divisor argument gives a smaller-rank reciprocal-moment estimate. Applied
to a separable piece of the same joined bilinear kernels, it pays an
O(C^2 N^sigma log(2N)) partial head price under explicit smaller-rank
Mertens premises. An explicit signed floor remainder and, in general,
short linear pieces remain. Neither a closed Mertens invariant nor a
complete raw-head, core, far-correlation or RH bound is proved.

1. Exact finite algebra and its information content
--------------------------------------------------
Fix a positive integer U. Work over the rational polynomial ring

 R_U=Q[m_2,...,m_U],   m_0=0, m_1=1,
 x_d=m_d-m_(d-1),
 G_n=sum_(d|n)x_d-delta_(n,1),  1<=n<=U.

G_1 is the zero polynomial, so it carries no additional information.
The x_2,...,x_U are another set of affine coordinates: x_1=1 and
m_t=1+sum_(d=2)^t x_d. In these coordinates,

 G_n=x_n+sum_(d|n, d<n)x_d,             n>=2.          (1)

The coefficient of x_n is1 and all other indices are strictly smaller.
Consequently the affine map (x_2,...,x_U)->(G_2,...,G_U) has a unit lower
triangular linear part. It is invertible over Q, with integer inverse.
Every subset of the rows G_2,...,G_U is linearly independent: in a
nonzero linear combination take the largest row index with nonzero
coefficient; its x_n coefficient cannot be canceled by smaller rows.

The equations G_n=0, 2<=n<=U, therefore have one and only one solution.
Call its increments mu(n). Explicitly mu(1)=1 and

 mu(n)=-sum_(d|n,d<n)mu(d).                            (2)

These are precisely the ordinary-mu divisor-inverse equations. Their
identification does not assume a Mertens estimate. For completeness,
the multiplicative function with prime values -1, squareful values0 and
unit1 has divisor sum product_(p|n)(1-1)=0 for n>1, and unit divisor sum1.
It solves (2), so triangular uniqueness identifies it with ordinary mu.
This proof of the arithmetic table contains no assertion about its sums.

Write a_t=sum_(n<=t)mu(n). The full ideal is

 I_U=(G_2,...,G_U)=(m_2-a_2,...,m_U-a_U).              (3)

To prove (3), use the invertible affine coordinate map, or successively
solve (1) and sum the increments. This is the maximal ideal of the single
ordinary-mu prefix point; it is radical. Its quotient is Q. In particular,

 P-Q belongs to I_U  iff  P(a)=Q(a).                  (4)

Equation (4) is a statement about this finite ring, not about growth as
U or the source horizon increases.

For a partial set S subset {2,...,U}, let I_S=(G_n:n in S). The quotient
has U-1-|S| free affine coordinates. One may choose the x_n with n not
in S as free variables and eliminate x_n for n in S in increasing order.
This gives a unique normal form after that coordinate choice. There is
no extra nonlinear arithmetic rigidity hidden in I_S: its zero set is
an affine space. Intersecting it with sign, squareful, multiplicative or
prefix-envelope constraints is an additional and separately specified
feasible-set operation.

This distinction matters for claims that a few polynomial multiples of
linear divisor equations discover a nonlinear law. Multiplication can
make an effective polynomial extension outside the affine zero set.
It does not add information to that zero set.

2. Complete elimination preserves degree but encodes the finite target
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Let P have total degree d>=1. Set e_t=m_t-a_t. Express P in the e variables
and expand. Its constant is P(a). Each remaining monomial contains at
least one e_t. Assign that monomial to one such variable and factor it.
This gives

 P(m)-P(a)=sum_(t=2)^U e_t H_t(m),
                         deg H_t<=d-1.               (5)

The change between e_t and G_n is linear, with no new variables. Hence
there are polynomial A_n of degree at most d-1 such that

 P(m)+sum_(n=2)^U A_n(m)G_n(m)=P(a).                 (6)

The sign of A is chosen to cancel (5). Constants and all cross terms in
P are essential to this statement. It is false to discard an extracted
center and apply (6) to only the visible nonconstant terms.

One need not be supplied the row target to construct (6). The triangular
recurrence (2) computes the smaller arithmetic table, and substitution
computes P(a) from the source geometry. Thus a prohibition on passing
target values to a producer does not by itself prevent finite-target
encoding. An automatic algorithm can reconstruct those values through
the constraints. Its exact certificate remains valid; its growth claim
still needs an independent estimate for the resulting constant.

The degree cap d-1 on multipliers is not a safeguard against this
degeneracy. Sparse restrictions, allowed indices, coefficient height,
monomial count and work can make particular eliminations unavailable,
but those are restrictions on representation and computation. They do
not turn a surviving constant into a paid all-scale bound.

A similar telescoping construction eliminates any contiguous block
G_2,...,G_B, B<=U, giving the representative

 P(a_2,...,a_B,m_(B+1),...,m_U).                      (7)

The remaining variables are free in the partial quotient. The coefficients
of (7) now contain exact small-prefix arithmetic. This is legitimate
smaller-domain information. It becomes a cancellation theorem only if
one proves a uniform price for those coefficients and the residual.

3. What an absolute polynomial objective can certify
----------------------------------------------------
For positive weights w_t, write a polynomial in collected monomials as

 Q(m)=sum_J q_J product_(t in J)m_t,
 ||Q||_w=sum_J |q_J| product_(t in J)w_t.              (8)

Repeated indices are retained, and the empty product is1. If |m_t|<=w_t,
then |Q(m)|<=||Q||_w. With w_t=C t^sigma, (8) is a nonnegative polynomial
in C, with degree equal to the largest surviving monomial degree.
The constant is charged exactly like the other collected coefficients.
Rational upward weight enclosures also give a valid price, with their
rounding error retained.

Suppose the box contains the ordinary-mu point a. Every correction by
I_U satisfies Q(a)=P(a), so

 ||Q||_w>=|P(a)|.                                    (9)

The constant representative (6) attains equality. Therefore unrestricted
full-domain correction minimizes (8) to exactly |P(a)|. The same is true
for any absolute range price whose value on a constant is its absolute
value and which dominates evaluation at a. This is an exact finite
optimization identity. It gives no bound for how |P(a)| scales.

If multiplier support and degree are bounded, the optimum can exceed
|P(a)|. An improvement then proves that a particular extension of P away
from the divisor variety has a lower robust price. That is useful finite
evidence. It is not by itself evidence that the normalized optimum is
bounded uniformly over source horizons.

For a fixed candidate scope and rational weights, (8) is a linear
program in coefficient variables after adding variables for absolute
values. Exact coefficient replay proves P-Q=sum A_n G_n. A rational
primal bound proves a valid upper price. A rational dual certificate can
prove optimality within the registered scope. These three certificates
have different meanings. A local optimum, a found feasible correction
or a replayed identity cannot be described as the unrestricted optimum.

Selecting a candidate at C=1 does not select the same candidate at a
general invariant constant C. A high-degree improvement at C=1 can lose
for the C required by base cases. Retain the whole polynomial price in C,
the source parameters, indices, degree, coefficient denominator and
weight enclosure; selection at one pilot value is a separate decision.

4. Exact defect propagation and the cost of approximate constraints
------------------------------------------------------------------
Introduce formal defect coordinates E_n=G_n, E_1=0. Let delta be the
Dirichlet-convolution unit and One(n)=1. Since x*One=delta+E and
mu*One=delta, finite coefficient inversion gives

 x_n=mu(n)+sum_(d|n,d>=2)mu(n/d)E_d.                  (10)

Only coefficients through n occur, so no convergence is used. Summing
over n<=t yields the exact affine inverse in prefix coordinates:

 m_t=a_t+sum_(d=2)^t a_(floor(t/d)) E_d.              (11)

Proof of (11): in (10), sum n=dm; the coefficient of E_d is
sum_(m<=t/d)mu(m)=a_(floor(t/d)).

Formula (11) exposes the cost of treating the divisor relations as
numerically small rather than exactly zero. If |E_d|<=epsilon, the
sharp real-coordinate operator price for the prefix displacement is

 epsilon sum_(d=2)^t |a_(floor(t/d))|.                (12)

It is sharp by choosing the signs of E_d to match those coefficients.
This sharpness concerns the unconstrained real defect coordinates; an
additional discrete sign domain can reduce its feasible perturbations.

For every t>=2, all d with t/2<d<=t have a_(floor(t/d))=a_1=1. Hence

 sum_(d=2)^t |a_(floor(t/d))|>=floor((t+1)/2).         (13)

The counted indices are all >=2, including at t=2. More conservatively,
the lower bound floor(t/2) also suffices for every t>=2. Unconditionally,
|a_s|<=s gives an upper bound t(1+log t). Even provisionally assuming
|a_s|<=C s^sigma, 0<=sigma<1, the triangle estimate is at most

 C t^sigma sum_(d=2)^t d^(-sigma)
 <= C t/(1-sigma).                                  (14)

The exact inverse therefore has at least linear unweighted defect gain.
A uniform absolute defect tolerance does not transfer to sublinear
prefix error. If that particular perturbation argument is to give an
O(t^sigma) displacement, its tolerance must decrease at least like
t^(sigma-1). A weighted defect norm could change the conclusion, but
its weights and uniform bound are a new premise. Exact identities have
zero defect and avoid this stability cost; they still do not bound a_t.

The lower bound in (13) was stated only as a coordinate-cost fact. It is
not a counterexample to ordinary mu and not an obstruction to every
coupled signed bound. It does show why an approximate annihilator or
floating residual cannot replace exact replay without a paid error law.

5. Fixed divisor constraints cannot defeat the finite-prime adversary
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The particular fake chi=delta_1-delta_2 has G_3=1. If U>=3 and G_3 is
used, it is excluded. If U<=2, it is exactly the ordinary-mu sequence on
the queried domain, and no local equation there can distinguish it.

There is a stronger and precise obstruction for a fixed collection of
divisor constraints. Let S be any finite subset of integers >=2. Choose
a finite prime set P that hits S: every n in S has at least one prime
divisor belonging to P. Define

 chi_P=*_(p in P)(delta_1-delta_p),
 Q_P=product_(p in P)p,
 alpha_P=product_(p in P)(1-1/p)>0.                   (15)

This is a multiplicative sequence, supported on the squarefree divisors
of Q_P, with unit1, prime values -1 on P, zero at other primes, squareful
zeros and absolute pointwise values at most1. Its divisor sum is

 (chi_P*One)(n)=product_(p|n,p in P)(1-1).             (16)

Thus G_n(chi_P)=0 for every n in S. It is enough to hit a composite
constraint with one included prime; all of its prime factors need not
belong to P. Prime constraints themselves require including that prime.
This hitting-set description is more precise than saying that every
new composite relation forces a new prime anchor.

For all t, its prefix magnitude is at most min(t,2^|P|). Thus the
unconditional envelope |m_t|<=t admits it for every P without enlarging
a constant. Also the scalar
envelope with C=2^|P| and sigma>=0 admits it. A smaller specified C is
admissible only after its actual finite prefix envelope is checked or
proved. This distinction is essential: the adversary does not establish
a barrier for every fixed small C. It remains an adversary even if the
relaxation includes pointwise magnitude, squareful zeros and coprime
multiplicativity.

Now use the admitted joined source p=3,q=5,r=1,N=15k,y=16k,j=1. As
proved directly from the source in069,

 kappa(n)=1_(8k,10k](n)-1_(16k,20k](n), d_1=0,
 K(t)=floor(10k/t)-floor(8k/t)
                     -floor(20k/t)+floor(16k/t).     (17)

For degree2, use the strict support gate (u+1)(v+1)>20k, let both cuts
contain Q_P and take 8k>=Q_P. For fixed P, balanced square-root cuts
satisfy these conditions for all sufficiently large k. All short
cutoff sequences at chi_P are exactly chi_P, and the two short linear
terms vanish. Since

 K(t)=-2k/t+e(t), |e(t)|<=4,
 sum_n chi_P(n)/n=alpha_P,

the complete original polynomial obeys

 |P_2(chi_P)-2k alpha_P^2|<=4*4^|P|.                 (18)

Proof: P_2=-sum_(a,b)chi_P(a)chi_P(b)K(ab). The rank-one part sums to
2k alpha_P^2. There are 2^|P| choices in each factor, so the bounded
floor error has at most 4^|P| signed terms. Terms with ab>20k cause no
problem: the same floor identity and error bound hold for every t.

Every polynomial correction using ONLY G_n for n in S, of any degree
and with any multipliers, has exactly the same value at chi_P. Thus,
whenever its registered relaxation admits this fake, every valid robust
absolute price is at least 2k alpha_P^2-4*4^|P|. This is linear for a
fixed S and P as k tends to infinity. It is a limitation of the stated
constraint relaxation, not a lower bound on the actual ordinary-mu row.

In particular, if all constraints through a fixed B are used, take P to
contain the primes <=B. The same barrier persists once all cuts contain
Q_P. Fixed arithmetic anchors do not become all-scale signed control
merely because arbitrary polynomial multipliers are allowed.

This argument also gives a quantitative test for growing sparse scopes.
At a given scale, any hitting prime set P for the used indices produces
(18) provided Q_P<=min cuts and 8k>=Q_P. To be useful as a lower bound,
the error 4*4^|P| must be small compared with k alpha_P^2, and the chosen
prefix envelope must admit chi_P. None of these conditions may be dropped
when P grows. The barrier becomes uninformative when the support no
longer fits the cuts or the specified C excludes the fake. Full constraints
through U fix the actual table and generally leave no such independent
fake within that query domain.

For any fixed higher convolution degree d>=3 and cuts containing Q_P,
the069 derivation similarly gives the nonzero leading response

 (-1)^d*2*alpha_P^d/(d-2)! * k*(log k)^(d-2)
                 +O_(d,P)(k*(log(2k))^(d-3)).         (19)

Corrections using the same S again leave it unchanged. This statement
is for fixed d and P. It provides no uniform assertion in growing degree
or growing prime sets. Degree2 already suffices for the exact obstruction
(18), without a divisor-asymptotic premise.

6. A finite reciprocal-moment estimate with explicit rank dependencies
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Here is a positive use of the divisor equations that is stronger than
replacing a target by a constant. Define the actual short reciprocal sum

 H_mu(u)=sum_(d<=u)mu(d)/d,   u>=1.

Let T>=u be an integer and suppose a proved or induction-licensed
Mertens premise is available on the entire finite domain 1<=t<=T:

 |M(t)|<=C t^sigma,    0<=sigma<1, C>=1.              (20)

The divisor identity summed through T is

 1=sum_(d<=T)mu(d)floor(T/d).                         (21)

Split d<=u and d>u. The short portion is T H_mu(u)+E, |E|<=u because
|mu(d)|<=1 and the floor error is below1. In the long portion interchange
the d,m sum. With L=floor(T/(u+1)), it equals

 sum_(m<=L)[M(floor(T/m))-M(u)].                      (22)

By (20), its absolute value is at most

 C T^sigma sum_(m<=L)m^(-sigma)+C L u^sigma
 <= C T u^(sigma-1)/(1-sigma)+C T u^(sigma-1).

The sum inequality follows by comparison with the integral from0 to L
of t^(-sigma); it also covers sigma=0. Divide (21) by T to obtain

 |H_mu(u)| <= (u+1)/T
                +C*(2-sigma)/(1-sigma)*u^(sigma-1).  (23)

No infinite series, prime number theorem or all-scale tail is used in
this proof. Every arithmetic argument in (21)-(22) is at most T.

Take T=ceil(u^(2-sigma)). Then

 |H_mu(u)|<=L_sigma(C) u^(sigma-1),
 L_sigma(C)=2+C*(2-sigma)/(1-sigma).                  (24)

Indeed (u+1)/T<=2u^(sigma-1). For rational sigma the integer ceiling
and all comparisons can be certified by integer powers, just as069
certifies its cuts. A fully rational upper constant for L_sigma is
immediate when sigma is rational.

For a scale rank R and u approximately R^theta, the auxiliary rank is
T approximately R^(theta(2-sigma)). It is strictly smaller than R,
apart from an explicitly handled finite base interval, if

                    theta(2-sigma)<1.               (25)

Balanced square-root cuts satisfy this for every fixed sigma>0. Their
auxiliary domain is approximately R^(1-sigma/2), which is larger than
the original mu-query radius sqrt R but still strictly smaller than R.
For sigma=1/2 it is R^(3/4). This increase in dependency radius must be
recorded; it cannot be reported as using only the original short domain.
Extreme complementary splits may violate (25). At sigma=0 balanced
cuts are on the boundary and this choice gives no strict reduction.

There is an explicit sufficient base threshold for the balanced case.
Set u_0=max(2,ceil(2^(1/sigma))). If u>=u_0 then u^sigma>=2, so
u^(2-sigma)<=u^2/2 and

 ceil(u^(2-sigma))<=ceil(u^2/2)<=u^2-1<=R-1.

Thus R>=u_0^2 suffices. Every smaller R is a finite declared base;
the much smaller threshold often available is not needed for validity.
For general u<=R^theta, put gamma=theta(2-sigma)<1. The conditions
R>=3 and R^(1-gamma)>=2 similarly ensure
T<=R^gamma+1<=R/2+1<R. These thresholds can be verified by exact integer
powers for rational exponents. They are not inferred from a floating fit.

Relation (23) is also useful with any other licensed T<R; the price
contains the explicit u/T term. There is no need to force the particular
choice in (24) if another domain transfer is cheaper.

A common tempting argument is to write H_mu(u) as minus the infinite
tail, use an all-scale Mertens bound, and infer O(u^(sigma-1)). Even if
the vanishing total reciprocal sum is established, that tail argument
queries prefixes beyond the current scale. It does not establish a
well-founded strong induction from only smaller ranks. Equations
(21)-(25) give a finite replacement and identify its actual extra rank.

The premise (20) is still unpaid in the current program. A bound for
the raw head is not itself (20). One may use (23) conditionally, or in a
simultaneous induction whose Mertens component has been proved to close.
The finite derivation must not be presented as having proved that component.

7. Source-specific rank-one extraction and its exact residual
------------------------------------------------------------
Return to the same joined source, not a new inverse-filled branch. Let
g be its coalesced finite step function, supported through D, with

 a_T=g(T)-g(T+1),
 g(d)=sum_T a_T 1[d<=T],
 kappa_j(n)=g(jn)-g((j+1)n).

Here a_T denotes the source step coefficient, not the arithmetic prefix
anchor a_t used in sections1-4. Let R_j=floor(D/j). The degree2 identity
below requires the strict gate (u+1)(v+1)>R_j. Without this gate the
long-long convolution residual must be retained; it is not (27).

Let h_(j,A) be the coalesced prefix-step coefficients of kappa_j. Then

 K_j(t)=sum_A h_(j,A)floor(A/t)
       =b_j/t+e_j(t),
 b_j=sum_A h_(j,A)A
    =sum_T a_T[floor(T/j)-floor(T/(j+1))],
 |e_j(t)|<=V_j:=sum_A |h_(j,A)|.                     (26)

All channels and adjacent-row endpoints are joined before these
quantities are formed. The identity holds for every positive t, including
t>R_j; there K_j(t)=0 but b_j/t and e_j(t) still cancel exactly.

For degree2 with cuts u,v the complete exact coefficient is

 c_j=d_j+sum_(a<=u)mu(a)kappa_j(a)
            +sum_(b<=v)mu(b)kappa_j(b)
            -b_j H_mu(u)H_mu(v)-E_j(u,v),
 E_j(u,v)=sum_(a<=u,b<=v)mu(a)mu(b)e_j(ab).            (27)

This extraction keeps a new deterministic center coupled with all
arithmetic factors. It does not delete pairs with common prime factors,
repeated factors or factors whose product exceeds R_j. Such deletions
would change E_j and destroy the exact identity.

On the barrier source (17), b_1=-2k, |e_1|<=4 and the short sums vanish
whenever both cuts lie below8k. Thus

 c_1=2k H_mu(u)H_mu(v)-E_1(u,v).                      (28)

For balanced cuts u=v=floor(sqrt(20k)), (24) bounds the separable part by
O(L_sigma(C)^2 k^sigma) under only the finite smaller-rank premises
through T=ceil(u^(2-sigma)). The constants and bases must be retained.
This pays the old fake's troublesome rank-one part on actual arithmetic,
conditionally on a genuine induction-licensed premise.

The remainder is still a bilinear ordinary-mu sum against a bounded
oscillating floor kernel. The direct absolute bound is

                    |E_1(u,v)|<=4uv,                 (29)

which is linear for balanced cuts. No claim of sublinear growth follows
from bounded e_1. Under the premise that the separable piece has already
been paid at O(k^sigma), an O(k^sigma) bound for E_1 and the same bound
for c_1 imply each other up to the paid main term, by (28). In this
sense E_1 is a precisely identified arithmetic leaf, not an automatically
cheaper copy of the original problem.

The correction language can represent this extraction. H_x(u), viewed
as a polynomial in the prefixes, is linear. For full constraints through
u, H_x(u)-H_mu(u) is a linear combination of G_n. The difference

 H_x(u)H_x(v)-H_mu(u)H_mu(v)

is therefore in the divisor ideal with affine-linear multipliers, by
factoring one moment difference at a time. Replacing the rank-one product
by its exact finite value is sound. Calling the value paid requires a
bound such as (23), with the declared larger auxiliary domain, rather
than mere substitution. Correcting E_j fully to its constant value is
still only target reconstruction unless its price has a separate proof.

8. A reusable conditional head cost for the separable bilinear part
------------------------------------------------------------------
There is a stronger aggregate cost than paying each b_j crudely. Define

                  M1=sum_T |a_T|T.

For 0<sigma<1 put beta=1-sigma. Quotient counting gives the exact identity
and elementary inequality

 sum_(j>=1) j^beta [floor(T/j)-floor(T/(j+1))]
   =sum_(n<=T)floor(T/n)^beta
   <=T^beta sum_(n<=T)n^(-beta)
   <=T/(1-beta)=T/sigma.                             (30)

The first identity assigns n to its quotient j=floor(T/n). The floor
differences are nonnegative; only finitely many j contribute. Using
(26), taking absolute a_T only after the source join, and restricting
to any head gives

             sum_(j in head)j^(1-sigma)|b_j|<=M1/sigma. (31)

Let R_j=floor(D/j), and take u_j=v_j=floor(sqrt(R_j)). For rows with
R_j>=4, u_j^2>=R_j/4 and R_j>=D/(2j). Since sigma-1<0, (24) implies

 |b_j H_mu(u_j)^2|
 <=8^(1-sigma)L_sigma(C)^2
                    D^(sigma-1)j^(1-sigma)|b_j|.

Summing and using (31) proves the explicit price

 sum_(j in head)|b_j H_mu(u_j)^2|
 <=[8^(1-sigma)/sigma] L_sigma(C)^2 D^(sigma-1)M1.    (32)

The finitely small-radius rows are licensed bases or priced separately.
For all remaining rows, every use of (24) requires the actual domain
T_j=ceil(u_j^(2-sigma)) and its strictly smaller-rank certificate.
One common C must cover all these Mertens premises.

For fixed p, D has order N. The admitted source's previously proved
M1=O(N log(2N)) envelope then turns (32) into

                   O_sigma(L_sigma(C)^2 N^sigma log(2N)). (33)

At sigma=1/2 this is a square-root head price with a logarithmic factor
for THIS SEPARABLE PIECE. It is a uniform algebraic cost rule rather than
a finite target evaluation. Its proof uses the same source step measure
M1 already paid in069, plus the explicit finite Mertens premise (20).
No numerical coefficient array is needed for (30)-(33).

Here is a direct same-source proof of that M1 envelope, included so the
cost does not depend on a fitted prior result. Write

 Z_(b,v)=floor(vN/b),    (v,c)=(4,1),(2,-3),(1,2).

The floor atom floor(vN/(bd)) equals floor(Z_(b,v)/d). Its decreasing
step differences have weighted total

 sum_(T>=1) T[floor(Z/T)-floor(Z/(T+1))]
    =sum_(d<=Z)floor(Z/d)
    <=Z(1+log(max(1,Z))).                            (33a)

This is finite summation by parts followed by the harmonic bound.
For F(d)=W_p(d)-rW_q(d), g(d)=-1[d>y]F(d). The tail cutoff introduces
only the step a_y=F(y+1); all steps at T>y are negatives of steps of F,
and those at T<y are zero. Write x=N/(bd)=m+f with 0<=f<1. The integer
part cancels in W_b, leaving floor(4f)-3floor(2f), whose values on the
four quarter intervals are 0,1,-1,0. Thus |W_b|<=1 and |F|<=1+r<=2.
The cutoff step costs at most2y. Joining may reduce the total; a safe
bound follows from (33a):

 M1<=2y+sum_(v,c)|c|[Z_(p,v)H_(Z_(p,v))
                                      +rZ_(q,v)H_(Z_(q,v))]
    <=2D+12N(1/p+r/q)(1+log(max(1,D))),              (33b)

where H_0 is interpreted as0 and H_Z<=1+log Z for Z>=1.
The factor12 is 1*4+3*2+2*1. This is an unconditional bound for the
same ordinary source for every allowed y and endpoint r. For fixed p,q
it is O(N log(2N)), with an explicit uniform constant. Substituting
(33b) into (32) proves the same-source claim without numerical data.

The short linear sums in (27) are another obligation. Their unconditional
absolute head price for balanced cuts is O(D^(1/2)H^(1/2)); for H of
order sqrt N this is O(N^(3/4)), not square root. A useful source-specific
exception is

                    (j+1)u_j<=y.                    (34)

Then g(ja)=g((j+1)a)=0 for every a<=u_j, so both short sums vanish
identically. For all j<=H, the sufficient common condition
y>=2sqrt(DH) implies (34), since (j+1)sqrt(D/j)<=2sqrt(Dj).
This is available when y is sufficiently large relative to that head;
it is not a premise for every allowed source prefix. In general those
short terms require their own bound or a higher-degree construction.
The069 cofinal-degree estimate pays only the original subset-size-one
terms; it must not be confused with a price for every linear term created
after extracting the higher-degree units.

The direct floor atoms remain paid independently. For a full atom
floor(Z/d), ordinary-mu inversion at row n gives
sum_(a<=Z/n)mu(a)floor(Z/(na))=1[n<=Z], by the divisor identity.
Its adjacent-row difference is1[n=Z]. Thus the joined direct coefficients
have absolute total at most(1+3+2)(1+r)<=12, with possible reductions
after coalescing equal Z. They add this fixed paid mass to any head
price; none of the conditional moment arguments changes them.

The last part of (27), the sum of |E_j| over the head or an appropriate
coupled signed energy, remains unpaid. Its absolute price
sum_j V_j u_j^2 does not inherit (32). The quotient-counting argument
prices b_j, not the oscillating signed remainder. A useful next theorem
would be an ordinary-mu estimate for this explicit remainder family,
with its source parameters, ranks and norm transfer all stated.

9. What local corrections might genuinely discover
--------------------------------------------------
A credible scalable correction should reveal a bounded-cost quotient
representative whose coefficients satisfy a structural law. Two examples
of useful discoveries would be a source-bound sparse multiplier rule
that removes a finite collection of low-rank separable moments with
uniformly priced moment lemmas, or a signed floor-remainder/energy rule
that closes with a norm decrease. A repeated triangular substitution
that produces the row constant is neither of these cost laws.

For the bilinear language, the hierarchy suggested by (26)-(33) is:

  * retain source step coefficients and their paid M1 envelope;
  * extract and bound reciprocal moments using the finite auxiliary
    divisor domain, with the rank increase from u to T made explicit;
  * keep the oscillating remainder as a named arithmetic state;
  * prove its signed bound or transfer, together with all short terms;
  * close the common Mertens/head invariant and only then use the result
    as an all-scale law.

This is a proposal for theorem discovery, not an assertion that the
last two steps can be completed by the present grammar. In particular,
e_j(ab) depends on horizons of order R_j through A. Its arithmetic
factor queries are short, but its geometric oscillations are at the
current rank. A lemma for E_j is not an instance of the original source
at a smaller N merely because a,b<=sqrt(R_j).

A concrete extension of the detector grammar could register (26) as a
kernel operator with exactly replayed b_j and e_j, and register (23) as
a supplied pricing operator with inputs (u,T,sigma,C). The checker would
reconstruct (21)-(22), check T>=u and T<R or a licensed base, and expose
every required M(t) premise through T. The detector could choose T or
balanced cuts within these gates, combine the resulting moment prices,
and leave an explicit E_j node. It could then synthesize sparse divisor
multipliers for that remainder, instead of reporting its target-valued
constant as a general bound. Formula (32), with certificate (33b), is a
supplied aggregate price operator for the separable nodes. This is a
reviewer-proved backend/next feature; the present analytical proof was
not autonomously discovered by the detector.

The parent reports that070's current finite search prices sparse local
corrections using |M(s)|<=s and retains conditional C-degree prices, with
scientific divisor domain T=U and an API permitting explicitly declared
T>=U. This review has not inspected that implementation or its results.
Its local T=U scope must not inherit (24): that reciprocal estimate
generally invokes M arguments beyond U. An extension using the proposed
operator would add those premises, rank gates and prices explicitly.
The unconditional envelope |M(t)|<=t corresponds to sigma=1 and does
not satisfy the sigma<1 reciprocal decay proof. Direct finite moment
evaluation at T=U remains exact but does not supply the missing rate.

Higher-degree constructions bring higher ordered-divisor kernels. Their
large-scale main terms involve reciprocal-logarithmic moments and
products of them, with coefficients and constants depending on degree.
It would be reasonable to extract such moments and search coupled
corrections. No uniform higher-degree cost is established here. The
number of subset terms, powers of the invariant constant, logarithmic
degrees, coefficient height and growing-degree dependence must all be
charged before that idea supplies an epsilon-cofinal all-scale theorem.

Full local constraints are useful as exact replay and as a diagnostic
upper limit: the finite problem can be solved completely. Partial or
structured constraints are useful for testing which arithmetic fact
removes a robust-price barrier. Neither use permits automatic promotion
from finite optimization to uniform cancellation.

10. Rank and invariant closure: exact requirements
--------------------------------------------------
A rank certificate must distinguish three different horizons:

  R: the original source/divisor geometry horizon;
  U: the arithmetic argument radius of the support-reduced polynomial;
  T: any auxiliary arithmetic domain used by its pricing lemmas.

The identity may have U<R while the price has T>=R, which invalidates a
claimed strong induction. The finite reciprocal rule provides one valid
way to keep T<R, but only with (25), the stated bases and the licensed
Mertens family. Coefficient constants can also hide horizon-R targets;
calling them geometry does not remove their arithmetic growth obligation.

A simultaneous induction must list all state families: Mertens prefixes,
raw head norms, the source endpoints and all admitted prefixes/cutoffs,
reciprocal moments if separately maintained, signed remainders or energies,
and any transfer to the filled core. Each leaf is either a paid base,
a proved uniform lemma, or a strictly smaller-rank instance of an
explicitly included family. Unlisted auxiliary kernels are not licensed.

For a one-constant scalar envelope, a sufficient normalized closure has
the form

 price_R(C)<=A+sum_(d=1)^D B_d C^d,
 A,B_d>=0 independent of R,
 C>=A+sum_d B_d C^d,                                 (35)

with the same C covering every base and every parameter instance.
Logarithmic or epsilon losses can be part of the normalization but must
be fixed before the induction. Finite empirical ratios do not prove the
uniform coefficients in (35).

Even the positive partial law (32) has L_sigma(C)^2 and therefore a
quadratic dependence on C. It is not a linear contraction. For a
quadratic right side A+B_1 C+B_2 C^2 with A>0 and B_2>0, feasibility
requires 1-B_1>0, discriminant (1-B_1)^2-4AB_2>=0, and a base-case
constant in the interval between the roots. Increasing C arbitrarily
does not fix failure. Several auxiliary constants require a vector
invariant with simultaneous feasible inequalities, not several
unrelated choices made after seeing finite outcomes.

The correction multipliers themselves may raise degree. If P has degree
d and a multiplier has degree r, its product with G_n has degree at most
r+1. Price closure uses the actual collected degrees, not the initial
support-reduction degree. Multipliers with index support outside the
registered U extend the arithmetic domain even though their products
vanish at actual mu. The domain of every symbol must be reported.

11. Exact certificates, scopes and permissible conclusions
----------------------------------------------------------
An exact finite correction certificate should state the original complete
joined polynomial P, every multiplier A_n, its used divisor index n,
the fixed conventions m_0=0,m_1=1, and the collected corrected polynomial
Q. A checker can verify the literal rational identity

                       Q-P=sum_n A_n G_n.            (36)

No actual target evaluation is needed to replay (36). The fact G_n(M)=0
is the separately proved ordinary-mu rule. Noncoprime products and unit
substitutions must agree between both sides, and absent monomials mean
zero coefficients. A bound certificate then needs its own exact box or
constraint domain and rational price enclosure. It cannot borrow a
finite actual envelope as a uniform premise.

If the search also uses pointwise mu identities, multiplicativity,
squareful zeros, prime values, interval bounds or source energies, list
them as additional rules with their own domains. They are not encoded
by an arbitrary strict subset of the G_n. A correction that is valid
modulo a larger ideal is not certified by (36) with a smaller list.

Every reported optimum must include the allowed multiplier monomials,
coefficient/rational limits, degree, selected divisor indices, search
method and objective. Exact feasible prices remain useful even when
optimality is not certified. Candidate comparison must preserve the
full C-price if a later invariant uses C other than the selection value.

The full-domain constant representative has a truthful label: an exact
finite target encoded by divisor reconstruction. It can be evaluated or
used as a finite base. It is an unpaid growth leaf until there is a
uniform estimate. That remains true when the synthesizer never receives
mu signs or an externally supplied target, because the full constraints
compute them internally.

Work and information also need separate labels. The sign-query radius
can shrink while geometry still reaches R, while the output is large,
and while the multiplier coefficient heights grow. Finite arithmetic
complexity bounds are not cancellation exponents. A proof based on
growing constraint scopes must specify their source-independent law and
charge all domains, coefficient heights, base data and representation
costs; it must not merely store one new exact target per scale.

12. Disposition
---------------
The proposed correction language is exact and potentially useful. It is
strictly stronger than a scalar-prefix relaxation when it includes a
divisor equation that the scalar fake violates. The full language is
also mathematically complete for every finite prefix target, with no
degree obstacle to replacing that target by a constant. Completeness
of finite determination is not all-scale signed control.

The new proved obstruction is that any fixed finite set of divisor
constraints leaves a finite-prime fake, and every multiplier correction
using those constraints preserves its linear joined-source response
whenever the stated envelope and support conditions hold. The precise
hitting-set and growing-scope qualifications prevent overclaiming.

The new positive cost law is (23)-(25), a finite smaller-rank reciprocal
moment estimate, and its same-source aggregate application (30)-(33).
It pays a separable bilinear contribution at the desired conditional
exponent, with explicit C powers, rank expansion and source M1 cost.
It leaves the signed floor remainder, generally the short sums, and
closure of the Mertens/head invariant as real unpaid obligations.

A stage070 finite improvement would therefore be mathematically
interesting as a discovered quotient representative and exact robust
certificate. It becomes a reusable bound mechanism only when a uniform
source-specific multiplier/cost rule, residual theorem and well-founded
invariant are proved. No scientific070 result is used in this conclusion.
