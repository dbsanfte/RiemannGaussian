CURRENT-SCALE FULL-FLOOR CONTROL: EXACT SYNTHESIS AND A DENOMINATOR BARRIER 068
============================================================================

Scope
-----
This is a new source-only mathematical review of the ordinary-Mobius raw
head from MOBIUS_SIGNED_LAG_HANDOFF.txt, SPARSE_HEAD_MECHANISM_REVIEW_067.txt,
mobius_sparse_prefix_066.py, and the newly supplied floor-control dictionary
rules. No scientific array, old study, numerical
Mobius sample, optimizer, archived result, hash, or test was executed.
Only this review is written; no subagent is used. The derivations below
are elementary and invoke no RH, Mertens estimate, prime-distribution
theorem, or random-sign model.

The new substantive result is a current-scale obstruction, different from
067's small-support divisor barrier. For the actual joined source
N=15k,y=16k, a fixed finite rational endpoint-quotient dictionary cannot
produce an o(N) positive residual price, even when horizon20k is present
and coefficients are arbitrary signed rationals learned per instance.
A three-chamber squarefree dual certificate proves this. A quantitative
version excludes an RH-strength positive price whenever the dictionary's
common rational denominator is O(N^rho), rho<1/4. The statement is about
this proof price, never a lower bound on the signed raw coefficient.

Growing closure k<=N^theta, theta>0, generally lies outside that denominator
regime. No unconditional square-root or sublinear approximation theorem
for those growing dictionaries is proved here. Conversely, a complete
quotient dictionary can encode the target using only O(sqrt X) distinct
horizons per indicator endpoint. Small dictionary cardinality and absence
of explicit mu-sign inputs therefore do not themselves exclude encoding.

1. Exact row theorem, with the known unit paid before triangle
-------------------------------------------------------------
Use the original joined source and conventions of067:

 g(d)=-1[d>y]*(W(N/(3d))-r*W(N/(5d))),
 W(t)=floor(4t)-3floor(2t)+2floor(t),
 D=floor(4N/3), r in[3/5,1],
 kappa_j(n)=g(jn)-g((j+1)n),
 c_j=d_j+sum_(n>=1)mu(n)*kappa_j(n).

Thus kappa_j is supported at n<=R0=floor(D/j), has rational geometry
at rational r, and satisfies |kappa_j(n)|<=4. d_j is the exact direct
full-floor coefficient. All channels and rows retain ordinary mu.

Let H be any finite set of positive integer horizons, with arbitrary
rational coefficients lambda_X, and set

 Q(n)=sum_(X in H)lambda_X*floor(X/n),
 R=max(R0,max H), with max(empty H)=0.

The complete ordinary divisor identity gives, for every X>=1,

 sum_(n>=1)mu(n)*floor(X/n)=1,

because regrouping by m<=X gives sum_(n|m)mu(n)=1[m=1]. No floor may
be clipped at R0 if its horizon exceeds R0. Consequently

 |c_j| <= |d_j+sum_X lambda_X|
           +sum_(n<=R)mu(n)^2*|kappa_j(n)-Q(n)|.             (1.1)

There is a stronger legitimate version: mu(1)=1 is known, so combine
the unit with the scalar BEFORE triangle. Since floor(X/1)=X,

 c0_j=d_j+kappa_j(1),
 center_j=c0_j+sum_X lambda_X*(1-X),
 P_j(lambda)=|center_j|
       +sum_(2<=n<=R,mu(n)^2=1)|kappa_j(n)-Q(n)|,

 |c_j|<=P_j(lambda).                                        (1.2)

The price(1.2) is no larger than(1.1), by scalar triangle. Horizon1
has no effect in(1.2), since 1-X=0 and its floor vanishes for n>=2;
it can be dropped. The empty dictionary remains a valid baseline.
The squarefree mask is unsigned geometry, not a substituted sign model.

These theorems are uniform finite identities. They allow current-scale
horizons, larger horizons with their complete support, arbitrary signed
coefficients, arbitrary rational quotient exponents, and adaptive
sign-blind coefficient synthesis. They do not prove any growth rate for
the synthesized price.

2. Exact LP and lower certificates, including a declared coefficient box
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Let den be a positive common denominator for d_j and kappa_j. Put
c=den*(d_j+kappa_j(1)), w_n=den*kappa_j(n), x_X=den*lambda_X,
and A_(n,X)=floor(X/n), for squarefree n>=2 through R. The integer
geometry LP minimizes

 L(x) = |c+sum_X x_X*(1-X)|
         +sum_n |w_n-sum_X A_(n,X)*x_X|.                    (2.1)

Then L(x)/den is an upper certificate for |c_j| for EVERY rational x.
Floating solvers propose x; exact rational replay proves the bound.
The solver need not see ordinary mu signs or the evaluated c_j.

Without a coefficient box, the exact dual is

 maximize epsilon*c+sum_n z_n*w_n,
 |epsilon|<=1, |z_n|<=1,
 epsilon*(1-X)-sum_n z_n*A_(n,X)=0 for every X.             (2.2)

The usual rational LP primal/dual certificates can be checked by integer
cross-multiplication. A positive dual is a lower certificate for this
dictionary's positive proof price, not for |c_j|. The actual ordinary-mu
vector epsilon=1,z_n=mu(n) is dual-feasible by the complete floor identity;
its negation is also feasible. Therefore every dictionary optimum is at
least |den*c_j|. This fact is theoretical, not permission to feed signs
or target values into a sign-blind search.

Now impose a declared box |x_X|<=C, C>=0. For ANY proposed multipliers
|epsilon|<=1, |z_n|<=1 define

 v_X=epsilon*(1-X)-sum_n z_n*A_(n,X),
 LB=epsilon*c+sum_n z_n*w_n-C*sum_X|v_X|.                  (2.3)

For every boxed x,

 L(x)>=epsilon*(c+sum_X x_X*(1-X))
             +sum_n z_n*(w_n-sum_X A_(n,X)*x_X)
       =epsilon*c+sum_n z_n*w_n+sum_X x_X*v_X
       >=LB.                                             (2.4)

Thus max(0,LB)/den is a rigorous lower bound for the DECLARED boxed
optimum, regardless of approximate stationarity or solver success.
Rationally rounding and clipping epsilon,z to [-1,1], then computing
v and LB exactly, is sufficient. The penalty C*sum|v_X| cannot be
omitted unless exact dual equalities have been established. A box such
as C=4*den*max(1,#H) is a finite search capacity, not a uniform theorem
about the unboxed optimum. A small boxed gap certifies optimality within
that box only; a large boxed lower bound cannot exclude unboxed controls.

For rationalized x and a proposed x', the exact Lipschitz loss is

 |L(x')-L(x)| <= sum_X |x'_X-x_X|*
       [X-1+sum_(2<=n<=R,mu(n)^2=1)floor(X/n)].             (2.5)

The bracket is at most sum_(n<=X)floor(X/n)-1
<=X*(1+log X)-1. Hence dyadic rounding with error<=1/(2G) per coordinate
has a completely charged explicit upper loss. Exact replay alone is
enough for validity; no roundoff-to-zero residual shortcut is allowed.
Endpoint y,r, original direct term, squarefree support, new horizons,
denominator, coefficient box and dictionary cap must remain in the
certificate. A finite solver status is not a mathematical growth law.

A fixed dyadic grid G=2^20 is legitimate finite replay precision. It is
not automatically harmless in an all-scale theorem: for #H bounded and
max H=O(N), the worst-case loss in(2.5), after division by den, is
O(#H*N*log(2N)/(den*G)). This envelope is linear up to logarithms when
G is fixed. A future ideal-coefficient rate must either control the
actual rounding residual more sharply, use coefficients already on the
grid, or let G grow sufficiently to pay this complete loss. For example
G>=#H*N^(1/2-eta)*log(2N)/den suffices for an
O(N^(1/2+eta)) rounding allowance under those horizon assumptions.
This is a sufficient precision condition, not a lower bound on actual
rounded certificates.

3. Full quotient span can encode the exact target at sparse cardinality
----------------------------------------------------------------------
For the single indicator h_A(n)=1[n<=A], regrouping finite divisor sums
proves the exact functional identity

 h_A(n)=sum_(m<=A)mu(m)*floor(floor(A/m)/n).                 (3.1)

Indeed floor(floor(A/m)/n)=floor(A/(mn)), and the complete m sum equals
1 whenever A/n>=1 and0 otherwise. Grouping equal positive quotients
B=floor(A/m) produces at most 2*floor(sqrt A)+1 distinct horizons.
Their grouped coefficients are sums of ordinary mu on quotient-index
intervals. The sum of all coefficients is M(A)=sum_(m<=A)mu(m).

The joined row kernel is already an exact signed sum of indicators:

 kappa_j(n)=sum_T a_T*
       [h_(floor(T/j))(n)-h_(floor(T/(j+1)))(n)].             (3.2)

If the dictionary contains EVERY positive quotient floor(A/m),m<=A,
for every nonzero indicator endpoint A in(3.2), then(3.1) gives zero
residual on ALL n, and the scalar center in(1.2) is exactly c_j.
The unboxed optimum therefore equals |c_j|. A bounded box may fail to
contain those coefficients, so dictionary coverage is an encoding-risk
flag rather than proof of actual boxed zero residual.

The representation(3.1) uses mu signs in its explanation. An optimizer
can reach its algebraic effect through floor geometry and the squarefree
mask without receiving a sign table. Sign-blind inputs do not certify
an independently paid all-scale estimate. This is why complete quotient
coverage must be flagged explicitly, even with O(sqrt A) horizons per
endpoint. Cardinality or runtime alone is not a noncircularity criterion.

A one-level quotient cutoff m<=sqrt A DOES NOT automatically contain
all distinct quotients: small quotient horizons usually require larger
indices. A nested cutoff is complete only when actual coverage has been
checked; nesting identities alone do not establish completeness.
Likewise a budget clip at512 can remove columns needed for(3.1).

4. Actual joined interior source used in the new obstruction
-----------------------------------------------------------
Take N=15k, D=20k, y=16k, integer k>=1. At both r endpoints, indeed
at every r in[3/5,1], the exact tail source is -1 on(16k,20k] and0
elsewhere. Both channels remain present: the q channel has already
exited below y. Its exact tail-step coefficients are a_(16k)=1 and
a_(20k)=-1. The first head row is consequently

 w(n)=kappa_1(n)=1[8k<n<=10k]-1[16k<n<=20k], d_1=0.         (4.1)

This is a required raw head row for all sufficiently large k under
the square-root head cutoff. It has no unit contribution. Its source
endpoint horizons are8k,10k,16k,20k; the six full atoms add only
3k,5k,6k,10k,12k,20k. Their nontrivial quotients are <=10k.

Thus dictionaries generated from these endpoint seeds by finitely many
declared quotient operations have ideal normalized horizons a_X in

 (0,16] union {20}.                                       (4.2)

For the usual integer quotient operation, a_X is rational and the
actual horizon is X=floor(a_X*k). Nested integer quotients collapse
exactly: floor(floor(A/s)/t)=floor(A/(st)). Current-scale horizon20k
is retained. Restricting the proof below does not discard that horizon.

5. Three-chamber exact floor dual with squarefree coordinates
------------------------------------------------------------
Assume the ideal rational horizons in(4.2) have a common positive
integer denominator L: b_X=L*a_X is an integer. Let k>=120*L^2.
Empty and duplicate horizons may be removed. The following argument
also permits horizon rounding errors of absolute size<=1.

For s=0,1,2 define the half-open integer chamber

 I_s={n: 18k/(18sL+1)<n<=19k/(19sL+1)}.                   (5.1)

Their real lengths are k*Delta_s, where

 Delta_s=1/[(18sL+1)*(19sL+1)].                            (5.2)

I_0 is(18k,19k], where w=-1. I_1 and I_2 are below8k,
where w=0. For k>=120L^2, the chambers contain no unit n=1.
They are disjoint and all lie within the original20k source radius.

Every n in I_s can be written n/k=1/[L*(u+s)], with
1/(19L)<=u<1/(18L). For the ideal horizon a_X*k,

 a_X*k/n=b_X*u+s*b_X.

If a_X<=16 then 0<b_X*u<8/9 and its floor is0. If a_X=20
then 20/19<=b_X*u<10/9 and its floor is1. Thus the integer
floor value throughout I_s is

 floor(X/n)=delta_X+s*b_X,
 delta_X=1[a_X=20].                                     (5.3)

Rounding X by at most1 changes the argument by at most
(2L+1/18)/k<3L/k. Since a_X>=1/L, the smallest lower
distance to an integer in the ideal argument is >=1/(19L).
The condition k>=120L^2 makes the perturbation less than half
that distance; upper distances are >=1/9. Hence(5.3) stays EXACT
for the actual rounded horizons. No limiting or floating argument
is used. If all horizons are exact a_X*k, the rounding condition
is unnecessary, apart from ensuring useful chamber populations.

Let C_s count the squarefree n>=2 in I_s. Whenever they are positive,
choose

 eta=min(C_0,C_1/2,C_2), epsilon=0,
 z_n=-eta/C_0 on squarefree I_0,
 z_n= 2eta/C_1 on squarefree I_1,
 z_n=-eta/C_2 on squarefree I_2,
 z_n=0 elsewhere.                                       (5.4)

These are exact rational multipliers with |z_n|<=1. Equations(5.3)
and(5.4) give, for EVERY admitted horizon,

 sum_n z_n*floor(X/n)
 =-eta*delta_X+2eta*(delta_X+b_X)
                    -eta*(delta_X+2b_X)=0.               (5.5)

Thus they satisfy the extracted-unit LP dual exactly, with epsilon0.
Their source objective is sum_n z_n*w(n)=eta, since only I_0
meets the support of w. For ANY coefficients, boxed or unboxed,

 P_1(lambda)>=eta.                                      (5.6)

The scalar penalty, including the original unit-extracted center,
cannot defeat this certificate: its dual multiplier is zero. The proof
uses only floor geometry, rational horizon data and the squarefree mask.
It does not evaluate a single ordinary-mu sign or signed source row.

6. Explicit elementary population bound and uniform consequences
----------------------------------------------------------------
An interval of real length B inside[1,20k] contains at least B-1
integers. Non-squarefree integers have a prime-square divisor. The
number divisible by any fixed a^2 is at most B/a^2+1. The union bound,
with primes bounded by sqrt(20k), and

 sum_(a>=2)1/a^2<=1/4+integral_2^infinity t^(-2)dt=3/4,

therefore gives, conservatively,

 C_s>=k*Delta_s/4-floor(sqrt(20k))-2.                      (6.1)

No prime-distribution result is involved. The minimum in(5.4), with0
used if a chamber is empty, is bounded below by

 eta>=max(0,
     k/[4*(36L+1)*(38L+1)]-floor(sqrt(20k))-2)
     >=max(0,k/(5772L^2)-floor(sqrt(20k))-2).              (6.2)

To check the middle chamber: Delta_1/8>=Delta_2/4 because
(36L+1)(38L+1)>=2(18L+1)(19L+1); the first chamber is larger.
The error in(6.1) remains safe after division by2. The source objective
is multiplied by den in the integer-scaled LP, then divided by den,
so(6.2) is the unscaled row-price lower bound.

Consequences:

* For ANY fixed finite rational quotient dictionary, L is fixed.
  Equation(6.2) gives P_1=Omega_L(k)=Omega_L(N). This forbids
  an o(N) positive-price theorem for that dictionary even if20k is
  included, coefficients depend on k, and no coefficient box is used.
  It is stronger in scope than the previous low-horizon support barrier.

* If a growing dictionary has common ideal denominator L(k)<=C*k^rho
  for a fixed rho<1/4, the rounding condition eventually holds and
  P_1=Omega_C(k^(1-2rho)). The O(sqrt k) population error is smaller
  than this main term. Such a grammar cannot prove every
  O_epsilon(N^(1/2+epsilon)) head rate: choose
  epsilon<1/2-2rho. This is a denominator-complexity condition, not
  a cardinality-only theorem.

* If quotient divisors are bounded by a fixed K and nesting depth by a
  fixed h, one may take L=lcm(1,...,K)^h. Retaining arbitrary subsets,
  a structural dictionary budget, or a box only weakens the primal
  controls and preserves the certificate. No closure with both K and h
  fixed is rescued by including the current-scale seed horizons.

* The same reasoning covers variable K,h whenever their actual common
  denominator meets the stated condition. A sufficient rough bound is
  L<=(K!)^h<=K^(Kh). For example fixed h and
  h*K*log K<=(rho-o(1))*log k with rho<1/4 are in the obstructed
  regime. This does not assert that L must be this large; use its actual
  value for a sharper certificate.

The obstruction does NOT cover arbitrary extra horizon seeds,
current-scale rationals without the common-denominator condition,
unbounded growing nesting, signed residual moments, or a different
proof price. It is NOT an Omega bound for |c_1|, the actual head,
Mertens windows, or RH. The exact signed row may cancel strongly.

6a. A rejected prefix-cap design and the final rank-spread scope
-------------------------------------------------------------
A development dictionary design visited divisor indices m=1,2,...
in order, visited the row anchors at each index, deduplicated horizons,
excluded horizons<2, and stopped at a fixed budget B. This design has
an additional all-scale obstruction on the actual source(4.1).

Its anchors are20k,16k,10k,8k. For sufficiently large k the B+1
horizons floor(20k/m),1<=m<=B+1, are distinct and>=2: adjacent
ideal values differ by at least20k/[B(B+1)]. They cannot all fit
under budgetB. Thus either requested depth K<B+1, or the dictionary
has stopped by indexB+1. Every admitted horizon has the form

 X=floor(a*k/m), a in{20,16,10,8}, 1<=m<=B+1.             (6a.1)

All requested dictionaries then have the SAME common denominator
L=lcm(1,...,B+1). Equation(6.2) gives an Omega_B(k) positive-price
lower bound uniformly over all requested K and all signed rational
coefficients. Arbitrary rational exponents, even depending on N, do not
escape that prefix-cap obstruction. Boxes and grids only increase the
price. For B=512, the constant has an enormous asymptotic threshold;
this claims no useful lower value at finite pilot scales.

This prefix-cap design was rejected during development. The final
declared dictionary instead enumerates ALL DISTINCT endpoint quotients
A//m for1<=m<=min(K,A//2), using quotient blocks. If the union exceeds
budgetB, it retains B equally spaced ranks of the sorted horizon set,
including its minimum and maximum; B=1 retains the maximum. Increasing
K can therefore change retained normalized denominators even at fixedB.
The proof of(6a.1) does not apply to that rank-spread selection rule.

The three-chamber theorem STILL applies to any final selected dictionary
whose rational-horizon and common-denominator hypotheses in sections5-6
hold. Fixed cardinality alone does not imply those hypotheses. The final
selection change avoids this particular fixed-prefix conclusion; it
does not prove a uniform rate or evade all possible obstructions. The
complete quotient-coverage warning, exact boxed certificates, and finite
precision scope remain applicable. No frozen final run is judged by the
rejected development rule.

7. Arbitrary rational quotient exponents: what is and is not established
------------------------------------------------------------------------
For theta=a/b>=0 rational, K=floor(N^theta) is well-defined by exact
integer powers, with any necessary arithmetic resource cap declared.
No mathematical denominator whitelist is needed. The cases theta0 and
theta>0 have different asymptotic behavior: at theta0, K=1 permanently;
at every fixed positive theta, K eventually exceeds every fixed divisor.
A finite pilot where very small theta yields the same K as theta0 does
not test that difference.

For a fixed number of closure levels with K=N^theta, the common L can
grow far beyond N^(1/4), so(6.2) generally becomes uninformative. It is
invalid to apply the fixed-L linear conclusion while allowing L to grow.
No assertion here excludes those current-scale growing dictionaries.
But source geometry alone does not supply a sublinear residual theorem
for them, and complete quotient coverage eventually restores the exact
target-encoding possibility of section3. Those are separate boundaries.

The genuine open constructive problem is to specify a source-bound rule
for the admitted horizons and rational coefficients, then prove a rate
for the COMPLETE residual plus scalar cost uniformly in N,y at both r
endpoints. A finite exact primal bound and dual capacity gap support
dictionary selection and rejection; they do not supply that rate.

8. Conditional raw-head and far-correlation transfer
--------------------------------------------------
For the required head1<=j<J, any family of exact floor certificates gives

 sum_(j<J)|c_j|<=sum_(j<J)P_j.                            (8.1)

A uniform theorem sum_(j<J)P_j=O_epsilon(N^(1/2+epsilon)),
for every epsilon>0, every prefix and both endpoint ratios, is therefore
a sufficient direct head closure. It must prove actual synthesis cost,
including any coefficient restrictions and complete new-horizon support.
For example the stronger envelope

 P_j<=C_eta*N^(1/2+eta)/j                                 (8.2)

uniformly on the head pays(8.1) after summing the harmonic series and
absorbing its logarithm into a chosen positive exponent gap.

This is an alternative route to067's signed far-correlation condition,
not automatically a bound for that condition. A head l1 estimate alone
does not control the dyadic Cauchy prefactors in the far-risk expression.
There is, however, a precise sufficient block transfer. Let I=[L,U],
|I|<=L, let M1 be the original coalesced step first moment, and choose
near-lag cutoff K>=0 as in067. Put

 S_I=sum_(j in I)(P_j+|d_j|)^2.

Since |z_j|=|c_j-d_j|<=P_j+|d_j|, one has E_I<=S_I.
With E_I=D_I+C_I^near(K)+C_I^far(K), D_I>=0, and the paid
|C_I^near(K)|<=8K*M1/L, it follows that

 (C_I^far(K))_+<=S_I+8K*M1/L,
 sqrt(|I|*(C_I^far(K))_+)
     <=sqrt(L*S_I)+sqrt(8K*M1).                          (8.3)

Thus a proved block family S_I=O_eta(N^(1+2eta)/L) gives paid far
risk after summing the O(log N) dyadic blocks, with the same explicit
near-band exponent cost as067. In particular(8.2), and the original
paid direct atoms handled exactly, give such control. For large N the
six original full horizons are outside the square-root head; finitely
many smaller N can be paid directly. No sign estimate for individual
lags is needed.

The theta parameter for floor-dictionary closure is independent of the
delta parameter for near-lag cutoff. Cofinal delta->0 remains necessary
for the stated every-epsilon far-risk transfer when a positive delta
is charged. A floor dictionary exponent is not an arithmetic cancellation
exponent merely because it has the same numerical value.

For affine r geometry and a FIXED common dictionary, the unboxed optimal
LP price is a seminorm of the target modulo the floor-column subspace,
hence convex in r. Endpoint
certificates can also be interpolated when their common dictionaries
and coefficient boxes permit it. The original raw-head norm is convex
in r regardless. The indefinite far-correlation polynomial itself need
not be convex; do not use endpoint convexity to bound it directly.

9. Disposition
--------------
Paid here: complete-floor and extracted-unit row identities; exact
unboxed and boxed dual certificates; explicit rationalization loss;
complete quotient target-encoding boundary; a new exact three-chamber
current-scale dual obstruction for actual joined sources; its explicit
squarefree population and denominator-complexity bounds; and precise
conditional head/far-risk transfer.

Open: any uniformly sublinear positive-price theorem for the admitted
growing N^theta quotient grammar over all interior prefixes, any
RH-strength head synthesis theorem outside the restricted barrier,
the remaining signed far-correlation control, and the full raw-head/RH
research goal. No new unconditional bridge admission is claimed.

An exact finite bound and declared-capacity gap are substantive detector
outputs. They become a uniform theorem only after an independent rate
for their complete cost has actually been proved. Fixed rational closure
is now concretely ruled out as an o(N) positive-price mechanism on an
admitted joined source; growing closure remains an honest open direction.
