1. THE PRIME-PAIR FIBRE THEOREM, INCLUDING DIAGONALS

For r>=2 put Phi_r(a,b)=ab(a+b)^r. Every unordered PRIME pair p,q has
a UNIQUE fibre among ALL prime-power input pairs a,b>=2. This is stronger
than bounded prime-pair injectivity and weaker than injectivity on all
prime-power inputs. The latter is neither claimed nor used.

For p!=q, gcd(pq,p+q)=1. The valuations at p,q are exactly one; every
other prime valuation is divisible by r. A competing prime-power pair
must have two visible residue primes, hence two distinct bases with the
same full valuations, by decode_proof.txt section 2. Its inputs are p,q.

For p=q odd, N=2^r*p^(r+2). Every competing input base lies in {2,p},
since ab divides N. Distinct bases give a sum coprime to 2p and larger
than one, which would introduce another prime into N. Two powers of 2
give valuation at 2 at least r+2, contradicting v_2(N)=r. Two powers
p^e,p^f, e<=f, give v_p(N)=(r+1)e+f. Equality to r+2 forces e=f=1.

For p=q=2, N=2^(2r+2). Both inputs must be powers of 2. Unequal powers
give an odd divisor 1+2^(f-e)>1 in their sum, impossible for this N.
Equal inputs 2^e give v_2(N)=r+(r+2)e, forcing e=1.

Thus all prime-pair images have unique unordered prime-power fibres.
The diagonal images have at most one nonzero residue prime (none at r=2),
and do not overlap the two-visible-prime distinct-pair images.

The 160-program grammar varies residue modulus, odd-prime diagonal
exponent offset, and the pure-two diagonal exponent offset. Five training
fits reduce to one on held-out diagonal p=2 and high-index primes. Its
full patterns are {2:2r+2} or {2:r,p:r+2}; it validates the reconstructed
image. These finite fits motivated the universal proof, not vice versa.

2. TWO LINEAR DECODERS AND THE GENERIC CONVOLUTION BRIDGE

On every formal arithmetic sequence h define
  B_r h(pq)=h(Phi_r(p,q)), p<=q primes; zero off prime semiprimes,
  A_r h(n)=sum_(p<=q primes, p+q=n) h(Phi_r(p,q)).
Both maps use finitely many input coordinates for each output coordinate.
The fibre theorem makes their encoded-index implementations unambiguous.
B_r is a partial coordinate permutation; A_r aggregates coordinates with
the same sum. Both send delta_1 to zero, rather than preserving the unit.

For arbitrary signed rational augmentation inputs a,b supported on primes,
the graft K_r from pair_proof.txt satisfies the universal two-product law
  B_r K_r(a,b)=a *_D b,
  A_r K_r(a,b)=a *_+ b.
The second is ordinary additive convolution on positive integer indices
(with zero outside the stated supports), not the shifted unit-group product
of mixed_index_proof.txt. The off-diagonal encoded coefficient is
a(p)b(q)+a(q)b(p), and the diagonal is a(p)b(p). This proves both identities.

The additive map has a nonzero kernel. At r=3,
  A_3(delta_21000-delta_25000)=0,
since 21000=Phi_3(3,7), 25000=Phi_3(5,5), and both sums are ten.
It has no two-sided inverse. A finite encoded prefix is also inadequate
at the same decoded cutoff: both index-ten queries exceed a prefix 20000.
Their absence cannot be replaced by zero in an infinite input series.

4. SCALAR ZETA BRIDGES AND THEIR ABSOLUTE DOMAINS

Let P(s)=sum_p p^-s be prime zeta, initially Re(s)>1. The product output
is P(s)^2. Its exact absolute abscissa is one, with divergence at one:
the coefficient at 2q is two for every odd prime q, and sum_p 1/p diverges.

The additive output is
  Z_+(s)=sum_(p,q primes) (p+q)^(-s), initially Re(s)>2.
Its exact absolute abscissa is TWO, but it CONVERGES absolutely at two.
This distinction between the abscissa and endpoint convergence matters.
For p<=q, (p+q)^-2<=q^-2 and pi(q)<=Cq/log(q), by the unconditional
prime number theorem. Thus the double sum is bounded by a constant times
sum_q 1/(q log q), which converges by partial summation and the same theorem.
For sigma<2, balanced prime blocks p,q in [X,2X] contribute at least
c*X^(2-sigma)/(log X)^2, unbounded as X grows. No pointwise assumption
about the number of Goldbach representations is used in either proof.

For Re(s)=sigma>2, centered beta Mellin inversion gives the native bridge
  Z_+(s)=1/(2pi)*integral_R
    Gamma(s/2+it)*Gamma(s/2-it)/Gamma(s)
    *P(s/2+it)*P(s/2-it) dt.
Absolute convergence and the gamma contour majorant justify interchanging
the sums and integral. The integrand is even in REAL t even when s is
complex; the half-line integral has normalization 1/pi.

Prime zeta is evaluated from actual native zeta by classical inversion
  P(z)=sum_(m>=1) mu(m)/m * LOG zeta(mz), Re(z)>1.
For a=Re(z)>1 and cutoff M its omitted tail is at most
  delta=2^(-(M+1)a)/[(M+1)(1-2^-a)]*(1+2/((M+1)a-1)).
Every evaluated LOG is on its Euler sheet since LOG zeta(a)<pi at these
arguments. The analytic flag is passed to native logarithms at quadrature
nodes. Only the finite Mobius polynomial is integrated: a real-contour
tail ball is never assumed valid at off-contour complex evaluation nodes.

For A=s/2=a+iu the whole-line L1 kernel mass is bounded by
  Gamma(2a)/(2^(2a)*|Gamma(s)|).
Cauchy--Schwarz reduces the numerator integral to
integral_R |Gamma(a+it)|^2 dt=2pi*Gamma(2a)/2^(2a).
For completeness the latter follows from Fourier Parseval applied to
f(x)=exp(a*x-exp(x)), followed by y=exp(x). Consequently the total finite
Mobius approximation error in the integral is at most the mass bound
times (2*L*delta+delta^2), L=LOG zeta(a).

The remaining t>T contour tail uses b=ceil(2a)/2, u=|Im(s)|/2,
T=u+24, d=2b-1, and C=2pi/(1-exp(-2pi)). It is bounded by
  C/(pi*|Gamma(s)|)*(Gamma(a)/Gamma(b))^2*L^2*exp(-pi*T)
  *sum_(j=0..d) binom(d,j)*(T+u+b)^(d-j)*j!/pi^(j+1).
The beta comparison for a<=b and exact half-integer/integer gamma modulus
formulas give this polynomial exponential majorant, as in pair_proof.txt.

Six fresh cases compare BOTH decoded outputs with native zeta references.
Additive coefficients through 2048 have tail N^(2-sigma)/(sigma-2), since
the ordered prime count is <=n. Product coefficients through 8192 have

It follows by enlarging the union to two integer quadrants and integrating

5. PRIME-ONLY ENCODING HAS A SHARP BOUNDARY, INCLUDING ITS ENDPOINT

Project kappa_r onto the prime-pair images. Its scalar series is
  C_r(s)=sum_(p,q primes) [pq(p+q)^r]^(-s).
This is the PRIME-ONLY component of the earlier full prime-power Gamma_r.
For r>=2 its exact absolute abscissa is sigma0=2/(r+2), and it converges
absolutely at sigma0. This endpoint result uses classical prime density;
it is not inferred from an AM-GM majorant that diverges there.

For fixed 0<sigma<1, partial summation with pi(x)<=Cx/log(x) gives
sum_(p<=x) p^-sigma <= C_sigma*x^(1-sigma)/log(x) for large x.
Splitting the double sum at p<=q then bounds C_r(sigma0) by a constant
times sum_q q^(1-(r+2)*sigma0)/log(q)=sum_q 1/(q log q), finite.
For 0<=sigma<sigma0, balanced prime blocks give the lower bound
c*X^(2-(r+2)*sigma)/(log X)^2, unbounded. Negative sigma gives nondecaying
positive terms. This proves the sharp abscissa as well as endpoint convergence.
No maximal analytic-continuation claim is made for either encoded series.

6. SHARP ABSOLUTE COEFFICIENT NORMS AND POWER WEIGHTS

For A_sigma with norm sum_n |h_n|n^-sigma and sigma>0:
  A_r:A_sigma->A_tau is bounded IFF tau>=(r+2)*sigma,
  ||A_r||=2^((2r+2)*sigma-2*tau) when bounded.
The norm is the supremum of column ratios because each encoded column
appears in only one output row. Put n=p+q. The ratio is
(pq)^sigma*n^(r*sigma-tau)<=4^-sigma*n^((r+2)*sigma-tau).
For a nonpositive exponent, n>=4 and equality at p=q=2 gives the exact
norm. Below threshold choose p=q along unbounded primes, so the ratio
grows like p^((r+2)*sigma-tau). Aggregation does not improve the l1 bound.

Similarly the product decoder satisfies
  B_r:A_sigma->A_tau bounded IFF tau>=(r+1)*sigma,
  ||B_r||=4^((r+1)*sigma-tau).
Rewrite its column ratio as
(pq)^((r+1)*sigma-tau)*(1/p+1/q)^(r*sigma).
Use pq>=4 and 1/p+1/q<=1, with equality at p=q=2. For unboundedness
below threshold use p=2, q growing through primes.

For the prime-only endpoint source sigma0, the generic bounded target
weights are exactly 2 for A_r and 2(r+1)/(r+2) for B_r. Increasing r
does not lower the additive edge. Pure power multiplication n^-lambda
replaces target tau by tau+lambda and shifts the scalar argument by lambda.
Using this endpoint source at target tau=1/2 requires
  lambda>=3/2 for A_r,
  lambda>=(3r+2)/(2(r+2)) for B_r.
These inclusive inequalities concern the projected prime-only component;
the earlier strict inequalities used the full prime-power certificate.
They are generic decoder bounds, not necessities for every special input:
P(s)^2 already has the better zeta-specific absolute abscissa one.

For B_3 the generic endpoint threshold is lambda>=11/10. Its critical
prime-zeta branch obstruction moves to real part<=-3/5. No corresponding
additive obstruction at an individual zeta zero is claimed: the additive
function is different, and its critical-strip continuation is unproved here.

7. THE HILBERT GATE: ADDITIVE ROW COUNTS CAUSE ADDITIONAL LOSS

Let H_sigma have squared norm sum_n |h_n|^2*n^(-2sigma). Distinct output
rows of A_r use disjoint encoded coordinates. Cauchy--Schwarz in each row,
with equality on its weighted row vector, gives the exact formula
  ||A_r||^2=sup_n n^(2r*sigma-2tau)
    *sum_(p<=q, p+q=n) (pq)^(2sigma).
For sigma>=0, this norm is finite IFF
  tau>=(r+2)*sigma+1/2.
Sufficiency uses at most n/2 unordered pairs and pq<=n^2/4. At equality
it gives the universal norm upper bound 2^(-2sigma-1/2); this upper bound
is NOT asserted to be the exact infinite norm.

For necessity, PNT supplies order X^2/(log X)^2 unordered pairs with
p,q in [X,2X]. Among at most 2X+1 possible sums there is one with at
least cX/(log X)^2 balanced pairs. Its squared row norm is at least a
positive constant times X^(2(r+2)*sigma+1-2tau)/(log X)^2. Below threshold
this is unbounded. This average argument does not assume the Goldbach
conjecture or an asymptotic for every individual even sum.

In particular A_r is UNBOUNDED on unweighted l2. The product decoder's
partial-isometry law cannot be transferred to the sum aggregation. Exact
finite row norms at sigma=1/2 and tau=2,5/2,3 through five cutoffs support
the implementation. They are not substituted for the universal proof or
called exact infinite maxima.

8. ORDINARY ZETA ZERO INFORMATION IS RETAINED IN THE PRODUCT OUTPUT

Locally near either reused simple zeta zero, write
  P(s)=LOG zeta(s)+sum_(m>=2) mu(m)/m*LOG zeta(ms).
The m=2 term is locally single-valued because zeta(2s) is certified nonzero
on the whole small disk. Every m>=3 term lies in the zero-free Euler
half-plane; its infinite sum is locally uniform. Thus a simple-zero loop
changes P by h=2pi*i, and changes the PRODUCT output P^2 by
  Delta(P^2)=4pi*i*P-4pi^2.
Every log sheet has the same real part; |Delta(P^2)|>=4pi*|Re P|.
The source bounds Re P with native log moduli through m=48 and an infinite
Euler tail. The independent route uses a degree-one zeta jet, changed
radius-1/40 Cauchy remainder, a radius-1/10 whole-disk zeta(2s) variation,
the exact sign mu(2)=-1, and real Euler norm bounds for m>=3. Both certify
negative Re P and positive jump lower bounds at both existing zero disks.

The encoded prime-only C_3 is holomorphic across these disks, since they
lie strictly to the right of sigma0=2/5. The decoded product's branch
information really returns, at the costs proved above. Its pole-cancelled
version [P(s)+LOG(s-1)]^2 has single-valued holomorphic continuation on
Re(s)>1/2 iff RH: the remainder is holomorphic there and an interior zeta
zero creates nonconstant squared-log monodromy. This supplies no estimate
proving the continuation. Values or monodromy of Z_+ at those zeros are
explicitly NOT inferred from the product result.
