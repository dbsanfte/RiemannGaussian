2. Exact prime-power, mixed-prime and composite-factor split
Let u_i be the restriction of h_i to prime powers, and v_i=h_i-u_i, supported
on integers with at least two distinct prime factors. Define
 U=u_1*u_2*u_3.
Its coefficients are all nonpositive: each tuple has three negative factors.
Split its complete summatory function into
 D(x): tuples with all three factors powers of the SAME prime;

Thus sum_{n<=x}U(n)=D(x)+T(x), both nonpositive. Define the remaining complete
composite-factor branch
 C(x)=sum_{empty != A subset {1,2,3}}
       sum_{n<=x} [(*_{i in A}v_i)*(*_{i not in A}u_i)](n).
Every term with at least one genuinely composite factor is included, whether
or not other factor primes overlap it. Its signs can vary. Exactly,
 J(x)=D(x)+T(x)+C(x)-N_1(x)-N_2(x)-N_3(x).
All squares and cross terms in this identity must be accounted for; C is
not a small error merely because fixed-index small-shift derivatives erase it.

4. A proved obstruction to a prime-tuple-only bound
Every tuple in T is negative, so there is no cancellation internal to this
prime-power-only branch. Fix the factors n_1=2,n_2=3,n_3=p with p>=5 prime.
Its absolute weight is at least the positive fixed constant
 a=36(1-2^(-1/6))(1-3^(-1/3))(1-5^(-1/2)).
Thus |T(x)|>=a[pi(x/6)-2]. This lower bound uses a subset of the full tuples;
it omits none from the actual state.

An elementary lower bound pi(y)>>y/log y is enough here and needs no PNT:
the central binomial coefficient is at least4^N/(2N+1), while its p-adic
valuation is at most floor(log(2N)/log p). Therefore
 2N log2-log(2N+1)<=log binom(2N,N)<=pi(2N)log(2N).
Taking N=floor(x/12) gives |T(x)|>>x/log x for every sufficiently large x.
Hence E_T(X)>>X/(log X)^2, incompatible with cofinal subpower moments.
The same-prime D is small, and each full smooth main term is O(x^(5/6)),
which is o(x/log x). Those terms cannot supply the required cancellation.

This does NOT refute the actual full J. It proves that the composite-factor
branch must cancel a large negative mixed-prime contribution. In block norm,
if the full gate holds, ||C-N_1-N_2||_block must be essentially as large as
||T||_block and its full cross correlation with T sufficiently negative.
Bounding T, or its prime diagonal, by square root would be false. This is a
precise signed arithmetic mechanism still needed, beyond norm/recovery facts.

There is an exact Euler-half-plane version of this balance. For real s->1+,
the prime-power restriction has series
 U_i(s)=-sum_p [(1-p^(-b_i))/b_i]*p^(-s)/(1-p^(-s-b_i))
       =-(1/b_i)log(1/(s-1))+O(1).
The difference from -(1/b_i)sum_p p^(-s) is absolutely bounded near1, and
sum_p p^(-s)=log(1/(s-1))+O(1) follows from the Euler product and its
convergent higher-prime-power tail. Thus U_1U_2U_3=-36 log^3(1/(s-1))+
O(log^2(1/(s-1))). The full P(s) instead tends to -36 at1, because D_i(1)=0.
The composite-factor series must cancel that cubic logarithmic divergence.
This is a proved signed structural identity near the Euler boundary, not
an x-uniform remainder bound or critical-half-plane growth estimate.

5. Squarefree partition law and finite prime attenuation
For squarefree n>1 let Q be its prime set, with size l>=1. A tuple of three
nonempty factors is an ordered surjection kappa:Q->{1,2,3}. Therefore
 p(n)=36*mu(n)*sum_{surjections kappa}
                  product_{p in Q}(1-p^(-b_{kappa(p)})).
The matched comparator has coefficient
 (mu-delta_1)^{*3}(n)=mu(n)[3^l-3*2^l+3],
the number of these surjections. Both vanish when1<=l<3 and have the
same sign otherwise. The unit n=1 has coefficient zero separately; the
displayed comparator polynomial does not apply to the empty prime set.
When l>=3 and all p in Q are at least Y,
 0<=|p(n)|/[36*|(mu-delta_1)^{*3}(n)|]<=1,
and the ratio is at least1-l*Y^(-1/6) when that lower bound is positive.
This follows from product(1-t_p)>=1-sum t_p, with every t_p<=Y^(-1/6).

Thus on integers with only sufficiently large prime factors the coefficient
approaches36 times its comparator, rather than retaining a uniformly tiny
coefficient. Small primes are genuinely attenuated, and the slow p^(-1/6)
scale can make finite data preasymptotic. This supplies a concrete possible
explanation of early finite gains. It is not a lower bound on the full signed
energy, because weighted sums of opposite mu signs can cancel differently.
Nonsquarefree and cross-factor repeated-prime branches do not obey this simple
squarefree sign law and remain in C and the full moment. Their density is
not negligible merely because prime-square labels occur.
