1. AN ALL-SCALE COMPONENT SIMPLIFICATION.

Let X>=1 be an integer, k>=2 an integer, and
u_k(X)=floor((4X-1)^(1/k)). Since
(X+1)^2-(4X-1)=(X-1)^2+1>0, we have u_k(X)<=X.
Slot one is the prefix of truncated mu, so it equals M(u_k(X))
throughout the entire block [X,2X). Its exact square energy is

 B_(k,1)(X)=X*M(u_k(X))^2.

Using |mu(n)|<=1 gives |M(u)|<=u and u_k(X)<(4X)^(1/k).
For any nonnegative rational p and lambda>=0,

 0<=lambda*B_(k,1)(X)/ceil(X^p)
   <=lambda*4^(2/k)*X^(1+2/k-p).

This uses the ceiling of the full rational power. If p>=1+2/k,
the feature is globally bounded by 4*lambda, since k>=2 and X>=1.
A finite sum of such terms is bounded by K=4*sum(lambda).
The proof is uniform in k; evaluating a finite large k may require resources.
For the singleton selected by the pilot, the sharper bound is
V(X)<=4^(2/3)*X^(-1/3). For its two-term lead it is
V(X)<=4/X+4^(2/3)*X^(-1/3). Both vanish as X tends to infinity.

2. WHAT THE BOUND DOES AND DOES NOT GIVE.

If 0<=V(X)<=K, then |V(2X)-V(X)|<=K. Therefore the existence
of a cofinal all-scale inequality for the innovation
I_V(X)=E(2X)-E(X)+V(2X)-V(X) is equivalent to existence of one
for I_0(X)=E(2X)-E(X), after increasing each free C_q by K.
Indeed ceil(X^(1/q))>=1, so the additive K can be absorbed into
a scale-independent constant in either direction. This equivalence gives
no bound on I_0 or I_V. A shrinking correction may improve finite behavior
but does not by itself establish arithmetic cancellation at large scales.
