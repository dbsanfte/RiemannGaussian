Fixed target and nonnegative storage
Let M(n)=sum_{k=1}^n mu(k), with its complete declared coefficient domain,
A_mu(X)=sum_{n=X}^{2X-1} M(n)^2, and E_mu(X)=A_mu(X)/X^2.
The candidate object is W(X)=E_mu(X)+V(X), not a transformed or filtered
replacement for E_mu. Generate V through finite positive-weighted sums of
squares with positive finite normalizers. Signed state mixtures inside those
squares are permitted. Structural nonnegativity of V gives E_mu<=W; no
independent critical upper bound on V is required for that implication.

The quantity to compare is the entire signed W(2X)-W(X). Its parent term
has a negative coefficient; it cannot be treated as another positive square
or dropped. Preserve the exact E_mu contribution, every signed cross term,
every declared index and complete initial W(1). Initial storage is part of
the resulting bound and cannot be treated as cost-free padding.

Conditional dyadic telescoping
Suppose a fixed candidate is finite and V(X)>=0 at every dyadic X>=1.
For a fixed integer q>=1, assume a finite C_q>=0 independent of X satisfies
  W(2X)-W(X)<=C_q*ceil(X^(1/q))
at EVERY dyadic X>=1. For X=2^J, sum over 1,2,...,2^(J-1), retaining W(1):
  E_mu(X)<=W(X)
          <=W(1)+2*C_q*(X^(1/q)-1)/(2^(1/q)-1).
This uses ceil(t)<=2t for t>=1 and the exact finite geometric-series formula.
For X=1 the additional term iszero. A finite set of tested scales or q values
does not discharge the premise. Critical all-scale use requires the stated
cofinal quantifiers, including all initial scales and q-dependent costs.
The denominator is positive; a floating approximation to it is not a proof
enclosure or a scale-independent constant certificate.

Conditional ordinary-zeta consequence
The premise for cofinal q yields A_mu(X)<=K_q*X^(2+1/q) for dyadic X,
with finite K_q including initial storage and the geometric factor. By
Cauchy-Schwarz on each complete block, its sum of |M(n)| is at most
sqrt(X*A_mu(X))<=sqrt(K_q)*X^(3/2+1/(2q)). Thus the dyadic contributions
to integral_1^infinity M(x)*x^(-s-1)dx converge absolutely and locally
uniformly when Re(s)>1/2+1/(2q). Cofinality provides such a q for every
compact subset of Re(s)>1/2. Its product with s is consequently holomorphic
there. On Re(s)>1, ordinary partial summation identifies it with1/zeta(s);
analytic continuation therefore excludes zeta zeros in Re(s)>1/2. The usual
functional equation then gives the conditional RH consequence for nontrivial
zeros. This is an implication of the missing all-scale arithmetic premise,
not a conclusion of finite sampling or a proof supplied by the candidate AST.

Moving domains and coupling
Evaluate the SAME candidate program at X and2X, recomputing all roots,
prime-bank endpoints, masks, denominators and state maps at their own scale.
An old parent cutoff must not silently serve as the child's moving cutoff.
Complete auxiliary domains may require more than4X-1 coefficient coverage;
derive the actual horizon from the AST and reject/miss insufficient coverage.
Never clip a requested index or publish a partial sum after exhaustion.

A coupled bank has the form square(sum_p state_p), whose expansion includes
both diagonal p=p' and all signed p!=p' interactions. It differs from
sum_p square(state_p). Both can be nonnegative candidate constructions, but
their semantics and costs must remain distinct. Resource limits must bound
complete bank construction and evaluation rather than dropping cross terms
or restricting to a convenient prime subset after observing responses.

Finite improvement and future proof priorities
The E_mu anchor prevents loss of target, but does not guarantee that a small
new V carries useful signed cancellation. Root-index padding, universally
bounded additions, existing identities or syntactic/behavioral aliases may
produce competitive finite scores. Compare every selected program with the
stored V=0 baseline, and never label selection itself a new all-scale result.
Candidate-specific analytical review must distinguish such padding/aliases
from an interaction that plausibly supports the complete innovation estimate.
That distinction needs a proof or explicit unresolved lemma concerning the
actual expression, with initial, moving-domain and normalization costs intact.
Control-source results are diagnostic comparisons, not alternative actual-mu
premises or proofs that a candidate uses enough signed arithmetic information.
