SHIFTED ZETA QUOTIENTS: EXACT ARITHMETIC, SIGNED MOMENTS, AND RECOVERY

Scope and outcome
-----------------
This is a proof review of the fixed rational shifts 0<b<1. It contains no
new arithmetic evaluations, numerical experiments, or response-score review.
The earlier shift_ratio_proof.txt, compensated_shift_proof.txt and
prime_twist_proof.txt were read. Finite shifts, pole compensation, and
same-height rightward zero chains are established mechanisms in this
workspace; none is claimed here as a newly discovered primitive.

The proposed prime-power law and fixed-x limit are correct. A complete,
all-scale cofinal signed-square moment bound for the exactly centered G_b
would imply RH for EVERY fixed b>0, including b<1/2. The shifted quotient's
pole-regularized holomorphy in Re(s)>1/2 is in fact equivalent to RH. This
review does not prove the required moment bound. Absolute inverse estimates
have an explicit critical-scale obstruction, and the b->0 limit is not
uniform in x. The proposed distinct-shift product grammar also retains every
ordinary off-line zero after exact pole compensation; its derivative-limit
sign must be corrected. All moment/global/RH proof flags remain false.

1. Exact coefficients and their actual-prime content
----------------------------------------------------
Put k_b(n)=n^(-b), a_b=mu*k_b, and D_b(s)=zeta(s+b)/zeta(s).
Dirichlet convolution is on positive integers, and all summatory functions
below have inclusive endpoints. For Re(s)>1,
  D_b(s)=sum_(n>=1) a_b(n)n^(-s).
The local Euler factor, with z=p^(-s) and q=p^(-b), is
  (1-z)/(1-qz)=1-sum_(k>=1)(1-q)q^(k-1)z^k.
Consequently
  a_b(1)=1,
  a_b(p^k)=-(1-p^(-b))p^(-(k-1)b), k>=1,
  a_b(n)=(-1)^omega(n) product_(p^k||n)
                         (1-p^(-b))p^(-(k-1)b).
These identities use the actual prime-power Euler factors. The sign is
(-1)^omega(n), not mu(n): prime powers and nonsquarefree integers remain.
In particular |a_b(n)|<=1. For distinct primes p,q,
  a_b(pq)=(1-p^(-b))(1-q^(-b))>0.
A prime-power-only approximation discards this and all higher complements.

Two inverses must not be confused:
  a_b^(-1)=r_b=1*(mu(n)n^(-b)),
  r_b(p^k)=1-p^(-b)>0 for EVERY k>=1,
  r_b(n)=product_(p|n)(1-p^(-b))>=0;
whereas the inverse of the smoothing kernel k_b is mu(n)n^(-b), and
  mu=a_b*(mu(n)n^(-b)).
The first inverse recovers the convolution unit, not mu. Its positive mean
is elementary. For integer N>=1, with R_b(N)=sum_(n<=N)r_b(n),
  R_b(N)=sum_(d<=N)mu(d)d^(-b)floor(N/d),
  |R_b(N)-N/zeta(1+b)|
     <= N^(1-b)/b + 1 + N^(1-b)/(1-b).
Indeed bound the floor error absolutely and the convergent tail of
sum mu(d)/d^(1+b) by the corresponding positive integrals. Thus this
positive inverse has a genuine linear mean; it is not a bounded critical
scale averaging kernel. The exact identity sum_(m<=x)r_b(m)A_b(x/m)=1
retains both the constant and every smooth/signed complement of A_b.

2. Fixed-x small-shift limit, including its limitations
------------------------------------------------------
For every fixed real x>=1, only finitely many coefficients occur. At a
prime power p^k,
  a_b(p^k)/b -> -log p=-Lambda(p^k).
At an integer with at least two distinct prime factors, a_b(n)/b->0.
It follows without any limit interchange over an infinite range that
  (A_b(x)-1)/b -> -psi(x),
where psi(x)=sum_(n<=x)Lambda(n).
The Laurent expansion zeta(1-b)=-1/b+gamma+O(b) gives
  c_b=1/((1-b)zeta(1-b))
     =-b-(1+gamma)b^2+O(b^3).
Therefore
  G_b(x)=(A_b(x)-1-c_b*x^(1-b))/b -> x-psi(x).
This is valid at integers with the stated inclusive endpoint, between
integers, and at x=1, where G_b(1)=-c_b/b->1.

The first correction keeps the omitted convolution visible. For fixed x,
  G_b(x)=x-psi(x)
    +b*[ (1/2)sum_(n<=x){(Lambda*Lambda)(n)+Lambda(n)log n}
          +x(1+gamma-log x) ] +O_x(b^2).
To verify it, write log D_b=b(log zeta)' + b^2(log zeta)''/2+O(b^3)
in an absolutely convergent Dirichlet half-plane and compare each fixed
coefficient; alternatively expand each finite local Euler factor.

The error subscript x is essential. The approximation 1-p^(-b)~b log p
requires b log p small; it fails uniformly as p and x increase with b fixed.
The fixed-x limit supplies neither a uniform G_b moment nor a transferable
bound for x-psi(x). Higher-support terms cannot be dropped at an all-scale
cutoff merely because they vanish in this finite-x derivative limit.

3. Correct main term and the exact compensated Mellin identity
--------------------------------------------------------------
Let kappa=1-b. Ordinary zeta is negative and nonzero at every real
0<s<1, so c_b is finite and negative. D_b has a simple pole at kappa with
residue 1/zeta(kappa). Its Perron main term is exactly
  c_b x^kappa, c_b=1/(kappa zeta(kappa)).
This identifies a pole term, not an unconditional assertion that it
dominates A_b: poles at unknown ordinary zeros may be farther right.
The subtraction 1 removes a_b(1), as required by the normalized increment.

Write H_G(s)=integral_1^infinity G_b(x)x^(-s-1)dx. For Re(s)>1,
  b*s*H_G(s)=D_b(s)-1-c_b*s/(s-kappa).                 (3.1)
This follows by integrating the inclusive step function; values at its
jump points do not affect the integral. In particular the complete smooth
term is c_b*s/(s-kappa), not just the principal fraction with its residue.
Its residue is c_b*kappa=1/zeta(kappa), exactly matching D_b.
Define
  Z_b(s)=D_b(s)-1-c_b*s/(s-kappa).
It has no pole at kappa. All constants in (3.1) are retained. For positive
RH output exponents an additional bounded constant would not affect the
moment threshold, but it would change this exact identity.

If the main coefficient is wrong by delta, the missing term is delta
x^kappa/b. Its own normalized block square is of order
(delta/b)^2 X^(2*kappa-1)=(delta/b)^2 X^(1-2b).
For b<1/2 this is incompatible with cofinal subpower bounds unless it is
canceled by another complete term. This is why exact pole compensation,
rather than a fitted constant or an omitted smooth part, matters. For
b>=1/2 that single scaling test cannot enforce the coefficient; the exact
arithmetic definition still does.

4. Complete integer moments really give half-plane holomorphy
-------------------------------------------------------------
Use the precise sufficient premise
  for an unbounded/cofinal set of positive q, there are finite C_q such that
  X^(-2)sum_(n=X)^(2X-1)|G_b(n)|^2 <= C_q X^(1/q)       (4.1)
for EVERY dyadic integer X>=1. Constants are independent of X. This is the
whole signed square: neither diagonal energy nor a finite list of gates is
a substitute. Fixed initial blocks can instead be explicitly absorbed into
the constants, but cannot simply be missing.

For n<=t<n+1, A_b(t)=A_b(n) exactly, hence
  |G_b(t)-G_b(n)| <= L_b n^(-b),
  L_b=|c_b|*(1-b)/b.
The jump at integer n is a_b(n)/b and has absolute size at most 1/b.
Thus an interval bound including a jump is at most 1/b+L_b(n-1)^(-b)
for n>=2. No smoothing has changed the integer endpoint convention.
For a complete dyadic block,
  integral_X^(2X)|G_b(t)|^2 dt
    <=2 C_q X^(2+1/q)+2 L_b^2 X^(1-2b).                (4.2)
The second term comes from complete unit cells; there is no untested tail.

For sigma>1/2 choose q with 1/q<2*sigma-1. Then
  ||G_b||_(sigma)^2:=integral_1^infinity
                              |G_b(t)|^2 t^(-2*sigma-1)dt
    <= 2 C_q/[1-2^(1-2*sigma+1/q)]
       +2 L_b^2/[1-2^(-2*sigma-2b)].                   (4.3)
For Re(s)>sigma, Cauchy--Schwarz gives the explicit bound
  |H_G(s)| <= ||G_b||_(sigma)/sqrt(2*(Re(s)-sigma)).    (4.4)
The same domination on compact sets, with logarithmic factors, proves
holomorphy of H_G throughout Re(s)>1/2. This avoids the incorrect inference
that an L2 block bound automatically gives a square-root pointwise bound.

5. All zero multiplicities and every positive shift
----------------------------------------------------
Assume the holomorphy just proved. Identity (3.1), first true to the right
of one, continues meromorphically, so D_b has no off-real pole in
Re(s)>1/2. Suppose an ordinary zero rho in that half-plane has order m>0.
The real compensator is analytic at rho. If m_+=ord_rho zeta(s+b), then
  ord_rho D_b=m_+-m.
Holomorphy requires m_+>=m. Thus rho+b is another ordinary zero, of at
least the same multiplicity. Repeat. All steps preserve imaginary part
and move right by the FIXED positive amount b. After finitely many steps
Re(rho+j*b)>1, contradicting the Euler-product zero-free half-plane.
No simple-zero hypothesis, global rightmost-zero hypothesis, or assumed
nonvanishing numerator is used. Coincident shifted zeros are handled by
the exact orders. Ordinary zeta has no positive-real zero, so none of this
can be concealed by the real main pole. Functional-equation symmetry then
puts every nontrivial zero on Re(s)=1/2: this is RH.

Conversely, RH makes the denominator nonzero in Re(s)>1/2; the sole
numerator pole there, if kappa>1/2, is canceled exactly. Thus Z_b is
holomorphic there. This analytic equivalence is established. This memo
has NOT supplied the arithmetic estimate (4.1), nor derived it just from
analytic holomorphy. Any converse growth claim needs the corresponding
all-height bounds and inversion estimates, not just absence of poles.

6. Quantitative recovery: what is bounded, and what is not
----------------------------------------------------------
The exact floor/divisor smoothing identity and its inverse are
  A_b(x)=sum_(m<=x)m^(-b)M(x/m),
  M(x)=sum_(m<=x)mu(m)m^(-b)A_b(x/m)
      =sum_(m<=x)mu(m)m^(-b)
       +c_b*x^kappa*sum_(m<=x)mu(m)/m
       +b*sum_(m<=x)mu(m)m^(-b)G_b(x/m).               (6.1)
Every term is retained. The first two truncated signed sums are not known
small merely because the third contains a centered candidate.

For f=0 below one, dilation f(t/m) has weighted-L2 norm m^(-sigma)||f||
with measure t^(-2*sigma-1)dt (or no larger on a truncated horizon).
An absolute triangle estimate for the smoothing inverse is bounded by
  sum_(m>=1)|mu(m)|m^(-b-sigma),
which is finite exactly when b+sigma>1 and is at most zeta(b+sigma).
The exact positive-series value for u=b+sigma>1 is zeta(u)/zeta(2u).
It diverges for u<=1: at u=1 this follows from its pole, and below one
by comparison. Thus for b<1/2 absolute inversion cannot reach arbitrary
sigma>1/2. This is a precise limitation of that method, not a refutation
of the analytic zero-chain bridge. At b=1/2 the cost is finite for every
sigma>1/2 but grows as sigma decreases to the boundary.

There is also finite rightward analytic recovery. Set F(s)=(s-1)zeta(s),
with F(1)=1, and
  E_b(s)=F(s+b)/F(s)=(s-kappa)D_b(s)/(s-1).
Under the moment premise, the apparent singularities at kappa and one
are removable. At one the numerator vanishes because D_b(1)=0; equivalently
(3.1) imposes H_G(1)=-(b+c_b)/b^2. This cancellation must be retained in
any numerical evaluation of the formula.
For K chosen so Re(s+K*b)>=R>1,
  1/zeta(s)=(s-1)*[product_(j=0)^(K-1)E_b(s+j*b)]/F(s+K*b),
  |1/zeta(s)| <= |s-1|*zeta(R)/|s+K*b-1|
                         *product_(j=0)^(K-1)|E_b(s+j*b)|.             (6.2)
Equations (3.1),(4.3),(4.4), plus removable-point evaluation, give finite
conditional factors. K grows as 1/b. This is a multiplicative finite-chain
cost; it is not a bounded all-height inverse moment operator or a new
uniform estimate for M. Analytic zero recovery cannot be silently upgraded
to such a quantitative norm equivalence.

7. Distinct-shift products: a genuine structural grammar, with correct sign
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Let B={b_1,...,b_k} be distinct fixed shifts in (0,1), k>=1. Define
  H_b(s)=(D_b(s)-1)/b,
  P_B(s)=product_i H_(b_i)(s),
  h_b(1)=0, h_b(n)=a_b(n)/b for n>1,
  h_B=h_(b_1)*...*h_(b_k), S_B(x)=sum_(n<=x)h_B(n).
These are finite Dirichlet products, not a change of the single parameter
b. They remove all coefficients with fewer than k factors >=2, and hence
h_B(n)=0 for n<2^k and at every prime when k>=2.

SIGN CORRECTION: H_b->+zeta'/zeta, not -zeta'/zeta. Therefore for each
fixed integer n, as all b_i->0,
  h_B(n)->(-1)^k Lambda^{*k}(n).
The prime-power tuples and their convolutions are actual arithmetic, but
this finite-index derivative limit does not erase the finite-shift dense
complements. For k=2 and distinct primes p,q,
  h_B(pq)=[(1-p^(-b_1))(1-q^(-b_2))
           +(1-q^(-b_1))(1-p^(-b_2))]/(b_1*b_2)>0.
At p^r the complete coefficient for r>=2 is
  (1-p^(-b_1))(1-p^(-b_2))/(b_1*b_2)
    *sum_(l=1)^(r-1)p^(-(l-1)b_1-(r-l-1)b_2).
Other composite factorizations may have either sign. A positive tuple
limit is not a proof of positivity of all finite-shift coefficients.

Every potential real pole at kappa_i=1-b_i is simple. Its exact residue is
  R_i=1/[b_i*zeta(kappa_i)]
       *product_(j!=i)[(zeta(kappa_i+b_j)/zeta(kappa_i)-1)/b_j].
The other factors are regular there, since the shifts are distinct and all
positive real zeta arguments involved are nonzero. R_i may be zero, in
which case the supposed pole is removable; no nonzero-residue assumption
is necessary. Define the COMPLETE centered state
  E_B(x)=S_B(x)-sum_i (R_i/kappa_i)x^kappa_i.
There is no extra constant coefficient: h_B(1)=0. For Re(s)>1,
  P_B(s)=s*H_(E_B)(s)+sum_i R_i*s/[kappa_i*(s-kappa_i)].                (7.1)
This includes the constant parts of every compensating rational term.
Poles to the left of the target half-plane may be unnecessary for zero
exclusion, but their declared arithmetic complements are still retained.

The same cofinal whole-square premise as (4.1), with E_B replacing G_b,
forces H_(E_B) holomorphic in Re(s)>1/2. For n<=t<n+1,
  |E_B(t)-E_B(n)|<=sum_i |R_i| n^(-b_i)
                     <=L_B n^(-b_min), L_B=sum_i|R_i|.
Thus (4.2)--(4.4) apply with L_B,b_min. To include discrete jumps as well,
put beta=product_i 1/b_i. Then
  |h_B(n)|<=beta*d_k(n),
where d_k counts ordered factorizations into k positive integers.
For every epsilon>0 this has an explicit subpower bound
  d_k(n)<=C_(k,epsilon)n^epsilon,
  C_(k,epsilon)=product_(prime p<k^(1/epsilon))(1-p^(-epsilon))^(-k).
For k=1 take C=1. For larger primes use
binomial(e+k-1,k-1)<=k^e<=p^(epsilon*e); for smaller primes bound each
binomial(e+k-1,k-1)p^(-epsilon*e) by its generating series
(1-p^(-epsilon))^(-k). These are symbolic finite bounds, not new prime
computations. They provide a deterministic increment estimate including
both all jumps and all main terms. The stronger within-cell estimate above
is what is needed for the L2 transfer.

8. Product zero retention, with all multiplicities and possible cancellations
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
At an ordinary zero rho of order m>0, if every zeta(rho+b_i) is nonzero,
then every H_(b_i) has an order-m pole with leading coefficient
  zeta(rho+b_i)/(b_i*c_rho),
where zeta(s)=c_rho(s-rho)^m+... . Their product has an order-km pole with
nonzero leading coefficient. The real compensators cannot cancel it.
Therefore holomorphy of the centered product forces at least one ordinary
zero rho+b_i farther right. Repeating uses steps >=b_min, remains at the
same height, and reaches Re>1 in finitely many steps: contradiction.

If some right-shifted zeros coincide, their orders, and possible higher
cancellation in zeta(s+b_i)-zeta(s), can change each factor's local order.
There is no universal claimed order-km formula in that case. The argument
only uses its exactly valid contrapositive: NO right-shifted zero implies
an uncanceled order-km pole. That suffices for all multiplicities. An
alternative is to select the rightmost zero at this fixed height within
the compact strip; every right-shifted value is then nonzero. This is not
the unsupported assumption that a globally rightmost zeta zero exists.
Consequently a cofinal complete E_B moment is RH-sufficient, and RH is
also equivalent to holomorphy of the real-pole-compensated P_B in this
half-plane. No E_B moment has been proved here.

9. Product recovery costs and a polynomial inverse inequality
-------------------------------------------------------------
There is no safe operation of dividing by an assumed nonvanishing numerator
product. Away from the real shifted poles, put z=1/zeta(s), r_i=zeta(s+b_i).
The exact relation is
  beta^(-1)*P_B(s)=product_i(r_i*z-1),
where beta^(-1)=product_i b_i. In particular this is a degree-k equation
for z, with an Euler-half-plane branch condition; not a globally unique
unqualified kth root.
Let M_right=max_i|1/zeta(s+b_i)|. If |z|>=2M_right, every factor satisfies
|r_i*z-1|>=|r_i*z|/2. Consequently, with Bprod=product_i b_i,
  |1/zeta(s)|
    <=2*M_right*[1+(Bprod*|P_B(s)|)^(1/k)].             (9.1)
The coefficient in this expression is Bprod, NOT beta. This follows by
applying the exact displayed polynomial; it does not assume any numerator
has a positive lower bound other than the rightward zeta reciprocals.

For |Im(s)|>=1 there are no real shifted poles; (7.1) and the moment bound
provide explicit P_B bounds. Recurse (9.1) at all needed right shifts until
Re>=R>1, where |1/zeta|<=zeta(R). Depth is at most
ceil((R-Re(s))/b_min), and a direct full tree has at most
1+k+...+k^depth nodes. Uniform slab bounds can combine maxima, but all
factors, depth, and constants remain part of the conditional recovery cost.
This gives a concrete finite-height majorant, not an all-height M moment.

Real-pole cancellation can be expressed without singular r_i by setting
F(s)=(s-1)zeta(s), L_i=s+b_i-1, and
  K_B(s)=Bprod*P_B(s)*product_i L_i
        =product_i[F(s+b_i)/zeta(s)-L_i].
This is the exact cleared-denominator identity. The elementary analogous
bound is
  |1/zeta(s)| <= 2*max_i |L_i/F(s+b_i)|
                 +2*(|K_B(s)|*product_i |1/F(s+b_i)|)^(1/k).
Apparent singularities in K_B at the distinct real kappa_i cancel.
At one ordinary zeta has its known pole and F(1)=1. Recovery near this
point uses that known removable behavior, not division by a sampled small
number. None of these finite conditional formulas supplies the missing
uniform signed arithmetic estimate.

10. A further exact compensation family, beyond changing one shift
-------------------------------------------------------------------
A useful generic finite-Euler-difference template is
  Q_c(s)=product_(j=0)^J zeta(s+j*b)^(c_j),
with integer exponents c_j, c_0<0, b>0, and all other shifts positive.
If every real pole part inside the target half-plane is removed, holomorphy
in Re>1/2 excludes
ordinary off-line zeros: at a zero with no farther-right zeros its order
is c_0*m<0. Thus cancellation requires a farther-right zero. This is a
zero-retention rule for a family, not an estimate or novelty claim.

The second difference c=(-1,2,-1) gives a particularly concrete proposed
structural hook:
  Q(s)=zeta(s+b)^2/[zeta(s)zeta(s+2b)].
Unlike multiplying two adjacent ordinary ratios, it does not telescope
to a reparameterized D. At each prime its local factor is
  (1-z)(1-q^2*z)/(1-q*z)^2,
and its exact prime-power coefficient is
  v_b(p^k)=-k*(1-p^(-b))^2*p^(-(k-1)b), k>=1.          (10.1)
All higher composites are the multiplicative products of these local
coefficients; no prime-support truncation is licensed. In particular
v_b(pq)=(1-p^(-b))^2(1-q^(-b))^2 for distinct primes p,q.
For each fixed n,
  (v_b(n)-1_(n=1))/b^2 -> -Lambda(n)log n.
This follows either from (10.1) or
log Q= -b^2(log zeta)''+O(b^3). It is a precise prime-level second-order
attenuation, with all multi-prime complements, rather than a claim of new
uniform signed cancellation. As with the product grammar, log-weighted
prime-power convolution/difference limits are already established objects.

For 0<b<1 the real main pole is the DOUBLE pole at kappa=1-b.
There is no pole at 1-2b from the denominator's zeta pole: it creates a
zero unless another factor is singular there. At this real location for
0<b<1 the other factors are finite, and zeta(1-2b) is nonzero
(its argument is in (-1,1)); so it is indeed a zero. At kappa define
  Z=zeta(kappa)zeta(1+b),
  C2=1/Z,
  C1=[2*gamma-zeta'(kappa)/zeta(kappa)
                  -zeta'(1+b)/zeta(1+b)]/Z.
The complete Perron main term of Q is
  x^kappa*[C2*log x/kappa + C1/kappa-C2/kappa^2].       (10.2)
A normalized centered state must retain both terms of (10.2), its n=1
constant, and the scale b^2. Dropping the logarithmic pole complement
would invalidate any critical-scale test. Other nonreal poles and their
multiplicities remain governed by the exact order
  -m(rho)+2*m(rho+b)-m(rho+2b).
If all right-shifted multiplicities vanish, this is negative. The same
finite zero-chain argument therefore applies to this template, but no
moment bound for its whole signed square is presently established.

This template could expose real cancellation by comparing the complete
attenuated prime-power sum AND its composite complements with (10.2).
It does not obtain cancellation merely from smaller prime coefficients.
The distinct-H product template instead exposes complete prime-power tuple
correlations. These are explicit structural grammar/proof hooks; neither
is endorsed through an observed score or asserted to overcome RH.

11. Exact remaining estimate and the analytical obstruction
-----------------------------------------------------------
For the base family the required arithmetic theorem is precisely (4.1)
with the actual a_b, exact c_b, and the whole expression
  |sum_(n<=x)a_b(n)-1-c_b*x^(1-b)|^2/b^2.
For the proposed product family it is the same cofinal complete moment
with E_B=S_B-sum_i(R_i/kappa_i)x^kappa_i, including every Dirichlet-product
coefficient and every real-pole complement. Both would settle RH, and
neither has been proved by this review.

The elementary bound |a_b(n)|<=1 only gives G_b(x)=O_b(x), hence a
normalized block square O_b(X), not cofinal subpower. For products the
absolute divisor bounds likewise control bookkeeping and increments,
not RH-quality signed cancellation. Density, attenuation, a finite-x
prime-power derivative limit, and a finite list of complete moments do
not imply the all-scale theorem. The absolute-summation bound for the
smoothing inverse diverges when b+sigma<=1; this alone does not establish
unboundedness of the signed inverse operator. Positive inverse feedback
retains signed integral constraints instead of erasing the unknown source
information. Finite-chain zero retention establishes a valid conditional
bridge while leaving this uniform signed estimate open.
