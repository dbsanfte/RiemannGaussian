Point-factor mixtures: exact algebra and remaining signed estimates

Scope and disposition

This is a new analytical memo, separate from the frozen pilot002 run. I read
only the source_mixture program payloads and source-free semantic annotations
for generation0 IDs9..16, the frozen soundness memo, and, at root's request,
the program payload of final mutation64. No training constants, response
scores, evidence values or final responses were read. No source tables,
arithmetic probes, tests, hashes or frozen-file edits were performed.

There are useful exact reductions: ID15 admits a discrete integration-by-parts
identity because its point source is the increment of M at the same index.
Mutation64 admits an even/odd pairing and a coprime-product reduction to
mu(n(2n+1)). Neither reduction closes a uniform signed estimate. The root-point
mixtures admit exact root-block Abel summation but have unresolved weights.
All eight original mixtures, and mutation64, are bounded above by fixed finite
combinations of the original energy at nearby dyadic scales. This is a useful
comparison theorem, not bounded padding and not a proof of the desired energy
bound. It gives no candidate-specific all-scale advantage for free.

Notation and complete candidate inventory

M(u)=sum_{1<=n<=u} mu(n), M(0)=0, chi(u)=mu(u)^2. All arguments below are
nonnegative integers, and a point mu(0) is never requested. Put

  L_d(t)=M(floor(t/d)), a_k(t)=mu(floor(t^(1/k))),
  [F]_X=X^-2 sum_{t=X}^{2X-1} F(t), E(X)=[M(t)^2]_X.

The actual programs, each with one state of weight1, are

  ID9:  U=M-a_2 L_2.       ID10: U=M+a_2 L_3.
  ID11: U=M-a_3 L_2.       ID12: U=M+a_3 L_3.
  ID13: U=M-a_6 L_2.       ID14: U=M+a_6 L_3.
  ID15: U=M-mu(t)L_2.      ID16: U=L_2+a_3 L_3.

Every potential is V(X)=[U(t)^2]_X, with W(X)=E(X)+V(X). In particular, IDs
9..15 have W(1)=2, whereas ID16 has W(1)=1. These follow from M(1)=mu(1)=1
and L_2(1)=L_3(1)=0; the initial contribution is retained, not fitted away.

No listed state depends explicitly on X. Consequently its child at 2X uses
the same global function U(t), evaluated on the fresh block 2X<=t<4X, with
normalization (2X)^-2. There is no parameter-change correction K in the
soundness memo's frozen-parameter decomposition. The entire block transport
still remains; this observation does not cancel root-selector changes in t.

Exact lag bookkeeping, including the divisor3 endpoint issue

Write H_d(X)=[L_d(t)^2]_X. For every positive integer X,d,

  H_d(X)=X^-2 sum_{n=floor(X/d)}^{floor((2X-1)/d)} m_{d,X}(n) M(n)^2,
  m_{d,X}(n)=max(0,min(2X-1,dn+d-1)-max(X,dn)+1).

This is an exact count of the original t-domain. If d divides X it gives
H_d(X)=E(X/d)/d. Thus H_2(X)=E(X/2)/2 for dyadic X>=2. H_3(X) is NOT exactly
E(X/3)/3 on any dyadic X: both endpoint multiplicities must be retained.
For dyadic X>=4, the n-domain for H_3 is contained in [X/4,X-1], and every
multiplicity is at most3. Partitioning this interval into the two dyadic
blocks gives the unconditional comparison

  H_3(X) <= (3/16)E(X/4)+(3/4)E(X/2).                 (1)

This bound uses positivity only. Its right side is unresolved original
energy, so it cannot be invoked as an independent subpower estimate.

IDs9..14: diagonal masks, signed root-block correlation, and Abel identity

For the pair (k,d) and sign s in the inventory, define

  B_{k,d}(X)=[chi(floor(t^(1/k))) L_d(t)^2]_X,
  C_{k,d}(X)=[mu(floor(t^(1/k))) M(t)L_d(t)]_X.

Then exactly

  V=E+B_{k,d}+2s C_{k,d},       W=2E+B_{k,d}+2s C_{k,d}.       (2)

The diagonal is a squarefree gate on a macroscopic lag prefix. The odd source
sign persists in C; factoring it out of the complete state would change U.
There is no small-root prefix M(floor(t^(1/k))) in these states. A bound for
that standalone prefix square does not bound their mixed term or diagonal.

Here is a requested-domain Abel reduction of C. Let

  l=floor(X^(1/k)), h=floor((2X-1)^(1/k)),
  J_r=[max(X,r^k), min(2X-1,(r+1)^k-1)],
  B_r^*=sum_{t in J_r} M(t)L_d(t),       l<=r<=h.

All intervals are inclusive and nonempty. Then

  X^2 C_{k,d}(X)=sum_{r=l}^h mu(r)B_r^*
    =M(h)B_h^*-M(l-1)B_l^*
       -sum_{r=l}^{h-1} M(r)(B_{r+1}^*-B_r^*).       (3)

The truncated first/last intervals are essential. The same formula on the
child uses l=floor((2X)^(1/k)), h=floor((4X-1)^(1/k)), and its own intervals.
The weights B_r^* are signed sums of products of large prefixes; no monotonicity
or bounded total variation has been established. In particular,

  Delta_t(M(t)L_d(t))
    =mu(t+1)L_d(t)+1_{d divides t+1}mu((t+1)/d)M(t+1).

Thus transferring variation to the weights reintroduces signed prefix/source
products. Treating their variation as harmless would be an extra theorem.
The endpoints in (3) can already have normalized size O(X) under |M(u)|<=u:
the root factor contributes O(X^(1/k)), a root block has length
O(X^(1-1/k)), and the product of macroscopic prefixes is O(X^2).
This is a cost estimate, not a lower bound or an assertion of actual growth.

The direct unconditional bound is

  0<=B_{k,d}<=H_d,  |C_{k,d}|<=sqrt(E B_{k,d}),
  2|C_{k,d}|<=epsilon E+epsilon^-1 B_{k,d} (epsilon>0).     (4)

It does not supply cofinal-q cancellation. Multiplicativity cannot simply
replace mu(r)mu(n) by mu(rn) throughout either prefix: coprimality is not
imposed on the summation domains. The complements and shared-prime cases
would have to be kept. The root block relation itself gives no such condition.

The precise remaining candidate-specific signed obligation is a uniform
upper estimate for

  2(E(2X)-E(X))+(B_{k,d}(2X)-B_{k,d}(X))
       +2s(C_{k,d}(2X)-C_{k,d}(X)),                    (5)

for the fixed (k,d,s) of the selected candidate, with no independent dropping
of diagonals or correlations. Equation (3) is an alternative exact form for
the last term, not a bound for it. Changing k from2 to3 to6 changes the root
blocks and correlation problem; it does not change the sign of (5) by algebra.

ID15: same-index source gives a stronger discrete identity

Set F(t)=M(t)^2 and L(t)=L_2(t). Since M(t)-M(t-1)=mu(t),

  2mu(t)M(t)=F(t)-F(t-1)+chi(t),
  (M(t)-mu(t)L(t))^2
    =F(t)-L(t)(F(t)-F(t-1))+chi(t)(L(t)^2-L(t)).       (6)

For a=X,b=2X-1, summation by parts gives the exact complete block identity

  X^2 V15(X)=sum_{t=a}^b F(t)-L(b)F(b)+L(a)F(a-1)
    +sum_{n=ceil((a+1)/2)}^{floor(b/2)} mu(n)F(2n-1)
    +sum_{t=a}^b chi(t)(L(t)^2-L(t)).                 (7)

An empty sum is zero. This formula is valid at X=1 and returns the initial
V15(1)=1. For dyadic X>=2, L(b)=M(X-1), L(a)=M(X/2), and hence

  V15(X)=E(X)+D15(X),
  X^2 D15(X)=-M(X-1)M(2X-1)^2+M(X/2)M(X-1)^2
    +sum_{n=X/2+1}^{X-1} mu(n)M(2n-1)^2
    +sum_{t=X}^{2X-1} chi(t)(L_2(t)^2-L_2(t)).        (8)

The final sum is nonnegative because M is integer-valued and z(z-1)>=0 for
every integer z. It is not a favorable negative error. The endpoint terms
retain their displayed source signs, and the interior sum is a signed
Möbius correlation against an odd-index energy. Estimating each of these
pieces independently by |M(u)|<=u gives O(X) after normalization. Any stronger
bound must exploit their joint cancellation or supply a genuinely stronger
estimate for these correlations. Their exact representation has cancellations
forced by positivity of the original square; that does not certify drift.

In this form the remaining drift is 2DeltaE+DeltaD15, with D15(1)=0, not merely
Delta of a masked lag. In the direct form it is (5) with
B=[chi(t)L_2(t)^2]_X and C=[mu(t)M(t)L_2(t)]_X, s=-1.
Equation (8) is a real reduction of that same-index correlation to endpoint
and half-scale terms; it is not a proof that it is small.

ID16: two macroscopic lags remain coupled

Let B=B_{3,3} and C23=[a_3(t)L_2(t)L_3(t)]_X. Then

  V16=H_2+B+2C23,  W16=E+H_2+B+2C23,
  |C23|<=sqrt(H_2 B), B<=H_3.                         (9)

The root-block formula (3) also applies here with M(t)L_d(t) replaced by
L_2(t)L_3(t). Its product increment is

  Delta_t(L_2(t)L_3(t))
    =1_{2 divides t+1}mu((t+1)/2)L_3(t)
       +1_{3 divides t+1}mu((t+1)/3)L_2(t+1).

These are two source/prefix correlations rather than a small bounded root
state. For dyadic X>=2 the exact lag part of its drift is

  DeltaW16=DeltaE+(E(X)-E(X/2))/2+DeltaB+2DeltaC23.    (10)

At X=1 use H_2(1)=0 and H_2(2)=E(1)/2, retaining W16(1)=1.
No E(X/3)/3 substitution is allowed on the dyadic domain.

Uniform comparison lemma and why naive absorption is circular

The elementary inequality (A+B)^2<=2A^2+2B^2 gives, for dyadic X>=4,

  E<=W9,W11,W13,W15<=3E(X)+E(X/2),
  E<=W10,W12,W14<=3E(X)+(3/2)E(X/2)+(3/8)E(X/4),
  E<=W16<=E(X)+(5/2)E(X/2)+(3/8)E(X/4).             (11)

All displayed comparisons are actual-mu unconditional statements, but their
right sides are unresolved target energies. The finitely many smaller scales
are retained through their actual initial/finite definitions. Neither (4)
nor (11) gives a contraction of child W by parent W: bounding positive child
energy by unknown child energy cannot be called absorption. Pointwise W>=E
also does not order DeltaW and DeltaE.

There is a precise conditional equivalence. For any one of these fixed
candidates, the required cofinal-q drift bound implies dyadic E(X)=O_epsilon
(X^epsilon) for every epsilon>0 by the telescoping lemma, with its W(1)
unchanged. Conversely, if those bounds for the original E are independently
known, (11) gives W(X)=O_epsilon(X^epsilon). Since W(X)>=0,
DeltaW<=W(2X), giving the required drift for every cofinally large q (indeed
every positive q) by choosing epsilon=1/q. Constants may depend on q and the
fixed candidate, never on X. This is an equivalence of theorem strength, not
a circular proof of either side and not an impossibility theorem for a new
signed-potential proof.

The original all-scale premise remains: one fixed program, some q0, and for
every integer q>=q0 a finite Cq independent of every dyadic X>=1 such that
DeltaW<=Cq ceil(X^(1/q)). Telescoping yields

  E(X)<=W(1)+2Cq (X^(1/q)-1)/(2^(1/q)-1).

A fixed positive power loss, including the O(X) trivial bounds above, cannot
close that cofinal requirement. No finite training or selector exposure can
replace it.

An algebraic obstruction to proof from norms and syntax alone

Replace the source formally by the constant sequence1, so its prefix is t.
This is not actual mu and supplies no counterexample to the desired theorem.
It is a symbolic test of which hypotheses a proposed absorption uses. All
bounded-source and square/ring inequalities still apply. IDs9,11,13,15 then
have U=t-floor(t/2)=t/2+O(1); IDs10,12,14 have U=t+floor(t/3)=4t/3+O(1);
ID16 has U=floor(t/2)+floor(t/3)=5t/6+O(1). Symbolic summation gives

  E_one(X)=(7/3)X-3/2+1/(6X),
  W_one(X)=(7/3)(1+c^2)X+O(1),

with c=1/2,4/3,5/6 respectively. Hence their signed drifts have a strictly
positive linear leading term and violate any q>1 subpower bound. Consequently
no argument using only these ring identities, positivity and |source|<=1
can prove cofinal cancellation. Such an argument must use actual Möbius
arithmetic beyond the retained algebra. This calculation is a symbolic
identity/asymptotic derivation, not a numerical probe or source table.

Selected mutation64: quotient commutator, pairing and polynomial correlation

The separately requested final program64 is exactly

  U64(t)=mu(t)*1_{floor(t/2)>=1}M(floor(t/4))
        -mu(max(1,floor(t/2)))*M(floor(t/2)).

At t=1 both prefixes are zero; no mu(0) is evaluated. Thus V64(1)=0 and
W64(1)=1. For t>=2 put n=floor(t/2), A_n=M(floor(n/2)), B_n=M(n).
For a dyadic block X=2Y, n ranges Y<=n<=2Y-1 and both t=2n,2n+1 occur.
Actual Möbius multiplicativity gives

  mu(2n)=-1_{n odd}mu(n), gcd(n,2n+1)=1,
  mu(n)mu(2n+1)=mu(n(2n+1)),
  U64(2n)=-mu(n)(B_n+1_{n odd}A_n),
  U64(2n+1)=mu(2n+1)A_n-mu(n)B_n.                  (12)

For even n, mu(2n)=0; the formula remains correct when n is nonsquarefree.
Expanding both squares and adding, the exact full paired square is

  D_n+2[1_{n odd}chi(n)-mu(n(2n+1))]A_n B_n,
  D_n=[1_{n odd}chi(n)+chi(2n+1)]A_n^2+2chi(n)B_n^2. (13)

Therefore V64(2Y) is (4Y^2)^-1 times the sum of (13) over the stated n-range.
The coprime product reduction is an actual arithmetic simplification. Its
remaining source is Möbius on the quadratic polynomial n(2n+1), weighted by
two dependent macroscopic prefixes. An unweighted polynomial-source estimate
would not by itself control this weighted correlation. For odd n the bracket
in (13) is nonnegative, but A_n B_n has no justified uniform sign. Even n
retains an additional signed bracket. Neither term may be dropped.

A second exact form makes the summation-by-parts limitation explicit. Put

  F_n=M(n)^2, p_n=mu(2n)+mu(2n+1), K_n=A_n p_n,
  a=Y, b=2Y-1.

Since 2mu(n)M(n)=F_n-F_{n-1}+chi(n), (13) can equally be written

  D_n-K_n(F_n-F_{n-1})-K_n chi(n).

Summation over a<=n<=b consequently gives

  (2Y)^2 V64(2Y)=sum D_n-K_b F_b+K_a F_{a-1}
      +sum_{n=a}^{b-1} F_n(K_{n+1}-K_n)-sum K_n chi(n).       (14)

All unqualified sums in (14) run a..b. Here p_n=M(2n+1)-M(2n-1) is a
two-increment source sum, not a smooth positive weight. Its next difference
is a four-source signed expression, while A_{n+1}-A_n contains half-scale
Möbius increments. Thus (14) exchanges the quadratic polynomial correlation
for endpoint and higher difference correlations; it does not eliminate it.
Endpoint costs may be O(X) after normalization under trivial prefix bounds.
Taking a first moment of U rather than its complete square would omit precisely
these mixed terms and supplies no bound for the required second moment.

For dyadic X>=4 the unmasked quarter-prefix identity is
H_4(X)=E(X/4)/4. Applying the norm inequality before any source cancellations
gives the unconditional comparison

  0<=V64(X)<=2H_4(X)+2H_2(X)
              =E(X/4)/2+E(X/2),
  E(X)<=W64(X)<=E(X)+E(X/2)+E(X/4)/2.                (15)

The gated source diagonals in (13) are not these unmasked exact lag energies;
(15) is a bound, not equality. It extends the conditional equivalence following
(11) to mutation64, with W64(1)=1. The exact remaining drift is DeltaE plus
the child-minus-parent normalized sum of (13), or equivalently (14). At child
2X its n-range is X..2X-1 and normalization is (2X)^-2; at parent X the range
is X/2..X-1 and normalization X^-2. Both dependent weighted correlations and
all diagonals/endpoints must therefore be estimated together.

What this analysis actually supplies

ID15's integration-by-parts reduction and mutation64's coprime pairing are
candidate-specific lemmas suitable for a proof queue. IDs9..14 and ID16 have
explicit root-block Abel representations, with no hidden endpoint deletion.
The common unconditional result is domination by nearby unresolved energies,
with the divisor3 boundary problem handled exactly. None is certified bounded
padding, a simple unmasked lag identity for the full V, or a signed innovation
estimate at cofinal q. No candidate preference is inferred from these lemmas.
The complete original target E, W(1), fresh child block, and cofinal quantifier
remain in every proposed proof obligation. Further progress requires an
actual uniform weighted correlation/absorption theorem; using the desired
Mertens energy bound as its premise would only recover the conditional
equivalence already proved here.
