PRIME ADJUNCTION, COMPENSATED FILTRATIONS, AND THE COMMON-CHANNEL FORCING

Scope and result
----------------
This is a new analytical memo. PRIME_BANK_HANDOFF.txt,
candidate10_ordered_prime_covariance_obligation.json,
PRIME_BANK_COVARIANCE_REVIEW_001.txt, PRIME_PAIR_FORCING_HANDOFF.txt and the
candidate-10 residue derivation are the inputs. No source table, arithmetic
probe, numerical response, test, hash, parameter search or frozen-file edit
was performed. All identities below concern the actual source mu.

There is no complete uniform moment proof here. The useful additions are:
  (i) complete prime-power transfer, including the non-small repeated-prime
      corrections to a tensor slide;
 (ii) an honest finite independent-valuation martingale, together with the
      exact integer-prefix remainder that prevents identifying it with the
      target energy;
(iii) a proved payment of every same-bank-prime sector involving a u or v
      weight at theta>=3/4, leaving only the unweighted common-channel square;
 (iv) its exact pole compensation and a strictly smaller-scale centered
      cofactor rule, suitable as one component of a closed coupled induction;
  (v) complete prime-index recurrences and positive inverse coefficients,
      which identify the remaining covariance forcing rather than erase it.

The local coefficient variance grows at most polylogarithmically across primes.
That is compatible with the cofinal objective. The obstruction is transferring
that coefficient statement to the whole prefix square, with all integer-floor,
rough-cofactor, pole and moving-cutoff terms retained. A fixed strict local
contraction is unavailable; its failure is not a disproof of cofinal growth.

1. Full multiplicative states and the unit
-----------------------------------------
Keep u=1/3, v=2/3, beta=9/2, a_b=mu*(n^(-b)), and B=a_u*a_v, where * is
Dirichlet convolution. B, a_u and a_v are multiplicative, with value one at
the unit. The coefficient of the product of normalized increments is
  p=beta*(B-a_u-a_v+delta).                         (1.1)
Thus p is not multiplicative. Let d be its COMPLETE same-prime prime-power
branch and a=p-d. Then a(1)=a(q^e)=0 for every prime q and e>=1. Removing
d is a proved critical-norm peel in the previous memo, not a deletion from
the actual candidate. R(x)=sum_(n<=x)a(n)-m_u*x^(2/3) remains the target.

For q prime put x=q^(-u), y=q^(-v), r_u=1-x and r_v=1-y. The complete local
power series, initially for |z|<1, are
  F_u(z)=(1-z)/(1-xz), F_v(z)=(1-z)/(1-yz),
  F_B(z)=(1-z)^2/[(1-xz)(1-yz)].                  (1.2)
All denominators are nonzero on this disk. In fact F_u,F_v have radius
greater than one, and F_B also does, so all local absolute sums used here
converge at z=1. For e>=1,
  a_u(q^e)=-r_u*x^(e-1), a_v(q^e)=-r_v*y^(e-1),
  B(q^e)=[r_u^2*x^(e-1)-r_v^2*y^(e-1)]/(x-y).   (1.3)
Here x>y, so the denominator is nonzero. Formula (1.3) follows by partial
fractions in (1.2), including the polynomial constant; that constant does
not affect e>=1. It retains all repeated primes and permits positive as
well as negative B(q^e). Treating B(q^e) as B(q)^e is incorrect.

If q does not divide k, the three-state column f=(B,a_u,a_v)^T obeys
  f(qk)=D_q*f(k), D_q=diag(x+y-2,x-1,y-1).        (1.4)
For k>1 this implies the COMPLETE candidate transfer
  a(qk)=-z_0(k)+q^(-u)z_u(k)+q^(-v)z_v(k),       (1.5)
where z_u=p+beta*a_v, z_v=p+beta*a_u, z_0=z_u+z_v on k>=2. All three
z channels are zero at k=1. Their naive multiplicative formulas must not
put a unit column back into a bank. For arbitrary k coprime to q, the p
transfer before that zero extension has the additional unit correction:
  p(qk)=-(r_u+r_v)p(k)-beta*[r_v*a_u(k)+r_u*a_v(k)]
                                      +beta*(r_u+r_v)*delta(k).
It gives p(q)=0 exactly. Equation (1.5) never uses k=1.

2. Tensor slide: the common channel and repeated-prime correction
----------------------------------------------------------------
Write t_j=f_j(q), and c_j=f_j(q^2)-t_j^2. Exact local algebra gives
  c_u=x-1, c_v=y-1, c_B=2x+2y-3-xy.              (2.1)
For q not dividing h or k, a single difference of adjunctions to the two
legs of f_i(h)f_j(k) gives the factor t_i(q)-t_j(q). For distinct primes
q,r, neither dividing h or k, the double difference gives
  [t_i(q)-t_j(q)]*[t_i(r)-t_j(r)].                (2.2)
This is an exact algebraic rule on its entire coprime domain. In particular
u/v gives q^(-u)-q^(-v); B/u gives y-1; B/v gives x-1. Only the u/v
pair has this small large-prime difference. Equal channels are annihilated
by a single slide, but the original symmetric tensor still has to be
recovered; annihilation of its image does not bound that original tensor.

For the SAME prime applied twice, the factor is instead
  f_i(q^2)-2t_i(q)t_j(q)+f_j(q^2)
    =[t_i(q)-t_j(q)]^2+c_i+c_j.                  (2.3)
For u/v it is (x-y)^2+x+y-2, tending to -2; for B/B it is
4x+4y-6-2xy, tending to -6. These are not suppressed repeated-prime
errors. The distinction between a coefficient h=k and a shared bank prime
q=r also matters: the latter can retain two DIFFERENT cofactors.

For any fixed positive definite matrix P, a uniform strict quadratic
contraction D_q^T P D_q<=rho P with rho<1 is impossible. Testing the
B-coordinate gives (x+y-2)^2<=rho, while the left side tends to four.
Even rho=1 fails for all sufficiently large q. This excludes that specific
local coercive-storage rule, not a compensated many-prime recurrence.

3. An actual martingale on an auxiliary product probability space
----------------------------------------------------------------
There is a legitimate probabilistic construction; it is important to say
which measure it uses. For a FINITE prime set P, let independent E_q have
  Pr(E_q=e)=(1-1/q)*q^(-e), e=0,1,2,... .
Use f_j(q^0)=1, including a fourth delta coordinate with delta(q^e)=0
for e>=1. The local expectations, all positive, are
  m_u(q)=(1-1/q)^2/(1-q^(-1-u)),
  m_v(q)=(1-1/q)^2/(1-q^(-1-v)),
  m_B(q)=(1-1/q)^3/[(1-q^(-1-u))(1-q^(-1-v))],
  m_delta(q)=1-1/q.                              (3.1)
Products M_j(P)=prod_(q in P)f_j(q^E_q)/m_j(q) are martingales as primes
are revealed. They are signed martingales for the first three coordinates.
Absolute local convergence from (1.2)-(1.3) justifies every expectation.

Let S_ij(q)=E[f_i(q^E_q)f_j(q^E_q)] and
C_ij(q)=S_ij(q)/(m_i(q)m_j(q))-1. This is a positive semidefinite covariance
matrix. For any positive semidefinite storage P0, conditioned on old values,
  E[M_new^T P0 M_new | old]-M_old^T P0 M_old
    =tr(P0*diag(M_old)*C(q)*diag(M_old))>=0.       (3.2)
There is no automatic negative martingale energy credit. A changing
predictable weight or a compensation term can give signed cross terms,
but those terms must themselves be established on the desired measure.

The leading local covariance is explicit. With coordinate order
(B,a_u,a_v,delta), set eta=(3,2,2,1)^T. Then as q tends to infinity,
  C(q)=eta*eta^T/q+O(q^(-1-u)+q^(-2))             (3.3)
entry by entry. For example
  S_uu=(1-1/q)[1+r_u^2/q/(1-x^2/q)],
  S_uv=(1-1/q)[1+r_u*r_v/q/(1-xy/q)],
  S_BB=(1-1/q)*[1+q^(-1)/(x-y)^2*
      {r_u^4/(1-x^2/q)-2r_u^2*r_v^2/(1-xy/q)
                                      +r_v^4/(1-y^2/q)}].       (3.4)
The apparent divided difference in (3.4) is simply the sum of squares of
(1.3); expansion can also be obtained directly from the bounded local
coefficients, without dividing an asymptotic error by x-y.
In particular S_BB/m_B^2=1+9/q+O(q^(-1-u)+q^(-2)). Per-prime factors
1+O(1/q) accumulate only a power of log of the prime cutoff, by an
elementary Chebyshev prime harmonic bound. They do NOT contradict the
cofinal epsilon goal, and no such contradiction is claimed.

The unit coordinate is essential even in this auxiliary calculation:
(1,-1,-1,1) dot eta=0. The first-prime local candidate cancellation at
the empty cofactor survives. After other primes are revealed, however,
the leading conditional projection for that FIXED-weight normalized
martingale combination is
  3*M_B(old)-2*M_u(old)-2*M_v(old)+M_delta(old),
not identically zero. Also the actual candidate weights become
beta*(prod m_B,-prod m_u,-prod m_v,prod m_delta), not constant weights
on the normalized martingale. Its changing weights and repeated-prime
overlap are separate pieces. A one-prime unit cancellation cannot erase
the common sector at a general cofactor.

4. Why this is not yet a martingale for actual integer-prefix energy
-------------------------------------------------------------------
For uniform integer n in [1,N] and a prescribed valuation vector
a=prod_(q in P)q^e_q, its EXACT probability is
  (1/N)*sum_(D divides Q)mu(D)*floor(N/(aD)),
  Q=prod_(q in P)q.                              (4.1)
It equals prod_(q in P)(1-1/q)/a plus a remainder bounded by 2^|P|/N
for this single vector, not uniformly small after summing all vectors.
Vectors a>N have probability zero on the actual domain and positive
probability in the product model. These are genuine omitted tails.

The full prefix energy has a further distinction. If F_j(n)=sum_(h<=n)f_j(h),
then exactly
  X^(-2)*sum_(X<=n<2X)F_i(n)F_j(n)
    =X^(-2)*sum_(h,k<2X)f_i(h)f_j(k)*W_X(h,k),
  W_X(h,k)=max(0,2X-max(X,h,k)).                  (4.2)
This is a pair-prefix kernel, not a diagonal coefficient second moment.
Under prime-adjunction, its weights become W_X(q^e*h,q^f*k), with all
e,f sectors. They do not become a product of geometric weights times a
common unchanged kernel. Domain intersections and unequal e,f remain.

For a precise compensated version, define f_j^P(n) by multiplying the
actual local factors at P and using factor ONE at primes outside P.
This agrees with actual f_j(n) for n<=N if P contains every prime<=N,
but for fixed P it is a different whole source. Let g_j^P=f_j^P*mu;
g_j^P is P-smooth, with local symbol (1-z)F_j(z). Put
  lambda_j(P)=sum_(a P-smooth)g_j^P(a)/a=prod_(q in P)m_j(q).
All these series converge absolutely for finite P. Exact divisor inversion
and its complete omitted tail give
  sum_(n<=N)f_j^P(n)=N*lambda_j(P)+E_j(P,N),
  E_j(P,N)=sum_(a<=N,P-smooth)g_j^P(a)[floor(N/a)-N/a]
                  -N*sum_(a>N,P-smooth)g_j^P(a)/a.               (4.3)
Both terms are mandatory. The crude absolute local masses are at most
four for the u/v g symbols and eight for the B g symbol, hence the first
remainder has only an exponential-in-|P| bound by this method. It is not
critical padding. The second term is not zero just because the first
geometric model has a known mean.
If the fourth coordinate is used here, its processed version is the
P-rough indicator 1_(gcd(n,Q)=1), not delta(n) on the whole infinite
source. It agrees with delta only on [1,N] when all primes<=N are in P.
This is another reason not to identify a finite product law with the
actual full unit without its rough complement.

There is an unconditional macroscopic example INSIDE the actual four-state
system, requiring neither RH nor PNT. Set P_X={q prime:q<=2X}. On the
whole block X<=n<2X the processed delta prefix is exactly one, so
  E_delta(P_X,n)=1-n*lambda_delta(P_X),
  lambda_delta(P_X)=prod_(q<=2X)(1-1/q)>0.        (4.4)
The elementary Chebyshev bound makes this remainder provably large.
For t>=4, split the primes at sqrt(t) and use Theta_prime(t)<=C_psi*t
to obtain pi(t)<=(2*C_psi+1)*t/log t. Partial summation then gives
sum_(q<=T)1/q<=D+(2*C_psi+1)*log log T for T>=4, for a fixed finite
D (the finite head can be bounded by integers). Since
log(1-1/q)>=-2/q, writing C_pi=2*C_psi+1 gives
  lambda_delta(P_X)>=exp(-2D)*(log(2X))^(-2*C_pi).
Eventually X*lambda_delta(P_X)>=2. Therefore
  X^(-2)*sum_(X<=n<2X)|E_delta(P_X,n)|^2
    >=(1/4)*exp(-4D)*X*(log(2X))^(-4*C_pi).      (4.5)
Indeed |1-n*lambda|>=n*lambda/2 and sum n^2>=X^3 on the block.
Thus even the genuine unit coordinate has an unbounded positive-power
filtration correction. It is not critical padding. Formula (4.5) concerns
the correction, not the unit's actual energy, which is 1/X. A signed
combination of the four coordinates may cancel these artificial model
means, but such cancellation must be established; the auxiliary independent
martingale does not supply it. This is a sharper obstruction than merely
observing the exponential crude bound in (4.3).

Each lambda_j(P) is positive. Finite-P coefficient second-moment control
can coexist with a prefix mean lambda_j(P)*N. This extends the logic of
the frozen partial-prime diagnostic without asserting that finite-P laws
determine actual mu. Complete all-prime laws DO determine the actual
coefficients; the missing step is a reusable uniform estimate for (4.3)
and its pair-kernel version, not extra normalization at finitely many primes.

Coefficient variance does not bound this mean at the required scale:
Cauchy gives |F_j(N)|^2<=N*sum_(n<=N)|f_j(n)|^2, losing one power of N.
An ordinary Doob martingale of f_j(U), U uniform on [1,N], starts at the
unknown mean F_j(N)/N. Its variance decomposition does not estimate that
initial mean. Using two independent U,V still leaves that same squared
mean. These are exact reasons that a coefficient martingale is not a
proof of the prefix-square target.

5. Complete finite-prime recurrences and their forcing
------------------------------------------------------
There are two useful, different filtrations.

(A) The genuinely smooth-supported prefix
  F_j(x;y)=sum_(n<=x,Pplus(n)<=y)f_j(n)
obeys, when the next prime is q,
  F_j(x;q)=sum_(e>=0,q^e<=x)f_j(q^e)*F_j(x/q^e;q-),              (5.1)
where q- means all earlier primes, and the base is F_j(x;1)=1 for x>=1,
zero below one. Every e>=1 uses a strictly smaller source argument; e=0
uses a smaller prime index. Thus it is an exact well-founded recurrence.
It retains q powers, unit and every smooth complement. Squaring (5.1)
requires all e,f cross terms and the kernels in (4.2). The triangle
recurrence is not a signed uniform estimate.

(B) For the prime-processed source of section 4, let g_j(q^e) have symbol
(1-z)F_j(z), including g_j(1)=1. Then
  F_j^(P+q)(x)=sum_(e>=0,q^e<=x)g_j(q^e)*F_j^P(x/q^e).            (5.2)
Writing E_j(P,x)=F_j^P(x)-lambda_j(P)*x for x>=0, with the prefix zero
below one, gives an ALL-e recurrence
  E_j(P+q,x)=sum_(e>=0)g_j(q^e)*E_j(P,x/q^e).      (5.3)
It is absolutely convergent for finite P. The terms q^e>x equal the
explicit below-one compensator -lambda_j(P)*x/q^e. Omitting them changes
the mean. The empty-P head is floor(x)-x on x>=1 and -x below one.

For a finite word of primes, equations (5.2)-(5.3) are exact recurrences
with smaller prime index and smaller arguments. They are a possible proof
grammar, not a proved critical norm recurrence. At the critical dilation
weight, the absolute factors of g are 1+O(q^(-1/2)), whose prime product
is not polylogarithmic. Signed cross terms are required. Moreover the
lambda_j(P)*x centering is not the actual real-pole centering as P grows.
The large deterministic change of centering must be kept; proving just
small E_j(P,x) uniformly in P is not a valid route to the desired state.

More explicitly, for a vector prefix and a coercive quadratic storage,
write (5.1) as F_new=F_old+H with H=sum_(e>=1)D_q(e)F_old(. /q^e).
The exact change of energy is
  2*<F_old,H>_P0+||H||_P0^2.                    (5.4)
Pole, unit and below-one corrections add their full analogous cross terms.
A useful proposed recurrence must bound THIS signed forcing jointly, at
the actual kernel intersections, while putting all companion states into
the same smaller-argument induction. Replacing the cross term by zero
through an independence assertion is not allowed by (4.1)-(4.2).

6. Prime-removal inverse is positive, but its absolute critical cost diverges
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The exact local inverse symbols are
  1/F_u(z)=(1-xz)/(1-z), 1/F_v(z)=(1-yz)/(1-z),
  1/F_B(z)=(1-xz)(1-yz)/(1-z)^2.
The inverse coefficients at e>=1 are respectively
  r_u, r_v, e*r_u*r_v+1-xy,                      (6.1)
all positive. Thus the q-free coefficient prefix is exactly the original
prefix plus the full positive-inverse sum of smaller arguments. This is
a valid removal identity; it supplies no arbitrary negative sign.

On the truncated weighted L2 norm integral_1^T |F(t)|^2*t^(-2sigma-1)dt,
with F below one zero, dilation by q^e has norm at most q^(-e*sigma).
The positive inverse's absolute bound is therefore its symbol at q^(-sigma).
Multiplying over all primes yields factors 1+c*q^(-sigma)+... , c>0.
For sigma<=1 their product diverges. In particular it is not a finite
critical recovery constant at sigma=1/2. Positivity also shows this is
not just an unfortunate triangle estimate for the unrestricted operator:
long plateaux after the weighted logarithmic change of variable approach
the sum of any finite collection of positive dilation weights. The
supremum of those sums is infinite. This excludes a universal bounded
positive-inverse operator on that norm, not a source-specific constrained
subspace using additional cancellation.

7. A proved diagonal payment, and what it does not pay
------------------------------------------------------
The true coefficient diagonal h=k in (4.2) is harmless. Since |a_b|<=1,
|B|<=d_2, and |a|<=beta*d_2, while d_2(n)^2<=d_4(n), divisor counting gives
  sum_(n<=T)d_4(n)<=T*(1+log T)^3.
Consequently the diagonal contribution for the target a is at most
  2*beta^2*(1+log(2X))^3.                        (7.1)
For B omit beta^2; for a_u/a_v it is at most two. Every fixed bilinear
coefficient diagonal follows by Cauchy with those constants. This is
unconditional cofinal padding. It does not pay h!=k, even if h,k have the
same largest prime, or the shared-prime bank sector q=r with k!=l.

8. A complete payment of the weighted shared-bank-prime sectors
----------------------------------------------------------------
For b=0,u,v put Z_b(t)=sum_(2<=k<=t)z_b(k), and keep Z_b zero below two.
The bank is L_b(n)=sum_(n^theta<q<=n/2,q prime)q^(-b)*Z_b(n/q).
Its EXACT same-prime bilinear sector is
  D_bc(n)=sum_(n^theta<q<=n/2,q prime)
                        q^(-b-c)*Z_b(n/q)*Z_c(n/q).             (8.1)
All distinct-prime terms remain in L_b*L_c-D_bc. This is a sector split,
not a deletion. With C_0=4*beta,C_u=C_v=2*beta, the proved coefficient
envelopes imply |Z_b(t)|<=C_b*t*(1+log t), so for n>=1
  |D_bc(n)|<=C_b*C_c*(2+b+c)/(1+b+c)
                  *n^(2-theta*(1+b+c))*(1+log n)^2.             (8.2)
The integer sum bound used is sum_(m>Y)m^(-2-b-c)
 <=(2+b+c)/(1+b+c)*Y^(-1-b-c), valid for every real Y>=1.
Empty banks obey the same bound. No prime asymptotic is needed.

After dyadic normalization, the power exponent in (8.2) is
  1-theta*(1+b+c).
For theta>=3/4 these are
  (0,0): 1-theta,       (0,u): 1-4theta/3<=0,
  (0,v),(u,u): 1-5theta/3<0,
  (u,v): 1-2theta<0,    (v,v): 1-7theta/3<0.      (8.3)
Hence every shared-prime sector except D_00 is rigorously paid by a
polylog block envelope; those with a strictly negative exponent also
have finite integral of |D_bc(t)|/t^2 by (8.2). At theta=3/4 D_0u has a
log-squared endpoint envelope, not a claimed finite critical integral.
This controls their full signed values, including all cofactor overlaps.
It does not control the distinct-prime sector of any of these products.
The leading shared-prime common channel D_00 is nonnegative and remains.

9. The common-channel pole must be centered before smaller-scale induction
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Write kappa_u=2/3,kappa_v=1/3 and
  c_u=1/[kappa_u*zeta(kappa_u)],
  c_v=1/[kappa_v*zeta(kappa_v)].
For real t>=1 put S(t)=sum_(n<=t)p(n), A_b(t)=sum_(n<=t)a_b(n),
J=S-m_u*t^kappa_u-m_v*t^kappa_v, and
M_b=A_b-c_b*t^(1-b). These definitions retain the whole unit values.
Then the EXACT cofactor centers are
  C_u(t)=m_u*t^kappa_u+(m_v+beta*c_v)*t^kappa_v-beta,
  C_v(t)=(m_u+beta*c_u)*t^kappa_u+m_v*t^kappa_v-beta,
  C_0=C_u+C_v,
  Z_u-C_u=J+beta*M_v, Z_v-C_v=J+beta*M_u,
  Z_0-C_0=2J+beta*(M_u+M_v).                    (9.1)
For t<1 the prefixes are zero; these center formulas are not silently
continued as zero in any convolution identity.

Let Z=zeta(2/3)<0 and W=zeta(4/3)>0. The leading common coefficient is
  gamma_0=2m_u+beta*c_u
    =beta/kappa_u*[2W/Z^2-1/Z]>0.               (9.2)
Thus uncentered Z_0 has a nonzero x^(2/3) pole channel. A cofinal
square-root envelope for its uncentered prefix would not even have the
correct pole compensation. The complete deterministic column from its
leading pole has the positive shared-prime square
  sum_(n^theta<q<=n/2)gamma_0^2*floor(n/q)^(4/3).
For each fixed theta<1 this is at least
  c_theta*n^((4-theta)/3)/log n                 (9.3)
eventually, with c_theta>0. One elementary proof uses the known two-sided
Chebyshev bounds to choose a fixed A so that (Y,AY] contains at least
c_A*Y/log Y primes, Y=n^theta; for large n, AY<n/2 and floor(n/q)>=n/(2AY).
Therefore its normalized block order is at least
X^((1-theta)/3)/log X. This is a lower bound for this deterministic
sector, not for the full signed target. It MUST cancel jointly with
other pole/smooth sectors; all their cross terms remain. The unit and
the x^(1/3) channels have their own paid envelopes, but cannot erase
the leading sector by an unproved independence argument.

There is nevertheless a useful STRICTLY SMALLER-SCALE proof rule for the
CENTERED shared-prime part. Set W_0(t)=Z_0(t)-C_0(t) and
W_0#(t)=W_0(floor(t)), C_0#(t)=C_0(floor(t)) on the bank domain t>=2.
Then Z_0(t)=W_0#(t)+C_0#(t) exactly; real versus integer pole arguments
are never silently interchanged. Suppose a closed
induction has established
  sum_(1<=m<=T)|W_0(m)|^2<=A_epsilon*T^(2+epsilon).                (9.4)
For a dyadic X, its centered same-prime square obeys
  X^(-2)*sum_(X<=n<2X)sum_(n^theta<q<=n/2)|W_0#(n/q)|^2
    <=2^(2+epsilon)*A_epsilon*(1+1/epsilon)
                                      *X^((1-theta)*epsilon).  (9.5)
For each q, each integer cofactor cell has at most q sample points,
so the inner n sum is at most q times (9.4) at T=2X/q. Summing
q>X^theta and using sum_(m>Y)m^(-1-epsilon)
 <=(1+1/epsilon)*Y^(-epsilon) proves (9.5). All needed induction
arguments are at most 2X^(1-theta), strictly below X once X is larger
than the fixed head 2^(1/theta). The actual support is smaller still.
A cumulative polylog version of (9.4) yields a polylog bound by the
same proof and sum_(q<=X)1/q<=1+log X.

This is a valid primitive rule: centered cofactor energy transports to
the centered common-prime square with a genuinely smaller horizon.
It is NOT a supplied auxiliary theorem. By (9.1) its state is the coupled
triple (J,M_u,M_v). All three must enter the SAME well-founded argument;
independently assuming cofinal bounds for M_u or M_v would import another
RH-strength source estimate. The exact expansion
  D_00=D_00(centered)+2*D_00(centered,pole)+D_00(pole)
keeps both mixed and deterministic terms. Equation (9.5) pays only its
centered square after a valid induction step; it does not authorize
dropping the other two terms or the distinct-prime covariance.

10. What a complete auxiliary proof would still have to do
----------------------------------------------------------
The finite-prime recurrence is computationally well founded, but an
energy induction requires a forcing estimate in addition. The remaining
inputs now separate into concrete pieces:
  * common-channel distinct-bank-prime correlations, with BOTH primes
    coprime to BOTH cofactors on the actual overlap kernel;
  * high-smooth/common-bank correlations with q>Pplus(h), without an
    assumption that h and k are coprime;

The weighted shared-prime sectors and coefficient diagonal are paid.
The u/v off-diagonal tensor slide of section 2 is a source-specific
small-factor identity, but it does not span or recover the B/common

incur the reciprocal power cost; placing an annihilated sector into a

A valid coupled energy certificate can use a coercive storage matrix,

has the needed sign. The positive martingale variance (3.2), analytic

It would be tautological to name the sum of these unpaid sectors the

weighted/diagonal sectors, exact pole split, and a smaller-scale centered
variance rule. A new signed source correlation must close the common
