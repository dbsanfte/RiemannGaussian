A GENERIC CENTERED HYPERBOLA RULE AND TWO FINITE-FACTOR PROOF ROUTES

Scope and disposition
---------------------
This is an analytically proved reusable inequality primitive for subsequent
machine synthesis of splits, finite factors and joined source bounds.
Only this new memo is written. No old/frozen source or result is changed;
no numerical source evaluation, experiment, test, rescore or hash occurs.
The generic theorem and both fully centered finite-factor routes below
are PROVED, with explicit constants. Neither route discards complementary
source terms or proves the full current core/RH target. Those remain OPEN.

All convolutions are Dirichlet convolutions. One(n)=1 and delta is their
unit. All logarithms in this memo are natural. Real prefix arguments use
the unit-cell step convention: P_h(t)=sum_(d<=floor t)h(d), zero for t<1.
The coefficient mass is M_h(t)=sum_(d<=floor t)|h(d)|. Parameters are fixed
for each identity; in a parent/child source reduction use the SAME cutoff
and moment coefficients throughout the shared horizon.

1. The actual finite inverse error, including its unit and head
-------------------------------------------------------------
Fix integer U>=1, A(d)=mu(d)1_(d<=U),
 c=sum_(d<=U)mu(d)/d,
 E=delta-One*A+c One.
The complete actual identity mu*One=delta gives at integer n>=1
 P_E(n)=1-sum_(d<=U)mu(d)floor(n/d)+cn
       =1+sum_(d<=U)mu(d){n/d}.                        (1.1)
At n<=U, the coefficient E(n)=c, since all its divisor factors are
present. In particular E(1)=c; its unit is not silently replaced by1.
Using(1.1) at nU yields |c|<=1+1/U<=2. At every integer n,
 |P_E(n)|<=1+U<=2U; the same holds at all real t>=1.

The proposed complete mass bound is also valid:
                      M_E(t)<=3t(1+log U).             (1.2)
To see this without a spurious extra unit, write A=delta+Aproper.
Then delta-One*A=delta-One-One*Aproper, whose first coefficient is0.
For t>=1 its mass is at most
 floor t-1+sum_(d=2)^U |mu(d)|floor(t/d)
 <=t H_U-1, H_U=sum_(d=1)^U1/d.
Adding c One costs at most2t. Since H_U>=1,
 M_E(t)<=t(H_U+2)<=3t H_U<=3t(1+log U).
This proof covers U1, where c1 and E=delta exactly. For t<1 the mass
and prefix vanish. No partial-prime counterfeit replaces actual mu.

2. Conditional moments and the COMPLETE infinite tail
-----------------------------------------------------
Let 0<theta<1 rational. A bounded prefix sequence has the convergent
ordinary conditional moment
 m_theta(h)=sum_(d>=1)h(d)d^(-theta)
           =theta int_1^infinity P_h(t)t^(-theta-1)dt.  (2.1)
The equality follows by finite summation by parts and a vanishing endpoint.
The integral is absolutely convergent as an integral of the bounded
prefix, even when the coefficient series is not absolutely convergent.
At every integer D>=1 its exact tail is
 sum_(d>D)h(d)d^(-theta)
 =-P_h(D)D^(-theta)+theta int_D^infinity P_h(t)t^(-theta-1)dt.
Consequently a prefix bound |P_h|<=C gives
                 |tail_D|<=2C D^(-theta).              (2.2)
The entire d>D tail, including d>n for an evaluation at n, remains here.

For E, split the moment at U. Its head is c sum_(d<=U)d^(-theta),
bounded by2 U^(1-theta)/(1-theta), and(2.2) bounds its tail by
4 U^(1-theta). Therefore
 |m_theta(E)|<=[2/(1-theta)+4]U^(1-theta).              (2.3)
This is a rigorous tail/head bound for all U>=1; no numerical zeta is
required by the bound rule. The exact symbolic value can be checked as
 m_theta(E)=1-zeta(theta)[A(theta)-c],
 A(s)=sum_(d<=U)mu(d)d^(-s).
Initially this identity follows by absolutely convergent convolution
in Re(s)>1. The bounded-prefix Abel integral is holomorphic in Re(s)>0;
the real pole at1 on the displayed right side is removable because
A(1)=c. Analytic agreement then establishes the expression at theta.
This is not an assertion of ordinary convergence for an unrelated
full-source canceled bracket at2/3. U1 gives m_theta(E)=1 as required.

3. A generic centered hyperbola theorem, with a free integer split
----------------------------------------------------------------
Assume an arbitrary real sequence h satisfies, for every real t>=1,
 |P_h(t)|<=C, M_h(t)<=L t, C>=1,L>=1.                  (3.1)
Define
 K(n)=n^(theta-1), Jtheta(n)=n^theta-(n-1)^theta,
 Ztheta(y)=sum_(a<=floor y)a^(theta-1), zero for y<1,
 T_h(n)=sum_(k<=n)(K*h)(k)-(m_theta(h)/theta)n^theta.
Because sum_(k<=n)Jtheta(k)=n^theta EXACTLY, T_h is the full prefix of
 K*h-(m_theta(h)/theta)Jtheta, with the actual Jtheta(1)=1 unit.

For any real y>=1, decreasing-kernel integral comparisons give
               0<=y^theta/theta-Ztheta(y)<=1/theta.    (3.2)
Indeed Ztheta(y)<=floor(y)^theta/theta<=y^theta/theta,
while Ztheta(y)>=((floor(y)+1)^theta-1)/theta.
This includes all noninteger quotient/floor cases.

For ANY integer D in[1,n], the exact complementary hyperbola identity is
 sum_(k<=n)(K*h)(k)
 =sum_(d<=D)h(d)Ztheta(n/d)
  +sum_(a<=floor(n/(D+1)))a^(theta-1)[P_h(n/a)-P_h(D)]. (3.3)
Every lattice pair ad<=n occurs once: d<=D in the first row, d>D in
the second. The second row is empty if D=n. There is no dropped overlap
or fictitious final cell. Subtract the complete moment, use(3.2) on the
first row and(2.2) on its infinite moment complement. The result is
 |T_h(n)|<=M_h(D)/theta+(4C/theta)(n/D)^theta
          <=L D/theta+(4C/theta)(n/D)^theta.            (3.4)
For the complementary finite row, |P_h(n/a)-P_h(D)|<=2C and
Ztheta(n/(D+1))<=theta^(-1)(n/D)^theta. Its bound contributes2C;
the full conditional-moment tail contributes the other2C. Both terms
are present; eliminating the infinite tail is unsound.

The detector can use(3.4) as TWO explicit error monomials and synthesize
an admissible split. Balancing the powers D and C(n/D)^theta yields
 a=1/(1+theta), r=theta/(1+theta), T=C^a n^r.
If n>=C, then1<=T<=n and the integer D=ceil T is admissible, satisfies
T<=D<=2T, and gives
                    |T_h(n)|<=6L T/theta.              (3.5)
No constant has to be optimized to prove this rule. If powers in C or
the proposed split are rational, integer inequalities can be verified
after raising their exact rational denominators. Floating rounding at
an integer boundary is not a certificate. The free-split form(3.4)
also permits the machine to generate alternative cutoffs and compare
their bound exponents; the stated balancing is an algebraic consequence
of the errors, not a nominated source-specific candidate.

4. The all-n head case and one rational uniform constant
-------------------------------------------------------
For n<C, do not use a split larger than n. First, since L>=1,
 |P_h(t)|<=L min(C,t).
Formula(2.1) then gives the useful general moment bound
 |m_theta(h)|<=L C^(1-theta)/(1-theta).                (4.1)
More explicitly the two integrals on[1,C] and[C,infinity) sum to
L C^(1-theta)/(1-theta)-theta L/(1-theta), a still smaller quantity.
The raw complete convolution satisfies
 |sum_(k<=n)(K*h)(k)|
 <=sum_(a<=n)a^(theta-1)M_h(n/a)
 <=L n sum_(a<=n)a^(theta-2)
 <=L[(2-theta)/(1-theta)]n.                            (4.2)
The final estimate uses the full convergent positive series majorant.

When n<C, n<=C^a n^r, and
 C^(1-theta)n^theta<=C^a n^r,
since their second ratio is(n/C)^(theta^2/(1+theta))<=1.
Thus (4.1)-(4.2) give |T_h(n)| at most
 L[(2-theta)/(1-theta)+1/(theta(1-theta))]C^a n^r.
Combining with(3.5), the following SIMPLE rational constant works in
EVERY case, including n1 and C1:

 |T_h(n)|<=Ctheta L C^a n^r,
            Ctheta=6/[theta(1-theta)].                 (4.3)

The small-n coefficient is at most2/[theta(1-theta)], while6/theta
is at most Ctheta. This proves the proposed generic rule with all
heads, floors and tails retained. C,L need not be integers, but must
be certified upper bounds satisfying(3.1), not fitted observations.

For E, C=2U and L=3(1+log U). Since2^a<=2, a convenient specialization is
 |T_E(n)|<=[36/(theta(1-theta))](1+log U)U^a n^r,
                         every integer n>=1.          (4.4)
No separate n<U exception is needed after this proof; that case was
explicitly covered in Section4. The sharper moment(2.3) is still useful
for the second proof route below.

5. Route I: apply the theorem to the full finite-factor error
------------------------------------------------------------
Let B(d)=mu(d)d^(-2/3)1_(d<=V), V>=1 integer, and let
 h0=A^p*B^q, h=h0*E, p,q nonnegative integers (currently at most2).
Both A and B, and therefore h0, have finite support. From(1.1),
 |P_h(t)|<=2U ||h0||_1
 <=2*3^q U^(p+1)V^(q/3)=C0 W,
 C0=2*3^q, W=U^(p+1)V^(q/3).                          (5.1)
Here sum_(d<=V)d^(-2/3)<=3 V^(1/3). The formula holds for all real t,
because prefixes below1 vanish. Similarly(1.2) gives
 M_h(t)<=3(1+log U)t sum_d |h0(d)|/d
 <=3(5/2)^q(1+log U)^(p+1)t=L t,                     (5.2)
using sum_(d<=U)1/d<=1+log U and sum_(d>=1)d^(-5/3)<=5/2.
These constants satisfy the generic C,L>=1 hypotheses.

The complete conditional moment factors EXACTLY:
 m_theta(h)=m_theta(E)m_theta(h0),
 m_theta(h0)=A(theta)^p B(theta)^q.                    (5.3)
Finite support of h0 justifies passage to the limit in each of its
shifted conditional E sums; absolute convergence of E itself is not
claimed. Applying(4.3) DIRECTLY to h therefore proves the fully centered
prefix of K*A^p*B^q*E, with main coefficient m_theta(h)/theta, bounded by
 [36(15/2)^q/(theta(1-theta))]
 (1+log U)^(p+1)U^((p+1)a)V^(q a/3)n^r.              (5.4)
The conservative simplification C0^a<=C0 makes its numerical constant
rational; leaving C0^a symbolically is also valid if certified.
This route automatically includes every centering commutator. It is
one reusable bound rule, not an assertion about an uncentered component.

6. Route II: centered E convolution PLUS the complete J commutator
-----------------------------------------------------------------
The following initially tempting equality is FALSE in general:
 h0*[K*E-(m_theta(E)/theta)Jtheta]
  =K*h0*E-[m_theta(E)m_theta(h0)/theta]Jtheta.
The correct complete identity is
 K*h0*E-[m_theta(E)m_theta(h0)/theta]Jtheta
 =h0*[K*E-(m_theta(E)/theta)Jtheta]
 +(m_theta(E)/theta)[h0*Jtheta-m_theta(h0)Jtheta].      (6.1)
For a simple counterexample take U1, so E=delta and m_theta(E)=1,
and finite h0(2)=1 with every other coefficient0. At n1 the prefix of the
bracket in the second row is -2^(-theta), not0. Erasing it changes the
actual coefficient/unit and is not a harmless main-term convention.

There is, however, an explicit bound that PRESERVES the exponent. For
ANY finite signed h0 and integer n>=1, the commutator prefix is exactly
 C_h0(n)=sum_(d<=n)h0(d)floor(n/d)^theta
                                      -n^theta m_theta(h0)
 =sum_(d<=n)h0(d)[floor(n/d)^theta-(n/d)^theta]
                       -n^theta sum_(d>n)h0(d)d^(-theta). (6.2)
Its full d>n finite-support tail is essential. Concavity gives
 |floor y^theta-y^theta|<=1 for y>=1. Because0<r<theta,
 1<=(n/d)^r for d<=n,
 (n/d)^theta<=(n/d)^r for d>n.
Therefore, writing M_h0(r)=sum_ALL_d|h0(d)|d^(-r),
                   |C_h0(n)|<=n^r M_h0(r).            (6.3)
The simpler all-n bound |C_h0(n)|<=||h0||_1 is also valid, but(6.3)
is better aligned with the generated split exponent.

Convolving the actual centered E bound(4.4) with finite h0 yields a
prefix at most [36/(theta(1-theta))](1+log U)U^a n^r M_h0(r).
By(2.3) and U>=1, U^(1-theta)<=U^a. Adding(6.3) to this bound via
(6.1), and using(2/(1-theta)+4)/theta<=6/[theta(1-theta)], proves
 the FULL centered K*h0*E prefix
 <=[42/(theta(1-theta))](1+log U)U^a n^r M_h0(r).      (6.4)
The commutator has been paid, not identified with0. The moment and all
tails are complete, including finite factors larger than n.

7. Finite-factor weighted masses: every threshold case
-----------------------------------------------------
For h0=A^p*B^q, positive weighted convolution gives
 M_h0(r)<=M_A(r)^p M_B(r)^q,
 M_A(r)<=U^(1-r)/(1-r)=(1+theta)U^a.
Put t=2/3+r. The following exact case distinction is mandatory:
 t>1: M_B(r)<=1+1/(t-1);
 t=1: M_B(r)<=1+log V;
 t<1: M_B(r)<=V^(1-t)/(1-t).                          (7.1)
Each inequality follows from the corresponding decreasing-series
integral comparison and is valid at V1. Here t>1 is equivalent to
theta>1/2, t1 to theta1/2, and t<1 to theta<1/2. The constants are
rational functions of the fixed rational parameter theta; no numerical
zeta is needed. If q0, the B factor is1 and no threshold cost occurs.

Combining(6.4)-(7.1), the second fully centered route is
 [42/(theta(1-theta))](1+theta)^p(1+log U)
                  U^((p+1)a) M_B(r)^q n^r.            (7.2)
For theta>1/2 this has no V power; at the boundary it carries the stated
log V, and below the boundary it has the explicit V power. Thus Route II
can improve the log/finite-factor cost relative to Route I without
discarding the J commutator. Neither is universally the better constant;
the detector can compare BOTH certified bounds on the SAME completely
centered object, rather than compare differently centered presentations.

For the core kernel theta2/3, a3/5,r2/5, the B mass in(7.1) is at most16.
For p2,q0, Route II specializes to the simple full bound
                      525(1+log U)U^(9/5)n^(2/5).      (7.3)
Route I instead has the same U power and a log-cubed cost. The exponent
U9/5 is therefore valid after full centering; it was not justified by
an uncorrected convolution identity. Its source moment is the exact
m_theta(E)A(theta)^2, not a fitted replacement.

8. Cofinal accounting when the detector grows cutoffs
----------------------------------------------------
A bound of the form B(U,V)n^r gives complete dyadic prefix energy at most
 2 B(U,V)^2 X^(2r-1), since r in(0,1/2).
This follows by bounding every n in[X,2X) by(2X)^r and using2^(2r)<=2.
It is an upper certificate, not an actual asymptotic. If a shared horizon
N is comparable to X, U<=N^alpha,V<=N^gamma, the rational exponents and
all logarithmic factors must be propagated AFTER squaring.

For Route II with theta>1/2, the energy power in X is
 [theta-1+2alpha(p+1)]/(1+theta).
It is cofinally paid if alpha<=(1-theta)/[2(p+1)]; equality pays the
remaining explicit log-squared cost. At theta2/3,p2 this requires
alpha<=1/18. A fixed U gives a decaying power, but allowing U to grow
can destroy that gain. No certificate may omit the U9/5 cost.
At theta1/2 the same power condition applies, with additional log(V)^(2q).
For theta<1/2 Route II's energy exponent is
 [theta-1+2alpha(p+1)+(2gamma q/3)(1-2theta)]/(1+theta).
Route I's energy exponent is
 [theta-1+2alpha(p+1)+(2gamma q/3)]/(1+theta),
and its log U degree is2(p+1). These formulas distinguish coefficient
prefix errors from energy errors. Exact N/X factors and constants must
be retained when compiling an actual certificate; these power tests
alone are not numerical estimates for a small scale.

9. What this primitive does NOT erase from the full source
--------------------------------------------------------
The source-specific finite inverse identity is exact, and the added
compensation gives R=E-c One. Any substitution into an inverse polynomial
must retain the complete One terms, every binomial companion and every
source/transport cross. One terms can contain macroscopic real-pole
contributions that cancel only after the full source join. Paid centered
E components are not permission to discard those complements or their
own main coefficients. The present theorem applies to ONE bounded-prefix
error with a FINITE convolution factor; it does not automatically cover
E^2 or an arbitrary additional infinite factor by repeating the rule.
Such an extension needs separately certified prefix and mass hypotheses.

All centering coefficients in a same-N parent/child decomposition must
be carried exactly. At different N the individual short-factor moments
and components can change, even though their joined source is global.
A component recurrence therefore needs uniform parameters or a fresh
same-horizon identity; it cannot borrow a mismatched smaller-horizon
center. The apparent gain here is a valid payment for particular complete
error components, not a closing bound on their remaining signed join.

The theorem is an analyst-supplied primitive. The detector can generate the
inverse expansions, free split/error monomials, admissible integer split
certificates and the two centering routes; compare exact exponent/log/
constant costs; pay qualifying components; and feed the FULL residual
arithmetic leaves onward. This is genuine proof-route synthesis once
implemented, while the unpaid joined all-scale bound remains an OPEN
premise. Finite numerical validation does not establish it. No RH claim,
source experiment or test is made; only this new review file was written.
