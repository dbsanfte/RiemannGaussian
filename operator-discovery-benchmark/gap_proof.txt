GAP COUPLING: EXACT LAWS, SHARP CONVERGENCE, AND ORDINARY ZETA ROOTS
2026-10-07. Written mathematical proofs; not theorem-prover certified.

This is a verified compound extension of the nonlinear logarithmic grammar,
not an eleventh primitive family or an established historically new operator.
The underlying additive prime-pair correlation is known. The experiments
suggested useful parameter choices; the universal statements below require

1. DEFINITIONS AND UNIVERSAL FORMAL LAWS

Work over a characteristic-zero coefficient field, with Dirichlet product D,
unit delta_1, augmented inputs h(1)=0, and normalized inputs f(1)=1. For an
integer r>=1, exclude a=b and put

  Phi^-_r(a,b)=ab|a-b|^r,  a,b>=2,
  K^-_r(h,k)(N)=sum_{a!=b, Phi^-_r(a,b)=N}h(a)k(b),
  P^-_r(f)=EXP_D(h+K^-_r(h,h)), h=LOG_D(f).

Phi>=ab>=2 max(a,b). Therefore each coefficient uses finitely many strictly
smaller input indices. K is a symmetric bilinear coefficient-local map.
The logarithmic map h -> h+K(h,h) has the exact recursive inverse

  h(N)=j(N)-sum_{a!=b,Phi(a,b)=N}h(a)h(b).

This proves a two-sided inverse on all formal inputs, not just the tested
prefixes. EXP_D and LOG_D then give a formal inverse for P. It does not
prove convergence of an unrestricted analytic inverse.

The index product is nonassociative: on delta_2,delta_3,delta_4, the two
iterates are delta_(24*2^r) and delta_(24*10^r). Also K(delta_a,delta_a)=0.
In particular a single-coordinate seed is fixed by the logarithmic inverse.
There is no ordinary Catalan inverse-mass law for this coupling.

For ordinary input and output powers, respectively,

  P_in,beta(f)=EXP_D(beta*h+beta^2*K(h,h)),
  P_out,beta(f)=EXP_D(beta*h+beta*K(h,h)),
  P_in,beta /_D P_out,beta=EXP_D(beta*(beta-1)*K(h,h)).

The maps generally fail both the ordinary unit-group homomorphism law and
universal input-root commutation. Their separate zeta bridge is proved in
section 5. Every Phi is even: if a,b are both odd their difference is even;
otherwise ab is even. Hence EXP_D(K(h,h)) has no nonunit odd coefficient,
and P(f)(N)=f(N) at every odd N. This is an exact quotient law.

For a positive seed h, write the inverse of z -> z+K(z,z), applied to t*h,
as sum_{ell>=1}t^ell G_ell, with

  G_1=h, G_ell=-sum_{i+j=ell}K(G_i,G_j).

Every coefficient in G_ell has sign (-1)^(ell-1), so collisions within a
fixed leaf count do not cancel. Setting M_ell=sum_N |G_ell(N)| gives

  M_ell=sum_{i+j=ell}M_i M_j
          -sum_{i+j=ell}sum_N |G_i(N)G_j(N)|.

This identity includes collisions of indices within and across leaf counts.
It holds for every finite positive seed. A conditional colored binary-tree
law can replace the overlap by 1_(ell even)*2^(ell/2-1)*M_(ell/2) when the
tree hashes have the required injectivity; that hypothesis cannot be dropped.
For seeds 2,3 at r=1,2,3, the tested masses through six leaves are
2,2,8,32,160,800. Seeds 2,3,5 at r=1 reject the extrapolation at four leaves:
M_2=6,M_3=36,M_4=232, whereas the simplified law predicts 240. The second-
degree coefficients are -2 at 6 and -4 at 30, whose squared mass is 20,
rather than 12. Indeed Phi_1(2,5)=Phi_1(3,5)=30. The complete INTEGER
fibre of 30 also includes (5,6); the prime-power fibre contains only the
first two pairs. Global prime-power injectivity is already false at r=1.

For independent implementation checks, a complete fibre is recovered by
enumerating d with d^r|N and testing whether d^2+4N/d^r is a square. The
quadratic roots give a=(sqrt(d^2+4N/d^r)-d)/2>=2 and b=a+d. This is complete
because d=b-a and ab=N/d^r for every contributing unordered pair.

2. ZETA INPUT AND THE KNOWN CORRELATION REPRESENTATION

Let u(n)=1. Its formal Dirichlet logarithm is

  g(n)=Lambda(n)/log(n)=1/e if n=p^e, and 0 otherwise; g(1)=0.

Writing PP for the prime powers, define the positive-coefficient series

  Gamma^-_r(s)=sum_{a,b in PP, a!=b}g(a)g(b)/[ab|a-b|^r]^s.

Absolute convergence depends only on sigma=Re(s). Rearranging positive
terms, and then complex terms where absolute convergence is established,
gives

  Gamma^-_r(s)=2 sum_{d>=1}d^(-rs) C_d(s),
  C_d(s)=sum_{n>=2}g(n)g(n+d)/[n(n+d)]^s.

This is weighted bilinear additive correlation of logarithmic zeta
coefficients, with the zero gap removed. Prime-pair Dirichlet correlations
with von Mangoldt weights were studied before this experiment. Changing
the weights to Lambda(n)/log(n), summing the power gap kernel, and composing
with LOG_D/EXP_D requires the present bounds; prior conditional zero-
distribution results for other weights are not imported.

3. EXACT ABSOLUTE ABSCISSA

For every integer r>=1,

  sigma_c(r)=max(2/(r+2),1/2).

Here is a proof, independent of any inference from finite FFT curves.

Sufficiency above sigma_c: use g<=1, order a<b, and split a<=b/2 from
a>b/2. In the first region the summand is at most a^-sigma b^(-(r+1)sigma).
For sigma<1, summing a gives O(b^(1-sigma)), so convergence follows from
(r+2)sigma>2. For sigma=1 the inner bound is O(log b), and for sigma>1
it is bounded; both also give convergence. In the second region, on a
dyadic block b~X the integer majorant is

  O(X^(1-2sigma) sum_{1<=d<=2X}d^(-r*sigma)).

This is summable in X=2^k when sigma>1/2 and (r+2)sigma>2, including the
logarithmic case r*sigma=1. Thus Gamma is holomorphic above sigma_c by
normal absolute convergence.

The bulk lower bound: PNT gives order X/log X primes in each of two
separated proportional intervals, for example [X,3X/2] and [7X/4,2X].
Their pair contribution is at least

  c X^(2-(r+2)sigma)/(log X)^2.

Disjoint dyadic blocks prove divergence below 2/(r+2).

The near-pair lower bound: let m be the prime count in [X,2X], so
m~X/log X. Partition that interval into J bins of width D=ceil(8X/m),
where J<=2X/D for large X. If n_j are the bin counts, Cauchy's inequality
gives sum_j binom(n_j,2)>=(m^2/J-m)/2>=c*m. Each such pair has gap at most
D=O(log X) and ab<=(2X)^2. Its contribution is therefore at least

  c X^(1-2sigma)/(log X)^(1+r*sigma).

Disjoint dyadic blocks prove divergence below 1/2, even with arbitrarily
large fixed r. The argument averages gaps of order log X; it requires no
assertion about infinitely many pairs of any fixed gap. These upper and
lower bounds prove the stated sharp abscissa.

4. ENDPOINT CLASSIFICATION

The series converges at sigma_c for r=1 and for every r>=3, and diverges
at sigma_c=1/2 for r=2. All claims here are unconditional.

For r=2, let A(D) count unordered prime pairs in [X,2X] with gap at most D.
The same bin argument, for 4X/m<=D<=X/2, gives

  A(D)>=(m^2 D/(2X)-m)/2>=m^2 D/(8X).

Layer-cake integration consequently gives

  sum_{p<q in [X,2X]}1/(q-p)
       >=integral_(4X/m)^(X/2) A(D)/D^2 dD
       >=c X/log X.

At sigma=1/2, the ordered contribution has additional weight
2/sqrt(pq)>=1/X. Each block is at least c/log X, and summing disjoint
dyadic blocks diverges. Slow apparent stabilization of finite prefixes
cannot prove endpoint convergence. The finite layered certificates in
gap_endpoint_probe.json are exact count inequalities only: each pair's
dyadic reciprocal weights sum to at most twice its reciprocal gap, so
their reported total is a valid finite energy lower bound.

For the remaining endpoints, the weighted PP counting bound from PNT is

  W(X)=sum_(p^e<=X)1/e=O(X/log X),
  sum_(p^e<=X)g(p^e)/(p^e)^sigma
       =O(X^(1-sigma)/log X), 0<sigma<1.

Proper powers number O(sqrt(X)*log X), so they do not change the first
bound. Partial summation gives the second. The unbalanced region a<=b/2
at either endpoint is at most a constant times

  sum_(b in PP) g(b) b^(1-(r+2)sigma)/log b.

For r=1,sigma=2/3 this is sum g(b)/(b log b), which converges by W; for
r>=3,sigma=1/2 the exponent is -r/2, which also gives convergence.

It remains to bound balanced blocks, where a,b are both comparable to X.
For r=1,sigma=2/3, the arbitrary-interval Brun--Titchmarsh upper bound
implies uniformly in a that the number of primes within distance D is
O(D/log D), D>=2. Partial summation, or dyadic summation in D, gives

  sum_(q prime,0<|q-a|<=2X)|q-a|^(-2/3)
       =O(X^(1/3)/log X).

There are O(X/log X) choices of prime a, and (ab)^(-2/3)=O(X^(-4/3)).
Thus the prime-prime block is O((log X)^(-2)). If at least one input is a
proper prime power, bound the other by all integers: there are
O(sqrt(X)*log X) choices and sum_d d^(-2/3)=O(X^(1/3)). The contribution
is O(X^(-1/2)*log X). Both bounds sum over dyadic blocks.

For r>=3,sigma=1/2, set alpha=r/2>1. A uniform unconditional sieve bound
for even-gap prime pairs is

  #{p<=2X: p,p+d prime} <= C S(d) X/(log X)^2,
  S(d)=product_(p|d,p>2)(p-1)/(p-2),

with a constant and starting X independent of d. Odd gaps between odd
primes are absent; small exceptions do not affect convergence. To sum the
gap weights, write

  S(d)=sum_(k|d,k odd squarefree) product_(p|k)1/(p-2).

Positive rearrangement gives the finite bound

  sum_(d>=1) S(d)/d^alpha
       =zeta(alpha)*product_(p>2)(1+1/((p-2)*p^alpha)) < infinity.

The balanced prime-prime block is O((log X)^(-2)) after the factor
(ab)^(-1/2)=O(1/X). Pairs with a proper prime power contribute at most
O(X^(-1/2)*log X), since sum_d d^-alpha is finite. Summing blocks proves
the endpoint convergence for every r>=3.

Since coefficients are nonnegative, the finite endpoint total also
majorizes all complex terms on the closed half-plane Re(s)>=sigma_c.
The series is uniformly and absolutely convergent there, so Gamma is
continuous on that closed half-plane and holomorphic in its interior.
This does NOT establish holomorphy across its boundary. For the integer
grammar, r=3 is the first choice reaching a continuous critical-line
correction; r=2 reaches the same abscissa but has a divergent endpoint.

5. ORDINARY ROOTS AND THE ACTUAL RH QUESTION

Initially for Re(s)>1, the Euler LOG zeta and ordinary Dirichlet EXP give

  P_in,beta(u)(s)=zeta(s)^beta exp(beta^2 Gamma^-_r(s)),
  P_out,beta(u)(s)=zeta(s)^beta exp(beta Gamma^-_r(s)).

This proves the scalar bridge from the exact operator, rather than merely
defining its scalar output. A branch of LOG zeta continues each identity
where appropriate. Both exponential factors are nonzero; they do not
erase ordinary zeta roots. For beta>0 the input and output coefficient
series are nonnegative and have exact absolute abscissa 1: their scalar
values diverge as s decreases to 1 because zeta(s)^beta does, while Gamma
is finite there. For arbitrary beta they converge absolutely for sigma>1
by the EXP majorant. The correction's smaller convergence half-plane is
not the full ordinary transformed series' abscissa.

For r>=3, Gamma is holomorphic on Re(s)>1/2. At any zero rho strictly
inside that half-plane, its factor is holomorphic and nonzero, so the
ordinary root obstruction has exponent m*beta when zeta's multiplicity
is m. At a critical-line zero rho, section 4 gives a finite Gamma(rho)
and a nonzero limiting exponential. With a local branch chosen on the
right half-neighborhood, as s approaches rho from Re(s)>1/2,

  P_in,beta(u)(s) ~ c (s-rho)^(m*beta), c!=0,

and similarly for the output family. A noninteger m*beta cannot be the
vanishing order of a holomorphic extension through rho. This is one-sided
root sensitivity, not a whole-loop monodromy claim: Gamma has not been
proved holomorphic on a disk straddling the critical line.

For precise RH relevance let F(s)=(s-1)zeta(s), removing its known pole,
and consider J_j=F(s)^(2^-j) exp(2^(-2j) Gamma^-_r(s)), normalized on the
real axis s>1. Then

  RH iff all J_j, j>=1, extend single-valuedly and holomorphically
        to Re(s)>1/2.

If RH holds, F is nonzero on this simply connected half-plane and has
a holomorphic logarithm, so the assertion follows. Conversely any zero
there has finite multiplicity m. A holomorphic extension of J_j would
require 2^j|m, since J_j^(2^j)=F*exp(2^-j Gamma). Requiring this for every
j excludes every such zero. Zeta's functional symmetry then gives RH.
This is a reformulation, NOT a new estimate proving the continuation.
For a single j, multiplicities divisible by 2^j remain an obstruction to
inferring zero-freeness from the root criterion.

The new convergence proof explains a source of the arithmetic 1/2
boundary: sufficiently close prime pairs, without referring to zero
positions. It does not locate zeros, exclude off-line zeros, establish a
self-adjoint RH operator, or evaluate Gamma at a critical zero by a
certified infinite computation. The six one-sided zero groups reuse the
two existing certified simple-zero disks near heights 14.134725 and
21.022040; no additional zero is located in this stage.

6. SPECTRAL REPRESENTATION, TAILS, AND NEGATIVE CONTROLS

For sigma>1/2 and r*sigma>1, set w_n=g(n)n^-s. Then
Q=sum |w_n|^2<=LOG zeta(2sigma)<infinity. The zero-diagonal Toeplitz kernel
T_s(n,m)=|n-m|^-rs has l2 norm at most C=2zeta(r*sigma), by the l1 kernel
bound. Thus the NONCONJUGATING bilinear form satisfies

  Gamma=B_s(w,w), |Gamma|<=C Q<=2zeta(r*sigma)*LOG zeta(2sigma).

This is classical additive correlation/Fourier convolution. The complex
bilinear form differs from the sesquilinear Hermitian correlation; using
both conjugated orientations produces a cosine phase and gives a different
answer. Adding the excluded diagonal also changes the operator. Both
mistakes, as well as small deliberate coefficient errors at index 6,
are rejected by the numerical certificates.

The zero-diagonal kernel is indefinite: at real s and h=delta_2-delta_3,
B_s(h,h)=-2. Equivalently K(h,h) has coefficient -2 at index 6. It is not
a positive semidefinite spectral RH operator. At sigma=1/2 the prime
contribution to Q is sum_p 1/p, which diverges. Section 4 nevertheless
proves finite off-diagonal Gamma for r>=3 using prime sparsity. The l2
argument alone cannot establish this endpoint and cannot evaluate it.

For numerical finite frames through N, take v_n=w_n for 1<=n<=N. On
sigma>1 one may add the synthetic Fourier coordinate
c_s=LOG zeta(s)-sum_(n<=N)w_n at n=0. This coordinate is an approximation
device, not Dirichlet unit delta_1 or a new index-0 arithmetic operand.
In both anchored and unanchored frames, putting c_s=0 for the latter,

  q=sum_(n<=N)g(n)^2/n^(2sigma)+|c_s|^2,
  epsilon^2 <= LOG zeta(2sigma)-sum_(n<=N)g(n)/n^(2sigma)+|c_s|^2,
  |B_s(w,w)-B_s(v,v)|<=C*(2sqrt(q)*epsilon+epsilon^2).

The first tail uses g^2<=g. The added coordinate contributes |c_s|^2 to
the squared approximation error. Polynomial multiplication of W(z) and
z^N W(z^-1) computes finite nonconjugating correlation coefficients
exactly as Arb balls. Its diagonal is excluded.

The independent reference sums pairs directly through 3072 and uses
Gamma_N(s)+2c_s LOG zeta(4s). The exact anchored finite form instead has
2c_s sum_(n<=N)g(n)n^(-4s); the difference is bounded by
2|c_s|*N^(1-4sigma)/(4sigma-1). The independent Toeplitz bound uses
C<=2(1+1/(r*sigma-1)) and the integer tail
N^(1-2sigma)/(2sigma-1), rather than the producer's native zeta tail.
Near critical zeros, its alternative tail majorant uses
LOG zeta(2sigma)<=log(1+1/(2sigma-1)), with the known prefix subtracted.

At the six full complex test arguments, LOG zeta(sigma)<pi, so native
principal LOG zeta(s) equals the Euler branch. Anchoring with LOG zeta(s)
is not used below sigma=1. Real LOG zeta(2sigma) is the positive Euler
logarithm even when larger than pi. At s=2+7.19i, five input/output root
groups compare exact EXP prefixes to native-zeta scalar references; a
nonnegative real total minus the real prefix bounds the complex tail.

For the reused zero centers and three positive offsets epsilon each,
the producer certifies nonzero native zeta values and strictly positive
bounds exp(-beta^2 M)<=|exp(beta^2 Gamma)|<=exp(beta^2 M), with
M=2zeta(3sigma)*LOG zeta(2sigma). The checker changes the input cutoff,
uses elementary integral bounds, and replaces direct zeta modulus checks
by Taylor/Cauchy upper bounds from a larger holomorphic disk. These bounds
are one-sided and grow as epsilon decreases. A nonzero BOUNDARY LIMIT
comes from the separate endpoint theorem, not from these pointwise bounds.
