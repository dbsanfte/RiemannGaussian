1. Complete core and the minimal witness
---------------------------------------
Use u=1/3,v=2/3,beta=9/2 and the fixed exact R_u. With F(x) the complete
prefix, zero below1, its Dirichlet series is initially absolutely convergent
for Re(s)>1 and equals
 D(s)=zeta(s+u)*[beta/zeta(s)^2
       -beta/(zeta(s)*zeta(s+v))-R_u/zeta(s+v)]-beta/zeta(s). (1.1)
This is the same full four-state core as _005, including its unit. Main
centering at the real pole2/3 has already been built into (1.1); it cannot
remove a nonreal critical pole by a finite-head or real-pole subtraction.

A sufficient minimal witness consists of:
 (i) an actual zero rho=1/2+i*t, with 14<t<15;
 (ii) its finite multiplicity m>=1, or simply a unique simple zero;
 (iii) zeta(rho+u)!=0, certified on a disk containing that actual rho.
No certification that it is globally the first zero is needed. Also
zeta(rho+v)!=0 automatically, since its real part is7/6>1 and the
absolutely convergent Euler product is nonzero there.

Write zeta(s)=c_m*(s-rho)^m+O((s-rho)^(m+1)), c_m!=0. Then
 D(s)=A*(s-rho)^(-2m)+O((s-rho)^(-2m+1)),
 A=beta*zeta(rho+u)/c_m^2 !=0.                              (1.2)
The terms with one reciprocal zeta have order at most m, and the term
with only zeta(s+v) is analytic locally. They cannot cancel this leading
term. Thus the exact order is d=2m, with every signed complement retained.

An acceptable extra certificate bounds zeta away from zero on this disk

2. Generic pole-order versus complete dyadic moment rule
-------------------------------------------------------
Let G be a constant-on-integer-cells prefix signal, zero below1, and
 E_G(X)=X^(-2)*sum_(X<=n<2X)|G(n)|^2, X=2^j.
Suppose for EVERY dyadic X>=1, with a fixed finite K and p>=0,
 E_G(X)<=K*(1+log X)^p.                                    (2.1)
For s=1/2+delta+i*t, delta>0, its Mellin transform exists absolutely:
 I_G(s)=integral_1^infinity G(x)*x^(-s-1)dx.
It is holomorphic in Re(s)>1/2. On a dyadic block, Cauchy gives
 |integral_X^(2X) G(x)x^(-s-1)dx|
 <=[integral_X^(2X)|G(x)|^2*x^(-2)dx]^(1/2)
       *[integral_X^(2X)x^(-1-2delta)dx]^(1/2)
 <=sqrt(K*log2)*(1+log X)^(p/2)*X^(-delta).                 (2.2)
The first integral is <=E_G(X), with EXACT integer endpoints and no
partial final cell. Summing (2.2) yields
 |I_G(1/2+delta+i*t)|=O_K,p(delta^(-1-p/2)), delta down to0. (2.3)
For example with L=log2 and 0<delta<=1, the sum is bounded explicitly by
 sqrt(K)/sqrt(L)*exp(delta*(L+1))*Gamma(1+p/2)
                                      *delta^(-1-p/2).
This follows by comparing each summand to the integral over its logarithmic
cell; no global zero-free assertion or heuristic cancellation is involved.

If continuation of I_G has a genuine order-d pole at rho=1/2+i*t, or more
generally an independently proved leading behavior
I_G(rho+delta)=B*delta^(-d)+o(delta^(-d)), B!=0,
comparison with (2.3) requires
                         p>=2d-2.                        (2.4)
Analytic identification is valid without assuming RH: the Mellin transform
under (2.1) is holomorphic to the right of1/2 and equals its known
Dirichlet expression on the absolutely convergent half-plane. Meromorphic
identity continues it; any earlier off-line pole would already contradict
the hypothesis. An isolated neighborhood of rho then gives the displayed
right-hand Laurent behavior. The argument never declares zeta zero-free
before imposing the hypothetical moment bound.

There is also a useful endpoint lower obstruction. If B is the leading
coefficient of I_G at rho, then
 limsup_(j->infinity) E_G(2^j)/(1+j*log2)^(2d-2)
                       >=log2*|B|^2/[(d-1)!]^2 >0.        (2.5)
If the limsup is finite, apply (2.2) above a finite head with K arbitrarily
close to that limsup and use the exact asymptotic of its geometric-power
sum; the finite head vanishes after multiplication by delta^d. If the
limsup is infinite the conclusion is immediate. This gives neither a
pointwise lower bound at every scale nor an upper bound at the endpoint.

3. Every generated real delay retains the critical pole
------------------------------------------------------
For G_a(x)=F(x)-a*F(x/2), exact Mellin change of variables gives
 I_(G_a)(s)=(1-a*2^(-s))*D(s)/s.                           (3.1)
At integers the second prefix is F(floor(n/2)), and both continuous terms
are constant on the same integer cells. The unit and the interval[1,2)
remain; no interpolation correction is needed for this delay.

The generated a are0 and plus-or-minus2^theta for
theta=0,1/6,1/3,1/2,2/3,5/6. For theta!=1/2 the multiplier cannot vanish
at rho by modulus: |a*2^(-rho)|=2^(theta-1/2)!=1.
For theta=1/2, vanishing would require t*log2 an integer multiple of pi,
with parity determined by the sign. But 14<t<15, 69/100<log2<7/10,
and 3<pi<22/7 give exactly
                3pi<t*log2<4pi.
These elementary rational bounds exclude either sign. For a=0 it is1.
Thus all13 delay filters, including the critical and oversized
causal-inverse cases, preserve d=2m, with Mellin leading coefficient
 B_a=(1-a*2^(-rho))*A/rho !=0.                             (3.2)
The pole rejection is DIRECT on E_G. It does not weaken merely because
a conservative inverse theorem adds logs when recovering E_F, and it
does not require that the delay inverse be cofinally admissible.

4. Log-Abel also preserves order, with complete head conventions
---------------------------------------------------------------
Let Z(x)=sum_(n<=x)f(n)log n and define continuous g(x)=Z(x)/log x for
x>=64, zero below64 for this Mellin calculation. Set
 I_g(s)=integral_64^infinity Z(x)/log x*x^(-s-1)dx.
Differentiating in an absolutely convergent half-plane gives
 I_g'(s)=D'(s)/s+E_head(s),
 E_head(s)=integral_1^64 Z(x)x^(-s-1)dx,                   (4.1)
an entire function. The identity then continues on any local branch in
the right-hand punctured disk. Substitution of (1.2) and integration along
s=rho+delta give
 I_g(rho+delta)=A/rho*delta^(-d)+O(delta^(-d+1)), d>=2.     (4.2)
Lower terms may involve a logarithm of s-rho; they cannot cancel this
leading term. Strictly, the entire local primitive need not be meromorphic
because of these lower logarithms; the leading one-sided behavior, which
is all the rejection rule needs, is unchanged. The division by log x
reverses the derivative's EXTRA pole
order; it does not reduce the original order d.

The frozen integer filter equals F(n) below64 and Z(n)/log n above.
With K0=3beta+ceil(R_u), the unconditional coefficient bound implies
 |Z(n)|<=K0*n*(1+log n)*log n.
On each cell[n,n+1), n>=64,
 |Z(n)/log n-Z(n)/log x|<=2K0.
Its Mellin difference is analytic for Re(s)>0; the below64 modification
is an entire finite head. Neither changes (4.2), so (2.4),(2.5) apply to
the ACTUAL saved log-Abel sequence with B_log=A/rho.

There is sharper BLOCK recovery than the frozen conservative extra log.
Put J(x)=integral_1^x F(y)dy/y. Exact summation by parts says
 g=F-J/log x, and therefore, for x>=64,
 F(x)=g(x)+J(64)/log64+integral_64^x g(y)/(y*log y)dy.       (4.3)
This follows by differentiating J/log x; no unknown coefficient series is
summed at the real pole. If E_saved-g(Y)<=K1*(1+log Y)^p on every dyadic
Y, the continuous g has the same block bound after the paid2K0 error.
Specifically its integral block norm is bounded by
 sqrt(K1)*(1+log Y)^(p/2)+2K0/sqrt(Y).
For its K1 part, blockwise Cauchy bounds the final integral in (4.3) by
 [sqrt(K1)/(log64*(1-2^(-1/2)))]
                          *sqrt(X)*(1+log(2X))^(p/2)
on X<=x<2X, X>=64. The bounded-error part contributes at most
2K0*log(log(2X)/log64); the fixed J head is explicit. Dividing its block
square by X gives paid O(loglog(2X)^2/X) terms. Minkowski and (4.3) give
                  E_F(X)=O((1+log X)^p).                 (4.4)
Constants depend only on K1,p,the finite head and K0; there is NO extra
log degree. Finite dyadic blocks below64 are part of the head. This is a
conditional generic recovery theorem, not a proof that either energy has
any such bound. Conversely g=F-J/log x gives the same-degree forward
bound by the identical blockwise argument. Direct pole proof (4.1) avoids
needing this refinement to reject the selected premise.

5. Recurrence certificate composition and the actual selection
-------------------------------------------------------------
Let A(X)=E_G(X)+w*E_H(X)>=E_G(X), w>=0, with complete signed squares.
For a universal proposed step
 A(2X)<=rho_step*A(X)+C*X^gamma*(1+log2 X)^k,
the gamma=0 induction gives the sharp log degree
 p=k when 0<=rho_step<1,
 p=k+1 when rho_step=1.
It follows by ordinary geometric summation or summing the drift. Hence
whenever G retains a pole of order d at a critical point, the following
is a reusable REJECTION certificate:
 gamma=0 and sharp p<2d-2 => the uniform premise is FALSE.  (5.1)
Positive auxiliary storage cannot evade it, because A dominates E_G.
Gamma>0 is outside this test: a positive power absorbs every fixed log
order, so the pole alone does not refute that proposed power bound.

For a simple surviving zero d=2, the frozen generated shapes imply:
 rho_step<1,k=0: bounded energy, rejected;
 rho_step=1,k=0: log-degree1, rejected;
 rho_step<1,k=1: log-degree1, rejected;
 rho_step=1,k=1: log-degree2, NOT rejected by this rule.
Changing C, retaining a finite head, or adding a positive auxiliary cannot
repair the first three at every scale. The last remains an open arithmetic
estimate, not a demonstrated bound. A multiple zero would impose the
stronger floor4m-2. These are boundary-pole facts consistent with RH,
not a counterexample to RH.

6. Detector-facing proof feedback and limits
--------------------------------------------
A compact generic certificate rule requires the exact source Dirichlet
formula, a certified boundary pole/order/leading coefficient, the exact
filter multiplier or derivative identity, its noncancellation witness,
the nonnegative storage domination, and the sharp induction degree.
It returns a rejected universal bound shape, rather than nominating a
new optimizer or changing frozen responses. It needs no fitted constant
and is reusable for other complete sources and filters.

The next bound language should preserve gamma=0,degree at least2 when
this pole survives; for example rho_step=1,k=1 is a legitimate NOT-YET-
REJECTED shape. This alone is not evidence that it is true. Alternatively
an exact source-specific filter may remove the nonreal leading pole, but
its recovery must retain that removed frequency/channel and its costs;
erasing an oscillation from the proof target is not full-target recovery.
No assertion here excludes critical zeros, proves a zero-free half-plane,
or proves RH. The missing shifted witness is explicitly separate from the
analytic theorem; after it is certified, the selected thirteen premises
can be marked analytically false despite their finite status.
