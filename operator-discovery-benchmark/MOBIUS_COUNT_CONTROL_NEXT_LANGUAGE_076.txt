1. Shared ordinary-Mobius stencil

For each source G(v), retain its four signed prefixes exactly. Compile
G(u)-L(u) into components {parent, symbolic_weight}, including all prime
quotient multiplicities and direct collisions. Count factors n^(-gamma)
are named real tokens with rational coefficients and certified intervals;
arbitrary admitted rational gamma is supported. Collect prefix endpoint
events before expanding to a bounded sparse coefficient map c_u(n).

The identity is R(u)=sum_n c_u(n)mu(n). Equal tokens/endpoints combine
symbolically before interval evaluation. Prefix endpoints, mu(1), empty
blocks and the full upper band remain present. All present operator
children are at most u//2; consequently c_u(n)=-1 on u//5<n<=u//4.
This is a support obstruction to coefficientwise annihilation by the old
operator grammar, not an impossibility of cancellation across n.

The compiler requires independently admitted actual prime child groups.
Matching multiplicity totals alone do not prove their arithmetic
completeness. Its source_admission_supplied flag stays false. Formal
identities plus interval bounds are not substitutes for source admission.

2. Search for exact arithmetic pairings

For any prime p and any finitely supported coefficient function,
 sum_n c(n)mu(n)=sum_(p does not divide m)(c(m)-c(pm))mu(m).
Rows divisible by p^2 vanish because mu is zero. A row with exactly one
p factor moves to n/p with a minus sign; all other rows remain. Merge
the complete symbolic coefficients before measuring a bound. Terms that
move across a support boundary are retained; outside coefficients are0.

Iterating distinct primes P leaves gcd(m,product(P))=1 and coefficient
 c_P(m)=sum_(d divides product(P))mu(d)c(dm).
The implementation folds sparse maps without enumerating all subsets.
Fold order gives the same exact coefficients, so the beam deduplicates
prime sets while keeping an explicit verification schedule. It searches
short schedules with a bounded width, ranks the exact coefficient l1
upper bound, and NEVER receives observed Mobius values to select a path.
Default primes2,3,5,7,11,13, depth3,width4 are an explicit finite pilot;
other bounded prime choices and depths are supported.

The l1 bound uses complete signed polynomial intervals at every remaining
index, then |mu(m)|<=1. Thus it is a proved finite constructive bound,
not a fitted residual maximum. It cannot increase under a correct fold.
Square energy CAN increase through collisions; the search does not use
an unproved l2 contraction. Removing all small-prime factors can also
become an expensive way of evaluating a finite sum, rather than providing
a uniform mechanism. No complexity or power-saving conclusion is free.
