1. A GENERIC SUPPORT-MOMENT CONTRACT

Let I be any finite index set, w_n>=0, W=sum_I w_n, and S a marked subset.
Suppose |b_n|=1 for n in S and |b_n|<=delta<1 for n outside S.
For any integer k>=1 put

 C=sum_(n in S) w_n, M_(2k)=sum_(n in I) w_n |b_n|^(2k),
 beta=delta^(2k).

Pointwise positivity and the gap imply

 C <= M_(2k) <= C+beta*(W-C).

For any independently certified enclosure M_lo<=M_(2k)<=M_hi,

 max(0,(M_lo-beta*W)/(1-beta)) <= C <= min(W,M_hi).         (1)

This applies to arbitrary complex scores, finite sets, nonnegative weights
and every scale. The caller must prove the score normalization, gap,
weight and moment-enclosure premises. Merely evaluating the right-hand
side does not certify those premises. The API rejects delta>=1 and
inconsistent moment/weight inputs. No prime or zeta object is needed to
discover or prove the generic transfer.

2. DISCOVERY AND UNIVERSAL AFFINE CERTIFICATION

The search fits unknown homogeneous affine forms a*M+b*W from generic
saturation cases: marked scores of modulus one, and unmarked scores of
modulus zero or delta. It uses the existing exact rational fitting backend,
with no QRH-specific character, exponent or theta object. Three gaps and
four moment orders give twelve learned pairs of inequalities. Fresh
lengths, score magnitudes and nonuniform rational weights are held out.

Each fitted lower form is proved by a+b<=1 on marked scores and
max(b,a*beta+b)<=0 on unmarked scores. Each upper form is proved by
a+b>=1 and min(b,a*beta+b)>=0. Affineness in the per-index moment means
that checking both endpoints proves the whole interval. Multiplication
by every nonnegative weight and summation prove all-set scope.
The sharp fitted lower coefficients are 1/(1-beta),-beta/(1-beta);
the upper coefficients are 1,0. Training only on zero unmarked scores
would fit the false equality C=M; an unmarked score 1/4 refutes it.
These are elementary support/positivity inequalities, not novel primitives.

3. AN ALL-SCALE ORDER SELECTION CERTIFICATE

If epsilon=M_hi-M_lo, the width of the clipped interval (1) is at most

 (epsilon+beta*W)/(1-beta).                                 (2)

For an integer-valued C, a certified width strictly below one implies
at most one integer lies in the interval. Existence follows from the
score and moment premises. Ceil of the lower endpoint and floor of the
upper endpoint then recover C exactly. This is not rounding the raw
moment or extrapolating from a finite table.

For desired width tau>epsilon, the strict sufficient condition is
beta*(W+tau)<tau-epsilon. The order compiler increases k until this
exact rational inequality holds. It terminates for every finite W
because 0<=delta<1. The least order is verified by checking k-1 too.
With fixed delta and error margin, k grows proportionally to log(W+1).
Changing operator order with scale must be stated explicitly; this
does not prove a fixed-order asymptotic estimate or critical-strip limit.

5. A GLOBAL FOURIER-FAMILY MOMENT TARGET, WITH ITS DOMAIN

Let the support indices be distinct modulo an integer H>=1. Put

 A_u^(k)=sum_I sqrt(w_n)*B_alpha(n)^k*exp(2*pi*i*u*n/H).

Complete finite-character orthogonality gives

 sum_(u=0)^(H-1)|A_u^(k)|^2 = H*M_(2k).                    (3)

Thus an independently certified global second-moment enclosure for this
actual arithmetic family supplies M_lo,M_hi in (1) by division by H.
Any interval of length at most H satisfies the residue-injectivity premise.
The modulus need not be prime. With aliases, the left side instead groups
coefficients sharing a residue, and (3) need not hold. Four all-one scores
at modulus two have row energy 16, versus the false diagonal value eight.

Coefficient moments sum |b_n|^(2k), whereas ordinary circle even moments
integrate |sum b_n z^n|^(2k). They differ already for 1+z at k=2: two
versus six. The correct circle analogue of M_(2k) is the SECOND moment
of the coefficientwise-power polynomial sum b_n^k z^n. The grammar and
audit explicitly reject substituting the ordinary fourth moment.
