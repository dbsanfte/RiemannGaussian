HIGHER PRIME MOMENT SHEARS: UNIFORM HEIGHT, ROOTS, AND A LIMIT TRAP
7 October 2026. Written mathematical proofs with independent exact/Arb
checks. These are not theorem-prover certificates or established historical
novelty. This is a compound extension of the prime logarithmic shear.

1. THE GRAMMAR AND THE ARITHMETIC OPERATOR

Use ordinary Dirichlet convolution *, with unit delta_1. For f(1)=1 let
h=LOG_* f. LOG and EXP are coefficient-local: a product of k indices
>=2 cannot contribute at n if 2^k>n. For a prime p, h(p)=f(p).

The expanded grammar permits an order m, m+1 multiplicative faces,
integer weights, and exact moment constraints. Set w_0=1 and require
sum_(j=0..m) w_j j^k=0, k=0,...,m-1. The Vandermonde system has one
solution, w_j=(-1)^j binomial(m,j). It is uniquely determined by the
zero of order m at z=1 of the polynomial sum w_j z^j. The first nonzero
moment is (-1)^m m!. The detector searches bounded integer weights at
m=2,4, holds out the final constraint, and checks higher orders 6..16
with exact rational elimination versus independently generated Pascal
rows. Recovery of these weights calibrates a known operation.

A separate six-model mask grammar tests q-p>=g, g=1,...,6. Finite
half-line edge envelopes through primes 127, with held-out cutoff 251,
select g=2 as the least restrictive mask whose envelope is below one.
This selection uses floating discovery values, independently rechecked
with Arb for every eligible edge. Unselected masks have no universal
domain certificate from those finite observations. Section 4 supplies
the separate universal theorem for the selected g=2. A 240-model
index-scale grammar A*B^m, A=1..16 and B=2..16, trains on the actual
minimal images at m=2,4,6 and uniquely selects A=4,B=6; held-out orders
8,12,16 pass. The universal minimum-image proof below is what licenses
the resulting coefficientwise limit, not the fit itself.

For EVEN m>=2 put r=m+2 and define the symmetric bilinear prime kernel

 J_m(h,k)=sum_(p<q prime, d=q-p>=2) (h(p)k(q)+h(q)k(p))/2
             * sum_(j=0..m) (-1)^j binomial(m,j)
                        delta_(p^(m-j) q^j d^(m+2)).

The condition d>=2 is an arithmetic predicate. It removes just the prime
edge (2,3), because all primes greater than two are odd. No zero ordinate
is used to choose this mask. Every image is composite. Its minimum face
p^m d^(m+2) is larger than q: when d=1 in the unmasked version, p^m>p+1;
when d>=2, d^(m+2)p^m>p+d. Thus only finitely many input primes can
contribute at any fixed coefficient, including for infinite formal inputs.

Define A_(m,t)(h)=h+t J_m(h,h) and S_(m,t)=EXP_* A_(m,t) LOG_*.
Prime projection pi satisfies pi J_m=0. Consequently

 A_(m,t) A_(m,u)=A_(m,t+u),   S_(m,t) S_(m,u)=S_(m,t+u),
 S_(m,t)^(-1)=S_(m,-t).

These identities hold on all normalized formal inputs, not just zeta.
They also follow directly from

 S_(m,t)(f)=f * EXP_*(t J_m(pi f,pi f)),

since multiplying by EXP of a composite-supported logarithm fixes the
prime coefficients. The map is generally not a homomorphism for *.

The smallest possible regularized image is n_0=4*6^m. Indeed, for p=2
one has d>=3, hence p d>=6 and d^2>=9. For odd p>=3 one has d>=2,
hence p d>=6 and d^2>=4, with equality only at p=3,d=2,q=5.
Each subsequent face multiplies that image by (q/p)^j>1. Hence

 S_(m,t)(f)(n_0)=f(n_0)+t f(3)f(5),
 S_(m,t)(f)(n)=f(n) for n<n_0.

In particular, S_(m,t) tends coefficientwise to the identity as even
m tends to infinity, on every formal input. The sparse exact audit
checks the first EXP coefficient by an independent Omega recurrence.
It never truncates the second composition to a convenient index prefix.

For an ordinary input root f^beta=EXP_*(beta h), the correction scales
by beta^2, independently of the cancellation order m. For an OUTPUT
root, the correction scales by beta. Thus

 S_(m,t)(f^beta)=EXP_*(beta h+t beta^2 J_m(h,h)),
 (S_(m,t)f)^beta=EXP_*(beta h+t beta J_m(h,h)).

Mellin cancellation degree m must not be confused with input degree two.
The exact ordinary EXP/LOG and root test at m=2 uses a full prefix of
256; higher-order exact EXP tests are sparse coefficient queries.

2. COMPLETE PRIME FIBRES AND SIGN SEPARATION

For a prime pair p<q, gcd(p,d)=gcd(q,d)=1. At an interior face 0<j<m,
the two endpoint valuations are exactly m-j and j, both smaller than
r=m+2. Every prime divisor of d has valuation a multiple of r. Reducing
the prime valuations of N modulo r therefore recovers the two endpoints
and j; require their valuations to sum to m, take the exact r-th root
of N/(p^(m-j)q^j), and check that it equals q-p>=2.

At a diagonal face j=0 or m there is exactly one nonzero valuation
residue, namely m, at an endpoint x. Require its FULL valuation to be m.
Take d=(N/x^m)^(1/r); check both x-d and x+d for primality. These are all
possible fibres. Diagonal fibres can aggregate; they must not be discarded
as a failed unique-edge decoder. Any other residue pattern has no image.

Because m and r are even, j even gives a square index and positive
weight; j odd gives a nonsquare index and negative weight. All contributions
at a fixed index have the same sign. Thus the absolute coefficient mass
of the zeta-input correction, at real sigma in its convergence domain, is

 B_m(sigma)=sum_(p<q,d>=2) d^(-(m+2)sigma)
                                      (p^-sigma+q^-sigma)^m.

The checker obtains coefficients by complete valuation reconstruction,
not by importing the forward kernel. The finite prime-input profiles
through 43 are explicitly finite-input profiles. Separate all-input
prefixes through 8192 at m=2,4 check every encoded integer, including
zero coefficients. Factorization sums repeated prime entries and checks
reconstruction, including encoded indices greater than 2^64.

3. THE ANALYTIC BRIDGE AND ITS TWO DOMAINS

On zeta, pi LOG_* zeta has prime coefficients one. Therefore its
correction is the ordinary Dirichlet series, initially on Re(s)>1/m,

 R_m(s)=sum_(p<q,d=q-p>=2) d^(-(m+2)s)(p^-s-q^-s)^m.             (1)

The identity follows from the finite binomial theorem, justified by
absolute summability. Indeed B_m(sigma) is at most
2^m (zeta(m sigma)-1)(zeta((m+2)sigma)-1) when sigma>1/m.
This proves ordinary correction absolute convergence there.

The PAIRED expression (1) has locally normal convergence on the larger
domain Re(s)>1/(m+1). To prove this, on a compact set write sigma=inf Re(s)
and T=sup |s|. For d<=p the integral identity gives

 |p^-s-(p+d)^-s| <= T d p^(-sigma-1).

The term is bounded by T^m p^(-m sigma-m) d^(m-(m+2)sigma).
Summing integer d<=p and integer p in dyadic blocks gives the bound
O(X^(2-(2m+2)sigma)) if the exponent of d is greater than -1.
At exponent -1 there is just an extra logarithm; below -1 the d sum
is bounded and the p sum already converges. For d>p, use
2^m p^(-m sigma) q^(-(m+2)sigma); the same dyadic estimate works when
m sigma<1, with a logarithm or bounded inner sum when m sigma>=1.
These estimates are summable for sigma>1/(m+1), uniformly on compacts.
The paired continuation equals the ordinary series on their overlap.

Do not call paired continuation below 1/m ordinary coefficient
convergence. The strict domains stated in the JSON need no prime-pair
conjecture, PNT, sieve theorem, or endpoint evaluation. For context,
their lower edges are sharp: a pair of separated prime intervals gives
the paired obstruction below 1/(m+1) by PNT; a positive proportion of
consecutive prime gaps are O(log X), giving the ordinary obstruction
below 1/m. Neither sharpness statement is a numerical convergence test.

On Re(s)>1, positive beta and any real fixed t have the full ordinary
transform and output-root identities

 S_(m,t)(zeta^beta)(s)=zeta(s)^beta exp(t beta^2 R_m(s)),
 (S_(m,t)zeta)^beta(s)=zeta(s)^beta exp(t beta R_m(s)).           (2)

Here LOG zeta is the Euler branch on Re(s)>1. The full ordinary series
has absolute abscissa exactly 1, for positive beta: the convolution
exponential absolute majorant proves convergence to the right; the
nonzero correction, holomorphic at s=1, preserves the zeta^beta pole
growth on approaching 1 from the right. Absolute convergence at 1
would bound that approach, contradicting the pole. Analytic continuation
of a factor in (2) is not convergence of the full ordinary series.

4. A UNIFORM-HEIGHT BOUND ON THE WHOLE CRITICAL RIGHT HALF-PLANE

For any sigma>0 put theta_(p,q)(sigma)=(p d)^-sigma+(q d)^-sigma.
Among the regularized prime edges the UNIQUE maximum is (3,5), giving

 rho(sigma)=6^-sigma+10^-sigma.                               (3)

For p=2, q>=5, the maximum is (2,5), giving 6^-sigma+15^-sigma.
For odd p>=3, d>=2 and both p d, q d increase with p,d, so (3,5)
maximizes. Among all remaining edges the maximum is (2,5): for p=3,
q>=7 one has p d>=12,q d>=28; for p>=5 the maximal edge is (5,7),
giving 10^-sigma+14^-sigma < 6^-sigma+15^-sigma. The last inequality
follows by comparing integrals of sigma*x^(-sigma-1) on [6,10] and
[14,15]. Denote this next envelope by rho_2(sigma).

For EVEN m>=4, all heights t, and sigma=Re(s)>1/4,

 |R_m(s)| <= C(sigma) rho(sigma)^(m-4).                       (4)

Proof: each absolute term is d^(-2sigma) times
|d^-s(p^-s-q^-s)|^m. Peel off m-4 powers and bound them by rho^(m-4).
The remaining sum is at most

 C_I(sigma)=16 (zeta(4sigma)-1)(zeta(6sigma)-1).

A smaller PRIME-specific majorant uses parity. For odd p the gap is
even; for p=2 the regularized gap is odd and at least three. Since
(p^-sigma+q^-sigma)^4<=16p^-4sigma, with P(x)=sum_p p^-x,

 C_P(sigma)=16[(P(4sigma)-2^-4sigma)2^-6sigma zeta(6sigma)
       +2^-4sigma((1-2^-6sigma)zeta(6sigma)-1)].                (5)

All quantities in (3)-(5) decrease with sigma. At sigma=1/2,
rho=0.7244760564807009495661033668... <1,
C_P<0.693425, whereas C_I<2.085015. Thus (4) holds UNIFORMLY for
every height and every Re(s)>=1/2. It is independent of zeta zero
positions. For t=1, m=64, for example,

 |R_64(s)| < 2.770e-9,
 |exp(R_64(s))-1| < 2.770e-9,   Re(s)>=1/2.

For arbitrary amplitude a the relative factor error is bounded by
|a| M exp(|a| M), or exp(|a| M)-1, with M from (4). This is a
uniform bound on the CORRECTION FACTOR, not a uniform absolute bound
on S_(m,t)zeta-zeta; the latter includes the height-dependent |zeta|.

The native prime bound in (5) uses
P(x)=sum_(k>=1) mu(k) LOG zeta(kx)/k, x>1, entirely on the positive
real axis. For cutoff M the tail is bounded by
u^(M+1)/((M+1)(1-u))*(1+2/((M+1)x-1)), u=2^-x.
This follows from log zeta(kx)<=zeta(kx)-1 and the integer tail
zeta(y)-1<=2^-y(1+2/(y-1)). The independent checker changes M from
40 to 48, computes mu from its own factor routine, and also checks
the looser integer-only majorant. All bounds use Arb enclosures.

5. A SHARP HEIGHT-UNIFORM LIMIT THRESHOLD AND A FALSE POSITIVE

Let sigma_* be the unique positive solution of

 6^-sigma_*+10^-sigma_*=1,
 sigma_*=0.34043184416498216969663367317... .

Strict monotonicity proves uniqueness. Independent rational bisections
bracket it; decimal printing is not the certificate. Above sigma_*,
(4) proves R_m tends to zero uniformly over ALL heights. To show
the threshold cannot be lowered, set t_*=pi/log(5/3). The 3/5 prime
phases are opposite, so the (3,5) term has modulus

 2^(-2sigma) rho(sigma)^m.

All other edges together have modulus at most
C_I(sigma) rho_2(sigma)^(m-4), sigma>1/4. Because rho_2<rho, the
dominant edge cannot be canceled by this remainder. At sigma_* the
leading modulus is constant and the remainder tends to zero; below
sigma_*, within sigma>1/4, the modulus grows without bound. The numeric
controls certify separated sides sigma_* +/- 0.01 at m=128,256,512;
the statement exactly at the threshold is the analytic dominant-edge
argument, not evaluation of a rounded threshold. If desired, the same
argument extends to any sigma>0 by replacing the base order four with
an even m_0 such that m_0 sigma>1. Its finite integer majorant is
2^m_0(zeta(m_0 sigma)-1)(zeta((m_0+2)sigma)-1).

WITHOUT the d>=2 mask, the only additional edge is (2,3):

 H_m(s)=R_m(s)+(2^-s-3^-s)^m.

At sigma=1/2 and t_0=pi/log(3/2), this edge has modulus
(1/sqrt(2)+1/sqrt(3))^m, whose base is 1.284457... >1.
Equation (4) bounds every other edge by a quantity tending to zero.
Hence |H_m(1/2+i t_0)| tends to infinity, even though its arithmetic
correction vanishes at every fixed index for all sufficiently large m
(the first unmasked index is 2^m). Each fixed sufficiently large m has
an ordinary absolutely convergent correction there. This is an explicit
failure of interchanging an operator-parameter limit and an infinite
Dirichlet evaluation. Coefficientwise convergence alone is insufficient.

6. ORDINARY ROOTS AND WHOLE ZETA ZERO DISKS

The correction (1) is holomorphic near a zero rho with real part near
one half. For m>=4 its ordinary coefficient series already converges
absolutely there, rather than requiring just paired continuation.
Write locally zeta(s)=(s-rho)^ell u(s), u(rho)!=0. On a chosen punctured
branch, the INPUT root in (2) is

 (s-rho)^(ell beta) u(s)^beta exp(t beta^2 R_m(s)).

The exponential is holomorphic and nonzero on the ENTIRE zero disk.
It preserves the exponent ell beta and fractional monodromy. The
OUTPUT root instead uses amplitude t beta. Increasing m makes these
correction factors tend uniformly to one wherever rho(Re(s))<1; it
does not remove the branch point or change the zeta multiplicity.

The two disk controls reuse the existing simple-zero certificates near
0.5+i14.13472514... (radius 1e-4) and 0.5+i21.02203964... (radius 1e-5).
Use sigma_min=1/2-radius in (4),(5); rho(sigma_min)<1. For m=8,16,32,64
and beta=1,1/2,1/4 the whole-disk factor modulus lies between
exp(-beta^2 M) and exp(beta^2 M), with relative error exp(beta^2 M)-1.
The bounds are valid on the containing rectangle, hence on the disk.
No new zeta zero is located; no many-digit R_m(rho) value is asserted.

For the direct RH connection, let D={Re(s)>1/2} and Z(s)=(s-1)zeta(s),
where Z(1)=1. Z is holomorphic on the simply connected D. For every
k>=1 let beta=2^-k and normalize Z^beta at a positive real basepoint.
The initially defined functions

 Z(s)^beta exp(t beta^2 R_m(s))

have holomorphic continuations to D for every k IF AND ONLY IF RH.
If RH holds, Z is zero-free on D and has a holomorphic logarithm there.
Conversely, a zero of finite multiplicity ell in D would force
ell/2^k to be an integer for every k, which is impossible. The nonzero
correction changes neither direction. One fixed dyadic root is not
enough: zeros whose multiplicity is divisible by its denominator remain
compatible. This criterion does not prove the required continuation.

For prime input cutoff N, omitted pairs have q>N. Set A=floor(N/2).

and p>A. The first part has d>A, the second has p>A and d>=2. Integer

 E_4 <=16(zeta(4sigma)-1) A^(1-6sigma)/(6sigma-1)
       +16(zeta(6sigma)-1) A^(1-4sigma)/(4sigma-1).
 E_m <=rho(sigma)^(m-4) E_4, sigma>1/4.

the finite exponential corrections by native zeta gives rigorously
bounded factored scalar comparisons, including below Re(s)=1.

NOT six new full transformed EXP coefficient-prefix comparisons.
