SHARP GLOBAL COMPOSITE GAP FOR THE RECORDED SIGNED OPERATOR
7 October 2026. Arithmetic theorem; historical novelty remains unaudited.

Operator: B = W(-5 R_(1/3)/11 + 16 R_(2/3)/11), evaluated at the
constant-one coefficient sequence of ordinary zeta. R and W are defined in
compensated_shift_proof.txt. This proof addresses the stronger gap recorded
as an unproved finite fit in compensated_shift_search.json. It does not infer
an infinite estimate from that finite fit.

CLAIM

For every prime p, B(p)=1. For every composite integer n,

                   |B(n)| <= 7/110.                     (G)

The constant is the sharp supremum over composite integers. There is no new
claim about prime counts, signed cancellation, RH, or publication priority.

1. POSITIVE SERIES AND A FACTOR-COUNT/GCD PARTITION

Write n=prod_(i=1..m) p_i^(e_i), m=omega(n), g=gcd(e_i),
x_i=log(p_i)/log(n), q_i=g*x_i. Then q_i>0 and
sum_i (e_i/g)*q_i=1, hence sum_i q_i<=1. No numerical factorization
assumption is used in this identity.

For j>=1, define the positive inclusion-exclusion polynomial

 A_j(x)=sum_(a_i>=1,sum a_i=j) [j!/prod a_i!]*prod x_i^(a_i).

It is zero for j<m. The identity follows by expanding (sum x_i)^j
and deleting terms that omit any variable. Expanding

 h_v(1-S)=(1-S)/(1-(1-v)S)
        =1-v*sum_(j>=1)(1-v)^(j-1)*S^j

gives R_v(n)=(-1)^(m-1)*v*sum_(j>=m)(1-v)^(j-1) A_j(x).
This series converges absolutely even at S=1, because 0<v<1.

The perfect-power stripper multiplies its degree-j coefficient by

 sum_(k|g) mu(k)*k^(j-1)
 = prod_(r|g,r prime)(1-r^(j-1)).

For j>=2 this has sign (-1)^omega(g); its absolute value is at most
g^(j-1). If g>1 its degree-one term is zero. Thus all atomic B_v(n)
have the same sign, independent of v, and

 |B_v(n)| <= U_v(m)/g,                    m>=2,          (1)
 |B_v(p^g)| <= (1-v)/g,                   g>=2.          (2)

To see (2), A_j(q)=1 for one variable q=1 and
v*sum_(j>=2)(1-v)^(j-1)=(1-v).

2. A UNIFORM BOUND AS THE NUMBER OF DISTINCT PRIMES GROWS

For positive q_i with sum<=1, put rho=v/(1-v). An elementary Laplace
representation of h_v gives

 |R_v(q)|=[v/(1-v)^2]*integral_0^infinity
        exp(-(rho+1)*y)*prod_i(exp(q_i*y)-1) dy.

Each factor increases with q_i. At fixed sum, log(exp(q_i*y)-1)
has negative second derivative, so its product is largest at equal q_i.
Its largest value over sum<=1 therefore has q_i=1/m. Define

 U_v(m)=[v/(1-v)^2]*integral_0^infinity
           exp(-rho*y)*(1-exp(-y/m))^m dy
       =[1/(1-v)]*m!/prod_(j=1..m)(m*rho+j).            (3)

The last equality follows by the substitution exp(-y/m) and elementary
integration by parts, or by finite binomial expansion. The derivative in
real m>0 of m*log(1-exp(-y/m)) equals
log(1-exp(-y/m))-(y/m)/(exp(y/m)-1)<0. Thus U_v(m) decreases in m.
Combining this representation with the degree-j bound in Section 1
proves (1), including every exponent pattern and every g.

Because the two atomic coefficients have the same sign, opposite weights
give the bound by the larger weighted magnitude, not their sum. Exact values
from (3) close the following unbounded regions:

 m>=4: |B| <= max((5/11)U_(1/3)(4),(16/11)U_(2/3)(4))/g
             <= 1/22 < 7/110.
 m=3,g>=2: |B| <= max(8/77,4/77)/g <= 4/77 < 7/110.
 m=2,g>=5: |B| <= max(5/22,16/55)/g <= 16/275 < 7/110.
 m=1,g>=8: (2) gives |B| <= max(10/33,16/33)/g
                         <= 2/33 < 7/110.

These are genuinely infinite tails of the arithmetic partition. A finite
sample in m, g, n or prime size is not substituted for them.

3. THE FINITELY MANY LOW-DIMENSIONAL REGIONS

The scalar ratio kernel of the signed operator is

 h(t)=9*t*(9*t+2)/(11*(t+2)*(2*t+1)).

For m variables define
F(x)=sum_(S subset {1..m}) (-1)^|S| h(1-sum_(i in S)x_i).
At gcd g the full coefficient is

 B_g(q)=sum_(k|g) mu(k)*F((k/g)q)/k.                    (4)

For m=1,g=2..7 this is an exact rational value, independent of the prime.
The checker computes all six values and verifies (G).

For m=2,g=1 and squarefree n, sum x=1. The previously verified pair
identity gives -K*z^2/((1+b_u*z)(1+b_v*z)), z=x_1*x_2,
K=140/99,b_u=4/3,b_v=1/6. It increases in magnitude for z in [0,1/4]
and has supremum 7/110 at z=1/4.

For m=2,g=1 with a repeated exponent, at least one exponent is >=2.
After swapping variables if necessary, 2*x_1+x_2<=1. It suffices to
check F(u/2,v) on u,v>=0,u+v<=1. For m=2,g=2,3,4 it suffices to
check (4) on q_1,q_2>=0,q_1+q_2<=1. For m=3,g=1 it suffices to
check F(q) on q_i>=0,sum q_i<=1, including all repeated exponents.

These are FIVE regions, each with two inequality sides. Introduce
nonnegative barycentric variables q_i and slack, T=sum variables, and
replace each argument t by (T-S)/T. Every atomic denominator is
v*T+(1-v)*(T-S)>0 for T>0, because 0<=S<=T and v>0.
Multiply the proposed bounds +/-B_g<=7/110 by the product D of these
strictly positive homogeneous denominators. The resulting homogeneous
polynomials P_+=7*D/110+H and P_-=7*D/110-H are computed exactly.

Certificates retain the primitive integer coefficients of each polynomial
and a nonnegative integer L for which T^L*P has ONLY nonnegative coefficients.
Since T>0, this proves the desired inequality throughout the closed simplex.
This is a Pólya-style coefficient certificate; the proof is the displayed
finite polynomial identity, not an appeal to a numerical solver or to the
general existence theorem. Nine sides certify at L=0. The remaining side
in the repeated two-prime region certifies at L=5.

The independent checker reconstructs (4) with rational polynomial arithmetic,
derives each denominator, checks positive scaling to the recorded coefficients,
and independently performs each multiplication by T. No production imports
are used for the mathematical audit.

4. COMPLETENESS AND SHARPNESS

Every composite has either m=1,g>=2; m=2,g>=1; m=3,g>=1; or m>=4.
The infinite tails in Section 2 and finite regions in Section 3 cover every
case. Therefore (G) holds for every composite integer, without a prime-size
cutoff or a presumed prime distribution.

Primes have coefficient one by the coefficient definition. The sharp pair
formula approaches 7/110 along distinct primes p,q with log p/log q->1.
Bertrand's postulate supplies q in (p,2p) for arbitrarily large prime p.
This imported classical theorem is used ONLY for the supremum's sharpness,
not the composite upper bound or prime value.

The operator's all-order logarithmic zero signature and any ordinary-zeta
continuation claim retain their separate analytic audit. Small positive
composite coefficients do not prove signed cancellation. The bound above
does not establish a new prime-distribution theorem or RH.
