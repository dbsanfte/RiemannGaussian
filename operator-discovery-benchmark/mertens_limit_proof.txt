1. A GENERIC PARAMETER LIMIT

For a compensated positive-order family with fixed rational c,b, set
S=sum c_j, B=sum b_j, Delta=B-S. Since T_0=delta_1-mu and
J_0(y)=y-1, its limit as a tends to zero is, at every fixed real x>=1,

 V_0(x)=S-B*x*m(x)+Delta*M(x),
 M(x)=sum_(n<=x)mu(n), m(x)=sum_(n<=x)mu(n)/n.               (1)

Thus a common-constant family bound |V_(a_q)(n)|<=C sqrt(n), every
n AND q, implies |V_0(n)|<=C sqrt(n). This deduction specifically
requires parameter power zero and log power zero. Positive log losses,
parameter bounds diverging as a tends to zero, and high-pass families
with S=B=0 are not rejected by this argument.

The bound at integers gives limsup |V_0(x)|/sqrt(x)<=C on the reals:
between consecutive integers the only change in (1) is -B*(x-N)m(N),
and |m(N)|<=sum_(d<=N)1/d<=1+log N=o(sqrt N).

2. CHARGE THE EXACT INVERSE BEFORE USING THE IMPORT

Assume S!=0. Exact partial summation gives xm=M+x integral_1^x M(t)/t^2 dt.
Writing F=S-V_0, r=B/S, lambda=1-r, the Volterra inverse is

 M(x)=F(x)/S-(r/S)*x^lambda integral_1^x F(t)t^(-lambda-1)dt.  (2)

This follows by differentiating the integral equation, or by substituting
and exchanging the finite-interval integrals. It retains every signed term.

If B=0, (1) is V_0=S(1-M) exactly, and its normalized M bound has
constant C/|S|. This applies to positive-degree families as well.

If r>1/2, then lambda<1/2. Formula (2), the real bound on V_0, and
the exact integral of t^(1/2-lambda-1) imply

 limsup |M(x)|/sqrt(x) <= C/|S| * [1+|r|/(1/2-lambda)].       (3)

The constant part of F, unit errors O(log x), and x^lambda endpoint terms
are o(sqrt x), including lambda=0 where logarithms occur.

If r<1/2 and B!=0, lambda>1/2. The uniform bound makes H_F(lambda)
convergent. The exact Mellin identity, first in Re(s)>1, continues to
Re(s)>1/2 and yields

 H_F(s)=S*(s-lambda)/[s*(s-1)*zeta(s)].                       (4)

Ordinary zeta has no real zero with real part >0; lambda!=1 since B!=0.
Therefore H_F(lambda)=0. Replace the integral in (2) by minus its
tail from x to infinity. The same calculation gives (3) with denominator
lambda-1/2. This uses a checked rational multiplier identity and a
consequence of the candidate premise, not an assumed RH estimate.

In summary the generic necessary inverse constant is

 K=1/|S| if B=0,
 K=[1+|B/S|/|B/S-1/2|]/|S| otherwise, B/S!=1/2.              (5)

The critical real inverse case B/S=1/2 and S=0 remain unsupported by
this norm argument. Their missing certificate is not a counterexample.

If C*K<=7/4, (3) contradicts the named Hurst consequence. The family
bound is globally false even if every finite point and parameter tested
passes. The gate is generic in c,b; it does not single out an archived id.
