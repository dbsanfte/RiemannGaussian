1. THE GLOBAL PRODUCT AND ITS ORDINARY ROOT-TYPE LIMITATION

For integer b>=2 let s_b(e) be the base-b digit sum and Omega the total
prime-factor degree. Define on arithmetic functions

 (f G_b g)(n)=sum_(ij=n) f(i)g(j)
        * 1[s_b(Omega(i))+s_b(Omega(j))=s_b(Omega(n))].            (1)

The indicator means every digit adds without a carry. For three factors,
either parenthesization is permitted precisely when, in every digit, the
sum of the three digits is less than b. This proves associativity.
Commutativity and delta_1 identity are immediate. Alternatively the
nonnegative potential V_b(n)=sum_(r>=1)floor(Omega(n)/b^r) has telescoping
defect V_b(ij)-V_b(i)-V_b(j); it vanishes precisely for this indicator.
For PRIME b this defect equals v_b(binomial(Omega(i)+Omega(j),Omega(i))).

That last prime condition matters. Replacing the indicator by binomial
nondivisibility modulo COMPOSITE b is wrong: at b=4 and degrees 1,1,2,
one parenthesization has nonzero weights binomial(2,1) and binomial(4,2),
while the other includes binomial(4,1)=0 modulo four. Its weights are
1 and 0, respectively, so the tempting replacement is not associative.
The true digit kernel gives zero on BOTH parenthesizations.

For h(1)=0, every nonzero k-fold G_b product requires k<=s_b(Omega(n));
this proves coefficient-local EXP and LOG. The standard nilpotent
formal identities therefore define inverse maps

 S_b(f)=EXP_(G_b)(LOG_D f),   S_b^(-1)(f)=EXP_D(LOG_(G_b) f),
 S_b(f D g)=S_b(f) G_b S_b(g).

They hold for ALL normalized formal inputs f(1)=g(1)=1. Their root law
is TYPED: S_b(f^gamma_D)=(S_b f)^gamma_(G_b). It is not a law for
ordinary OUTPUT roots. The exact b=3 counterexample at n=8 is

 S_3(zeta^(1/2)_D)(8)=1/6,
 ((S_3 zeta)^(1/2)_D)(8)=-1/48.

To check, S_3 zeta has local coefficients f(p)=f(p^2)=1, f(p^3)=1/3.
Its ordinary LOG at p,p^2,p^3 is 1,1/2,-1/3. The ordinary half-root
coefficient at p^3 is -1/6+1/8+1/48=-1/48, whereas the carry transfer
of the input root has only its prime-cube logarithm 1/(2*3).

The product couples distinct primes. At b=3, S_3 zeta(4)=S_3 zeta(3)=1
but S_3 zeta(12)=0. At 30 its three prime degrees overflow the lowest
digit, giving zero. This fails multiplicativity. It is not the usual
regular convolution that has historically been called "ternary".

2. ZETA COEFFICIENTS AND THE EMPIRICAL POLYNOMIAL GRAMMAR

LOG_D zeta is supported on p^e with coefficient 1/e. For 0<beta<=1,
EXP_(G_b)(beta LOG_D zeta) removes nonnegative terms from ordinary EXP.
Thus its coefficients a_(b,beta)(n) satisfy

 0<=a_(b,beta)(n)<=d_beta(n)<=1,

where d_beta is the ordinary zeta^beta coefficient. Its prime coefficient
is beta, so the ordinary series has absolute abscissa exactly one.
Convergence to the right follows from domination; divergence at or below
one already follows from the prime subseries. Initially on Re(s)>1 write

 F_(b,beta)(s)=sum_n a_(b,beta)(n)n^-s.

Grade by Omega. The grade-e primitive logarithm sum is beta P(e*s)/e,
where P(z)=sum_p p^-z. The grade product is x^i G_b x^j=x^(i+j) if no
base-b carry occurs, and zero otherwise. Exact graded EXP of unrelated
rational higher-grade inputs, with an anonymous grade-one coefficient z,
is sampled at z=0..6; held-out z=-3,7,8,11 check a fitted polynomial.
The recovered degree at b=2,3,4,5,7 is b-1, with a 49-model linear radix
degree fit and the scale-recursive leading coefficient checked separately.
These finite fits are evidence. The following proves their universal law.

3. LOG-ZETA POLYNOMIAL AND ITS SELF-SIMILAR LEADING COEFFICIENT

On Re(s)>1/2 set
 K=beta*sum_(e>=2)P(e*s)x^e/e, Q=sum_(e>=2)P(e*s)/e.
Their grade l1 norms converge locally uniformly. On a chosen zero-free
logarithmic patch put P_1=Log zeta(s)-Q(s), continuing the Euler branch.
The primitive grade-one element x has G_b-nilpotence index b. Consequently

 EXP_(G_b)(beta P_1 x)=sum_(j=0..b-1)(beta P_1)^j x^j/j!.

Let E=EXP_(G_b) K and H_j=eval_1(x^j G_b E). A grade n in E contributes
to H_j precisely when n mod b <=b-1-j. Thus

 F_(b,beta)(s)=sum_(j=0..b-1) (beta P_1)^j H_j(s)/j!.         (2)

All H_j are holomorphic on Re(s)>1/2. In the highest coefficient,
H_(b-1) uses just grades divisible by b. A no-carry product with lowest
digit zero has EVERY participating lowest digit zero. Restricting K to
these grades and rescaling n=b*r shifts off one base-b digit, preserving
the carry rule. Its primitive coefficients become

 beta P(b*r*s)/(b*r)=(beta/b)P(r*(b*s))/r.

Therefore the EXACT self-similarity identity is

 H_(b-1)(s)=F_(b,beta/b)(b*s),
 [ (Log zeta)^(b-1) ] F_(b,beta)
    =beta^(b-1)/(b-1)! * F_(b,beta/b)(b*s).                   (3)

The last coefficient has an ordinary absolutely convergent series and
holomorphic continuation on Re(s)>1/b, a larger domain than all the
lower coefficients together. This statement does not extend the full
F through all zeta zeros. Replacing P_1 by Log zeta-Q makes (2) a
degree b-1 polynomial in Log zeta with holomorphic coefficient functions.

4. A HEIGHT-UNIFORM NONZERO LEADING COEFFICIENT

The shifted operator in (3) has nonnegative coefficients dominated by
the ORDINARY root coefficients d_(beta/b). For b*s of real part b*sigma>1,

 |H_(b-1)(s)-1| <= zeta(b*sigma)^(beta/b)-1,
 |H_(b-1)(s)| >= 2-zeta(b*sigma)^(beta/b).                    (4)

For b>=3, 0<beta<=1 and sigma>=1/2 this lower bound is positive.
Indeed zeta(b/2)^(beta/b)<=zeta(3/2)^(1/3)<3^(1/3)<2, using
zeta(3/2)<1+integral_1^infinity x^(-3/2)dx=3. The Arb bound at b=3,
beta=1 gives a stronger lower bound exceeding 0.6226 on the critical
right half-plane. Bounds at the slightly smaller real parts of the two
old critical-zero disks also remain positive. Unlike a finite set of
sampled heights, (4) applies at EVERY height.

5. COMPLETE MONODROMY LADDERS AND A SINGLE-LOOP BLIND SPOT

Suppose a small loop surrounds a zeta zero of multiplicity m and the
coefficient functions in (2) are single-valued there. Each loop changes
P_1 by h=2*pi*i*m. For a polynomial of degree b-1, the (b-1)-st forward
branch difference annihilates every lower term. Its exact value is

 Delta_h^(b-1) F_(b,beta)=(beta h)^(b-1) H_(b-1)(s).          (5)

The height-uniform lower bound (4) makes this nonzero in the critical
right half-plane. Radix three requires THREE successive branch values
F_0,F_1,F_2, giving F_2-2F_1+F_0=-(2*pi*m*beta)^2 H_2.

A SINGLE pointwise branch jump is an incomplete test. At b=3, beta=1,
write (2) as c0+c1 P+(H_2/2)P^2. Choose a basepoint value
P_*=-c1/H_2-h/2. Then F(P_*+h)-F(P_*)=0 EXACTLY, while
F(P_*+2h)-2F(P_*+h)+F(P_*)=h^2 H_2!=0.
This is an algebraic obstruction to judging zero sensitivity from a
single returned scalar value after one loop.

The implemented control uses a whole zeta-zero-free disk near 3/4+1.37i
and inserts one model zero. A nonzero constant multiplier normalizes its
logarithm at the basepoint to this P_*. The coefficient functions are
fixed from the zeta-derived criterion. This is NOT a claim that applying
S_b to a different arithmetic input leaves those coefficient functions
unchanged. The first-jump cancellation is the exact constructed-model
identity; numerical balls illustrate it, rather than prove cancellation
of an unspecified infinite function. The second difference is rigorously
nonzero by (4),(5).

For b>=3 and 0<beta<=1, a direct RH criterion uses
Z=(s-1)zeta(s), Z(1)=1, on D={Re(s)>1/2}.
Define, initially to the right of one,

 Phi_(b,beta)=sum_(j=0..b-1) beta^j H_j/j! * (Log Z-Q)^j.

This is a polynomial pole correction, not just multiplication by s-1,
and is not an arithmetic transform of a claimed Dirichlet series for Z.
RH implies Z is zero-free on simply connected D, hence Phi is holomorphic
and single-valued there. Conversely, any zero in D gives the nonzero
branch difference (5), forbidding that continuation. Thus this is an
RH-equivalent continuation criterion. Its required continuation remains
unproved. The bound on H does not itself exclude any zeta zero.

7. NATIVE APPROXIMATIONS AND CAUCHY ERROR BOUNDS

For Re(w)>1 use P_M(w)=sum_(m<=M)mu(m)LOG zeta(m*w)/m. The omitted
complex modulus is at most
u^(M+1)/((M+1)(1-u))*(1+2/((M+1)Re(w)-1)), u=2^-Re(w).
This follows from the Euler logarithm and the integer integral bound.
All numerically evaluated k>=2 arguments in these global patches have
k*sigma>=3/2. Since |Euler LOG zeta(w)|<=log zeta(Re(w))
<=log zeta(3/2)<log 3<pi, their principal LOG equals the Euler branch.
The separate old-zero disks have k=2 across Re(w)=1; their continuation
instead uses the explicitly certified Euler-to-local LOG corridors.

At grade cutoff D define the finite analytic primitive series h_MD by
 h_k=beta P_(M_k)(k*s)/k, 2<=k<=D,
 h_1=beta LOG zeta(s)-sum_(k=2..D)h_k.
The source uses M_k=min(32,max(1,floor(96/k))); the independent checker
uses min(40,max(1,floor(128/k))). These cutoffs are fixed per grade,
so each route defines ONE analytic finite approximant on the entire disk.
The source obtains EXP by a grade Euler recurrence; the checker uses
finite EXP powers with a Lucas-binomial-modulo-three product kernel.

Let delta=sum_(k=2..D)error(P_(M_k)(k*s))/k. The higher-prime primitive
tail is bounded by
 Qtail=(1+2/((D+1)sigma-1))*2^(-(D+1)sigma)
                         /((D+1)(1-2^-sigma)).
The primitive grade l1 error is E=|beta|(2delta+Qtail); its grade-one
error is E1=|beta|(delta+Qtail). With C0=sum_(k<=D)|h_MD,k|, the finite
EXP error is at most E exp(C0+E) by submultiplicativity of the carry
Banach algebra. For R>1 with R*2^-sigma<1, its true weighted primitive
norm is at most

 C_R=R(|h_MD,1|+E1)+|beta| R^2/(2(1-R*2^-sigma))
                          *(2^(-2sigma)+2^(1-2sigma)/(2sigma-1)).

The missing EXP grades have modulus at most exp(C_R) R^(-D-1).
The source uses R=2^(sigma/2), the checker R=2^(sigma/3).
Summing these two errors yields U for the infinite operator.
For the leading-component controls at sigma=3/4, the missing coefficient
grades start at D-(b-1)+1. Its weighted tail multiplies the corresponding
EXP bound by R^(b-1)/(b-1)!, which is at most one for b>=3 and either
chosen R. Removing grade one only decreases the primitive norm. Thus
the displayed full-operator U also bounds those leading components.

On rectangles, high-degree P_M and individual LOG zeta terms are
enclosed by |P_M(k*s)|<=2^(-k*sigma)(1+2/(k*sigma-1))+its Mobius tail,
and |LOG zeta(m*k*s)|<=2^(-m*k*sigma)(1+2/(m*k*sigma-1)). These are
enclosures of the SAME finite analytic approximant, not terms set to
zero or assumptions about interval numerics. They avoid slow native
interval evaluation. Point and quotient values use native evaluations.

To certify Rouche, let c be the candidate, r the disk radius, R0 the

8. PUBLICATION-HISTORY SCOPE AND THE NEXT SEARCH GATE

Global b=2 extends to a radix family by a known digit-carry potential,
formal LOG/EXP and truncated graded algebras. These ingredients do not
establish a new primitive. The specific nonlinear global-degree transfer,
scale recursion, native spurious-zero application and complete ladder
criterion have unresolved historical originality.

Do NOT equate this with the "ternary convolution" in Snellman, Greedy
Regular Convolutions, arXiv:2504.02795v4 (14 August 2026), equation (6),
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.02795. That known REGULAR primewise product
requires shared-prime exponents to be even. Its delta_p times delta_p
is zero, whereas G_3 gives delta_(p^2). Its name is not a match to the
global radix-three kernel. This distinction also does not prove novelty.
Nonvanishing weighted convolutions and their cocycle equation have
prior art, e.g. Popa Fotino, Generalized convolution ring of arithmetic
functions (1975),
https://msp.org/pjm/1975/61-1/pjm-v61-n1-p11-s.pdf.
That paper's classification assumes a nowhere-zero kernel. It is
framework evidence, not a classification theorem applied directly to
this degenerate carry indicator.

This is the nineteenth verified known-kernel consequence, with ten
candidate families retained. Verified-historically-new operators remain
zero. The detector now rejects composite-radix binomial nondivisibility,
misidentified regular ternary prior art, ordinary-output-root fidelity,
and single-point/single-loop zero-sensitivity tests. Full branch ladders
and actual-zero-free controls are required before assigning a new
operator a root-sensitive RH role. The research objective stays active.
