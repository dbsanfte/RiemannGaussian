3. A PROVED TWO-PRIME ARITHMETIC ESTIMATE AT EVERY SCALE

Let b_u=b(u)>b_v=b(v)>=0. Substituting (1) in (2) cancels the term
linear in z and gives

Thus every product of two distinct actual primes satisfies

 |B_uv(p*q)|<=K*[log(P^-(p*q))/log(p*q)]^2.               (4)

No cutoff, prime-size ratio or presumed prime distribution is needed.
Since z^2/[(1+b_u*z)(1+b_v*z)] is increasing for 0<=z<=1/4,
its sharp supremum is K/[16(1+b_u/4)(1+b_v/4)]. Prime pairs whose
logarithm ratio tends to one approach it; Bertrand's theorem supplies
such pairs. This is a coefficient estimate, not a prime-count estimate.

Every prime still has coefficient one. On EVERY composite the atomic

The certificate only asserts strict prime separation when this upper

5. AN ALL-ZERO-ORDER TEST FOR SIGNED COMBINATIONS

The old local theorem gives logarithmic coefficient m/v^m at a zero of
order m, under its zero-free corridor and locally uniform integral-tail

 c_m=m*sum_i w_i/v_i^m.                                 (7)

The pair in (2) has c_m=0 exactly when

Both sides are positive rationals and v/u>1, so there is at most one
positive integer solution. Factor numerator and denominator exactly and
compare their signed prime valuation vectors. Equality in (8) holds iff
the target vector is the same positive integer multiple of the base vector.
Extra prime support or inconsistent exponent ratios rules it out for EVERY
integer m, not merely a sampled range. This exact finite factor certificate
is distinct from primality used in the coefficient engine, which has none.
