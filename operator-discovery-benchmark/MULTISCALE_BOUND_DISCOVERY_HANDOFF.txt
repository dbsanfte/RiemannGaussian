What the executable discovers
Given only a scalar observation callable x(n) and an integer base b, it starts
with x(n) and generates sections x(b^k*n+r). It proposes rational linear state
laws. Validation and independently produced all-index counterexamples cause
new sample constraints and structural state expansion. No successful companion
state, transition matrix, metric or bound exponent is passed to the generator.

From proposed transitions T_r it forms A=sum_r T_r. It automatically discovers
the target's observable row space R and exact reduced dynamics R*A=L*R. This
avoids demanding small sums of auxiliary coordinates invisible to the target.
It searches rational alpha=p/q and positive definite Q satisfying
 (L^q)^t Q L^q <= b^(2p) Q.
Finite Gram sums and rational Stein equations construct Q. Candidate exponents
include square, cube and sixth roots, without rounded eigenvalue proofs.

For recursive benchmark definitions, a separate implementation proves the
state laws on the COMPLETE reachable source space. It checks leading-zero/unit
consistency, exact observable recovery, strict PD, PSD, all R^t Q R crosses,
finite phases, and base-digit recovery of EVERY integer prefix. The general
induction/block theorem is a supplied mathematical rule; its constructions,
applications and certificates are searched by the executable. This is a
specific finite-section grammar, not an unrestricted mathematical inventor.

Frozen withheld experiment
Producer, checker, adapter and protocol bytes were frozen before eleven source
instances were materialized. The generator received anonymous source callables;
source definitions went only to evaluation and independent proof checking.
Expected categories were opened after all benchmark proof attempts. The source
designer knew the grammar: this is controlled implementation-level discovery,
not analyst-blind novelty or an estimate of RH success probability.

Ten of eleven benchmark cases received checked all-integer prefix bounds.
Eight were sublinear: two alpha0 logarithmic bounds, three square-root bounds,
two alpha2/3 bounds and one alpha5/6 bound. All eight failed to close when
auxiliary-state generation was disabled. Two noncancelling controls received
linear bounds in both searches. No historical novelty is claimed.

Actual arithmetic result and important exposure limit

     -R_u*K*mu_(2/3)-(9/2)mu, K(n)=n^(-1/3),
with x(n)=f(n+1). The unit and complete signed coefficients are retained.
This is a DIRECT full-core alternative; it does not numerically isolate Z033.
The inherited identity g=f-e, all paid terms and opposite drifts remain in the
unchanged parent. A future successful direct bound would still need its exact
transfer to the requested parent norm and exponent. No such transfer is claimed.

Both base2 and base3 searches hit the ten-state cap without a closed state law.
Crucially, their actual queries covered only original coefficients1..63 and
1..71 (48 and61 distinct queries). They stopped before large-scale validation.
The archive's horizon262143 is availability, NOT achieved scale exposure.
The learner's rational proposal cap256 also restricts its fitted laws; a failure

Thus the run demonstrates new bound-synthesis capability on recursive sources,
but supplies ZERO new global arithmetic relations and ZERO core bound payments.
It would be misleading to present the benchmark count as evidence that the
current detector is powerful enough to solve the joint remainder.

Next substantive capability target
The bottleneck is now explicit: invent and prove arithmetic state laws that
can retain prime/cofactor information as their parameters change. Increasing
the same finite-section cap alone would not establish suitability for this
purpose. Candidates may require parameter-dependent states, products, signed
pairings or growing prime/cofactor families. These extensions must generate
their constructions and attempt their proof obligations, with exact original
source recovery. A primitive source identity and a numerical recurrence fit
remain different evidence types. Further calibration cannot replace a bound
on the actual complete arithmetic target.
