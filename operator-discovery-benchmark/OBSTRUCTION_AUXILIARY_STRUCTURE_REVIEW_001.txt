------------------------

frozen study, source, result and analytical memo. I read the current
SEMANTIC_POTENTIAL_DISCOVERY_HANDOFF.txt, its contract and generator, the
legacy exact state language, and the candidate-10 analytical review. No
new arithmetic evaluation, numerical experiment, test, hash, or parameter

The next search should generate a triple: an operator, a nonnegative
auxiliary/storage state, and an exact argument reducing a NAMED unresolved
term. The main priority is a checked recurrence, improved exponent, or
unconditional bound for an actual mixed source term. Finite constant gains
and semantic labels are secondary. Merely expressing the same remainder
in a longer AST is a reduction, not a new bound.

For candidate 10, the best concrete integration point is its exact joint

partition or the already proved outer hyperbola decomposition. Distinct

No full uniform moment bound is proved here. The new exact largest-prime

1. Fixed target, complete square, and the current proved frontier
----------------------------------------------------------------
Keep u=1/3, v=2/3, w=1/(uv)=9/2 and actual Mobius source mu. Define
  a_b=mu*(n^(-b)), h_b=(a_b-delta)/b, p=h_u*h_v,
  S(x)=sum_(n<=x)p(n)=D(x)+T(x)+C(x),
  R(x)=T(x)+C(x)-m_u x^(2/3).
The frozen output is
  J(x)=R(x)+D(x)-m_v x^(1/3).
The complete same-prime D and the smaller pole term have known finite
critical weighted L2 costs. Thus cofinal subpower full moments of R and J
are equivalent, with the finite head and enlarged constants included.
They have not been proved. Removing a component from an analytic proof
does not change a frozen candidate's output or finite scores.

Put B(x)=C(x)-m_u x^(2/3). The actual next square is
  E_R(X)=E_T(X)+E_B(X)+2<T,B>_X,
  <f,g>_X=X^(-2)sum_(X<=n<2X)f(n)g(n).             (1.1)
The proved T(x)>=c x/log x eventually makes its separate energy large.
The required anticorrelation is not optional. Bounding E_T and E_B
separately by bulk estimates and dropping <T,B> cannot prove this target.
A new name for T+B or its negative correlation is not a new theorem.

The other proved frontier is the complete outer hyperbola identity in the
candidate-10 review. Its kernel remainder obeys |Delta_K(y)|<=19/2. On
the short side U=x^(1/3), the full convolved error is
  O(x^(1/3)(1+log x)),
with a finite critical norm. The corresponding two single-channel short
errors are O(x^(1/9)) and O(x^(-1/9)). These are valid paid leaves.
The following remaining terms are NOT paid leaves:
  weighted mu*mu and mu finite-prefix mismatches at 2/3 and 1/3;
  the short prefixes M_2(U), M(U);
  the coupled long-side T_K-T_u-T_v;
  the rectangle -M_2(U)K(V)+M(U)[S_u(V)+S_v(V)].     (1.2)
The mismatch against 1/zeta(1/3) is not an ordinarily convergent tail.
Even a conjectural pointwise square-root M bound does not justify treating
the 1/3 series as such a tail. It remains a finite-prefix/analytic-value
compensation with its whole partners.

The new renewal review also proves
  (r_u*r_v)*p=(r_u-delta)*(r_v-delta)/(uv)>=0,
where r_b=a_b^(-1) has positive coefficients. Its kernel has x log x
mass and is unbounded on critical weighted L2, including on a positive
compact input. This closes the NATURAL ABSOLUTE-CONTRACTION route as an
invalid argument. It neither proves nor refutes a signed solution of (1.1).

3. Joint state and exact distinct-new-prime transport
----------------------------------------------------
Let b=a_u*a_v, and use the coefficient vector
  z(n)=(b(n),a_u(n),a_v(n))^T.
All three sequences are multiplicative. For a prime p not dividing m,
put rho_u(p)=1-p^(-u), rho_v(p)=1-p^(-v). Then exactly
  z(pm)=D_p z(m),
  D_p=diag(-(rho_u(p)+rho_v(p)),-rho_u(p),-rho_v(p)).               (3.1)
This is actual source arithmetic. The selected coefficient for n>1 is
  p(n)=w(1,-1,-1)z(n),
while p(1)=0. Equivalently
  S(x)=w[B_0(x)-A_u(x)-A_v(x)+1],                (3.2)
where B_0 is the prefix of b and A_b the prefix of a_b. The unit correction
is indispensable. In the selected p basis the same identity reads, m>1,
  p(pm)=-(rho_u+rho_v)p(m)
           -w[rho_v a_u(m)+rho_u a_v(m)].        (3.3)
Thus a coefficient-only rule for p which discards the other two source
channels is false. Introducing them as AUXILIARIES is legitimate; assuming
they already satisfy separate RH-strength estimates is not.

Prime-power transfers have their complete local diagonal coefficients
z(p^e); they are not all given by D_p^e. In particular b(p^e) may have a
different sign because of the overlap of the two convolution factors.
A generated bank must include those local terms when its domain permits
p^2<=x. A squarefree-only transfer cannot be extended to all n by analogy.

For an energy argument, retain the entire joint Gram matrix
  G_X=X^(-2)sum_(X<=n<2X) Z(n)Z(n)^T,
for the chosen complete centered/partitioned prefix state Z. A selected
energy is a specified quadratic projection c^T G_X c. The off-diagonal
entries are arithmetic covariances, not unconstrained PSD parameters to
fit. A nonnegative storage can be generated as a finite sum of exact
coupled squares (l^T Z)^2 with nonnegative fixed weights; that automatically
certifies nonnegativity without inventing a numerical Gram inequality.

4. A concrete complete largest-prime reduction
----------------------------------------------
Fix real x>=1 and y>=sqrt(x). Partition the coefficients of S(x) by their
largest prime factor. Let S_sm(x;y) retain every n<=x with all prime
factors <=y, including all powers and overlaps. If a largest prime p>y
occurs, it occurs exactly once, and n=pm with m<=x/p<p. Thus p does not
divide m and all prime factors of m are below p automatically. The entire
other part, with no unlisted factor-order complement, is
  L(x;y)=-sum_(y<p<=x/2)[(rho_u(p)+rho_v(p))S(x/p)
                   +w(rho_v(p)[A_u(x/p)-1]
                        +rho_u(p)[A_v(x/p)-1])].                 (4.1)
The upper x/2 is exact because p at a prime is zero. Every argument on
the right is at most sqrt(x). Directly in the joint basis, the same formula
is minus w times
  sum_(y<p<=x/2)[(rho_u+rho_v)B_0(x/p)
                         -rho_u A_u(x/p)-rho_v A_v(x/p)].         (4.2)
The m=1 terms cancel in (4.2); the explicit minus-one corrections in
(4.1) express that cancellation without an implicit convention.

Every same-prime D term belongs to the smooth part, since p^2<=x requires
p<=sqrt(x)<=y. Consequently the COMPLETE reduced target is
  R(x)=[S_sm(x;y)-D(x)]+L(x;y)-m_u x^(2/3).        (4.3)
This is a useful operator/auxiliary/argument family. It converts one
large-prime sector into smaller arguments through a proved source law.
It does not call the smooth sector subcritical: numbers with many small
prime factors can have full-size signed bulk. Nor does it prove the
weighted prime sum in (4.1) is small by independently bounding its channels.

At an integer doubling block one may choose, for example, a single
Y(X)=ceil(sqrt(2X-1)) to make (4.1) valid for every n in that block.
Child blocks must use Y(2X). If an auxiliary distinguishes smooth and
large sectors, the primes between the old and new cutoffs generate a
COMPLETE reclassification boundary, including their source coefficients,
all newly allowed quotient domains, and every mixed square with the old
state. Combining the two sectors leaves S unchanged, but their storage
energies do change. Freezing the parent cutoff in the child or bounding
only the newly entering primes drops terms from the full argument.

For cutoffs below sqrt(x), the analogous rule must sum all powers p^e
and impose the actual smaller-prime restriction on m. Those domains and
local coefficients are valid grammar objects, but their omitted complements
cannot be inferred from the simpler (4.1). A minimal first implementation
should keep the simpler above-square-root partition and its exact complement.

5. Auxiliary families generated from the obstruction
----------------------------------------------------
Each proposed auxiliary should carry its parent obligation and the exact
subexpression it is intended to transform. Four small structural productions
are enough to create new search ability without another coefficient fit.

(a) COMPLETE PARTITION / RECOMBINATION.
Partition a named signed sum by literal prime-power/composite support,
largest-prime sector, coprime factor placement, or hyperbola short/long/
rectangle domains. Generate a coupled square from two or more resulting
pieces, and retain their exact recombination into R. A partition changes
the unknown's structure; it proves no size estimate by itself. Smooth
mainterms stay attached to the complete projection unless an exact residue
identity supplies an allocation. Fitting independent pole shares to each
sector is not an argument.

(b) SOURCE-LAW TRANSPORT AND ITS DEFECT.
Apply (3.1), the full prime-power law, or an actual coprime Mobius identity
to a selected summand BEFORE squaring. Generate the transported state and
the entire defect/complement. A new prime bank then controls a named
cross term in (1.1), rather than appearing because bank states were cheap
on finite samples. Moving domain corrections become explicit obligations.

(c) SHARED HYPERBOLA / QUOTIENT COMMUTATOR.
Use the already paid short kernel errors to generate a joint long state
from (1.2), with its rectangle and weighted-prefix mismatches. Mutations
change a factor placement or cutoff through an exact difference identity;
the newly created commutator is the next obligation. Taking absolute
values on separate T_K,T_u,T_v abandons the shared-source cancellation.
The old semantic search could write a quotient commutator, but did not
bind it to this particular paid frontier and its argument dependencies.

(d) SIGNED SQUARE COMPLETION / TELESCOPE.
Use 2ab=(a+b)^2-a^2-b^2 or the exact prefix increment
  F(n)^2-F(n-1)^2=2F(n-1)g(n)+g(n)^2,
  g(n)=p(n)-[the complete smooth-center increment].                (5.1)
Generate storage from the coupled factors in the residual 2F g, not from
an arbitrary target copy. All endpoint and weighting differences remain.
The identity exposes the needed signed source correlation; the positive
g^2 term alone is not a full drift estimate. Point multiplication outside
a whole square can lose a mu sign; signed factors must occur inside the
specific coupling whose cross is being transformed.

Weights can come from exact source laws, discrete fibers, a fixed small
rational structural menu, or an exact square completion. None should be
chosen by a numerical optimizer or refitted to final responses. The search
may reject a candidate whose new auxiliaries import harder unexplained
RH-scale channels; it must first record them as unresolved rather than
pretending their inclusion in storage proves them.

6. What an argument certificate must actually prove
--------------------------------------------------
A candidate with nonnegative V and W=E_R+V preserves the complete target.
If for cofinal q and every dyadic X a checked argument proves
  W(2X)-W(X)<=B_q X^(1/q),                         (6.1)
then telescoping, including W(1), proves the required subpower E_R bound.
The initial center is not generally zero. Every stored component and
child source domain must be recomputed at the child scale.

A stronger route is a joint smaller-scale recurrence. An argument might
prove nonnegative energies E_j obey
  E_j(X)<=sum_(k,d) a_jkd(X) E_k(X/d)+B_jq X^(1/q),                (6.2)
with a_jkd(X)>=0, strictly smaller admissible scales, exact dyadic covering costs,
and a complete finite head. To use induction, it must supply positive
h_j and a uniform rho_q<1 such that
  sum_(k,d) a_jkd(X)h_k d^(-1/q)<=rho_q h_j.       (6.3)
This is a genuine absorption/exponent test. Constants may be large for
large q; they must remain finite for every member of a cofinal family.
Smaller arguments alone do not prove (6.3). A coefficient sum greater
than one may impose a positive growth exponent and block cofinal q.
A coefficient sum equal to one can still give rho_q<1 through d^(-1/q),
so a uniform strict gap at q=infinity is sufficient but not necessary.

Scale-dependent recurrence coefficients require an exact all-scale product
or summation bound. For example logarithmic losses can be subpower, whereas
a fixed factor >1 repeated at every doubling gives a positive power.
Such distinctions are analytically checked, not extrapolated from sampled
coefficients. Multiple non-dyadic quotient arguments require their full
covering map; monotonicity of a signed block energy cannot be assumed.

An exact identity which maps the full target to another full target is
recorded as an EQUIVALENT REDUCTION. A paid subcritical remainder, a reduced
unresolved source domain, or a verifiable recurrence strengthens that
reduction. None by itself closes the bound. A finite verified square
identity proves its finite instance, not its all-scale quantifiers.

7. Adversarial filters: aliases, cycles, and incomplete pairing arguments
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Every obligation record needs the original source, full domain/endpoints,
center, signed expression, exponent and quantifiers, together with every
premise imported by its proof rule. Exact canonical payload equality,
Dirichlet algebra, recombination identities and inverse rule pairs detect
aliases without hashes. Finite response agreement cannot establish an
alias. R renamed as a parity balance or a Gram cross remains the same
unknown unless a new estimate is actually proved.

The dependency graph must mark a chain
  R moment -> M or A_b RH-scale moment -> RH -> R moment
as circular if none of its RH-scale nodes has been established independently.
Similarly a square-root M_2 estimate cannot pay (1.2) for free. Back edges
are allowed for a valid scale induction, but only with strictly decreasing
scales, complete base cases and a checked absorption condition such as
(6.3). An unexplained recursion over the same scale is not induction.

An arithmetic involution can be a useful argument production: add/remove
a prime in a coprime factor configuration, or pair two source terms with
opposite signs. It must retain ALL weight differences, repeated-prime
cases, outside-domain partners and unmatched boundary terms. Pairing n
with pn typically leaves a fresh interval near the cutoff. Interior
cancellation does not prove that this boundary is subcritical. For the
joint state, (3.1) shows exactly which weights and source complements are
left when a new prime is paired; they cannot be declared equal by sign alone.

There is a practical analytic falsifier for overly local arguments. A
squarefree sign source which agrees with actual mu at finitely many small
primes but has positive signs at all remaining primes obeys those same
small-prime coprime laws, squarefree support, and |source|<=1. Its Dirichlet
series has a positive pole at one. Actual-zeta center terms with exponents
below one cannot remove the resulting bulk. Therefore an alleged full moment proof
using only those finitely many laws and generic envelopes must also fail
on that source, or must identify which additional actual-source premise
excludes it. It has not obtained global signed cancellation merely by
finding a fixed small-prime pairing. This does not forbid using small-prime
rules as components of a proof that also retains unbounded-prime laws.

8. Minimal concrete integration with the existing repository workflow
---------------------------------------------------------------------
The smallest useful implementation has three linked payloads, all using
ordinal IDs and complete equality comparisons rather than new hashes.

An OBLIGATION payload names R's exact complete square or a residual term
in (1.1)/(1.2), lists paid component lemmas, and marks unresolved source
claims. It includes source/domain/center and the cofinal quantifiers.

An AUXILIARY payload reuses arithmetic ASTs but records its production
from that obligation: complete partition, transport-plus-defect, shared
hyperbola, or square completion. It preserves E_R and stores only proven
nonnegative additions. Integer selectors and real algebraic weights have
different types; their transition and child-domain obligations are explicit.

An ARGUMENT payload is a short composition of exact rules: sum expansion,
source multiplicativity under a checked coprime guard, domain bijection,
hyperbola with rectangle, exact square completion, signed summation by
parts, paid component inequality, dyadic cover, and recurrence closure.
The checker verifies the identities and costs independently and returns
the remaining obligations; a rule cannot mark an imported unknown lemma
proved. Initially these rules can be narrow, including (3.1), (4.1) and
the candidate-10 paid hyperbola frontier, rather than a general theorem
prover or another broad expression sweep.

Generation should consume the checker's residual obligations. If a cross
remains, partition or transport one of its factors and generate the full
new cross/defect. If a boundary fails, generate compensation using its
actual source domain rather than enlarging a numerical constant. If a
recurrence coefficient blocks cofinal exponents, seek a source-law or
coupled cancellation that changes that coefficient, not a fitted weight.

Selection first rewards a checked closed component, lower unresolved
exponent, valid smaller-scale transfer, or fewer independent unresolved
source claims. Exact equivalent reductions are retained as useful leads
but cannot outrank an actual bound merely by lowering finite constants.
Finite arithmetic evaluation can reject proposed constants at observed
cases and help order open leads within the same proof-progress class;
it does not set all-scale coefficients, exponents, or proof status.
The earlier scoped semantic annotations and exposure planner remain
useful diagnostics, with their existing limitations stated.
