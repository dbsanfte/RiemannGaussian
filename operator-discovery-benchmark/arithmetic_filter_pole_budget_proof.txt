Conditional fixed-filter pole-budget theorem for actual arithmetic

Fix nonnegative integers r,l with k=r+l>=1. Define a=mu^{*r}*lambda^{*l} by complete ordinary Dirichlet convolution, with the unit sequence used at arity zero. In Re(s)>1, absolute Euler products give D_a(s)=zeta(2s)^l/zeta(s)^k. A(x)=sum_{n<=x}a(n) denotes the actual unsmoothed prefix.

For a fixed finite exact rational filter, first merge repeated positive integer divisors and remove zero weights. Write its distinct nonzero terms as (d_j,w_j), 1<=j<=m. G(x)=sum_j w_j A(floor(x/d_j)) is the prefix of b(n)=sum_{d_j|n}w_j a(n/d_j). Hence D_b(s)=P(s)D_a(s), where P(s)=sum_j w_j exp(-s log d_j), initially in Re(s)>1. There are no moving indices or omitted channels in this identity.

At any complex point s0, if P and its first m-1 derivatives vanished, the vector (w_j exp(-s0 log d_j)) would be killed by the m-by-m Vandermonde with entries (-log d_j)^i, 0<=i<m. Distinct positive divisors have distinct logarithms, so this matrix is invertible. Every entry of that vector is nonzero, a contradiction. Thus a zero of nonzero P has multiplicity at most m-1 globally.

Suppose rho is a zero of ordinary zeta with Re(rho)>1/2 and multiplicity nu>=1. The numerator zeta(2rho)^l is nonzero (for l=0 it is 1), since Re(2rho)>1 and the absolutely convergent Euler product is nonzero. The reciprocal denominator contributes pole order k*nu. The filter can cancel at most m-1 orders. For m<=k the resulting pole order is at least k*nu-(m-1)>=k-m+1>0. Thus this class retains every possible ordinary off-critical zero regardless of its unknown multiplicity. This is sufficient admission, not a converse: a filter with m>k may also retain all relevant zeros, but this gate does not prove it.

Now assume a genuine complete cofinal critical filtered energy estimate: exists q0>=1, every integer q>=q0 admits finite Kq>=0 independent of X, every dyadic X>=1 satisfies sum_{n=X}^{2X-1}|G(n)|^2<=Kq X^(2+1/q). For a compact set Re(s)>=sigma>1/2, choose q so sigma>1/2+1/(2q). Cauchy-Schwarz bounds the dyadic integral/sum of |G| with weight t^(-sigma-1) by a constant times X^(1/2+1/(2q)-sigma); the geometric dyadic tail converges uniformly. The Mellin integral s integral_1^infinity G(t)t^(-s-1)dt therefore defines a holomorphic D_b(s) throughout Re(s)>1/2 and agrees with the original Dirichlet series on Re(s)>1. G is piecewise constant between integers; exact integer energy estimates control that integral. Analytic continuation contradicts every retained pole above. It follows that ordinary zeta has no zero with Re(s)>1/2; its functional equation yields RH. This conclusion is CONDITIONAL on the unproved all-scale/cofinal filtered bound. Neither ordinary A-energy recovery nor a fixed-divisor inverse norm is needed for this implication.

Sharpness of the universal filter multiplicity budget: P(s)=(1-2^(-s))^k has k+1 nonzero distinct terms with exact integer weights (-1)^j binomial(k,j), divisors 2^j. It has k-fold zeros at s=2*pi*i*n/log 2. The example demonstrates that the m-1 bound is sharp for arbitrary complex points. Those zeros lie on Re(s)=0; this is not a claim that this specific filter cancels an ordinary off-critical zeta zero, nor a mathematical refutation of filters outside the sufficient gate.

Actual target semantics matter. The theorem does not apply to an arbitrary sequence whose observed samples resemble a, to independently fitted signs, or to a moving divisor multiplier. Exact prime identities and complete convolution define the target, not finite data. Zero filters carry no information. Duplicate aliases must be canonicalized before counting support. Passing this certifier proves only a conditional bridge, never the filtered estimate or RH itself. No actual score or held-out numerical claim is made here.

A sharpness example within the relevant half-plane is P(s)=(1-3*4^(-s))^k. Its k+1 terms have divisors 4^j and weights (-3)^j binomial(k,j). It has a zero of exact order k at sigma=log(3)/log(4), strictly between 1/2 and 1 because 2<3<4. The exact derivative moment check is sum_j (-3)^j binomial(k,j) (1/3)^j j^t=0 for 0<=t<k, and equals (-1)^k k! for t=k (up to the nonzero derivative factors (-log4)^t). Thus the universal filter zero budget is sharp even inside the half-plane of interest. This asserts no ordinary zeta zero at sigma or at any zero of this filter. It shows why support m>k is unsupported by this uniform gate, not why such an actual filtered target fails.
