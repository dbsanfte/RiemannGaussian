1. Unconditional analytical inputs
-----------------------------------
Write M(t)=sum_(n<=t)mu(n), pi(t)=#{prime p<=t}.  All upper boundaries
mean their exact integer floors; t itself may be real.  We use:

 (M) For every fixed B>0,
     |M(t)|<=C_B t/(log t)^B for all sufficiently large t.

This follows from the unconditional Mertens estimate in Ethan Simpson
Lee and Nicol Leong, "New explicit bounds for Mertens function and the
reciprocal of the Riemann zeta-function," Theorem1.1, equation(9), with
its general version in Theorem2.2.  The displayed bound has order
t log(t) exp(-c sqrt(log t)), which implies (M).  This is the same primary
input used in MOBIUS_PRIME_STORAGE_ASYMPTOTIC_REVIEW_073.txt.
[Lee and Leong, primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.06141)

 (P) pi(t)=t/log t+t/(log t)^2+O(t/(log t)^3)
     as t->infinity, uniformly for all sufficiently large real t.

For a checked primary unconditional source, Tim Trudgian, "Updating the
error term in the prime number theorem," Ramanujan Journal39 (2016),
225-234, Theorem2, bounds |pi(t)-li(t)| by an explicit constant times
t(log t)^(-3/4) exp(-sqrt(log t/6.455)) for t>=229.  This error is smaller
than t/(log t)^3 eventually.  Two integrations by parts give
li(t)=t/log t+t/(log t)^2+O(t/(log t)^3): the remaining integral of
1/(log v)^3 is O(t/(log t)^3), as follows by splitting at sqrt(t).
Thus (P) is an immediate sufficient consequence of that primary theorem.
[Trudgian, primary paper, Theorem2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.2689)

These imported unconditional theorems do the analytical work.  They are

2. Exact unique-large-prime identity, with every factor gate
-----------------------------------------------------------
Define

 A_mu(x,y)=sum_(n<=x,P+(n)<=y)mu(n), P+(1)=1,
 U=x/y,

and suppose y>=1 and y>=sqrt(x).  Every n<=x has at most one prime
factor>y, counted with multiplicity: two such factors would have product
>y^2>=x.  In particular a prime square p^2 with p>y is outside the domain.

A nonsmooth n with nonzero mu(n) therefore has a unique representation
n=pm with p>y prime and m<=x/p<x/y<=y.  Every prime factor of m is<=m<y,
so p does not divide m: the coprimality and largest-prime gates hold
automatically.  Hence mu(pm)=-mu(m).  Conversely, any integer m<=U and
prime y<p<=x/m satisfy these gates and create exactly one nonsmooth n.
Squareful m have mu(m)=0; their terms are retained as zeros.  Squareful n
also contribute zero.  The unit is in A_mu and M and is never deleted.

Counting the primes with the strict lower and closed upper endpoints gives
the exact finite identity

 A_mu(x,y)=M(x)+sum_(m<=U)mu(m)[pi(x/m)-pi(y)]
         =M(x)+sum_(m<=U)mu(m)pi(x/m)-pi(y)M(U).   (6)

If m=U happens to be an integer, x/m=y and its bracket is exactly zero.
If y itself is prime, it is excluded from the large-prime sum because
pi(y) is subtracted.  No separate prime-endpoint or square correction
is implicit.  This is the specialization of the exact largest-prime
identity in MOBIUS_PRIME_BLOCK_REVIEW_073.txt, equations(6)-(7).

Using a real algebraic y or floor(y) in the smooth indicator gives the
same prime mask.  We retain the real y in (6), since integer primes p>y
are exactly the primes p>floor(y).  The cofactor cutoff U=x/y then has
the zero-bracket convention just explained.  There is no numerical
rounding of a cutoff anywhere in this argument.

4. Exact truncation and payment of both cofactor/boundary tails

Split the bracketed sum in (6) at the real T:

 H_short=sum_(m<=T)mu(m)pi(x/m)-pi(y)M(T),

These two terms exactly add to the complete sum in (6); in particular

 H_tail=sum_(y<p<=x/T, p prime)[M(x/p)-M(T)].      (9)

m<=U follows automatically from p>y.  If x/p=T, the bracket is zero,
so a prime at the displayed upper boundary causes no error.  The strict
lower cutoff m>T agrees exactly with M(x/p)-M(T), including floors.

Define epsilon(T)=sup_(t>=T)|M(t)|/t.  Input(M) gives
epsilon(T)<=C_B/(log T)^B when T is sufficiently large.  Both arguments
x/p and T in (9) lie in this domain.  A harmonic all-integer bound and

 |H_tail|<=epsilon(T)[x sum_(y<p<=x/T)1/p

          <=epsilon(T)x(2+log x).               (10)

No prime reciprocal asymptotic, unproved cofactor independence, or M

5. The two exact signed reciprocal moments and quantitative tails
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Input(M) proves ordinary convergence of

 V_0=sum_(m>=1)mu(m)/m,
 V_1=sum_(m>=1)mu(m)log(m)/m.                    (14)

For example, partial summation bounds their tails beyond T by

 |sum_(m>T)mu(m)/m|=O_B((log T)^(1-B)),
 |sum_(m>T)mu(m)log(m)/m|=O_B((log T)^(2-B)),     (15)

for B>2.  The first boundary is M(T)/T; its integral is bounded by
C_B integral_T^infinity dt/[t(log t)^B].  For the second boundary use
M(T)log(T)/T; the derivative of log(t)/t has magnitude at most
(1+log t)/t^2.  The resulting integral is of order (log T)^(2-B).
Both boundaries are included, and the estimates apply to real T.

For s>1 the absolutely convergent Euler product gives

 sum_(m>=1)mu(m)m^(-s)=1/zeta(s),
 sum_(m>=1)mu(m)log(m)m^(-s)=-(1/zeta)'(s).       (16)

Partial summation with input(M) makes the tails of both series uniformly
small for1<=s<=2: replace t^(-s) by at most t^(-1), and bound its
derivative by2t^(-2); for the logarithmic series the derivative is at
most(1+2log t)t^(-2).  These give the same integrable majorants as in
(15).  Consequently the s->1+ limits in (16) equal the ordinarily
convergent sums in (14); an unsupported Abel-limit identification is
not being substituted for convergence.

The simple pole of zeta at1 implies that its reciprocal has a simple
zero with derivative1 there.  More explicitly,
1/zeta(s)=(s-1)+O((s-1)^2), with analytic remainder.  Thus

                         V_0=0, V_1=-1.         (17)

For an elementary justification of the analytic qualification, partial
summation of sum n^(-s) gives
zeta(s)=s/(s-1)-s integral_1^infinity {t}t^(-s-1)dt for real s>1.
The bounded fractional-part integrand extends the integral analytically
to Re(s)>0, since its derivatives insert only fixed powers of log t.
Therefore zeta(s)=1/(s-1)+h(s) with h analytic near1, and its reciprocal
is (s-1)/[1+(s-1)h(s)].  Its derivative at1 is exactly1, without a
critical-strip zero assertion or a differentiated informal O estimate.

The negative sign in the log moment is essential.  Differentiating the
Dirichlet series for1/zeta contributes a minus sign, and the derivative
of1/zeta at1 is+1.  With B=16 and log T=L^(1/4), the truncated sums obey

 sum_(m<=T)mu(m)/m=O(L^(-15/4)),
 sum_(m<=T)mu(m)log(m)/m=-1+O(L^(-14/4)).        (18)

No absolute convergence of either series at1 is asserted or required.

6. Uniform short-cofactor expansion and its absolute remainder
---------------------------------------------------------------
For every m<=T, put u=log m.  Then0<=u<=L^(1/4),
log(x/m)=L-u, and x/m>=x/T->infinity.  Input(P) holds throughout this
entire range with one constant.  Since u/L->0 uniformly,

 1/(L-u)=1/L+u/L^2+O(u^2/L^3),
 1/(L-u)^2=1/L^2+O(u/L^3).

The geometric first expansion has a denominator bounded away from zero
for large L; the second follows by the mean-value theorem.  Substituting
these into(P) gives, uniformly for all m<=T,

 pi(x/m)=(x/m)[1/L+(u+1)/L^2
                              +O((u^2+u+1)/L^3)]. (19)

Sum (19) against the actual mu(m).  The leading terms keep their signs;
only the remainder is bounded absolutely.  Its total is at most

 O(x/L^3)sum_(m<=T)(1+log m+(log m)^2)/m
 =O(x(1+log T)^3/L^3)
 =O(x/L^(9/4)).                                 (20)

The elementary harmonic/log-moment bounds here follow by integration
and hold also for a noninteger T.  There is no claim of cancellation
inside this error.  In particular, this is why the full cofactor range
m<=U cannot simply be Taylor-expanded and its error summed absolutely:
log U is of order L, which would not give (20).  The signed tail was
paid first by (9)-(11).

Using (18) in the two leading terms yields

 sum_(m<=T)mu(m)pi(x/m)
 = (x/L)sum_(m<=T)mu(m)/m
  +(x/L^2)[sum_(m<=T)mu(m)log(m)/m
                         +sum_(m<=T)mu(m)/m]
  +O(x/L^(9/4))
 =-x/L^2+O(x/L^(9/4)).                          (21)

For clarity, the error from the first reciprocal tail is
O(x/L^(19/4)), and the logarithmic reciprocal tail contributes
O(x/L^(22/4)); both are much smaller than (20).  The +1 from the
second PNT term multiplies V_0=0 and does not change the coefficient.

Combine (6), (8), (11), (12), (21) and the bound for M(x).  This proves

 A_mu(x,y)=-x/(log x)^2+O_theta(x/(log x)^(9/4))  (22)

in the domain(7), with complete factor gates and all boundary/tail
terms paid.  Equation(3) is (22) at the four admitted source endpoints.
The preceding weaker O(k/log^(3/2)k) review does not imply this equivalent;
the new exact tail reordering and signed log moment supply the missing
second-order information.

8. All-scale exclusion for every constant and every finite base
----------------------------------------------------------------
Suppose some finite real C paid the full q=1 recurrence

 F(k)-F(floor(k/2))<=C for EVERY integer k>=2.    (26)

Telescoping the actual floor chain to1 gives

       F(k)<=F(1)+max(C,0)floor(log_2 k).         (27)

Nonnegative F excludes a genuine negative C along k=2^h; replacing C
by max(C,0) merely makes the displayed contradiction valid for every
proposed finite real value.  But (25) divided by k gives a lower bound
lambda k/[64(log k)^4], and its ratio to1+log k tends to infinity.
This contradicts (27).  The stronger asymptotic lower bound is (5).

The same contradiction excludes an eventual recurrence valid only for
k>=K_0 with any fixed finite K_0 and all smaller bases paid: telescope
to the finite base set and use its finite maximum.  Hence, for EVERY
finite C, the complete frozen F has infinitely many positive drift
violations above C.  Otherwise the recurrence would hold eventually.
This last statement does not identify their numerical locations and
does not claim that every sufficiently large complete-state drift is
positive.  Other terms can affect individual drift differences while
remaining unable to cancel the positive level lower bound.

For the one ray alone, its normalized q=1 energy is
lambda R(k)^2/k~lambda k/[16(log k)^4].  Its own floor-parent drift is
therefore~lambda k/[32(log k)^4].  That observation is not an asymptotic
formula for the full candidate's drift; (25)-(27) are the rigorous
full-candidate exclusion that does not require such a formula.

More generally the SAME fixed positive ray rules out every fixed q<2:
E(k)/k^q is bounded below by a positive constant times

to another candidate only if it actually contains this ray or another

9. Necessary grammar change and the remaining open obligations
----------------------------------------------------------------
For a fixed finite list of smooth exponents in(1/2,1), a direction using
only those coordinates and Z has

     =2(sum_i c_i)k/(log k)^2

because the same input(M) gives Z(k)=O_B(k/(log k)^B) at the four source
endpoints for every fixed B.  If sum_i c_i is nonzero, any fixed positive

Z/high-smooth ray family.  The active ray has sum c_i=1/8 and fails it.

After the preceding sector-nullspace conditions, balanced sector terms
reduce exactly to an additional multiple of Z, so this condition also
applies to rays of that balanced form when their remaining smooth
coordinates all lie in the present range.  It is not asserted for an
arbitrary ray also containing theta<1/2 coordinates: those could have
other second-order coefficients, and their signed interaction has not
been analyzed here.  Similarly, passing sum c_i=0 only removes THIS
leading bulk coefficient; it does not prove a drift law.

A next grammar can impose these proved, profile-specific necessary constraints
before scoring, or use a fully specified exact centered signed stencil
with its complete remainder price.  Retuning C is mathematically ruled

dyadic drift is bounded.  Further logarithmic terms or a valid complete

truncation may be called paid merely because its first bulk term cancels.

This review proves an unconditional obstruction for the specified raw
storage ray.  It proves neither a lower bound for |Z(k)| nor an
RH-strength upper bound for Z.  It does not disprove RH; it excludes a
chosen positive storage formula that adds a deterministic smooth bulk
mode before dividing by k.  The full source recurrence after a legitimate
grammar change, the joined head/core transfer, and RH remain open.
