Notation and the complete target

Let M(n)=sum_{j=1}^n mu(j), M(0)=0, and
  E(X)=X^-2 sum_{t=X}^{2X-1} M(t)^2.
Let chi(n)=mu(n)^2=1_{n squarefree}, for n>=1. All four selected states have
weight1 and contain the lagged sum L(t)=M(floor(t/2)). Their potentials and
full objectives are V_i(X)=X^-2 sum_{t=X}^{2X-1} U_i(t)^2 and
W_i(X)=E(X)+V_i(X). In particular E is never replaced by a masked or lagged
energy. Every t endpoint below is inclusive, and X is a positive integer.

The exact selected states are
  ID4:  U_4(t)=L(t).
  ID25: U_25(t)=L(t)*mu(floor(sqrt(t))).
  ID26: U_26(t)=L(t)*mu(floor(t^(1/3))).
  ID73: U_73(t)=L(t)*mu(floor(t^(1/3)))*mu(t).
There is no explicit X inside these particular U trees; child evaluation
still uses its own interval [2X,4X-1]. Roots are exact integer floors.

ID4: exact lagged-energy recurrence, including the initial exception

For X=2Y, t=2s and t=2s+1 each have floor(t/2)=s. As t runs through
[2Y,4Y-1], s runs through [Y,2Y-1], with multiplicity EXACTLY2. Therefore
  V_4(2Y)=(4Y^2)^-1*2*sum_{s=Y}^{2Y-1} M(s)^2=E(Y)/2.
Consequently, at every dyadic X>=2,
  V_4(X)=E(X/2)/2,
  W_4(X)=E(X)+E(X/2)/2,
  W_4(2X)-W_4(X)=E(2X)-E(X)/2-E(X/2)/2.                 (1)
Equivalently, if Delta E(X)=E(2X)-E(X), the last expression is
Delta E(X)+Delta E(X/2)/2. It is a fixed lag filter of the original signed
energy increments, not an arithmetic cancellation identity controlling them.

X=1 has only t=1 and L(1)=M(0)=0, so V_4(1)=0. E(0) is NOT defined and
the displayed lag formula must not be extended to X=1 by inventing E(1/2).
All four selected states likewise vanish at t=1. Since mu(1)=1,
  V_i(1)=0 and W_i(1)=E(1)=1, for i=4,25,26,73.
For ID4 the initial innovation is E(2)-E(1)/2; that expression retains both
the complete initial anchor and the child's lag storage. W(1) is not omitted.

Writing e_j=E(2^j), j>=0, an assumed upper bound epsilon_j on ID4's full
innovation at X=2^j, j>=1, would imply
  e_{j+1} <= (e_j+e_{j-1})/2 + epsilon_j.
The homogeneous recurrence has characteristic roots1 and-1/2. This explains
the lag smoothing, but supplies no bound on the actual epsilon_j. It neither
proves monotonicity nor replaces the missing signed arithmetic estimate.

IDs25,26,73: exact squarefree-mask reductions

Define fixed integer weights
  a_25(t)=chi(floor(sqrt(t))),
  a_26(t)=chi(floor(t^(1/3))),
  a_73(t)=chi(floor(t^(1/3)))*chi(t),
and a_4(t)=1. Then, identically for actual mu,
  V_i(X)=X^-2 sum_{t=X}^{2X-1} a_i(t)*M(floor(t/2))^2.   (2)
The signs of the EXTRA factors mu(root) and mu(t) do not survive the final
square: their direct contribution is precisely these zero/one gates. This
does not remove signed information from L(t). Expanding L(t)^2 still gives
all mu(n)*mu(m) cross terms, including their signs, for n,m<=floor(t/2).
The root index can also occur inside L(t), so this observation does NOT say
that globally flipping a source coefficient leaves the whole potential
unchanged. It says that the additional scalar multiplication introduces a
squarefree mask rather than a new retained signed cross interaction.

The root masks are piecewise constant on complete integer intervals
  [h^2,(h+1)^2-1] for ID25, and [h^3,(h+1)^3-1] for ID26.
ID73 additionally masks individual t by squarefreeness. No interval endpoint,
root-boundary term or masked-out cost is silently discarded from (2). A
density statement for squarefree integers alone cannot replace the weighted
sum: its required correlation with the lagged M(s)^2 remains unknown.

For an exact paired version set
  b_i(s)=(a_i(2s)+a_i(2s+1))/2, and
  E_{b_i}(Y)=Y^-2 sum_{s=Y}^{2Y-1} b_i(s)*M(s)^2.
Here b_i takes values0,1/2,1; b_4 is identically1. Pairing the two t values
gives, for every even X>=2,
  V_i(X)=E_{b_i}(X/2)/2,
  W_i(2X)-W_i(X)=E(2X)-E(X)
                   +(E_{b_i}(X)-E_{b_i}(X/2))/2.        (3)
This is a masked lagged energy with complete normalization, rather than
bounded local padding. Formula (3) includes any root-mask change across the
pair2s,2s+1 by using BOTH mask values. Its masks are not constant statistical
density factors or a parent cutoff reused by the child.

Pointwise domination and its limits

For every positive X,
  0<=V_73(X)<=V_26(X)<=V_4(X), and 0<=V_25(X)<=V_4(X).
There is no analogous automatic order between V_25 and V_26. Each W_i>=E.
These orders do NOT order the full signed innovations: subtracting the parent
potential can reverse a comparison. Thus an innovation estimate for ID26
does not automatically pass to ID73 with the same constant, nor does a smaller
potential automatically provide a stronger signed cancellation mechanism.

For dyadic X>=2 all four satisfy V_i(X)<=E(X/2)/2. The elementary
|mu(n)|<=1 bound gives only V_i(X)<=V_4(X)<X. No scale-independent O(1)
bound follows from the AST or its squarefree gates. Conversely, this memo
does not prove these actual-mu potentials unbounded. Their exact structural
classification is lagged or masked lagged energy; a stronger actual-mu bound
would itself require an additional estimate for M.

As a formal sanity distinction, on the diagnostic source identically1 every
extra source multiplier is1 and all four ASTs reduce to ID4. There M(s)=s,
and V_4(2Y)=(2Y^2)^-1 sum_{s=Y}^{2Y-1}s^2>=Y/2. Hence these ASTs are not
universally bounded-padding identities for arbitrary bounded scalar sources.
This algebraic comparison is not an actual-mu counterexample or an experiment.

Precise missing signed estimates

The required obligation for each fixed selected candidate remains:
  exists integer q0>=1 such that, for EVERY integer q>=q0,
  there exists finite C_q>=0, independent of dyadic X>=1, with
  W_i(2X)-W_i(X)<=C_q*ceil(X^(1/q)).                     (4)
For ID4, at dyadic X>=2 the left side is exactly (1). For IDs25,26,73 it
is exactly (3), with the specific b_i above. Equivalently, for all four it is
the complete difference
  (4X^2)^-1 sum_{t=2X}^{4X-1}[M(t)^2+a_i(t)M(floor(t/2))^2]
    -X^-2 sum_{t=X}^{2X-1}[M(t)^2+a_i(t)M(floor(t/2))^2]. (5)
The parent term is signed and cannot be dropped, made positive, replaced by
a separate square, or bounded by a finite fitted constant without proof.

No identity in this review establishes (4). For example, pointwise
V_i<=E(X/2)/2 leaves open both the unmasked and masked energy increments.
If the cofinal upper-bound premise were proved, ordinary dyadic telescoping
would retain W_i(1)=1 and give E(X)<=W_i(X)<=K_q*X^(1/q), with finite q-
dependent K_q. Conversely, such an all-dyadic bound on E for cofinal q would
already bound all these W_i by the same exponent, since their storage is
dominated by the lagged E. Their cofinal obligations are therefore tied to
the same substantive subpower energy control, not discharged by the lag
identity or positive storage alone. The conditional ordinary-zeta consequence
still requires that genuine all-scale premise and its full quantifiers.

ID73's stored C_q=0 at q2,3,6,12 is a FINITE training hypothesis only.
It is not a universal proof, not information about omitted q, and not a
license to assume W_73 is nonincreasing at all dyadic scales. If universal
zero innovation were actually proved, telescoping from W_73(1)=1 would give
E(X)<=W_73(X)<=1 at every dyadic X. That is a strong separate missing lemma.
Its finite training fit does not establish it or justify a favorite after
seeing final responses; this review has made no such nomination.

Disposition

ID4 supplies an exact lag recurrence. IDs25/26/73 supply precise squarefree-
masked versions, with ID73 losing the direct sign of its added mu(t) factor
on squaring. All preserve the complete target, W1, and the lagged M square's
signed cross terms. None supplies the missing all-scale signed innovation
bound. No universal-estimate, global-bound, RH or candidate-admission claim
is established, and all frozen-run evidence remains unchanged.
