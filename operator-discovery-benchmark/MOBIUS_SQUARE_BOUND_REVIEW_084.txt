2. Relation to the diagonal SDP: useful mechanism and limitation
For fixed R, minimize sum d subject to diag(d)-R>=0. Signed d are allowed;
PSD forces d_i>=R_ii. If the implementation additionally requires d>=0,
it searches a smaller legitimate grammar and must disclose that restriction.
For any factor Q, set d_i=(R+Q)_ii+sum_(j!=i)|(R+Q)_ij|. Then
 diag(d)-(R+Q) is symmetric diagonally dominant PSD: each offdiagonal
edge is |S_ij|*(e_i-sign(S_ij)e_j)*(...)^T. Hence
 diag(d)-R=Q+that PSD matrix>=0 and sum d=B(R+Q).
Thus no square-plus-Boolean certificate beats the full diagonal SDP optimum.
Conversely, any feasible diagonal majorant gives Q=diag(d)-R>=0 and the
formal exact square certificate has R+Q diagonal, with price=sum d.
Real factors can be approximated by rational/dyadic factors arbitrarily
closely for finite dimension; continuity of B gives convergence in value.
Rational d with a small positive identity slack also gives robust exact
PSD constructions. This is a real enlargement beyond L=0 coefficientwise
absolute pricing, but it remains a Boolean/correlation relaxation, not full
signed arithmetic. It is not a uniform theorem merely because dimension
and factor rank grow with u.

For a fixed operator, an exact dual witness X>=0 with diag(X)=1 gives
 tr(R X)<=sum d and therefore lower-bounds EVERY square/Boolean certificate.
Known anchor y0=1 poses no extra issue: quadratic forms are invariant under
y->-y; Boolean assignments can globally flip to anchor1, and their rank-one
correlations lie in this elliptope. This dual is a bound-cone obstruction,
not a counterexample using actual unknown mu values. To certify across all
operator theta, the dual must also price the full theta boxes and equality
constraints: for Rtheta=R0+sumtheta Z, certify inf_allowedtheta tr(Rtheta X).
A fixed-theta dual does not prove the detector's full operator grammar fails.
Rational factored PSD dual witnesses with exact diagonal1 are implementable,
though not required to establish validity of primal finite certificates.
