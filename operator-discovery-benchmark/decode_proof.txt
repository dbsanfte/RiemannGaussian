PRIME-SUM SECTOR DECODER: INFORMATION RECOVERY AND SHARP DOMAIN LOSS

Status: universal written proofs and independent exact/Arb audits.
The decoder is a linear partial coordinate permutation, not a unital
Dirichlet-algebra homomorphism. Its precise arithmetic application has
unresolved originality. No new estimate establishing RH is proved.

1. WHAT THE EMPIRICAL FIBRE SEARCH ACTUALLY FOUND

Use Phi_r(a,b)=ab(a+b)^r. The r=3 probe on integers 2..512 has 130816
unordered pairs and 44 output collisions. Its first is
  Phi_3(3,45)=Phi_3(16,20)=14929920.
Thus the integer index map is not injective. A further exact family, t>32,
is
  Phi_3(2(t-32),2t(t-1))=Phi_3(32(t-1),t(t-32)).
The first pair has twice the second pair's sum, and one eighth its product,
proving the equality for every integer t>32. These are not misrepresented
as collisions on prime-power inputs.

The bounded prime-power probe has 604 inputs <=4096 and 182710 unordered
pairs, with no collisions. A separate deep probe, prime bases<=97 and
exponents 1..12, has 300 inputs and 45150 pairs, also with no collisions.
The prime-only probe has 159330 pairs including diagonals and no collisions.
None of these finite observations proves injectivity on ALL prime powers.
That stronger claim remains unestablished and is not used below.

The decoder grammar scores 96 programs: exponent modulus 2..9, visible
prime count 1..4, and full/residue/quotient exponent modes. Five training
pairs leave two fits. Fresh fourth exponents reject the residue mode;
one full-exponent modulo-three decoder survives. Large held-out indices
are retained rather than avoided when they expose a factor-parser bug.

2. UNIVERSAL INJECTIVITY ON AN EXPLICIT PRIME-POWER SECTOR

Let a=p^e, b=q^f, p!=q, r>=2, with neither e nor f divisible by r.
Then gcd(ab,a+b)=1. In N=Phi_r(a,b), the valuations at p,q are exactly
e,f; every other prime valuation is divisible by r. Hence the primes
with nonzero exponent residues modulo r are exactly p,q. Recovering
their FULL valuations recovers a,b, and thus their unordered pair.

Conversely, on ANY prime-power input pair, the output has exactly two
nonzero exponent residues modulo r iff the inputs have distinct prime
bases and both exponents are nonmultiples of r. If the bases coincide,
only that single base can have nonzero residue: write, for e<=f,
p^e+p^f=p^e(1+p^(f-e)) for e<f, and 2p^e for e=f. All other prime
contributions from the sum are multiplied by r. Therefore a second
prime-power pair producing an admissible N must have the same two bases
and the same full valuations. No other prime-power fibre can contribute.

This is a universal uniqueness theorem on the stated sector, without
assuming any global prime-power injectivity or experimental cutoff.

3. THE LINEAR OPERATORS AND THEIR EXACT GENERIC LAWS

Let C_r be the set of m=p^e*q^f, p!=q, with r not dividing e or f.
Define E_r to push coefficient h(m), m in C_r, to Phi_r(p^e,q^f), and
zero elsewhere. Define D_r on all arithmetic coefficient sequences by
  (D_r h)(p^e*q^f)=h(p^e*q^f*(p^e+q^f)^r), m in C_r,
and zero off C_r. Each output asks for one finite input coordinate.
An equivalent encoded-index implementation recovers the two visible
prime powers from exponent residues and then VALIDATES their image.
The validation is necessary: N=6 has two visible primes but is not an image.

E_r is injective on sequences supported on C_r by section 2. It follows
  D_r E_r=Pi_Cr,
  E_r D_r=Pi_encoded,
where the two Pi operators are diagonal sector projections. Both composites
are idempotent. D_r and E_r are linear partial coordinate permutations and
are partial isometries in unweighted coefficient l2. D_r(delta_1)=0,
so a normalized-unit-group interpretation is explicitly rejected.

For augmentation inputs a,b supported on prime powers, let a_r,b_r discard
exponents divisible by r, and let diagonal keep same-prime interactions.
The universal identity is
  D_r K_r(a,b)=a_r *_D b_r - same_prime_diagonal(a_r,b_r).
Different prime bases yield the unique encoded fibre; equal bases are
discarded. This proves the identity on unrelated signed rational inputs,
not just the zeta coefficients. The independent checker enumerates ALL
possible sums s with s^r|N and solves the integer discriminant equation
X^2-sX+N/s^r=0. Its fibre mechanism imports no production residue decoder.

The coefficient map is not causal at the same prefix length. For example
the decoded coordinate 10 requests encoded coordinate 3430. An encoded
prefix through 2048 does not provide it. Unavailable input may not be
substituted by zero. Sparse exact queries are distinguished from a full
finite encoded Dirichlet-series cutoff.

On RAW zeta input u(n)=1, D_r(u) is simply the familiar indicator of C_r.
That action reduces to a known arithmetic mask. What is studied here is
the decoding of the zeta-derived sum-grafted correction, together with its
inverse laws and analytic domain loss; raw mask recovery is not novelty.

4. THE ZETA-DERIVED COEFFICIENTS AND ACTUAL SCALAR IDENTITY

For g=LOG_D u, g(p^e)=1/e and other coefficients vanish. Let
kappa_r=K_r(g,g), whose Dirichlet series Gamma_r was proved absolutely
convergent and holomorphic on Re(s)>2/(r+2) in pair_proof.txt. Put
t_r=D_r(kappa_r). Then universally
  t_r(p^e*q^f)=2/(e*f), m in C_r, and t_r(n)=0 otherwise.

The production route first enumerates the entire 182710-term bounded
prime-power correction, without pre-filtering it to the proposed decoder
law. It then obtains 3078 admissible exact queries through decoded index
8192. The independent complete-sum/discriminant checker validates EVERY
query, including possible prime-power fibres beyond the source input cutoff.
The result agrees with the universal two-prime formula through 8192.

Let L(s)=normalized Euler LOG zeta(s), initially Re(s)>1, and
  G_r(s)=L(s)-L(rs)/r,
  ell_r(x)=-log(1-x)+log(1-x^r)/r,
  Q_r(s)=sum_p ell_r(p^(-s))^2.
G_r is the Dirichlet series of g with r-multiple exponents removed.
Removing the same-prime diagonal from G_r^2 gives the actual scalar bridge
  T_r(s)=sum_n t_r(n)n^(-s)=G_r(s)^2-Q_r(s), Re(s)>1.

The decoded ordinary series has EXACT absolute abscissa 1. Convergence
above 1 follows from 0<=t_r(n)<=2. At m=2q for every odd prime q, its
coefficient is 2, so it diverges at sigma=1 by the divergent prime harmonic
series, and also at every smaller sigma. The latter classical fact follows
from zeta's pole and its Euler logarithm: the higher-prime-power contribution
is bounded as sigma approaches 1, while L(sigma) diverges. This is an
absolute-convergence theorem, not a maximal analytic-continuation claim.

Q_r is holomorphic on Re(s)>1/2 since |ell_r(x)|<=|x|/(1-|x|). Locally
it has a stronger continuation
  Q_r(s)=LOG zeta(2s)+B_r(s),
where B_r is holomorphic on Re(s)>1/3. Indeed ell_r(x)^2 has leading
coefficient x^2 for r>=2. Adding log(1-x^2) cancels that coefficient;
the remaining coefficient at x^k is bounded in modulus by k for k>=3.
Summing over primes proves the B_r domain. LOG zeta(2s) can continue
across any disk where zeta(2s) is nonzero, using an appropriate local sheet.

Six fresh complex points compare this scalar identity against decoded
coefficients through 4096, with proved infinite tails. The source Q_3
reference uses exact local-square coefficients and native zeta/Mobius
prime-zeta sums. The independent route uses coefficients through 6144,
prime-local Euler logarithms through prime 384 and a changed diagonal tail.
Every case rejects a coefficient-6 perturbation, including the smaller
independent perturbation. These are genuine native-zeta comparisons of
the decoded output, not comparisons of two finite coefficient routines.

5. NUMERICAL TAILS AND THEIR DOMAINS

For Re(z)=sigma>1, prime zeta equals
  P(z)=sum_(m>=1) mu(m)/m * L(mz).
After m=M the omitted tail is at most
  2^(-(M+1)*sigma)/[(M+1)*(1-2^(-sigma))]
  *(1+2/((M+1)*sigma-1)).
Use L(v)<=zeta(v)-1<=2^(-v)*(1+2/(v-1)) and the geometric majorant.
The source uses M=24 and local-square degree K=48. If q_k is the square
coefficient, 0<=q_k<=k. Its omitted prime sum is bounded by
  (1+2/((K+1)*sigma-1))*t^(K+1)
  *((K+1)/(1-t)+t/(1-t)^2), t=2^(-sigma).
All evaluated native logarithms in the six comparisons are on the Euler
half-plane and have the correct principal branch, since L(Re argument)<pi.

The independent diagonal prime-cutoff tail at P0 is at most
  P0^(1-2*sigma)/[(2*sigma-1)*(1-(P0+1)^(-sigma))^2].
The full decoded coefficient tail after N is at most
  2*N^(1-sigma)/(sigma-1).
Native ball integration is not needed in this stage. Every finite reference
has an explicit infinite error bound; printed balls include rounding error.

6. THE ROOT OBSTRUCTION REALLY RETURNS AFTER DECODING

Reuse the independently checked simple-zeta-zero disks near
1/2+14.1347251417i, radius 1/10000, and 1/2+21.0220396388i, radius 1/100000.
The encoded Gamma_3 is unconditionally holomorphic across BOTH disks,
because they lie in Re(s)>2/5. The decoded function T_3 is not single-valued
holomorphic there. A simple-zeta-zero loop changes G_3 by h=2*pi*i,
while Q_3 is single-valued on the disk, so its jump is
  Delta T_3=2*h*G_3+h^2=4*pi*i*G_3-4*pi^2.

At the right-hand boundary basepoint s0, every local logarithm sheet has
the same real part
  Re G_3=log|zeta(s0)|-(1/3)*log|zeta(3s0)|.
This is certified negative, and |Delta T_3|>=4*pi*|Re G_3|. The source
lower bounds are approximately 117.7468 and 145.5973. These are bounds
for the DECODED LOGARITHMIC SERIES jump, not full zeta-root jumps or
monodromy bounds for an exponential correction. No global Euler sheet of
the local basepoint log is asserted; the bound is independent of that sheet.

The source directly certifies zeta(2s) and zeta(3s) nonzero on the whole
small disks. The independent checker uses a first-degree zeta Taylor ball
and radius-1/32 Cauchy remainder for the basepoint modulus. It replaces
the actual zeta(3s) log by its unconditional Euler norm bound. A separate
radius-1/8 Cauchy variation controls zeta(2s) on the entire disk. Its jump
lower bounds are approximately 108.0995 and 138.4536. Thus Q_3's local
extension and the nonzero jump pass two distinct enclosure mechanisms.
The existing zeros are on the critical line; no off-line zero is inferred.

A consequence, not a new primitive, is the pole-cancelled criterion:
  Phi_r(s)=[LOG((s-1)*zeta(s))-L(rs)/r]^2-Q_r(s).
RH iff this initially right-half-plane function has single-valued holomorphic
continuation to Re(s)>1/2. If RH holds, (s-1)zeta is nonzero there and its
log exists in the simply connected domain. Conversely an interior zero
of multiplicity m produces a nonconstant quadratic-log monodromy; Q_r and
L(rs) are holomorphic there and cannot cancel it. This criterion supplies
no new estimate proving the required continuation.

7. SHARP DECODER NORM AND THE POWER-WEIGHT TRADEOFF

Let A_sigma be coefficient sequences with norm sum_n |h(n)|n^(-sigma).
For sigma>0, D_r:A_sigma -> A_tau is bounded IFF
  tau >= (r+1)*sigma.
If bounded, its EXACT norm is
  ||D_r||=6^(sigma-tau)*5^(r*sigma).
Proof: each encoded coordinate at N=ab(a+b)^r maps to m=ab. Its basis
norm ratio is m^(sigma-tau)*(a+b)^(r*sigma). Re-express it as
  m^((r+1)*sigma-tau)*(1/a+1/b)^(r*sigma).
For distinct prime-power bases, m>=6 and 1/a+1/b<=5/6. Both extrema
are attained at a=2,b=3, yielding the exact norm in the bounded range.
If tau<(r+1)*sigma, use a=2,b=q as q runs through infinitely many primes:
the ratio grows as a positive constant times q^((r+1)*sigma-tau).
This proves unboundedness, rather than extrapolating a finite norm plot.

In particular ordinary holomorphy of the encoded correction does NOT
pass through this decoder at the same Dirichlet weight. Linear coefficient
invertibility alone supplies no bounded operator on that analytic space.
For r=3 the available encoded norms have sigma>2/5, whereas the corresponding
bounded target weights require tau>8/5. Special decoded zeta coefficients
have the better abscissa 1, but still no unconditional critical-strip bound.

Consider ONLY pure power regularizations R_lambda D_r, multiplying the
decoded coefficient at m by m^(-lambda). Their sharp boundedness condition
is tau+lambda>=(r+1)*sigma. Their zeta-derived scalar output is T_r(s+lambda),
so every zero-related logarithmic obstruction shifts left by lambda.
To use the available source norms sigma>2/(r+2) and target tau=1/2 requires
  lambda>(3r+2)/(2(r+2)).
The critical-line obstruction then has real part
  1/2-lambda < -r/(r+2).
At r=3 these bounds are lambda>11/10 and shifted real part<-3/5. This
uniform tradeoff is for this decoder and these power weights; it is NOT
a no-go theorem for all possible arithmetic operators or RH approaches.
Multiplying coefficients by a power is a classical shift, not a new primitive.

8. FACTOR REPRESENTATION AND VERIFICATION SCRUTINY

The held-out N=2*65537*65539^3=36899117956198039606 exposes a native
representation issue. python-flint 0.9.0 returns factor entries
  (2,1),(65537,1),(65539,1),(65539,2).
The product is correct, but a last-entry dictionary loses an exponent.
Both implementations now SUM repeated prime exponents and require exact
product reconstruction. The independent checker also verifies every prime
component by its own integer factor routine. A separate regression retains
this beyond-word-size case. No generic trusted factor result is assumed to
be canonical without checking its representation and reconstructed product.
The parser fault affected this new stage; no earlier source uses this
fmpz-factor-to-dictionary pattern, as confirmed by a project-wide code search.
