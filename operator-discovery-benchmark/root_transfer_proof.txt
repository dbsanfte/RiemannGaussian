NONLINEAR TRANSFER BETWEEN DIRICHLET AND NO-CARRY ARITHMETIC

These are mathematical proofs with exact coefficient and inequality witnesses,
not theorem-prover certificates. The proposed operator's historical novelty
remains under review. Ordinary logarithm, formal exponential, infinitary
convolution and set-partition transforms each have prior art.

1. OPERATOR, DOMAIN AND UNIVERSAL LAWS

Let * denote Dirichlet convolution and A denote binary no-carry convolution:
  (f A g)(n)=sum_{ij=n, v_p(i)&v_p(j)=0 for every prime p} f(i)g(j),
where & is bitwise AND of the nonnegative prime exponents. This is exactly
the t=0, ell=2 carry product proved associative in root_proofs.txt. It has
identity delta_1. Arithmetic functions have rational values and positive
integer arguments. For each product define LOG and EXP by their usual
coefficient-local power series at the identity. If h(1)=0, every nonzero
term of its kth power has n>=2^k, for EITHER product. Therefore each
coefficient has only finitely many terms and the power-series identities
used in root_proofs.txt remain valid separately in both commutative algebras.

For f(1)=1 define
  T(f)=EXP_A(LOG_*(f)).
It has the coefficient-local inverse EXP_*(LOG_A(f)). For all normalized f,g,
  T(f*g)=T(f) A T(g),
because LOG_*(f*g)=LOG_*(f)+LOG_*(g), followed by the EXP_A addition law.
It is therefore a bijection of the normalized UNIT GROUPS with their
respective multiplication laws. These claims hold on all rational inputs.

T is nonlinear. For f=delta_1+delta_2, retaining n<=4 gives
  T(f)=delta_1+delta_2-delta_4/2,
  T(delta_1+2*delta_2)=delta_1+2*delta_2-2*delta_4.
The latter differs from 2*T(f)-delta_1. Also delta_2 A delta_2=0 but
delta_2*delta_2=delta_4. The ordinary Dirichlet algebra has no zero divisors:
if f,g are nonzero, their least nonzero indices i,j force
(f*g)(ij)=f(i)g(j)!=0. The no-carry algebra has a nonzero nilpotent.
Consequently these two algebras cannot be linearly isomorphic as algebras.
The operator is distinct from the NONZERO scalar-weight transports previously
audited. This fact alone does NOT establish historical novelty.

2. COEFFICIENTWISE ROOT-ORDER LIMIT

Let P_alpha^*(f) be the ordinary normalized power and write
  H_alpha=(P_alpha^*(f)-delta_1)/alpha.
Every coefficient of H_alpha is a polynomial in alpha, extended to alpha=0,
where its value is LOG_*(f). For alpha!=0 define the mixed-power program
  L_alpha(f)=P_(1/alpha)^A(P_alpha^*(f)).
For each coefficient, its finite binomial expansion is
  sum_k [product_{j=0}^{k-1}(1-j*alpha)/k!] H_alpha^{A k}.
This follows by multiplying binom(1/alpha,k) by alpha^k. It is a polynomial
in alpha at each fixed n, so it extends to alpha=0. At that point it equals
EXP_A(LOG_*(f))=T(f). Thus T is exactly the coefficientwise q->infinity
limit of the q-fold no-carry product of the ordinary qth root, alpha=1/q.
No analytic interchange of limits and infinite Dirichlet series is required
to establish this coefficientwise law.

3. EXACT LOCAL POLYNOMIALS AND INDEPENDENT COMBINATORIAL FORM

Apply this to u(n)=1. The local root coefficient is c_alpha(e)=(alpha)_e/e!.
Put d_e(alpha)=c_alpha(e)/alpha for e>=1. It is a polynomial of degree e-1
and d_e(0)=1/e. In a no-carry factorization of a prime exponent e, its set
bits must be divided among the nonunit factors. At most s_2(e) factors can
be nonunit. The polynomial construction in root_transfer_probe.py uses
this tighter termination bound, rather than a numerical truncation.

An independent formula labels the set bits of e by their actual values
1,2,4,... . For every set partition pi of these labels, let e_B be the sum
of bit values in a block B. The local q-root lift coefficient is
  h_e(alpha)=sum_pi [product_{j=0}^{|pi|-1}(1-j*alpha)]
                         *product_{B in pi}d_(e_B)(alpha).
For integer q, the |pi| blocks can be assigned to distinct factors in
(q)_(|pi|) ways; multiplying each local coefficient by its alpha factor
gives the displayed expression. This also shows degree h_e<=e-1 for e>=1.
At alpha=0 it reduces to
  h_e(0)=sum_pi product_{B in pi}1/e_B.
This is a set-partition transform of the prime-exponent logarithm coefficients.
The independent verifier computes the polynomials by this partition formula,
not by the weighted-polynomial convolution implementation.

4. MULTIPLICATIVITY, CONVERGENCE AND UNIFORM TAIL

For alpha=1/q with integer q>=2, h_e(alpha) is a sum of nonnegative terms
from the ordinary q-fold root product, with some terms removed by the
no-carry restriction. The unrestricted coefficient is exactly 1. Therefore
  0<=h_e(1/q)<=1 for every e and every integer q>=2.
Taking coefficientwise polynomial limits gives 0<=h_e(0)<=1 as well.
Each root lift is multiplicative, as is T(u): independently split the
finite convolution sums by prime. Hence their arithmetic coefficients are
bounded in absolute value by 1, and their Dirichlet series converge
absolutely for Re(s)>1.

Their local correction after dividing by zeta is
  R_alpha(x)=(1-x)*sum_{e>=0}h_e(alpha)*x^e,
with r_0=1, r_1=0, r_e=h_e-h_(e-1). The bounds above give |r_e|<=2 for
alpha=1/q (integer q>=2) or alpha=0. Thus on |x|<=r<1 its tail beyond
degree m is <=2*r^(m+1)/(1-r), uniformly in ALL those root orders.
The correction Euler product converges uniformly on compact subsets of
Re(s)>1/2 because its local factors are 1+O(p^(-2*s)), uniformly on the
required compact sets. This proves holomorphy there, before a zero audit.

5. EXACT BERNSTEIN CERTIFICATE: EVERY q>=2 AND THE LIMIT

Each computed r_e(alpha) is a polynomial on alpha in [0,1/2]. Change
variables to alpha=a+(b-a)*y for a certificate interval [a,b]. A polynomial
p(y)=sum_j c_j*y^j of degree d has Bernstein coefficients
  beta_k=sum_{j=0}^k c_j*binom(k,j)/binom(d,j), 0<=k<=d,
so p(y)=sum_k beta_k*binom(d,k)*y^k*(1-y)^(d-k).
The basis functions are nonnegative and sum to 1 on [0,1]. Consequently
  |p(y)|<=max_k |beta_k|.
All bounds and beta values in the certificate are EXACT RATIONAL NUMBERS.

For m=32 and r=71/100, summing these per-coefficient bounds times r^e and
adding the uniform tail gives the serialized rational S in
transfer-results/root_transfer_probe.json. It satisfies S<1; its decimal
diagnostic is approximately 0.6945190291. Hence
  |R_(1/q)(x)-1|<=S<1
for every |x|<=r, every INTEGER q>=2, and q=infinity (alpha=0).
This proves all these local factors are nonzero on the disk. Since
r>1/sqrt(2), every p^(-s) with Re(s)>1/2 lies in that disk. The full
correction products are therefore holomorphic AND NONZERO throughout
Re(s)>1/2. The corresponding transforms, including T(u), have exactly
the same zeros as zeta there, with the same multiplicities.

The Bernstein head bound holds for the whole real alpha interval, but the
tail proof above is stated ONLY for reciprocal INTEGER q and alpha=0.
No continuous-real-root-order theorem is asserted from this certificate.

6. ROOT ZETA ISSUE AND NOVELTY LIMIT

The construction transforms actual arithmetic root coefficients and gives
an exact analytic bridge to the half-plane relevant to RH. It preserves,
rather than constrains, the zeros there. RH progress would need a new
zero-free estimate or other usable global property of the transformed
function. No finite coefficient match or formal unit-group law supplies it.

The operator is a new candidate to this detector, assembled from classical
operations used in different arithmetic algebras. A first literature search
did not identify the exact transfer/zero-preserving statement, but absence
of a search hit is insufficient proof of historical originality. It remains
outside the verified-new list until that question is adequately resolved.

7. CERTIFIED ACTUAL-ZETA COMPARISONS AND ANALYTIC LIMIT

transfer_analytic_probe.py obtains H(n)=T(u)(n) from the generic arithmetic
LOG/EXP program. Independently it obtains h_e(0) from bit partitions and
forms the local correction polynomial of degree M. The two coefficient
routes are checked exactly through n=N. The reference function is the
actual zeta evaluated by Arb at new complex arguments, not a fitted model.

For sigma=Re(s)>1, |H(n)|<=1 gives series tail
  sum_{n>N}|H(n)|n^(-sigma)<=N^(1-sigma)/(sigma-1).
Set A=2/(1-2^(-sigma)). Each full or truncated local correction satisfies
  |R_p-1|<=A*p^(-2*sigma).
Both products have norm at most E=exp(A*S), where
  S=2^(-2*sigma)+2^(1-2*sigma)/(2*sigma-1)
majorizes the sum over all primes. The omitted primes beyond P contribute
  B=A*P^(1-2*sigma)/(2*sigma-1),
and their product differs from 1 by at most exp(B)-1. Replacing the local
factors for p<=P by degree-M polynomials changes their product by at most
E*K, by telescoping one factor at a time, with
  K=A*[2^(-sigma*(M+1))
       +2^(1-sigma*(M+1))/(sigma*(M+1)-1)].
Thus the total correction-product error is <=E*(exp(B)-1+K). The code
adds this times |zeta(s)| to the series tail. All reported comparisons use
Arb balls and these proved errors. A controlled coefficient perturbation
is rejected at every point, so this tolerance does not simply accept any
nearby Dirichlet series.

The q->infinity limit also holds analytically for correction products,
uniformly on compact subsets of Re(s)>1/2. At each fixed e the coefficients
are polynomials in alpha=1/q and converge to h_e(0). On a compact subset
with sigma>=sigma_0>1/2 their differences are dominated by a constant times
sum_p sum_{e>=2}p^(-e*sigma_0), a finite quantity. Dominated convergence
applied to this double sum gives convergence of local-factor corrections,
and standard telescoping/product estimates give uniform product convergence.
Multiplying by zeta gives locally uniform convergence away from its pole.
This is an analytic consequence of the coefficient bounds, not an assumed
interchange of unbounded root limits and numerical Dirichlet sums.
