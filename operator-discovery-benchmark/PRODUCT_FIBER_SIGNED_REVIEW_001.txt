PRODUCT FIBERS, PRIME/COMPOSITE SIGNS, AND COMPLETE CROSS-PRODUCT TRANSPORT

Scope and outcome
-----------------
This new analytical memo addresses
candidate10_integrated_prime_recovery_obligation.json. The source is actual
mu, u=1/3, v=2/3, beta=9/2. Previous studies and analytical memos remain
unchanged. No numerical response, arithmetic table, experiment, test, hash
or subagent was used. No complete uniform bound or RH theorem is obtained.

There are three useful exact reductions and two scoped obstructions.
  * The full prefix-square has a product-fiber identity, retaining every
    divisor, gcd, repeated prime and the complete main-term cross.
  * The entire product region hk<=X*B and the entire primitive-height<=B
    region have explicit absolute costs. Fixed/polylogarithmic B is paid.
  * Across products m and qm, prime adjunction gives an exact four-color
    bulk / three-color birth / prefix-exit identity, with the unit removed.
  * At squarefree m, every nonzero a(h)a(k) in the SAME product fiber has
    sign mu(m). Positive prime-allocation or divisor-orbit reweighting
    cannot pair opposite signs there. This is a proof, not parity folklore.
  * The squarefree-product projection is not a positive energy. Moreover
    repeated-prime fibers can have opposite signs, but the opposite branch
    can be power-suppressed or entirely outside the original prefix.

A next grammar must admit a real change of product prime parity, or a
repeated-valuation transition with its amplitude and outside support paid.
The lower-color birth channel is a concrete auxiliary correlation, not an
established cofinal estimate. The full centered smooth/common-bank credit
still remains open. Global/RH flags remain false.

1. Source, product geometry, and the centered square
----------------------------------------------------
Let a_b=mu*(n^(-b)), B=a_u*a_v, p=beta*(B-a_u-a_v+delta), and a=p-d,
where d is the COMPLETE same-prime prime-power part. Prefixes are inclusive
and below-one zero. Thus a(1)=a(q^e)=0; elsewhere a=p. Write
  A(n)=sum_(h<=n)a(h), R(n)=A(n)-c*n^kappa,
  c=m_u, kappa=2/3.
For X dyadic integer>=1 put N=2X-1 and
  W_X(h,k)=max(0,2X-max(X,h,k)),
  Psi_X(h)=sum_(max(X,h)<=n<2X)n^kappa,
  M_X=sum_(X<=n<2X)n^(2*kappa).
Every index and endpoint in these formulas is integer. Then EXACTLY
  E_R(X)=X^(-2)*[sum_(h,k<=N)a(h)a(k)W_X(h,k)
                  -2c*sum_(h<=N)a(h)Psi_X(h)+c^2*M_X].         (1.1)
The cross and main square are NOT removed by regrouping the quadratic part.

Define the complete product fiber
  Gamma_X(m)=sum_(h divides m)a(h)a(m/h)W_X(h,m/h).
The weight is zero if either factor is above N, so the quadratic term is
  sum_(m<=N^2)Gamma_X(m).                       (1.2)
It is generally invalid to replace Gamma_X(m) by (a*a)(m) times a weight
depending only on m: max(h,m/h), hence the divisor balance, remains.
All products through N^2 occur, even though the original source prefix
only requested coefficients through N.

For the actual input at theta>=3/4,
  R=K-L0+Lu+Lv-main=R_new+Lv.
The existing whole Lv bound makes R and R_new cofinal-equivalent by the
two-direction triangle/Young inequality. Their finite energy relation is
  E_Rnew=E_R+E_Lv-2<R,Lv>_X.                    (1.3)
This memo works with (1.1), which retains the full a source. Equation (1.3)
and the old D/smaller-pole corrections are kept when recovering the precise
input target. No individual Gram entry is identified with the whole energy.

2. Complete gcd and valuation parametrization
---------------------------------------------
Put h=g*r,k=g*s, gcd(r,s)=1. Then h*k=g^2*r*s, and
  Gamma_X(m)=sum_(g^2 divides m)
       sum_(r*s=m/g^2,gcd(r,s)=1)a(g*r)a(g*s)W_X(g*r,g*s).       (2.1)
There is NO condition gcd(g,r*s)=1. At a prime with product valuation e,
write gamma=min(v_q(h),v_q(k)); then
  0<=gamma<=floor(e/2),
  (v_q(r),v_q(s))=(e-2gamma,0) or (0,e-2gamma),
with the pair (0,0) included once when e=2gamma. This parametrizes every
shared/repeated prime exactly. It does not replace them by squarefree density.

For au/av alone, each nonunit prime-power local coefficient is negative.
Their pair sign at h,k is therefore
  (-1)^(omega(h)+omega(k))
   =(-1)^(omega(rad(m))+omega(g)).              (2.2)
That is not simply mu(rad(m)), because every shared prime contributes
twice on the two legs. For B and the linear combination p, higher prime
powers have additional signs; equation (2.2) is not imposed on a.

3. Two independently paid quadratic regions
-------------------------------------------
The actual coefficient envelope is |a(n)|<=beta*d2(n), including every
zero prime-power coefficient. Also d2*d2=d4, d2(n)^2<=d4(n), and
sum_(n<=T)d4(n)<=T*(1+log T)^3 for real T>=1, by ordered divisor counting.

(A) Let B>=1 and T=X*B. The ENTIRE low-product region, with all gcd and
repeated-prime sectors, has absolute normalized cost
  X^(-2)*sum_(h*k<=T)|a(h)a(k)|W_X(h,k)
     <=beta^2*B*(1+log(X*B))^3.                (3.1)
Indeed W_X<=X and sum_(h*k<=T)d2(h)d2(k)=sum_(m<=T)d4(m). Products with
a factor>N contribute zero automatically. For fixed or polylogarithmic
B(X), this is cofinal padding. The unresolved quadratic products may be
restricted to m>X*B after retaining this complete signed component and cost.

(B) In (2.1) let the primitive height H=max(r,s) be <=B. Since
d2(g*r)<=d2(g)d2(r), with no coprimality assumption, and g<=2X/H,
its complete absolute normalized cost is at most
  2beta^2*(1+log(2X))^3
          *sum_(r,s<=B,gcd(r,s)=1)d2(r)d2(s)/max(r,s)
     <=4beta^2*B*(1+log B)^2*(1+log(2X))^3.     (3.2)
For the last bound, ignore the gcd restriction, order the two legs, and
use sum_(r<=s)d2(r)<=s*(1+log s), then sum_(s<=B)d2(s)<=B*(1+log B).
The diagonal is included safely by the doubled bound. Fixed/polylog B
again gives a paid sector. These two regions overlap; an implementation
must use their union once, or a disjoint peel with no duplicated subtraction.

Equations (3.1)-(3.2) concern the quadratic term only. They do not authorize
discarding the linear main cross in (1.1), or treating the remaining
signed quadratic projection as a nonnegative energy. All full squares and
their original main terms are recovered before making an energy claim.

4. Exact squarefree sign and four nonempty prime-color bins
----------------------------------------------------------
For prime q define r_q=1-q^(-u)>0, s_q=1-q^(-v)>0. For squarefree n set
  U(n)=prod_(q divides n)r_q,
  V(n)=prod_(q divides n)s_q,
  T(n)=prod_(q divides n)(r_q+s_q),
  gamma(n)=beta*[T(n)-U(n)-V(n)+delta(n)].
Empty products are one. Actual multiplicativity of B,au,av gives
  a(n)=mu(n)*gamma(n) for EVERY squarefree n.    (4.1)
Here gamma(1)=gamma(q)=0; gamma(n)>0 when omega(n)>=2. Positivity follows
by expanding T: its all-u and all-v terms are U and V, and all other
terms are strictly positive. It does not depend on a numerical prime sample.

If m is squarefree, every factorization h*k=m is automatically coprime,
both factors are squarefree, and
  a(h)a(k)=mu(m)*gamma(h)*gamma(k).              (4.2)
Thus
  Gamma_X(m)=mu(m)*Phi_X(m),
  Phi_X(m)=sum_(h divides m)gamma(h)gamma(m/h)W_X(h,m/h)>=0.       (4.3)
It is nonzero only if omega(m)>=4 and some allowed balanced factorization
puts at least two primes on each leg. This is the exact scoped obstruction:
within a fixed squarefree product, ALL nonzero terms have the same sign.

The amplitude has a useful combinatorial interpretation. Each prime of m
goes into one of four labeled bins hu,hv,ku,kv, with ALL four bins nonempty.
A u-colored prime contributes r_q, a v-colored prime s_q; the product of
bins hu,hv is h, and of ku,kv is k. Their geometric weight is W_X(h,k).
This preserves the complete divisor balance, with no independence assumption.

For comparison, WITHOUT the geometric weight, put ell=omega(m), m>1.
The complete amplitude sum divided by beta^2 is
  Q(m)=(2^ell+4)*T(m)+(2^ell-2)*[U(m)+V(m)]
        -2*prod_(q divides m)(2r_q+s_q)
        -2*prod_(q divides m)(r_q+2s_q).        (4.4)
It follows by expanding (T-U-V+delta)*(T-U-V+delta) on squarefree m.
The formula vanishes for ell<4 and is positive for ell>=4. At m=1 the
omitted delta*delta term restores zero; (4.4) was explicitly scoped to m>1.
Formally letting all r_q,s_q tend to one gives
  Q=4^ell-4*3^ell+6*2^ell-4,
the number of surjections into four bins. This is a symbolic prime-location
limit, not a source experiment or a new signed estimate. Equation (4.4)
does not replace the actual W_X geometry in (4.3).

5. What divisor-fiber and prime-permutation transport cannot do
--------------------------------------------------------------
A permutation of factorizations at a fixed squarefree m, or any regrouping
with nonnegative coefficients and nonnegative kernel weights, preserves the
common sign mu(m). It cannot pair two nonzero opposite-sign source terms.
A permutation of the existing prime labels between the legs likewise
preserves the number of prime factors of the product and this same sign.
Permuting the numerical prime labels to another squarefree product with
the SAME number of prime factors also preserves its parity sign, though
the amplitude and all prefix/cutoff geometry may change.

This does not exclude an algebraic relation with signed auxiliary coefficients,
a signed changed kernel, or a main-term complement. Such a relation contains
negative terms added by the transformation; they must be recovered with
their full costs. There is no opposite raw source sign in (4.3) to make
that recovery automatic. A change of squarefree product parity, e.g. real
prime adjunction/removal, is the basic source transition not admitted by
the fixed-fiber orbit. Repeated valuations provide another possible
transition, as section 7 demonstrates.

Moreover the squarefree-product part of the quadratic kernel is NOT a
positive energy. On coefficient indices h>=2 its diagonal is zero because
h^2 is nonsquarefree. It has nonzero off-diagonals whenever allowed coprime
factors h,k occur. Such a two-index submatrix is [0,w;w,0], w>0, with
one positive and one negative eigenvalue. Hence it cannot be a coercive
quadratic storage by itself. A negative scalar coefficient on this signed
projection cannot be called energy contraction. The full centered square
in (1.1), with all nonsquarefree/gcd and main terms, supplies positivity.

6. A complete cross-product prime-pairing identity
-------------------------------------------------
Fix a prime q, and let m be squarefree with q not dividing m. For every
squarefree q-free h define t_q=r_q+s_q and
  G_q(h)=beta*[s_q*U(h)+r_q*V(h)-t_q*delta(h)].
Then exactly
  gamma(q*h)=t_q*gamma(h)+G_q(h).               (6.1)
G_q(1)=0 and G_q(h)>0 for h>1. In particular the unit correction is
essential; gamma(q)=0 is not a nonzero birth at the empty cofactor.

Every factorization of qm assigns its unique q to the left or right leg,
so define complete nonnegative boundary and birth fibers
  Boundary_X,q(m)=sum_(h*k=m)gamma(h)gamma(k)
        *[W_X(h,k)-W_X(q*h,k)+W_X(h,k)-W_X(h,q*k)],
  Birth_X,q(m)=sum_(h*k=m)[G_q(h)gamma(k)W_X(q*h,k)
                                        +gamma(h)G_q(k)W_X(h,q*k)].
The EXACT identity is
  Phi_X(q*m)=2*t_q*Phi_X(m)
                     -t_q*Boundary_X,q(m)+Birth_X,q(m).         (6.2)
There is no omitted factorization with a prime or unit leg: their amplitudes
and G values supply the appropriate zero or newly nonzero term.
Every prefix exit is explicit; for example
  W_X(h,k)-W_X(q*h,k)
     =max(0,min(2X,q*h)-max(X,h,k)).            (6.3)
It counts integers where the base pair is in the prefix and its q-lift
is not. Both legs' exits are needed. Outside-domain lifted pairs are not
silently treated as source zeros; their original base contribution is
the retained boundary in (6.3).

Partitioning ALL squarefree products into q-free m and qm gives
  X^(-2)*sum_(m squarefree)Gamma_X(m)
   =X^(-2)*sum_(m squarefree,q not dividing m)mu(m)
       *[(1-2t_q)*Phi_X(m)+t_q*Boundary_X,q(m)-Birth_X,q(m)].     (6.4)
All fiber functions have their natural zero outside the finite prefix
product range; the identity includes products that enter or exit under
the mapping. If a low-product peel is imposed, its indicator must be
included at m AND qm, with the additional threshold crossing retained.

The bulk coefficient is negative: q^(-1/3)<4/5, q^(-2/3)<16/25 for q>=2,
so t_q>14/25 and 1-2t_q<-3/25. Nevertheless the remaining mu(m) projection
in (6.4) is signed and indefinite by section 5, and the two complete
defect channels have no established signed bound. This is not a contraction.

There is a genuinely different typed auxiliary here. Phi has FOUR nonempty
prime-color bins. Each G_q(h) term is monochrome on its cofactor leg, while
gamma(k) is mixed on the other; Birth therefore has THREE nonempty bins.
The distinguished q fills the missing fourth bin. This is a full arithmetic
identity linking color-occupancy states and DIFFERENT products, with exact
kernel transport. A next language can encode occupancy, which leg receives
q, and both entry/exit kernels, rather than only higher local multipliers.
Boundary retains the old four-bin state. A degree/color induction is not
closed by this fact alone: lower-color companions, at their base, still
contain actual mu/au-type signed prefix information. Independently assuming
their RH-strength bounds would remain circular. The new testable input is
their joint signed correlation in (6.4), with a well-founded scale/prime
or color argument and the entire boundary paid, not its fitted scalar value.

7. Repeated-prime fibers can have opposite signs, with explicit costs
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The same-sign theorem is deliberately scoped to squarefree m. There are
actual repeated-prime opposite signs within one fiber. This can be proved
symbolically, without choosing or evaluating a new source sample.

Take distinct primes q,r,s,t with q>=27 and put x=q^(-1/3), y=x^2.
The local B coefficients satisfy
  B(q^2)=1-2(x+y)+x^2+xy+y^2>0,
  B(q^3)=(x+y)*B(q^2)-xy*(x+y-2)>0.             (7.1)
Indeed x<=1/3,y<=1/9 gives the first expression >=1/9. For every other
prime r, B(r)<0 and au(r),av(r)<0, while au(q^e),av(q^e)<0 for e>=1.
Equation p=beta*(B-au-av) off the unit implies
  a(q*r)>0, a(s*t)>0, a(q^3*r)<0,
  a(q^2*s*t)>0.                                (7.2)
The third follows because all three contributions are negative; for the
fourth, B(q^2)B(s)B(t)>0 and the au/av products are negative and subtracted.
None of these indices is a prime power, so a=p there.

Thus the product m=q^3*r*s*t has a positive pair
(q*r,q^2*s*t), and a negative pair (q^3*r,s*t). Every nonsquarefree/gcd
sector must remain: removing one of these by a squarefree analogy is false.

Sign availability is not cancellation with bounded cost. Hold r,s,t fixed
and let q tend to infinity through primes. Then
  B(q^2)->1, B(q^3)/q^(-u)->1,
  au(q^2)=O(q^(-u)), au(q^3)=O(q^(-2u)),
and the av analogues decay faster. Consequently the positive pair above
has a positive nonzero limiting amplitude, whereas the negative pair is
of order q^(-u), with a nonzero negative leading constant. Pairing those
two magnitudes requires a coefficient of order q^u, or an explicitly
retained remainder. This is an exact asymptotic in prime parameters, not
a numerical fit. Other negative q^3 assignments for this m are similarly
suppressed; the q^(1+2) assignments with both legs having other prime
factors are positive under (7.1).

There is also a complete SUPPORT example. Let L=max(rs,rt,st), choose q
large, and choose dyadic X with q^2*L<=X<2q^2*L. If q>4L/min(r,s,t),
every nonzero q^(1+2) split fits inside X and has weight X, but every
negative q^3-on-one-leg assignment is above 2X and has weight zero. For
three other primes, the only nonzero q^3-on-one-leg assignments put ONE
of r,s,t on that leg and the other TWO on the opposite leg; all other
assignments have a unit or prime-power/prime zero leg. Thus this complete
fiber is positive in that prefix geometry despite its negative algebraic
branches existing at larger coordinates. Their recovery requires actual
outside support and changed kernels. This narrow example does not give
a lower bound for the full energy or disprove its uniform target.

8. Divisor inversion: an identity with a recorded recovery cost
--------------------------------------------------------------
The exact actual source law mu*1=delta gives
  sum_(d divides n)mu(d)=delta(n),
  sum_(d<=N)mu(d)*floor(N/d)=1.                 (8.1)
It couples DIFFERENT divisor products d. It does not cancel two terms
whose fixed squarefree product is m and common sign is mu(m).
For a nonconstant weight F(d), the complete identity is instead
  sum_(d divides n)mu(d)F(d)
   =F(1)*delta(n)+sum_(d divides n)mu(d)[F(d)-F(1)].             (8.2)
The weighted difference is precisely what cannot be discarded. The balance
weights and source amplitudes in (4.3) are nonconstant.

A legitimate complete convolution regrouping is g=a*1, hence a=mu*g,
with
  g=beta*[mu*(n^(-u)*n^(-v))-n^(-u)-n^(-v)+1]-d*1.             (8.3)
Stars inside (8.3) remain Dirichlet convolutions; the final 1 is the
constant-one sequence, not delta. Thus for G(x)=sum_(k<=x)g(k),
  A(x)=sum_(d<=x)mu(d)*G(x/d).                  (8.4)
This retains repeated-prime removal, the unit convolution and every floor.
There is still actual mu in the leading g state, and its critical recovery
cannot be supplied for free. On the weighted L2 norm with parameter sigma,
dilation by d has norm at most d^(-sigma); the absolute recovery cost is
  sum_(d>=1)|mu(d)|d^(-sigma)=prod_q(1+q^(-sigma)),
which diverges at sigma<=1, in particular at the critical sigma=1/2.
This is a failure of that absolute recovery certificate, not a claimed
impossibility of a signed source-specific inverse. The finite floor law
also gives a bounded harmonic mu prefix, but not the missing prefix-square
covariance. Any use of (8.4) must prove its signed dilation estimate or
put its companions in the same closed induction, with full centering.

9. Concrete next correlation domain and limitations
---------------------------------------------------
After the two paid quadratic peels, the remaining pairs have large product
and large primitive height. Squarefree fibers have exact common sign mu(m);
their parity cancellation must occur across different products. Equation
(6.4) supplies one explicit domain and source transition: a q-free squarefree
cofactor product, prime adjunction into either leg, complete three-color
births and four-color exits, with all cut crossings. Equation (7.1)-(7.2)
supplies another: repeated-valuation redistribution at fixed nonsquarefree
product, with unequal amplitudes and possibly outside-prefix coordinates.

A sufficient next lemma must give a signed integrated estimate for those
complete correlations together with the retained main/pole Gram entries
and nonsquarefree complement. Proving just an auxiliary four-to-three-color
identity, its positive envelope, or a finite orbit equality does not recover
the original energy. Treating the whole right side of (6.4) as a new named
cofinal premise would also be a rephrasing rather than a reduction. Useful
progress would prove one of its boundary/birth sectors with a genuinely
smaller dependency, or a joint estimate which closes a coercive full-state
recurrence without independently assuming a lower-color RH bound.
