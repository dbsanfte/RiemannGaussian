INTEGER SIZE CARRIES, PAIRED ZETA ROOTS, AND THE DOMAIN OF EACH CLAIM

Research record, 7 October 2026. Written universal proofs below are
supported by exact rational and Arb checks. They are not theorem-prover
certificates. No historical originality or new RH estimate is claimed.

1. EXACT INTEGER HEIGHTS AND TWO ASSOCIATIVE PRODUCTS

Fix an integer b>=2. For positive integers n define
  h(n)=floor(log_b(n)), H(n)=ceil(log_b(n)),
  theta(n)=log_b(n)-h(n), psi(n)=H(n)-log_b(n),
  I(n)=1[n=b^k for an integer k>=0].
The implementation computes h and H by integer comparisons, including
exactly at arbitrarily large powers of b; floating logarithms are not used.
We have theta+psi=1-I. Both defects
  c_-(a,d)=h(ad)-h(a)-h(d)=theta(a)+theta(d)-theta(ad),
  c_+(a,d)=H(a)+H(d)-H(ad)=psi(a)+psi(d)-psi(ad)
take values in {0,1}. Each vanishes if either argument is 1. Each satisfies
  c(a,d)+c(ad,e)=c(d,e)+c(a,de).
This is an exact telescoping identity, not a fitted associativity rule.

For either choice of c, let
  (f K_t g)(n)=sum_{ad=n} (1-c(a,d)+t*c(a,d))*f(a)*g(d).
For t!=0 the weight is t^c, so the cocycle identity proves associativity.
Both sides of associativity are polynomials in t; it therefore also holds
at t=0. Alternatively, when t=0 the product of two weights equals 1
exactly when the common total defect is 0. All K_t are commutative, with
identity delta_1, and ordinary Dirichlet convolution is K_1.

The carry-only product C, with weight c, is associative too: the product
of two carry weights is 1 exactly when the total defect is 2. It has no
identity, since delta_1 C f=0. Thus ordinary convolution splits into two
compatible associative pieces K_0+C; their arbitrary linear pencil K_t
is associative. This assertion includes the absence of a unit for C.

The nonzero-t family is a classical change of scalar weights. In the
floor case M_t(f)(n)=t^(-h(n))*f(n) satisfies
  M_t(f K_t g)=M_t(f)*M_t(g).
In the ceiling case use M_t(f)(n)=t^(H(n))*f(n). These products are instances
of established weighted divisor convolution. The degenerate t=0 products
are the selected candidates; a general associative-kernel framework was
already studied by Davison and later authors. Their definition alone is
not evidence of an undiscovered arithmetic primitive.

2. TRANSFERS, ROOT LIMITS, AND WHAT IS KNOWN ANALYTICALLY

Work with arithmetic functions over a characteristic-zero field, f(1)=1.
Write *=ordinary Dirichlet convolution and delta=delta_1. For h(1)=0,
coefficient-local EXP_K(h)=sum_{j>=0}h^{K j}/j! terminates at every n,
because each nonunit factor contributes at least one to Omega(n).
LOG_K is its usual formal inverse. In particular
  U_-(f)=EXP_Kminus(LOG_*(f)), U_+(f)=EXP_Kplus(LOG_*(f))
are normalized unit-group isomorphisms, with inverses EXP_* LOG_K. They
satisfy U(f*g)=U(f) K_0 U(g) on every normalized input. This abstract
LOG/EXP transfer construction is established mathematics.

Let L_q(f) be the q-fold K_0 product of the ordinary normalized qth root
f^{*1/q}. In the coefficient-local topology,
  f^{*1/q}=delta+(1/q)*LOG_*(f)+O(q^-2),
and q*LOG_K(f^{*1/q}) tends to LOG_*(f). Hence L_q(f) tends to U(f).
For the special zeta input u(n)=1, the ordinary root coefficients
  d_alpha(n)=product_{p^e||n} (alpha)_e/e!
are nonnegative for alpha>=0. The K_0 weights are 0 or 1. The ordinary
q-fold root product is u, so discarding terms gives 0<=L_q(u)(n)<=1 for
every n, base b, orientation and positive integer q. Passing to the
coefficient limit gives 0<=U(u)(n)<=1. Thus all these zeta-input series
converge absolutely on Re(s)>1, and the q-limit is locally uniform there,
by domination with sum n^-sigma on every closed smaller half-plane.

The subsequent size_resonance_proof.txt rules out making either individual
U holomorphic at s=1 by any finite real power of s-1. Finite-q lifts also
create artificial singularities on Re(s)=1. Other continuations remain
unestablished. The paired operation in section 5 has a separate, stronger
bridge. Its bridge must not be attributed to the individual transfers.

3. INTEGER VALUES MATTER, WITH A LIMIT TO THAT CONCLUSION

In base 2, Kminus(2,3)=1 whereas Kminus(5,7)=0: 6 has height 2 and 35
has height 5. This distinguishes two products of distinct primes. The
floor transfer has U(u)(6)=1, U(u)(35)=0, U(u)(9)=1/2; the ceiling
transfer has U(u)(6)=1, U(u)(35)=1, U(u)(9)=1. Both have coefficient 1
at n=4. They are not invariant under arbitrary relabeling of prime values.
The extraction f(n)->f(n^2) is not a Kminus algebra map: in base 2,
delta_2 Kminus delta_2=delta_4, but extraction sends delta_2 to zero
and delta_4 to delta_2.

These tests establish dependence on an assigned multiplicative size.
The height construction still makes sense on a free multiplicative monoid
with assigned generator norms; it does not by itself single out the
ordinary prime distribution. The integer gap used in section 7 is an
additional fact about actual integer inputs. Neither observation supplies
a new estimate about zeta zeros.

Sparse coefficient validation avoids hiding missing arithmetic terms in
a giant zero-padded array. For a fixed n=product p^e, define polynomials
C_{p,0}(z)=1 and
  e*C_{p,e}(z)=alpha*sum_{j=1}^e z^{h(p^j)}*C_{p,e-j}(z).
These are the coefficients of
  exp(alpha*sum_{j>=1} z^{h(p^j)}*x^j/j).
The floor-transfer coefficient EXP_Kminus(alpha LOG_* u)(n) is the
coefficient of z^{h(n)} in product_p C_{p,e}(z). Indeed the sum of heights
of factors is at most h(n); equality holds exactly when all accumulated
floor carries vanish. This yields an independent cycle-height evaluator.
For alpha=1, C_{p,e}(1)=1. The usual labeled-permutation cycle expansion
also shows Omega(n)! times the coefficient is an integer. For rational
alpha=a/d, Omega(n)!*d^{Omega(n)} clears the denominator: the number of
cycles is at most Omega(n). The checker's sparse profiles include alpha
1/2 and 3/2 and integers far beyond the dense cutoff.

4. CALIBRATION, COUNTEREXAMPLES, AND A COLLAPSING FAMILY

The additive-looking condition gcd(a+d,ad)=1 is equivalent to gcd(a,d)=1:
a prime dividing the sum and product divides both factors, and conversely.
It rediscovers ordinary unitary convolution. This is an equivalence of
coprimality conditions, not equality of the two gcd values.
The shifted condition gcd(a-1,d-1)=1, with the unit forced, fails
associativity on (2,2,4), with left/right weights 0 and 1. An additive
residue carry modulo 5, used without changing the underlying multiplication
of indices, fails on (2,2,6), also with weights 0 and 1. The grammar must
respect the algebra in which a carry was derived.

For m>=1 put g_m(n)=floor(n/m)-floor(1/m). It is superadditive under
multiplication. For m>1 and a,d>=2, ad>=a+d implies
floor(ad/m)>=floor(a/m)+floor(d/m); unit cases are exact. For m=1,
the defect is (a-1)(d-1). If the defect is zero, then for m>1
  ad-a-d<m, so (a-1)(d-1)<=m,
and ad<=2m+2, since a+d<= (a-1)(d-1)+3 for a,d>=2.
The m=1 case has no allowed nonunit split. Consequently the zero-defect
product has no allowed nonunit split at any n>2m+2. For every normalized f,
  EXP_K(LOG_* f)(n)=LOG_* f(n), n>2m+2.
On the zeta input its Dirichlet series is log(zeta) plus a finite Dirichlet
polynomial. These surviving floor-quotient kernels are recorded as a
classical collapse, rather than more interesting new operators.

5. A UNIVERSAL PAIRED IDENTITY, INCLUDING COMPOSITE BASES

Let R_b(f)(n)=I(n)*f(n); it keeps exactly the coefficients at b^k,
including k=0. From theta+psi=1-I,
  c_minus(a,d)+c_plus(a,d)=1-I(a)-I(d)+I(ad).
Subtracting the defects from 2 and summing divisor terms proves, on all
inputs a,d (now arithmetic functions), the bilinear law
  a Kminus d+a Kplus d
    =a*d+R_b(a)*d+a*R_b(d)-R_b(a*d).
For any normalized f and any complex beta, set g=f^{*beta}. Then
  B_{b,beta}(f)
    =1/2*(g Kminus g+g Kplus g-f^{*2beta}+R_b(f^{*2beta}))
    =g*R_b(g).
This is a universal identity; it is not merely a fit to u. It is a verified
consequence of the two products and a projection, not an additional
primitive counted by naming its composition.

R_b is linear, unital and idempotent. It is an ordinary convolution
homomorphism on all inputs exactly when b is prime. For prime b the
submonoid of b-powers is divisor-closed. If b is composite, choose a
proper factor a and d=b/a, both >1 and <b. With f=delta+delta_a,
g=delta+delta_d, R_b(f)=R_b(g)=delta but R_b(f*g)=delta+delta_b.
In particular b=6 gives a=2,d=3; b=4 already fails with a=d=2.
The paired identity did not assume this false composite-base homomorphism.

6. A ZERO-FREE CORRECTION BY A BETA MOMENT REPRESENTATION

For u, the classical ordinary root is d_beta. Write b=product p^{e_p},
  a_k=d_beta(b^k)=product_p (beta)_{e_p*k}/(e_p*k)!,
  x=b^-s, R_{b,beta}(s)=sum_{k>=0}a_k*x^k.
For 0<beta<1 choose independent random variables T_p with the probability
density t^{beta-1}(1-t)^{-beta}/B(beta,1-beta), 0<t<1. The beta integral
gives E[T_p^j]=(beta)_j/j!. Therefore with V=product T_p^{e_p},
  a_k=E[V^k], R_{b,beta}(s)=E[1/(1-x*V)], Re(s)>0.
This follows by an absolutely convergent geometric series, since 0<=V<=1
and |x|<1. For beta=0 use V=0, and for beta=1 use V=1, with V^0=1.
Thus the statement holds for every real beta in [0,1] and every integer
base b>=2, not just the rational test parameters or prime bases.

For any |w|<=r<1,
  Re(1/(1-w)) >= 1/(1+r).
This follows either by minimizing on the boundary circle or directly
from Re(1/(1-w))=(1-Re(w))/|1-w|^2. Averaging gives
  Re R_{b,beta}(s)>=1/(1+b^{-Re(s)})>1/2, Re(s)>0.
The correction is holomorphic and nonzero on this entire half-plane.

The explicit series is classical hypergeometric. Its coefficient ratio is
  a_(k+1)/a_k=product_{p^{e_p}||b} product_{j=0}^{e_p-1}
                         (beta+e_p*k+j)/(e_p*k+j+1).
Its numerator parameters are (beta+j)/e_p; denominator parameters are
(j+1)/e_p, with one parameter 1 removed for the hypergeometric k!.
For b prime it is (1-b^-s)^(-beta); for b=6 it is
2F1(beta,beta;1;6^-s). At beta=1/2 this is the classical complete elliptic
integral (2/pi)*K(sqrt(6^-s)), using the NIST modulus convention K(k).
The hypergeometric identity and beta moment integral are not new primitives.

The paired zeta series satisfies, initially Re(s)>1,
  B_{b,beta}(u)(s)=zeta(s)^beta*R_{b,beta}(s).
All powers use the branch normalized on the positive real axis. This
identity follows from the exact coefficient law and absolute convolution
of the two factors. Put Z(s)=(s-1)*zeta(s), holomorphic on H={Re(s)>1/2}.
The initially pole-cancelled paired series is
  Phi_{b,beta}(s)=(s-1)^beta*B_{b,beta}(u)(s)=Z(s)^beta*R_{b,beta}(s).

Fix any b>=2. RH is equivalent to every Phi_{b,2^-j}, j>=1, admitting
single-valued holomorphic continuation to H. If RH holds, Z is zero-free
on simply connected H and has a holomorphic logarithm, so all these powers
exist. Conversely a zero of Z of multiplicity m in H can admit such a
power only if m/2^j is an integer. No positive finite m has this property
for every j. The nonzero R factor cannot remove its monodromy. The usual
functional equation and symmetry turn absence of zeros in H into RH.
A square-root test alone misses even multiplicities; finitely many dyadic
orders miss sufficiently divisible multiplicities. Integer beta=1 never
has this branch obstruction and serves as a control.

A corollary for the universal real-parameter theorem: a single fixed
irrational beta in (0,1) also gives an RH-equivalent holomorphic-continuation
criterion, since m*beta is never an integer. The numerical scalar grammar
in this experiment uses rational beta only; no implementation of exact
irrational arithmetic or verification of global continuation is claimed.

7. A SEPARATE VERTICAL-ZETA FORMULA FOR THE FLOOR SQUARE

Let F_beta(s)=zeta(s)^beta and
  J_beta(s)=sum theta(n)*d_beta(n)*n^-s.
The floor kernel is 1-theta(a)-theta(d)+theta(ad), so for g=d_beta,
  (g Kminus g)(s)=F_beta(s)^2-2*F_beta(s)*J_beta(s)+J_(2beta)(s),
initially Re(s)>1. Put tau=2*pi/log(b). The Fourier series for the
periodic sawtooth has midpoint value 0 for theta-1/2 at integers.
To recover theta(n)=0 at exact b-powers one must subtract half their
Dirichlet series. The Fejer-summed identity is
  J_beta(s)=(F_beta(s)-R_{b,beta}(s))/2
       -lim_{M->infinity} (1/(2*pi*i))*sum_{0<|m|<=M}
              (1-|m|/(M+1))*F_beta(s-i*m*tau)/m.
Fejer means of the bounded sawtooth are uniformly bounded, so absolute
Dirichlet convergence permits dominated convergence for Re(s)>1,
0<=beta<=1. Omitting R at exact powers gives an incorrect formula.

Here is an explicit bound used for the fresh actual-zeta checks. Let
N=M+1 and L_N=(1+2*log(N))/(4*N). The Fejer kernel on [-1/2,1/2]
is bounded by min(N,1/(4*N*t^2)). Its first absolute moment is <=L_N;
the mass outside [-delta,delta] is <=1/(2*N*delta). If n is not an exact
b-power, the distance delta of log_b(n) to the nearest integer is
  delta>=1/(sqrt(b)*n*log(b)).
To see this, the nearest power b^k is at most sqrt(b)*n and differs from
integer n by at least 1; the mean-value bound for log gives the assertion.
Thus the pointwise Fejer error is at most
  L_N+sqrt(b)*n*log(b)/(2*N).
At exact powers, after the explicit resonance subtraction, it is zero.
Since 0<=d_beta(n)<=1, the resulting absolute error for sigma=Re(s)>2 is
  E(b,sigma,M)=L_N*(zeta(sigma)-1/(1-b^-sigma))
   +sqrt(b)*log(b)/(2*N)*(zeta(sigma-1)-1/(1-b^{1-sigma})).
For 0<=beta<=1/2 the floor-square error is <=(2*|F_beta(s)|+1)*E,
using F_(2beta)=F_beta^2 with its normalized branch.

The independent verifier uses ordinary partial Fourier sums instead,
with a different cutoff. Abel summation bounds the sawtooth remainder
by 1/(pi*N*|sin(pi*theta)|)<=1/(2*pi*N*delta). Hence its J error is <=
  sqrt(b)*log(b)/(2*pi*N)
       *(zeta(sigma-1)-1/(1-b^{1-sigma})), sigma>2.
Both verifiers use a separate Dirichlet truncation error
N_arith^{1-sigma}/(sigma-1) for series coefficients bounded by 1.

These are proved formulas and explicit errors in their stated domains.
An infinite sum of vertically shifted zeta roots does not automatically
continue to H. No extension of this individual floor square, or of the
full floor/ceiling root-limit transfers, is inferred from these formulas.

8. ACTUAL ZETA CHECKS, NEGATIVE CONTROLS, AND PUBLICATION STATUS

size_carry_search.py scores 447 kernels: 334 training fits, 281 finite
held-out survivors, 60 distinct fingerprints through 1024. These numbers
are empirical search counts, not 281 proved operators or 60 new laws.
Only the selected floor/ceiling kernels and the controls above have the
stated universal arguments. check_size_carry.py imports no production
arithmetic helpers, recomputes every decision, uses triangular Euler-degree
LOG/EXP and binomial finite lifts, and verifies the selected sparse profiles
by independent prime-cycle polynomials. Generic input laws and the paired
identity are tested beyond the zeta coefficient input.

size_analytic_probe.py uses actual Arb zeta values at six fresh complex
points, three bases (2,6,12) and three beta values (1/2,1/4,1/8). It
compares both the paired identity and the individual floor-square Fourier
formula with exact arithmetic series, using proved tails. Doubled
coefficients and omission of the power-resonance term are rejected.
check_size_analytic.py uses different arithmetic and spectral cutoffs,
ordinary Fourier sums in place of Fejer means, and an exact ratio series
with a geometric tail in place of native hypergeometric evaluation.

At rho near 1/2+14.13472514173469379046*i, a Taylor/Rouche calculation
certifies an actual unique simple zeta zero in a disk of radius 1e-4.
Reflection puts it on the critical line. The independent verifier uses
a Cauchy remainder bound on a larger circle. At that disk, twelve paired
correction cases (three bases and beta=1/2,1/4,1/8,1) are certified to
have positive real part. Nine fractional cases have strictly positive
root-monodromy lower bounds; the three beta=1 controls have no branch
phase. This detects a genuine zeta zero, rather than a substituted zero
model. A critical-line boundary test is compatible with RH. It neither
finds an off-line zero nor proves its absence.
