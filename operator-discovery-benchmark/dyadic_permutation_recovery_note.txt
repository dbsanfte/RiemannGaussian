Candidate-language extension suggested by the divisor-circuit obstruction
2026-10-09. Root analytical note; no experiments, source tables, hashes, changes
to frozen circuit code, or source-specific moment claim.

Every circuit currently tested is divisor triangular. Its diagonal has unit
magnitude, while its other input coordinates are proper divisors <=n/2. At a
prime the output retains the entire M(p) coordinate and only a bounded initial
correction. Invertibility is useful, but this is a genuine representational
restriction. A grammar allowing reversible changes of integer coordinates can
break that restriction without losing the original critical-energy target.

An elementary, unconventional primitive
For n>=1, uniquely write n=2^k+r with0<=r<2^k. Put m=2^k. Define
 P(n)=m+((r+2r^2) mod m).
This is a permutation of EVERY dyadic block, with P(1)=1. Indeed
 (x+2x^2)-(y+2y^2)=(x-y)(1+2(x+y)),
and the second factor is odd. Therefore equality modulo2^k is equivalent to
x=y modulo2^k. Injectivity on the finite residue ring gives bijectivity.
This argument holds uniformly over all k, and uses no prime-distribution or
Mobius cancellation assumption. More general maps with the same odd-difference
property, odd affine residue permutations, and bit permutations give additional
source-free grammar productions. They should be generated by general rules,
not labeled successful because this example was nominated by the model.

Finite exact inverse
The inverse residue can be lifted one binary digit at a time. If r_j solves
r+2r^2=y modulo2^j, its two lifts r_j and r_j+2^j have outputs differing by
2^j modulo2^(j+1), so precisely one solves the next congruence. Start with
the unique residue modulo1 and keep the required endpoint bounds. No floating
root, search through all residues, or content hash is needed.

Original energy and recovery
For S_P f(n)=f(P(n)), the complete dyadic moment
 E_f(2^k)=2^(-2k) sum_{2^k<=n<2^(k+1)} |f(n)|^2
is preserved EXACTLY by S_P and its inverse. Thus a solitary outer permutation
is an energy alias and should not get a candidate slot on that basis.

Inside a divisor circuit it generally does not commute with convolution and
creates fresh within-block arithmetic interactions. For
 H_sigma,T(f)=sum_{n<=T}|f(n)|^2/n^(2sigma+1), sigma>1/2,
the input/output indices lie in the same dyadic block and have ratio in(1/2,2).
On a CLOSED dyadic horizon T=2^K-1,
 H_sigma,T(S_P f)<=2^(2sigma+1) H_sigma,T(f),
and the same bound holds for its inverse. The unsquared norm factor is
2^(sigma+1/2). Combining this with the proved divisor-circuit inverse gives
uniform finite-horizon recovery, with one such factor per permutation. Closed
horizons exhaust the integers, so the conditional weighted-L2/Mellin/RH bridge
continues to hold. Source phases must still use the same fixed arithmetic
source in forward and inverse circuits. Every generated circuit stays fixed
as the scale and sigma vary.

Domain discipline for a future implementation
An arbitrary prefix horizon is NOT closed under P. Evaluation must load the
complete enclosing dyadic block, or reject an unclosed horizon; it must not
clip P(n), silently discard coordinates, or manufacture a zero extension.
Our completed study happens to use closed horizons511,2047,32767, but none of
its frozen candidates includes this proposed primitive. No stored response
or certificate is retroactively changed. All output squares and every recovery
factor would remain required in a new prospective study.

This supplies an exact new recovery rule and an implementation specification,
not evidence that the permuted arithmetic moments cancel. It explains how to
expand structural access beyond proper-divisor past coordinates while keeping
a proved route back to the original Mertens energy. A source-specific cofinal
moment bound remains the indispensable open part.
