Interleaved reversible circuits 42 and 34: signed analytical attempt

Scope and disposition
This is a pure analytical review. Only program, certificate and obligation
payloads for IDs42/34 in reversible-circuit-results/pilot-001/proof_queue.json
were read. No discovery/training/final numerical responses, source tables,
tests, hashes or frozen files were read or changed. The original fixed source
is the actual Mobius function; M(n)=sum_{j<=n}mu(j), with mu(0)=0 for the phase
syntax. No cofinal transformed moment bound is proved here.

The useful positive result is a stronger uniform forward AND inverse norm
theorem for this class of interleaved fractional circuits. The useful negative
result is that even extremely sparse adjacent phase boundaries cannot imply a
small divisor commutator. Exact shifted-Mobius identities leave signed mixed
correlations unresolved. The stronger recovery theorem is separate from these
unresolved cancellation estimates; the frozen conservative certificates and
candidate ordering are not changed.

1. Fixed programs and notation
Let T_{alpha,chi}f(n)=sum_{d|n}a_{alpha,chi}(d)f(n/d), where the multiplicative
kernel has local series (1-chi(p)z)^(-alpha). All coefficients and phases are
fixed for all stages and their inverses. Let
 h(n)=n-floor(sqrt(n)),
 S(n)=-1 if mu(h(n))=-1, and +1 otherwise.
This is a unit phase, including the zero-source case.

ID42 applies, in order, T_{-1/2,chi3}, S, T_{1/3,chi3}, where chi3(p)=-1 for
p congruent1 mod3, and +1 otherwise, including p=3. ID34 applies
T_{-1/2,1}, S, T_{1/6,chi5}, where chi5(p)=-1 for p congruent1 or4 mod5,
and +1 otherwise, including p=5. Their saved recovery powers are both2.

Write the generic circuit as O=BSA, with
 A=T_{-a,chi1}, B=T_{b,chi2}, 0<b<a<1,
 u(d)=a_{-a,chi1}(d), v(d)=a_{b,chi2}(d), c=u*v.
For these candidates, a=1/2 and b=1/3 or1/6. Let C=BA M and
 D=[B,S]A M. Then, exactly,
 O M=S C+D.
For42, C=T_{-1/6,chi3}M. For34 the two coefficient sources differ, so
C=T_{1/6,chi5}T_{-1/2,1}M remains a two-source convolution; these convolution
nodes commute but cannot be merged by adding their exponents at one source.

2. Exact arithmetic commutator and the complete square
For every input f and integer n,
 [B,S]f(n)=sum_{d|n}v(d)[S(n/d)-S(n)]f(n/d).
Consequently the defect in the complete circuit is
 D(n)=sum_{de|n}v(d)u(e)[S(n/d)-S(n)]M(n/(de))
     =sum_{k|n}delta(n,k)M(n/k),
 delta(n,k)=sum_{d|k}v(d)u(k/d)[S(n/d)-S(n)].
The k=1 term is zero. Multiplying by the unit phase yields the exact gate
 S(n)delta(n,k)
 =-2 sum_{d|k}v(d)u(k/d)1_{S(n/d) != S(n)}.
This retains signed kernel weights and every divisor-pair complement. The gate
does not make the expression nonnegative because u and v are signed.

Equivalently, the complete output has an output-dependent divisor kernel
 K(n,k)=sum_{d|k}v(d)u(k/d)S(n/d),
 (O M)(n)=sum_{k|n}K(n,k)M(n/k).
At k=1, K(n,1)=S(n): the original prefix remains with full unit diagonal.
Every other input is a proper-divisor prefix at most n/2. On a dyadic block,
 D_O(X)=X^-2 sum_{X<=n<2X}(O M)(n)^2
       =E_C(X)+E_D(X)+2X^-2 sum_{X<=n<2X}S(n)C(n)D(n).
There is no orthogonality or favorable sign established for the last term.
Expanding the full square instead gives every k,l divisor pair, with factors
 v(d)u(k/d)v(e)u(l/e)S(n/d)S(n/e)M(n/k)M(n/l),
summed over k,l|n and d|k,e|l. Neither the same-chi merger nor any phase gate
authorizes discarding these mixed terms. The exact proof gate concerns this
complete square, not just C or D separately.

3. A uniform order-one majorant theorem
Suppose also a+b<=1, and allow arbitrary fixed prime coefficients
|chi1(p)|,|chi2(p)|<=1 and any intervening fixed phase S(n) in{-1,+1}.
Both O and O^-1
have weighted norm at most zeta(1+epsilon) for sigma=1/2+epsilon>1/2.
This improves the generic product bound zeta(1+epsilon)^2 for the present
two stages without relying on cancellation in S or on any bound for M.

Here is the coefficient proof. For 0<a<1 all nonconstant coefficients of
(1-z)^a are negative. The absolute local majorant for T_{-a} is therefore
 2-(1-z)^a.
For T_b it is (1-z)^(-b). Their positive convolution majorant is
 H(z)=[2-(1-z)^a](1-z)^(-b)
     =2(1-z)^(-b)-(1-z)^(a-b).
For k>=1 its coefficient is
 H_k=2 U_b(k)+V_{a-b}(k),
 U_beta(k)=(beta)_k/k!, V_gamma(k)=|(-gamma)_k|/k!.
When 0<beta,gamma<1 both U_beta(k) and V_gamma(k) decrease with k>=1:
their successive ratios are (k+beta)/(k+1) and (k-gamma)/(k+1).
Thus H_k<=H_1=2b+(a-b)=a+b<=1, and H_0=1.
Prime coefficients of modulus at most1 can only reduce this majorant. By
multiplicativity, the global positive convolution majorant H(k) is a product
of numbers <=1. It follows that |K(n,k)|<=H(k)<=1 for every n,k|n,
including phases depending on the same fixed actual source as M.

The inverse is O^-1=T_{a,chi1} S T_{-b,chi2}. Its positive local majorant is
 G(z)=(1-z)^(-a)[2-(1-z)^b]
     =2(1-z)^(-a)-(1-z)^(-(a-b)).
Its k>=1 coefficients are G_k=2U_a(k)-U_{a-b}(k)>0. They satisfy
 G_{k+1}-G_k
 =[-2(1-a)U_a(k)+(1-a+b)U_{a-b}(k)]/(k+1)<=0,
because U_a(k)>=U_{a-b}(k) and 2(1-a)>=1-a+b follows from a+b<=1.
Therefore G_k<=G_1=a+b<=1; G_0=1. The same multiplicative argument bounds
the inverse's output-dependent divisor kernel by1 as well.

For completeness, use the norm with square
 H_{sigma,T}(f)=sum_{n<=T}|f(n)|^2/n^(2sigma+1).
For fixed k, multiplication of 1_{k|n}f(n/k) by any coefficient bounded by1
has norm at most k^(-sigma-1/2)||f||_{sigma,T}. Triangle inequality over the
divisors, with no omitted finite tails, proves
 ||O f||_{sigma,T}, ||O^-1 f||_{sigma,T}
 <=zeta(sigma+1/2)||f||_{sigma,T}
 <=(1+1/epsilon)||f||_{sigma,T}.
These are UNSQUARED norm factors; the corresponding H recovery factor is
(1+1/epsilon)^2. Invertibility is the exact finite triangular inverse using
the same chi and S, not merely the majorant argument. Passing to infinity
uses monotone limits. Both selected circuits satisfy the exponent conditions.

Also |delta(n,k)|<=2H(k)<=2 for k>=2, so
 ||D f||_{sigma,T}<=2[zeta(1+epsilon)-1]||f||_{sigma,T}.
This is an unconditional component estimate, but its right side is the
unknown weighted norm of the original input. It is not a subpower moment
bound, negative transport credit, or contraction sufficient to close RH.

4. What the phase geometry actually gives
At consecutive integers, h(n+1)-h(n)=1 except when n+1 is a square, when it
is0. The O(sqrt(X)) square crossings in a long block are duplication events;
at all other locations the phase queries a NEW actual Mobius source value.
The root's slow change is not the phase's slow change. No sparse adjacent
phase-boundary assertion follows from it.

More importantly, even sparse adjacent boundaries would not suffice. Consider
the comparison unit phase S0(n)=(-1)^floor(log2(n)). It has only O(log X)
adjacent jumps up to X, but S0(2n)=-S0(n) for every n. For an input impulse
f=1_{n=N}, the exact defect at2N is
 D_f(2N)=v(2)[S0(N)-S0(2N)]=-2b chi2(2) S0(2N).
For |chi2(2)|=1 and T>=2N,
 ||D_f||_{sigma,T}/||f||_{sigma,T}>=2b*2^(-sigma-1/2).
This lower bound is independent of N. Thus the commutator need not become
small on arbitrarily late input supports even with logarithmically many
ordinary phase boundaries. This is a rigorous comparison-operator obstruction,
not a counterexample for the actual shifted-Mobius phase and not a claim about
its uncomputed sign-mismatch density. An actual-input cancellation could still
occur among other divisor terms, and would need proof.

5. Exact squarefree/source decomposition and its remaining correlations
For the actual source, the complete phase identity is
 S(n)=1-mu(h(n))^2+mu(h(n)).
It separates squareful/zero values from squarefree signed ones without
discarding either. If Q(u)=1-mu(u)^2, then
 S(i)S(j)=Q(h(i))Q(h(j))+mu(h(i))Q(h(j))
          +Q(h(i))mu(h(j))+mu(h(i))mu(h(j)).
On the sector where both arguments are squarefree the last term is their
signed correlation, rather than a nonnegative density. On the one-zero
sectors a signed term remains. Squarefree counting alone controls neither
these correlations nor their products with M(n/k)M(n/l) in the full square.

The actual prime law mu(pk)=-mu(k)1_{p does not divide k} does not directly
relate the arguments of a phase under divisor scaling. Indeed
 h(dk)-d h(k)=d floor(sqrt(k))-floor(sqrt(dk)).
For fixed d>1 this equals (d-sqrt(d))*sqrt(k)+O(d), not0. Replacing mu(h(dk))
by a multiplicative multiple of mu(h(k)) would omit an unbounded shifted
source complement. The squarefree identities do not repair that omission.
The resulting object is a divisor-weighted shifted-source/prefix correlation,
with the squarefree and root-transition sectors retained. No unconditional
subpower estimate for it is obtained here. This is a limitation of the
available identities and norm/boundary argument, not an impossibility theorem
for an arithmetic proof using additional structure of the actual source.

6. Exact residue-kernel simplifications, without a hidden analytic premise
The residue sources have a useful formal Euler description. Let eta3 and
eta5 be the real quadratic Dirichlet characters mod3 and5. For p!=q,
chiq(p)=-etaq(p), while chiq(q)=1. In the absolutely convergent half-plane
Re(s)>1, or as formal local series, the coefficient series is
 sum_n a_{alpha,chiq}(n)n^(-s)
 =[zeta(2s)(1+q^(-s))/L(s,etaq)]^alpha.
This follows locally from 1+eta(p)x=(1-x^2)/(1-eta(p)x), with the exceptional
prime-q factor kept. It uses only the defining Euler products and fractional
powers fixed by their convergent logarithms; it assumes no zeros or bounds
for L in a critical half-plane.

Thus42's merged core coefficient series is
 [L(s,eta3)/(zeta(2s)(1+3^(-s)))]^(1/6).
For34 the core coefficient series is
 zeta(s)^(-1/2)[zeta(2s)(1+5^(-s))/L(s,eta5)]^(1/6).
These are exact coefficient identities, not moment estimates. The input is
the PREFIX SEQUENCE M, whose ordinary Dirichlet series is not 1/zeta(s).
Consequently one cannot multiply these series by the source series 1/zeta(s)
and call the result the observer on M. Nor do these identities remove S or
the commutator correlations in the complete output. They can identify known
Euler cores while keeping the genuinely nonmultiplicative defect visible.

7. Whole-target obstruction and the precise proof still needed
The unit diagonal K(n,1)=S(n) retains each fresh prefix M(n). Proper-divisor
terms use earlier prefixes, but the actual phase also queries near-n source
values. For an unrestricted input vanishing below a dyadic block, the circuit
on that block is just S(n)f(n) and preserves its entire energy there. Hence
no input-independent norm/past argument gives a subpower bound for arbitrary
fresh blocks. On the actual input, the dependence between the fresh source,
these phases and divisor prefixes is available arithmetic information, not
already proved cancellation. The obstruction does not replace that arithmetic
problem with an artificial independent-source assumption.

The required estimate is: for this ONE fixed circuit and cofinally many
positive integers q, there exists finite Cq, independent of every dyadic
X>=32, such that the COMPLETE D_O(X)<=Cq ceil(X^(1/q)). Finite head energy
must remain. Proving E_C alone, bounding phase boundaries, or proving a
favorable sign for only some mixed terms does not satisfy this gate.
For every epsilon>0, the existing dyadic bridge and exact recovery imply
 H_{1/2+epsilon}(O M)<infinity iff H_{1/2+epsilon}(M)<infinity.
The new majorant improves constants in this equivalence and does not change
the target class. A cofinal moment estimate would therefore give the reviewed
ordinary-zeta/RH implication. Such an estimate has not been found here.

One sufficient but still unproved route is cofinal moment bounds for BOTH
C and D, using the complete cross term via Cauchy-Schwarz. A genuinely signed
route could instead control the full divisor-pair sum directly and absorb
E_D plus the mixed term into a proved complement/credit identity. Either
route must supply new source-specific correlation estimates; applying the
component norm bound to unknown H(M) is circular for this purpose.

8. Consequences for future structural generation
Maintain separate annotations for an exactly mergeable Euler core and the
nonmultiplicative divisor-phase defect, including the zero/squareful sectors.
For a proposed analytically simple phase, require a proved law for S(n)S(n/d)
on the relevant divisor domains, rather than a count of adjacent jumps.
Completely multiplicative phases fold into prime coefficients and are aliases
at this level; periodic phases can have persistent dilation mismatches. A
shifted source query is not a multiplicative law merely because its source is.

The condition 0<b<a<1, a+b<=1 defines a generic interleaving family with the
proved order-one recovery guarantee above; it does not select a favorable
moment result. Real analytic progress would come from an exact compensation
or a bound for the signed shifted-source/prefix correlations exposed here,
with every discarded-domain complement paid for. A future nontriangular
dyadic permutation could change the proper-divisor dependency pattern and
would require a new expansion; its own energy isometry supplies no moment
cancellation. No such primitive or new experiment is reviewed or run here.

Final status
Proved: exact complete commutator/full-square decomposition, order-one uniform
forward and inverse majorants for both selected circuits, and insufficiency of
adjacent-boundary sparsity for a tail-small divisor commutator. Unproved: the
actual shifted-Mobius defect's cofinal subpower moment and the complete fixed
observer moment. No RH, global signed bound or arithmetic cancellation theorem
is claimed. Reviewer research/test work is zero; this new memo alone is edited.
