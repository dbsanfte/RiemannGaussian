1. Commuting Boolean algebra, with its precise state space
==========================================================
For an actual prime p define E_p f(n)=f(pn). These operators commute on any
function. They are idempotent ONLY on the radical-invariant state space:
f(p^2 n)=f(pn) there, hence E_p^2=E_p. K is radical-invariant for n>=2;
its insertion companions remain on the same state space. Idempotence cannot
be imported for arbitrary functions, the b>0 kernels, or rounded source
profiles without an additional proof.

Let D_p=I-E_p. For a finite set S of DISTINCT actual primes put

 R=product_{p in S}p,
 D_S=product_{p in S}D_p,
 D_S K(n)=sum_{U subset S}(-1)^|U|K(n*product_{p in U}p).

Then D_p^2=D_p, D_S D_T=D_(S union T), and E_p D_p=0. Repeating the same
prime does not create a higher-order gain: (I-E_p)^k=I-E_p for k>=1 on
this state space. This is an exact canonicalization rule for the grammar.
The empty set is the identity, R=1, D_empty K=K.

For n>=2,

 gcd(n,R)>1 => D_S K(n)=0,
 gcd(n,R)=1 => D_S K(n)
   =I_alpha^-1 int_0^infinity
        R^-t product_{p|n}(1-p^-t)*t^(-alpha-1) dt
   =-sum_{d|n}mu(d)*(log(Rd))^alpha >0.                         (2)

Every prime-power valuation of n is retained. The external coprime mask is
ESSENTIAL: the unmasked shifted-log expression is generally positive even
when n shares a prime with R, whereas the actual Boolean difference is zero.
In particular a prime product in the syntax is not a license to remove its
mask or replace n by its prime-only part.

The unit has a different exact answer. For nonempty S,

 D_S K(1)=-(log R)^alpha.                                      (3)

To prove it, sum the integral for all nonempty U: their integrands sum to
R^-t-1, whose integral is -(log R)^alpha. The absent U=empty term is K(1)=0,
not the divergent empty-product integral. At S=empty the unit is zero.
Thus B_S(X) must start at n=2; assigning it a positive n=1 atom changes the
filter. No unit is encountered when E_p acts on the domain n>=2 itself.

2. Actual pointwise control and a complete compensation decomposition
=====================================================================
Equation (2) gives, for all n>=2,

 0<=K_S(n):=D_S K(n)<=K(n)<=(log n)^alpha.                      (4)

For nonempty S and gcd(n,R)=1, writing k=omega(n), it also gives

 K_S(n)<=min_{p|n}[(log R+log p)^alpha-(log R)^alpha],
 K_S(n)<=alpha*product_{j=1}^{k-1}(j-alpha)
                 *product_{p|n}log p/(log R)^(k-alpha).         (5)

The denominator is positive because R>1; the empty-set case must not use it.
Dropping factors in (2), or integrating its k-fold derivative with base
log R, proves (5). These are pointwise source-specific bounds, not estimates
of the signed prefix filter.

There is also an exact fully compensated partition for any finite prime set S:

 K=sum_{U subset S} E_U D_(S minus U) K,                        (6)

obtained by expanding product_{p in S}(E_p+D_p)=I. Each component is
nonnegative at n>=2 and at most K(n). Its masks and shifted arguments are
inherited from (2); none may be dropped. The filter is linear, so the full
target is the sum of the filtered components, with ALL their signed energy
cross terms. The residual E_S component is needed for recoverability.
Discarding it and controlling only D_S states is not a bound for K.

This supplies a compositional operator+complement language for later proof
search. It does not supply a strict contraction: the partition's integral
weights sum to 1, and can concentrate on a remaining component. Applying
Cauchy to the component sum introduces its dimension rather than a free
coefficient gap. A source-specific correlation or transport rule is still
needed after applying the signed scale filter.

3. A genuine bounded all-scale Laplace-tail remainder
=====================================================
Let S be fixed, R its squarefree prime product, s=|S|, and
B_S(X)=sum_{2<=n<=X}K_S(n). Define

 W(y)=floor(4y)-3floor(2y)+2floor(y),  |W(y)|<=1,
 t_S(N)=[B_S(4N)-3B_S(2N)+2B_S(N)]/alpha.

Here W is the exact binary digit difference, including endpoints. Put

 C_R(y)=number of positive integers k<=y with gcd(k,R)=1
       =sum_{e|R}mu(e)floor(y/e).

Finite divisor expansion inside the positive representation, followed by
the three-term filter, gives the exact complete formula

 t_S(N)=1/(alpha I_alpha) int_0^infinity R^-t H_S(N,t)
                                     *t^(-alpha-1) dt,
 H_S(N,t)=sum_{d<=4N, gcd(d,R)=1}mu(d)d^-t
                              *sum_{e|R}mu(e)W(N/(de)).        (7)

Explanation of every term: n=dk is coprime to R exactly when both d and k
are. Expansion of C_R gives the cofactor divisors e. Terms de>4N have zero
floor weight automatically. The n=1 exclusion subtracts 1 from each prefix
integrand; its filtered correction is zero because P(1)=2-3+1=0. The mixed
unit (3) cannot be omitted for arbitrary filters with P(1)!=0. There are no
infinite-source rearrangements in (7), and all prime powers are retained.
Convergence at zero follows from the original FINITE n>=2 positive products
in (1)-(2); expansion (7) must preserve their exact cancellations.

For the empty set R=1, the d=1 term in H is W(N)=0 for INTEGER N. Thus for
all t>=2,

 |H_empty(N,t)|<=sum_{d>=2}d^-t<=3*2^-t.                        (8)

The last bound is elementary: the d=2 term is 2^-t and the remainder is at
most int_2^infinity x^-t dx=2^(1-t)/(t-1). For nonempty S, including d=1,

 |H_S(N,t)|<=2^s*zeta(t)<=2^(s+1), t>=2.                       (9)

Here each of the 2^s cofactor terms is bounded by |W|<=1; restricting d to
gcd(d,R)=1 only decreases the absolute upper estimate. Since R>=2, the
external R^-t then gives exponential decay. At noninteger N, W(N) need not
vanish, so the empty-set bound (8) must not be reused unchanged. The primary
recurrence and target are integer-domain statements.

For completeness, the normalization has the elementary uniform bound

 alpha I_alpha >=1-exp(-1)>1/2, hence 1/(alpha I_alpha)<2<=3.

It follows just by integrating the normalizer over u>=1; exp(1)>2 follows
from its defining positive series. No Gamma asymptotic or RH result is used.
Also log(2)>=1/2 follows from int_1^2 du/u>=1/2.

For any FIXED INTEGER threshold tau>=2, split (7) at tau. The high-t part
is an explicitly PAID all-N bounded remainder:

 |t_empty,tail(N)| <=18*2^-tau,
 |t_S,tail(N)| <=12*2^s*R^-tau, nonempty S,
 every integer N>=2.                                          (10)

Proof: t^-alpha-1<=1 on this tail; integrate 2^-t or R^-t, use (8)-(9),
1/(alpha I)<=3, and 1/log R<=2 when R>=2. The displayed constants are
deliberately conservative rational bounds; no numerical integral is needed.
They can be made arbitrarily small by increasing a fixed tau. The missing
arithmetic is now confined to the finite parameter interval 0<t<tau.

The whole potential must retain the tail crosses. If t_i=l_i+e_i with the
tail bounds (10), then for the form below let
v_low=sum_i a_i l_i, e_v=sum_i a_i e_i. Exactly,

 F=[l0^2+lambda*v_low^2]/N
    +[2l0 e0+2lambda*v_low e_v+e0^2+lambda*e_v^2]/N.            (11)

Only the last two squares have an unconditional O(1/N) bound. The crosses
cannot be dropped. A rational Young allocation gives a valid target-majorant
recovery from a proved low-t bound, with explicit tail cost; it does not
automatically transfer the SAME drift constant or prove that low-t bound.
This bounded tail is substantive paid source control, while the small-t
joint source inequality remains OPEN.

4. Coercive potential and a source-specific complete drift expression
=====================================================================
Take a finite fixed family of insertion sets, fixed signed integer a_i,
and fixed rational lambda>=0. Each source has its actual coprime mask and
unit exclusion. Define

 v(N)=sum_i a_i t_i(N),
 F(N)=[t0(N)^2+lambda*v(N)^2]/N>=t0(N)^2/N.                    (12)

This retains the original target even when a companion vanishes, changes
sign, or cancels another companion. The signed mixture must be formed BEFORE
its square; replacing it by sum |a_i|t_i^2 is a different, usually larger
auxiliary. Including i=0 inside v is algebraically allowed. lambda=0 is
the explicit target-only control, not a new arithmetic mechanism.

Write z_N=(t0(N),v(N)), Q=diag(1,lambda), M=2N+r, r in {0,1},
and xi=z_M-z_N. The EXACT work identity is

 M*[F(M)-F(N)]
   =2<z_N,Q xi>+||xi||_Q^2-(1+r/N)||z_N||_Q^2.                (13)

No odd-child denominator is replaced by 2N. The increments are actual
source intervals:

 t_i(M)-t_i(N)=1/alpha *
 [2 sum_{N<n<=M}K_i(n)-3 sum_{2N<n<=2M}K_i(n)
                         +sum_{4N<n<=4M}K_i(n)].             (14)

All births, gcd masks, signs, units, and current-scale entries are retained.
Equations (13)-(14) expose a concrete joint cross/innovation proof leaf for
the detector. The identity alone is a re-expression of the energy drift;
it pays nothing about the sign of <z_N,Q xi>. A genuine argument must bound
those explicit arithmetic correlations, not fit a predictor and declare
its exact work a new source theorem.

5. All-integer induction, precise constants, and paid base
=========================================================
The proposed uniform leaf is

 F(2N+r)<=F(N)+C/N^delta,
 EVERY integer N>=2 and BOTH r=0,1,
 fixed rational C>=0, delta>=0.                               (15)

It is OPEN. Assuming it, halving any integer to 2 or 3 gives a rigorous
recovery rule. Let B>=max(F(2),F(3)). If delta=0,

 F(N)<=B+C*max(0,floor(log_2 N)-1).                            (16)

If delta>0, the forward ancestor chain grows at least by a factor 2 from
a base >=2, hence

 F(N)<=B+C/(2^delta-1)<=B+2C/delta.                            (17)

The latter constant is fully rational: 2^delta-1>=delta log(2)>=delta/2.
No positivity of an innovation/work term is assumed, but F itself is the
nonnegative quadratic majorant. If C=0 the stronger bound F<=B applies,
regardless of the nominal decay exponent. Both parities are necessary;
an even-only recurrence does not cover odd source chains. Finite base probes
or selected chains do not pay the universal quantifier in (15).

There is a wholly unconditional conservative base, without evaluating any
source atom. By (4), for N=2 or 3 and all n<=4N<=12, K_i(n)<3, since log(12)<3
and 0<alpha<1. The filter is a difference of a positive child block and twice
a positive parent block, each at most 6N. Thus |t_i(N)|<=6N/alpha. With
L1=sum_i |a_i|,

 B_generic=108/alpha^2*(1+lambda*L1^2)                         (18)

dominates both base energies. At the fixed alpha=1/5 it can be sharpened to
600*(1+lambda L1^2): use |t_i(N)|<=2N(log(4N))^alpha/alpha and
(log(12))^(2/5)<2. These base estimates are paid inequalities, not empirical
normalizations or substitutes for (15). The generic base (18) is sufficient.

Combining (12), (16), and (17), the conditional target growth is

 delta=0: |t0(N)|<=sqrt(N)*sqrt(B+C log_2 N),
 delta>0: |t0(N)|<=sqrt(N)*sqrt(B+2C/delta).                    (19)

The first is sqrt(N) times at most a square-root logarithm; the second is
a uniform square-root bound. Each supplies a 053 all-epsilon source premise
and hence RH conditionally. Neither rule promises the old constant C=2 in
the alpha-normalized target. It is the growth theorem that is recovered,
with explicit and possibly large constants.

6. Algebraic limitations and noncircular proof graph
====================================================
Positivity of K_i does not imply positivity or decay of its signed filter.
Projection identities alone cannot guarantee even zero forcing in (15).
A simple universal counterexample is a radical-invariant nonnegative source
which equals 1 on powers of one actual prime p and zero otherwise, with its
unit excluded. Its fresh-prime insertion operators are commuting positive
Boolean differences: inserting p keeps its value, inserting any other prime
annihilates it. Nevertheless a parent horizon can contain no supported term
while its child's new block contains p, giving F_child>0=F_parent. Choose
p>16 and N=ceil(p/4), then floor(N/2)>=2, 2N<p<=4N, and no power p^2 fits.
This violates a universal C=0 monotonicity assertion. It does not refute the
ordinary fractional-mu source; it shows which information the abstract
positive insertion algebra omits.

The exact projection D_S also kills all integers sharing a prime with S;
controlling only that projection has a real nullspace. A companion alone
cannot replace the retained t0 channel. The compensated identity (6) is an
honest way to extend the grammar if it includes all remaining components
and their full signed filter cross terms. No coefficient gap follows for
free from a positive partition or a pointwise product saving.

A safe obligation graph has paid nodes for source definition, distinct
actual primes, coprime/unit masks, (2), coercivity (12), the base (18),
bounded tail (10), and induction (16)-(19). Its principal unpaid node is
the EXACT uniformly quantified drift (15), or a finer arithmetic estimate
of (13)-(14) or the low-t joint expression (7) implying it. That node must
not depend on the recovered target bound, an equivalent unknown companion
RH theorem, or finite fitted constants. A detector may propose and falsify
these nodes, but cannot certify a cycle by relabeling one unknown square.
