Cancellation language: full quadratic obstruction and factor-block extension

The objective remains an all-scale signed arithmetic bound strong enough for RH. No such bound has been obtained.

The new full-Gram experiment searches two shared 5 by 5 positive-semidefinite matrices over prefix, half-prefix, even-index, residue-one-modulo-three and prime-prefix blocks. This includes every positive quadratic combination of these atoms, instead of a finite list of coefficient ratios. The existing 106 square and 231 relation groups remain available. Matrix weights and all structural weights are shared over X=1,2,3,5; final scales are X=11,13 without refitting. The critical program uses the necessary zero-cubic constraint, q=6 and C=32. The second program is a supplied unsigned calibration.

The critical numerical SDP failed to propose a certificate. That numerical failure is preserved as uncertified. A separately prescribed exact dual search then produced a rational separator, independently checked against every original square/relation/slack coefficient and both complete Gram moment matrices. Its lhs pairing is -1, all relation pairings vanish, all nonnegative-generator pairings are nonnegative, and both Gram dual matrices are PSD. Thus the obstruction excludes the ENTIRE declared shared PSD cone, even though the dual search itself imposed diagonal moment matrices.

All ordinary and auxiliary constant dual coordinates are zero. The exact separator therefore applies to every rational C>=0 and integer q>=1 for this fixed dictionary and zero-cubic constraint. For q>1 that constraint is necessary for the intended uniform template family; for q=1 it is an additional restriction. This excludes a proof language, not the actual arithmetic inequality. New atoms, relations, scale-dependent coefficient programs, or other proof structures are outside its scope. The older separator invalidated by adaptive additions is not reused here.

The unsigned program admitted an exact rational PSD certificate. It reduces identically at every integer scale to the old unsigned Cauchy bound: scalar child-half weight 65/64 plus child-half Gram diagonal 63/64 equals 2, and each parent prefix appears twice in the child half-prefix sum. Every offdiagonal Gram entry is zero. The cubic coefficient remains 56/3. Final X=11 was verified; X=13 remains missing_resource (expansion work/time), even though the independent algebraic reduction explains the template uniformly. No missing observation is relabelled successful.

The new factor language changes arithmetic structure. It compiles a full ordered product domain ab<=floor(cX), rational polynomial kernels in a,b,X, optional coprimality, and exact factor cutoffs floor(d X^(u/v)). It retains inside and complement blocks, all product multiplicities, noncoprime remainders and the complete coprime rewrite xa*xb-xab with its multiplier. No signed coefficient response table is used. A shared-group adapter preserves the original target, binds the same block specification AND formal multiplier across scales, namespaces the semantic group identity, and adds empty auxiliary rows. All added formal scalar terms are zero. These groups can enter the existing shared-group search; they have not yet entered a new bound-search experiment or the fixed five-atom Gram experiment.

Verification: the frozen full-Gram run passed 21 focused tests and the independent complete run audit. The supplied factor calibration passed 10 tests and six independently checked fixtures, including square-, cube- and sixth-root cutoffs, both coprime modes, a signed rational kernel and a fixed x1-x2 multiplier. Root rechecked all fixture/protocol/source bindings. This calibration tests supplied identities, not discovery of an estimate.

Next experiment: freeze a complexity-bounded enumeration of factor-block and relation-multiplier programs, add them to the shared search with complete complements, and retain all attempts. Moving prime bands and paired dilation differences should be additional compositional productions, with explicit prime-square, bank-change and boundary residuals. A finite accepted template must still earn both an all-scale structural proof and a uniform critical scalar/remainder estimate. Neither the expanded syntax nor invalidating an old dual supplies that estimate.
