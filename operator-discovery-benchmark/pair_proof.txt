NONLINEAR PRIME-SUM COUPLINGS WITH ORDINARY-ZETA ROOT CONTROLS

Status: written universal proofs with independent exact and Arb checks.

The operator is a nonlinear arithmetic map, not an associative product or

1. A RESTRICTION THE EARLIER ROOT GRAMMAR IMPOSED

Let D be ordinary Dirichlet convolution, with unit delta_1. Normalized
inputs have f(1)=1; augmentation inputs have h(1)=0. LOG_D and EXP_D
are inverse coefficient-local maps in characteristic zero.

Suppose each coefficient of a normalized map P is polynomial in finitely
many input coefficients. If P(f^(D 1/2))=P(f)^(D 1/2) for EVERY such f,
put Q(h)=LOG_D P(EXP_D h). Then Q(h/2)=Q(h)/2. In each output coordinate,
decompose Q into homogeneous polynomial parts Q_j. Comparison gives
  (2^(-j)-1/2) Q_j=0.
Only j=1 survives, including the vanishing constant term. Thus Q is linear
and P=EXP_D L LOG_D for a coefficient-local linear L. The same argument
works coefficientwise for a formal power series at the unit, interpreting
the identity as a formal identity. It does not cover arbitrary nonregular
maps that are merely homogeneous under real scaling.

Consequently universal INPUT-root commutation would exclude all nonlinear
logarithmic couplings before an empirical search. The new gate instead
requires a separate ordinary-zeta zero/branch bridge on the selected input.
The toy polynomial search scores 3125 programs and keeps five linear
scalars. This is a calibration of the theorem, not its proof or new operators.

2. THE BILINEAR INDEX RULE AND ITS NONLINEAR INVERSE

For integer r>=0 and augmentation inputs a,b define
  Phi_r(x,y)=xy(x+y)^r, x,y>=2,
  K_r(a,b)(N)=sum_(Phi_r(x,y)=N) a(x)b(y),
  P_r(f)=EXP_D(g+K_r(g,g)), g=LOG_D f.

Every fibre is finite and Phi_r(x,y)>max(x,y). Hence P_r is a
coefficient-local polynomial map with an exact inverse as a SET map:
given h=LOG_D(output), recover
  g(N)=h(N)-sum_(Phi_r(x,y)=N) g(x)g(y)
in ascending N and return EXP_D g. All arguments on the right are smaller
than N. This proves existence, uniqueness, two-sided inversion and prefix
consistency on all normalized characteristic-zero inputs.

K is commutative and bilinear. At r=0 it is classical D convolution on
the augmentation ideal. At r>=1 it is NOT generally associative: impulses
at 2,2,3 give indices Phi(Phi(2,2),3) and Phi(2,Phi(2,3)), which differ in
each tested positive r and, at r=1, are 912 and 1920. No EXP_K is used.
For all r>=1 the two values are respectively
  12*(16*4^r+12)^r and 12*(30*5^r+10)^r.
The second base is strictly larger, proving nonassociativity for every
positive r, beyond the finite examples.

The inverse log solves g=h-K(g,g). Its homogeneous degree-l term is
(-1)^(l-1) times the sum over ordered full binary trees with l leaves,
each leaf carrying h and each internal node carrying K. The recursion
splits a tree at its root. Coefficient-local termination follows because
every image is at least the product of its leaf indices, hence >=2^l.

For h=t*delta_2, summing coefficients in degree l gives
(-1)^(l-1) C_(l-1), with the CLASSICAL Catalan number C_j. The learned
mass ratio is C_l/C_(l-1)=(4l-2)/(l+1): 4032 rational programs, fourteen
training fits, one held-out survivor. The independent checker explicitly
enumerates all ordered tree shapes rather than using the production
coefficient recursion. At r=0 the inverse is the known expression
  g=(sqrt_D(delta_1+4h)-delta_1)/2.

At r=3 tree shapes acquire genuinely different integer labels. The
four-leaf balanced label is 8796093022208 with coefficient -1; the comb
label is 11954910012443774468835132966078494343168 with coefficient -4.
Their total -5 is classical; their labelled arithmetic content is separate.
This does not by itself establish historical originality.

3. INPUT ROOTS, OUTPUT ROOTS, AND THE EXACT ANOMALY

For beta in the coefficient field, keep the following families distinct:
  I_beta(f)=P_r(f^(D beta))=EXP_D(beta*g+beta^2*K_r(g,g)),
  O_beta(f)=P_r(f)^(D beta)=EXP_D(beta*g+beta*K_r(g,g)).
Their ordinary D quotient satisfies the universal exact identity
  I_beta /_D O_beta=EXP_D(beta*(beta-1)*K_r(g,g)).
The amplitude grammar scores 625 polynomials, has four training fits and
one fresh survivor beta^2. This confirms the nonlinear amplitude, whose
universal proof follows from bilinearity. In particular P is neither a
unit-group homomorphism nor universally input-root-equivariant. These
stronger claims are explicitly rejected on generic rational inputs.

4. PARITY QUOTIENT AND ACTUAL PRIME LABELS

For every r>=1, Phi_r(x,y) is even: either xy is even, or x,y are both
odd and x+y is even. Thus K_r takes values in the ideal of functions
supported on even indices. Restriction O to odd indices is a D algebra
homomorphism, because all divisors of an odd integer are odd. It commutes
with coefficient-local LOG_D and EXP_D. It follows universally that
  O(P_r(f))=O(f).
The inverse also fixes this quotient. Both root families restrict to the
same ordinary odd part of f^(D beta). For r=0 this statement fails.

For zeta input u(n)=1 and r=3, the first coupling is at Phi(2,2)=256,
with log-correction coefficient 1. Its exponential has no other nonunit
divisor of 256. Therefore P_3(u)(256)=2. In contrast 6561=3^8 is odd,
so P_3(u)(6561)=1. Swapping the labels 2 and 3 preserves u but changes
these values, proving actual-prime-label dependence on zeta itself.
The separate generic impulse counterexample is 256 -> 3^8=6561 versus
Phi(3,3)=1944. Generic inversion does not require factorization labels.
The actual zeta application is also nonmultiplicative: P_3(u)(750)=3
while P_3(u)(2)=P_3(u)(375)=1, and gcd(2,375)=1. The mixed pair 2,3
contributes twice at 750=2*3*5^3. These exact coefficients are included
in both previously verified full arithmetic profiles.

The odd-part Dirichlet series is (1-2^(-s))*zeta(s) at beta=1, and
((1-2^(-s))*zeta(s))^beta for either root family initially Re(s)>1.
This exact known-factor recovery is arithmetic structure, not an RH bound.

5. ABSOLUTE CORRECTION DOMAIN AND THE SCALAR ZETA BRIDGE

For zeta input g=LOG_D u, g(n)=1/e at prime powers n=p^e and zero
otherwise. Write the NORMALIZED Euler logarithm L(z)=LOG zeta(z) on
Re(z)>1. Define the ordinary positive-coefficient correction series
  Gamma_r(s)=sum_(x,y>=2) g(x)g(y)/[xy(x+y)^r]^s.

For sigma=Re(s)>2/(r+2), AM-GM gives
  Phi_r(x,y)>=2^r*(xy)^((r+2)/2),
  sum |terms| <=2^(-r*sigma)*L((r+2)*sigma/2)^2.
This proves absolute and locally uniform convergence, hence holomorphy.
It is a sufficient half-plane, not a claim of the exact maximal domain.
At r=3 the half-plane is Re(s)>2/5, covering both sides of the critical
line and the two existing simple-zero disks. The seven-r grammar selects
r=3 as the least r with this certificate crossing Re(s)=1/2. Absence
of that particular certificate for r=1,2 does not disprove continuation.

Initially Re(s)>1 the ordinary coefficient series of the root families are
  I_beta(u;s)=zeta(s)^beta * exp(beta^2*Gamma_r(s)),
  O_beta(u;s)=zeta(s)^beta * exp(beta*Gamma_r(s)).
The exponentials are holomorphic and zero-free in the correction domain
for EVERY fixed complex beta. At beta=1 the transform preserves all zeta
zeros and their multiplicities in that domain. Coefficients of both
families are nonnegative for real beta>=0.

For r>0, beta Mellin inversion gives, with A=rs/2 and B=(r+2)s/2,
  Gamma_r(s)=1/(2*pi)*integral_(t real)
    Gamma(A+it)*Gamma(A-it)/Gamma(rs)
    *L(B+it)*L(B-it) dt.
Indeed apply the Mellin transform of (1+v)^(-rs), supplied by Euler's
beta integral, to (x+y)^(-rs), and interchange the double sum and integral.
The sums become L(s+z) and L((r+1)s-z) on the contour z=rs/2+it. Their
real parts equal Re(B)>1, and exponential gamma decay justifies Fubini.
The integrand is even in t; integration from 0 to T uses factor 1/pi.

This is a prime-power-weighted Mordell--Tornheim-type construction. The
Tornheim kernel and beta Mellin inversion are classical, not discoveries
of this detector. The specific formal nonlinear map and its application
have unresolved historical status. The source evaluates native zeta in
the contour integral; the independent bridge uses one native zeta value
and a grouped arithmetic double sum, with different cutoffs and tails.

6. EXPLICIT CERTIFIED CONTOUR AND COEFFICIENT TAILS

Let a=r*sigma/2>0, U=|r*Im(s)/2|, b=ceil(2a)/2, p=2b-1>=0, and T>U+1.
The beta integral implies |Gamma(a+iy)|<=Gamma(a)/Gamma(b)*|Gamma(b+iy)|;
at a=b use equality. For integer or half-integer b, reflection and gamma
recurrence give, whenever |y|>=1,
  |Gamma(b+iy)|<=sqrt(C)*(|y|+b)^(b-1/2)*exp(-pi*|y|/2),
  C=2*pi/(1-exp(-2*pi)).
For |t|>=T, the product's exponential is exp(-pi*|t|). The total omitted
contour contribution is bounded by
  C/(pi*|Gamma(rs)|)*(Gamma(a)/Gamma(b))^2*L(Re(B))^2 * J,
  J=exp(-pi*T)*sum_(j=0)^p binom(p,j)*(T+U+b)^(p-j)*j!/pi^(j+1).
J is the exact integral of (t+U+b)^p*exp(-pi*t) from T to infinity.
Arb lower-bounds |Gamma(rs)| and encloses all other factors outwards.

The finite integral uses python-flint/Arb acb.integral, propagating its
analytic flag to the native logarithm. The general formula uses the Euler
LOG branch. At all numerical contour instances L(Re(B))<pi, so the native
principal logarithm is that branch. The small initial segments near the
critical line prevent indeterminate wide log enclosures. All final native
integrals are finite; no failed integral is silently accepted.

For a direct prime-power square cutoff M, put alpha=(r+2)*sigma/2,
S=L(alpha) and tail E<=M^(1-alpha)/(alpha-1). The square's omitted terms
have bound 2^(-r*sigma)*2*S*E. Independently, subtract the exact positive
prime-power prefix S_M from native S and use
  2^(-r*sigma)*(2*S_M*E+E^2), E=S-S_M.

For full arithmetic coefficient cutoff N, beta in [0,1] and c=beta or
beta^2, the baseline zeta^beta coefficients lie in [0,1]. The correction's
positive 1-norm is exp(c*Gamma_r(1)). Hence the entire output tail at
sigma>1 is bounded by
  (1+1/(sigma-1))*N^(1-sigma)*exp(c*Gamma_r(1)).
Use the proved AM-GM bound on Gamma_r(1), or independently replace L by
zeta-1. This follows by summing the ordinary convolution and applying the
integral bound to its baseline tail; it also covers correction indices>N.
Source N=4096, square M=64; independent N=6144, square M=80.
All six fresh complex cases pass. Each rejects a coefficient-2 perturbation,
a first-kernel-coefficient perturbation, and an incorrect contour factor.
The independent kernel-only beta integral verifies the normalization with
a separate real positive pair and its own explicit gamma tail.

7. ORDINARY-ZETA ZERO AND BRANCH SCOPE

Reuse existing independently checked simple-zeta-zero certificates near
1/2+14.1347251417i (radius 1/10000) and 1/2+21.0220396388i (radius 1/100000).
These are operator controls at existing certified zeros, not new zeta zeros.
Both entire disks lie in Re(s)>2/5. For their minimum real part sigma_0,
  |Gamma_3(s)|<=8^(-sigma_0)*L(5*sigma_0/2)^2=M.
Thus |exp(c*Gamma_3)|>=exp(-c*M)>0 for real c>=0 on the WHOLE disk.
The independent proof replaces L by zeta-1 and obtains another positive
whole-disk bound. Native centre integrals are additionally compared with
arithmetic double-sum enclosures; loose boundary double-sum tails are
explicitly recorded and are not claimed to give precise point values.

Around a simple zero, BOTH root families acquire the phase exp(2*pi*i*beta).
Their correction amplitudes differ (beta^2 versus beta), while the ordinary
zeta obstruction survives in each. Twelve controls test beta=1/2,1/4,1
in both families on both disks. The reported positive phase-factor bound is
  |(exp(2*pi*i*beta)-1)*C(s)|,
NOT the full H jump, which has the additional vanishing factor zeta^beta.
The integer phase is exactly zero. This stage says nothing new about
off-critical-line zeta zeros.

The r=0 known composite has Gamma_0=(LOG zeta)^2. Its correction changes
by exp(4*pi*i*LOG zeta-4*pi^2) after a loop around a simple zero. It is
therefore not a single-valued holomorphic correction across that zero.
A scoped toy logarithm numerically checks the nontrivial ratio; the
universal local argument is algebraic and uses the certified simple zero.

For either r=3 root family, single-valued holomorphic continuation of ALL
pole-cancelled dyadic roots to Re(s)>1/2 is equivalent to RH, by the existing
multiplicity/divisibility argument. The needed continuation is unproved.
Preserved zero multiplicities, invertibility, Catalan inverse trees, parity
and a zero-free explicit factor provide no new estimate establishing RH.
