EXACT CENTERED PRIME-DILATION REDUCTION074 AND ITS UNPAID PRICE
===============================================================

Conclusion and limited status
--------------------------------
Every centered_high ray on the admitted source reduces exactly to

 R(k)=a Z(k)+sum_(j=1..r-1) C_j
                         sum_(y_j<p<=y_(j+1), p prime) Z(k/p),
 y_j=(20k)^theta_j, C_j=sum_(i=1..j)c_i,          (1)

where1/2<=theta_1<...<theta_r<1 and sum_i c_i=0.  The SAME four-endpoint
stencil defines Z at both the integer parent and rational child scales.
The coefficient signs C_j are cumulative signed coefficients, not their
absolute values.  Every prime above the largest cutoff has zero weight.
Every remaining cofactor horizon is less than sqrt(20k).

For fixed coefficients and a fixed finite cutoff list, the centered
smooth part in (1) is O_A(k/log^A k) for EVERY fixed A>0.  Thus exact
centering removes every fixed inverse-log bulk order, not just the
leading2k/log^2 k term.  This is an unconditional upper bound, not any
fixed sublinear-power theorem or any uniform storage recurrence.

A hypothetical pointwise Mertens power bound gives a generic triangle
price growing like k^(alpha+theta_*(1-alpha))/log k for0<=alpha<1,
where theta_* is the largest upper exponent of a nonzero coefficient
block.  Its multiplier relative to k^alpha is unbounded.  Smaller
cofactor radius does not make that triangle rule a scalar contraction.
The exact signed prime-dilation aggregate and its correlation with
the separate current-scale term aZ(k) remain the new obligations.

This is a postrun analytical review, outside the frozen074 scientific
files.  It writes only this file, reads no new scientific dataset or
numerical output, performs no source-scale query, uses no hashes, and
changes no frozen073/074 implementation or result.  No numerical failure
location is needed for its proof.  It imports the unconditional Mertens
and PNT facts already identified in the073 analytical reviews.  All
new reductions, bounds and scope distinctions are proved below.  No
actual candidate recurrence, joined-head bound, or RH theorem is claimed.

1. The actual source and its exact rational-scale extension
-------------------------------------------------------------
For EVERY real t>0 define

 w_t(n)=1_(8t,10t](n)-1_(16t,20t](n),
 Z(t)=sum_(n>=1)mu(n)w_t(n)
     =M(10t)-M(8t)-M(20t)+M(16t),                (2)
 M(u)=sum_(1<=n<=u)mu(n), M(u)=0 for0<=u<1.

Each M argument means its exact integer floor.  Therefore(2) agrees
literally with the admitted original source at integer k and defines
the same stencil at real or rational scales, without interpolation or
rounding of an observed integer-scale value.  An endpoint n=8t or16t
is excluded from its corresponding band; n=10t or20t is included.

For an integer parent k>=1 its unit coordinate w_k(1)=0.  The complete
signed squarefree factor sectors satisfy
U_1+U_2+U_3+U_ge4=Z(k).  Hence a general physical ray with balanced factor
coefficients v_2=v_3=v_4=v_5=b and arbitrary unit coefficient reduces to

          R(k)=(v_Z+b)Z(k)+sum_theta c_theta S_theta(k). (3)

centered_high sets every theta<1/2 coefficient to zero and imposes the
exact zero sum of the remaining smooth coefficients.  Put a=v_Z+b and
list those remaining fixed exponents as theta_1,...,theta_r, retaining
zero coefficients if desired.  The empty list or a one-element list
with zero coefficient has a zero centered smooth part.

The parent unit elimination does NOT apply automatically to the children
in (1).  A rational child t=k/p may be less than1 at small parent scales,
and w_t(1) can then be nonzero.  For example its positive unit band is
1/10<=t<1/8 and its negative unit band is1/20<=t<1/16.  M(t)'s unit
contributions in (2) are therefore essential finite terms.  A child
argument below1 is an empty prefix; an argument in[1,2) contains exactly
the unit.  Neither may be silently removed or treated as a large-scale
asymptotic value.

2. Exact prefix identity with all prime and square gates
----------------------------------------------------------
Let

 A_mu(x,y)=sum_(n<=x,P+(n)<=y)mu(n), P+(1)=1,

where x>=0, y>=1, and y>=sqrt(x).  A nonzero nonsmooth term has a unique
large prime p>y.  A product of two primes>y, including a repeated prime,
would exceed y^2>=x.  Thus n=pm has m<=x/p<x/y<=y<p; p cannot divide m,
all factors of m are below p, and mu(pm)=-mu(m).  Squareful m and n
contribute zero.  Conversely every p prime with y<p<=x and every
integer1<=m<=x/p satisfies these gates.  Counting these terms gives

       A_mu(x,y)=M(x)+sum_(y<p<=x, p prime)M(x/p). (4)

This is the finite reordering of the exact cofactor identity established
in MOBIUS_PRIME_STORAGE_CANDIDATE_REVIEW_073.txt, equation(6).  It is not
an independence approximation or a theorem about only prime anchors.

All endpoints are literal: p=y is excluded, p=x is included with its
M(1) term, and a prime p with x/p an integer includes that integer
cofactor.  At y=sqrt(x), the strict p>y inequality still excludes a
large prime square.  If y>x the sum is empty and(4) gives A_mu=M.
For x<1 both prefixes and the prime sum are empty.  The unit is included
in A_mu and M when x>=1, y>=1.

For real algebraic y, the prime/smooth indicators are unchanged by
floor(y).  In particular a prime p>floor(y) is also p>y.  We keep the
real y in factor-gate inequalities and in the open-left/closed-right
prime blocks; this avoids any erroneous claim that floor(sqrt(x))
itself has square at least x.

3. Exact source identity and shorter rational children
---------------------------------------------------------
Put X=20k for integer k>=1 and y=X^theta with1/2<=theta<1.  Then
y^2>=X>=x for EACH source endpoint x in{8k,10k,16k,20k}.  Apply(4) to
all four prefixes with the SAME y.  Extend each prime sum to p<=X,
using M(x/p)=0 whenever p>x.  The exact result is

 S_theta(k)=Z(k)+sum_(y<p<=X, p prime)G_k(p),
 G_k(p)=M(10k/p)-M(8k/p)-M(20k/p)+M(16k/p)
       =Z(k/p).                                 (5)

The last equality invokes precisely the rational-scale extension(2).
The four signed endpoints and all their shared ordinary mu values
remain inside ONE child stencil.  There are no independent copies of
M, no removed composites, and no replacement by its absolute value.

Every prime in(5) is>y>=sqrt(X).  Consequently

 20(k/p)=X/p<X/y<=sqrt(X),                       (6)

so its integer cofactor access is indeed strictly shorter than the
parent source horizon X.  This gate includes squarefree/coprimality
conditions already paid in(4).  The prime-query horizon is nonetheless
that of the displayed parent cutoff interval, not the cofactor horizon.

For integer k and prime p, k/p is generally a positive rational, not an
integer.  The frozen073/074 dyadic recurrence premises quantify over
integer scales only.  They DO NOT license applying their F(t), source
invariant, or parent relation at t=k/p.  Rounding t to floor(t) changes
all four endpoints and the exact function in(5).  A recursive proof using
these children must explicitly admit the rational dilation domain,
or prove a separate theorem that transfers an integer invariant to it
with every boundary price.  Smaller arithmetic access alone does not
pay that additional domain obligation.

4. Centering as an exact signed block operator
-------------------------------------------------
Let y_j=X^theta_j, c_j fixed, and sum_j c_j=0.  Summing(5) gives

 H(k):=sum_j c_j S_theta_j(k)
 =sum_j c_j Z(k)+sum_(p<=X, p prime)W_k(p)G_k(p),
 W_k(p)=sum_j c_j 1[p>y_j].                      (7)

The first term is EXACTLY zero.  Also W_k(p)=0 for p<=y_1 and for
p>y_r, the latter because it equals the complete sum of c_j.  On each
block(y_j,y_(j+1)] it equals

                         C_j=sum_(i<=j)c_i.     (8)

Thus

 H(k)=sum_(j=1..r-1) C_j B_j(k),
 B_j(k)=sum_(y_j<p<=y_(j+1), p prime)Z(k/p),
 R(k)=aZ(k)+H(k).                               (9)

The sign is important: for c_1=1,c_2=-1, the difference
S_theta1-S_theta2 is PLUS the prime-block sum in(9), since the source
contribution newly admitted by the larger smooth cutoff has sign
-mu(m).  A prime exactly equal to y_(j+1) is still in block j and does
not yet activate c_(j+1); a prime exactly y_j is excluded from block j.
These conventions exactly match the indicator p>y_j in(7).

Real cutoffs are increasing.  Their integer floors may coincide or a
block may contain no primes; the corresponding sum is exactly zero.
No negative/inverted prime count is manufactured.  The empty/one-term
cases and all zero cumulative coefficients give H=0.  Primes above
the largest cutoff disappear by exact cancellation before a norm is
taken; their cofactor units and other small terms are cancelled, not
discarded individually.  Prime/cofactor units still present in a
nonempty retained block remain inside its Z(k/p).

Equations(7)-(9) define an exact signed prime-dilation operator

          (P_(c,theta)f)(k)=sum_j C_j
                        sum_(y_j<p<=y_(j+1)) f(k/p). (10)

Its cutoff geometry depends on the integer parent k.  Its input f must
be defined on the displayed rational children.  The current source
obligation is R(k)=aZ(k)+(P_(c,theta)Z)(k), with aZ(k) kept separate.

5. Uniform cofactor growth after complete centering
------------------------------------------------------
Assume H is not identically zero as a formal block expression.  Let
theta_max be the largest retained fixed theta, let theta_min be the
smallest, and put delta=1-theta_max>0.  Every contributing prime obeys

                 X^theta_min<p<=X^theta_max.

For each of its four positive endpoint arguments, with b in{8,10,16,20},

 (b/20)X^delta <= b k/p < (b/20)X^(1-theta_min),
 log(b k/p)>=delta log X+log(b/20).             (11)

Hence EVERY endpoint cofactor argument tends to infinity uniformly over
ALL primes in ALL the retained blocks.  This is the new fact supplied
by vanishing prime weights above the largest fixed sublinear cutoff.
For sufficiently large k, their logs are at least(delta/2)log X and
the quantitative Mertens theorem applies throughout the entire range.

The existing primary unconditional input is

 |M(t)|<=D_A t/(log t)^A
            for all sufficiently large t and every fixed A>0. (12)

It follows, for example, from Lee and Leong, "New explicit bounds for
Mertens function and the reciprocal of the Riemann zeta-function,"
Theorem1.1, equation(9), and Theorem2.2.  This is already imported and
verified in the073 analytical reviews.  Only its unconditional
asymptotic consequence(12) is used here.
[Lee and Leong, primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.06141)

Using(11)-(12) at all four endpoints gives

 |G_k(p)|<=54 D_A (2/delta)^A
                        k/[p(log X)^A].        (13)

The number54 is8+10+16+20, the sum of the absolute endpoint sizes.
The constants and the starting scale depend on A and the FIXED delta,
but not on k or the individual prime/cofactor.  Below that starting
scale, the exact identities still hold with all empty/unit prefixes;
those finitely many integer parents are legitimate finite base costs,
not silently replaced by(13).

6. All inverse-log bulk orders disappear: a proved upper bound
----------------------------------------------------------------
Put C_*=max_j|C_j|, with C_*=0 when there are no blocks.  Using only
the all-integer harmonic bound, for any desired fixed A>0 apply(13)
with Mertens exponent A+1.  Then

 |H(k)|<=54 C_* D_(A+1)(2/delta)^(A+1)
           k/(log X)^(A+1) sum_(p<=X)1/p
        <=constant_(A,c,theta) k/(log X)^A.       (14)

Thus no additional prime-distribution theorem is needed to prove

       H(k)=O_(A,c,theta)(k/(log k)^A)
                         for EVERY fixed A>0.  (15)

There is a slightly sharper constant bookkeeping if the existing
unconditional PNT is used.  It implies pi(t)<=K t/log t for large t.
Partial summation gives, for L_0=X^theta_min,U_0=X^theta_max,

 sum_(L_0<p<=U_0)1/p
 =pi(U_0)/U_0-pi(L_0)/L_0
                         +integral_(L_0..U_0)pi(t)/t^2 dt
 <=K[1/log U_0+log(log U_0/log L_0)]
 =K[1/(theta_max log X)+log(theta_max/theta_min)]. (16)

The prime reciprocal price is bounded for fixed cutoffs; since
theta_min>=1/2, its limiting ratio term is at most log2.  Equation(13)
with exponent A then suffices directly.  One may keep each block's
|C_j| price separately instead of using C_*, preserving all parameter
dependence.  The unsharpened harmonic argument(14) already proves(15).

Z(k) itself is O_A(k/log^A k) by(12) at the four original endpoints.
Therefore every fixed centered_high ray R=aZ+H satisfies the same
all-log-power upper-bound family.  For a fixed finite positive quadratic
storage, it follows only that

 E(k)=O_A(k^2/(log k)^(2A)),
 E(k)/k^q=O_A(k^(2-q)/(log k)^(2A))              (17)

for every fixed A, with constants depending also on the actual fixed
ray coefficients, their nonnegative weights and the finite list.

Equation(15) rigorously means that no nonzero fixed inverse-log bulk
coefficient can survive in H.  It does not assert convergence of a
complete asymptotic series, and it does not prove any fixed power gain.
For any fixed A and beta<1, k/log^A k still exceeds k^beta eventually.
Even the family for EVERY fixed A does not imply O(k^beta): the scalar
example k exp(-sqrt(log k)) satisfies that family and exceeds every
k^beta with beta<1.  This example diagnoses the logical scope of the
estimate; it is not asserted to equal the actual arithmetic source.
One cannot let A vary with k while suppressing D_A, base thresholds and
the delta-dependent constants.  Nothing in(15)-(17) pays logarithmic
growth of F, constant dyadic drift, or an RH-strength power bound.

7. Generic triangle price under a hypothetical Mertens power bound
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Suppose, purely as a CONDITIONAL input, that

          |M(t)|<=B t^alpha for every real t>=1, (18)

where B is fixed and0<=alpha<1.  For arguments0<t<1, M(t)=0 makes the
same inequality harmless; all units at larger arguments remain included.
Define

 K_alpha=8^alpha+10^alpha+16^alpha+20^alpha,
 T_alpha(k)=sum_j |C_j|
                         sum_(y_j<p<=y_(j+1))p^(-alpha). (19)

Then the exact four-endpoint stencil gives

 |Z(t)|<=B K_alpha t^alpha,
 |H(k)|<=B K_alpha k^alpha T_alpha(k),
 |R(k)|<=|a| |Z(k)|+B K_alpha k^alpha T_alpha(k). (20)

If(18) is licensed only over the smaller child range rather than the
current parent endpoints, only the last form with separate |aZ(k)|
is available.  If it holds globally, one can further bound that term
to obtain B K_alpha k^alpha[|a|+T_alpha(k)].  Neither version is an
unconditional consequence of the logarithmic estimates above.

The exact prime-count price can be exported without replacing it by
the number of all integers.  For a block(L,U] and0<=alpha<1,

 sum_(L<p<=U)p^(-alpha)
 =U^(-alpha)pi(U)-L^(-alpha)pi(L)
                  +alpha integral_(L..U)pi(t)t^(-alpha-1)dt. (21)

This is a Stieltjes/partial-summation identity with the same strict
lower and closed upper endpoints.  At alpha=0 it is exactly pi(U)-pi(L).
It includes empty prime blocks and is not an unpaid replacement of
prime counts by a density.  The elementary inequality
sum_(L<p<=U)p^(-alpha)<=L^(-alpha)[pi(U)-pi(L)]
is another finite price, though generally less sharp than(21).

For assessing asymptotic operator size, the existing unconditional PNT
pi(t)~t/log t implies, for every fixed0<=alpha<1,

 sum_(p<=U)p^(-alpha)
           ~U^(1-alpha)/[(1-alpha)log U].        (22)

To see this directly, use partial summation from2, and the elementary
integral equivalent
integral_2^U t^(-alpha)/log t dt
~U^(1-alpha)/[(1-alpha)log U].  The latter follows by differentiating
the proposed equivalent or by l'Hopital's rule.  Its endpoint term
and alpha times the integral combine to the factor1/(1-alpha).
The alpha=0 case is the PNT itself.  No short-interval PNT is needed,
since the two block endpoints are fixed distinct powers of X.

A checked primary PNT source already used in the073 candidate review
is Trudgian, "Updating the error term in the prime number theorem,"
Theorem2.  Its unconditional quantitative error implies the PNT and
the upper bound used in(16).
[Trudgian, primary paper, Theorem2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.2689)

For a fixed block L=X^theta_j,U=X^theta_(j+1), the lower endpoint
contribution to(22) is negligible because theta_j<theta_(j+1).  Thus

 sum_(y_j<p<=y_(j+1))p^(-alpha)
 ~X^(theta_(j+1)(1-alpha)) /
             [(1-alpha)theta_(j+1)log X].        (23)

If some C_j is nonzero, let j_* be its largest index and put
theta_*=theta_(j_*+1).  The nonnegative triangle price then obeys

 T_alpha(k)~|C_(j_*)| X^(theta_*(1-alpha)) /
                    [(1-alpha)theta_* log X].   (24)

All smaller block exponents are lower-order in this price.  If every
C_j=0, T_alpha=H=0 instead; do not assign an artificial positive cost.

Consequently the generic child-triangle upper envelope in(20) has
order

 B K_alpha |C_(j_*)|20^(theta_*(1-alpha)) /
                     [(1-alpha)theta_*]
        times k^(alpha+theta_*(1-alpha))/log(20k). (25)

This is the size of THAT permitted upper envelope, not a lower bound
on actual |H| or |R|.  Its parameter dependence includes B, all four
stencil endpoints, the actual cumulative coefficient,1/(1-alpha),
theta_*, and the fixed cutoff gaps that allow the lower-order block
comparison.  The asymptotic is not claimed uniform as alpha approaches1
or a cutoff gap shrinks with k.

At alpha=1/2 the envelope exponent is(1+theta_*)/2, strictly above1/2
and at least3/4.  Its multiplier relative to the hypothetical k^alpha
invariant is T_alpha(k), which tends to infinity whenever a block is
active and alpha<1.  Thus the generic prime-by-prime triangle transfer
cannot close a scale-independent scalar contraction in that norm.
It may give a fixed sublinear bound CONDITIONAL on(18), but does not
preserve its input exponent or supply the desired constant drift.

At alpha=1 the price instead tends to
sum_j |C_j|log(theta_(j+1)/theta_j), by prime reciprocal summation.
Even if coefficients make that multiplier small, the admitted invariant
has linear exponent and is not an RH-strength gain.  That endpoint
should not be recovered by substituting alpha=1 into(24), whose
1/(1-alpha) form is for fixed alpha<1 only.

8. The exact remaining signed obligation for detector search
---------------------------------------------------------------
An auditable supplied operator can export the following symbolic data:

 * Registered profile(2), integer parent k>=1, X=20k.
 * Fixed finite theta list in[1/2,1), rational c_j with exact zero sum.
 * Exact cumulative coefficients C_j and prime blocks(y_j,y_(j+1)].
 * Shared child stencil Z(k/p) at positive rational scales, including
   every floor, empty argument, unit and squareful zero convention.
 * Child cofactor horizon X/p<sqrt(X); prime-query horizon y_r.
 * Separate current-scale coefficient a and term aZ(k).
 * The complete signed aggregate aZ(k)+sum_j C_j B_j(k), with no
   per-prime or per-block absolute value in its definition.

The analytical leaf can be a genuine uniform signed aggregate price
for this family, for example

 |sum_j C_j sum_(y_j<p<=y_(j+1))Z(k/p)|
                        <=D k^(1/2+epsilon)     (26)

for EVERY integer k beyond a declared paid finite base, with D depending
on the fixed coefficients, cutoffs and epsilon but not on k's evaluated
values.  This would be a new prime/cofactor correlation theorem, not a
consequence of smaller child rank or positive semidefiniteness.  It
would still leave the separate aZ(k) term unless that term is paid or
a complete coupled price is proved.  A coupled target is

 |aZ(k)+(P_(c,theta)Z)(k)|<=D k^(1/2+epsilon),    (27)

or directly the full storage drift inequality with its actual base,
weights, normalization and every cross term.  Merely defining D as a
finite observed maximum does not license either assertion.

If a detector factors a block energy, its genuine within-block square
contains ordered prime pairs Z(k/p)Z(k/q), with the same underlying
ordinary mu values in all four child endpoints.  Multiple blocks and
the aZ(k) term introduce their corresponding cross terms as well.
Dropping those terms changes the energy and can lose the actual signed
interaction.  A small finite variance or a finite successful recurrence
does not pay its all-scale operator norm or drift.  Conversely the
growing triangle price(25) does not prove the signed aggregate cannot
cancel; it pinpoints the cost a successful signed rule must beat.

Any iterative proof must carry the explicit additional scale-domain
obligation from section3.  Starting with integer k, repeated prime
dilations produce rational k/(p_1...p_m).  An integer-only dyadic law
is not a theorem for that family, and a floor substitution requires
new endpoint correction estimates.  The radius decrease in(6) is a
real arithmetic fact, while the number and weights of prime children,
current prime access, rational domains, coefficient dependencies,
and the full signed correlation price remain separate requirements.

Disposition
--------------
The exact reduction(9) is valid for EVERY centered_high ray, including
the registered theta=1/2 boundary, general balanced factor coefficients,
arbitrary Z/unit coefficients, empty lists and empty prime blocks.
It preserves the original signed source stencil at rational children
and keeps aZ(k) separate.  Complete zero-sum centering removes all fixed
inverse-log bulk orders through the proved unconditional family(15).
That family supplies no fixed sublinear power, no scalar contraction,
and no actual candidate recurrence theorem.  The precise remaining
object is the signed prime-dilation operator(10), with rational-scale
admission and the bound for its correlation with the current source
both still unpaid.
