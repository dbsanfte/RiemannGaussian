1. Modewise infinite Euler factorization and its actual domain
--------------------------------------------------------------
Write u=1/3, v=2/3, kappa=1-u=2/3, beta=9/2. Let J be an allowed-bin
subset of {hu,hv,ku,kv}, with indicators a_h,b_h,a_k,b_k in {0,1} for
its u/v bins on each leg. Put k_h=a_h+b_h, k_k=a_k+b_k and
  r_q=1-q^(-u), w_q=1-q^(-v),
  R_J(q)=a_h*r_q+b_h*w_q, S_J(q)=a_k*r_q+b_k*w_q.
The infinite cumulative-mode series, initially Re(s),Re(t)>1, is
  F_J(s,t)=prod_q [1-R_J(q)*q^(-s)-S_J(q)*q^(-t)].             (1.1)
Its complete full-occupancy combination is
  F_SF(s,t)=sum_J (-1)^(4-|J|)*F_J(s,t).                       (1.2)
The coefficient of h^(-s)k^(-t) in beta^2*F_SF is exactly
  a(h)*a(k)*1_(hk squarefree),
where a=p-d is the candidate-10 mixed-prime coefficient. On squarefree
integers d=0. Each leg must occupy both of its bins. All prime-absent and
coprime conditions are included; this is not the unrestricted tensor square.

Separate the first-order prime terms to obtain
  F_J(s,t)=zeta(s)^(-k_h)*zeta(s+u)^(a_h)*zeta(s+v)^(b_h)
           *zeta(t)^(-k_k)*zeta(t+u)^(a_k)*zeta(t+v)^(b_k)
           *E_J(s,t).                                        (1.3)
Here the q factor of E_J is
  [1-R_J*q^(-s)-S_J*q^(-t)]
   *(1-q^(-s))^(-k_h)*(1-q^(-s-u))^(a_h)*(1-q^(-s-v))^(b_h)
   *(1-q^(-t))^(-k_k)*(1-q^(-t-u))^(a_k)*(1-q^(-t-v))^(b_k).
Its difference from one is bounded on compact sets by a constant times
  q^(-2 Re(s))+q^(-2 Re(t))+q^(-Re(s)-Re(t)).
Consequently E_J is an absolutely normally convergent holomorphic product
for Re(s),Re(t)>1/2. The finitely many local factors may have zeros, not
poles. Formula (1.3) is a meromorphic continuation on this domain.
It explicitly RETAINS poles from zeros of zeta(s) or zeta(t); no zero-free
half-plane is inferred. Near the real point (kappa,kappa), however, both
zeta denominators are nonzero, so a sufficiently small local polydisc is
legitimate without any RH hypothesis. The v-shift pole at 1/3 lies outside
this remainder domain; this argument makes no unsupported continuation
claim there.

The physical variables for a product weight (hk)^(-gamma) are
  s=z1+gamma, t=z2+gamma.
Thus all of (1.3), including its poles, shifts with gamma. A finite prime
polynomial is entire in z1,z2 and is not its infinite analytic continuation
uniformly on arbitrary left half-planes. The earlier finite Perron proof
never claimed such a limiting interchange.

2. Strictly nonzero full-row double residue
-------------------------------------------
For real 0<x<1, zeta(x)<0: the alternating eta series is positive and
zeta(x)=eta(x)/(1-2^(1-x)). In particular Z=zeta(kappa)<0 and
W=zeta(kappa+v)=zeta(4/3)>0.

Every local E_J factor is positive at (kappa,kappa). To see the only
nontrivial numerator issue, set y=q^(-1/3). The full-bin local factor,
which is the smallest among the allowed-bin factors at this point, is
  1-2*(r_q+w_q)*q^(-kappa)
    =1-4*y^2+2*y^3+2*y^4
    =(1-2*y^2)^2+2*y^3*(1-y)>0, 0<y<1.                       (2.1)
All normalizing factors are positive. Absolute convergence then gives
E_J(kappa,kappa)>0, rather than merely a nonnegative limiting product.

Only the four J containing hu AND ku contribute a double kappa pole.
Index their optional hv,kv by epsilon,delta in {0,1}. Since the zeta
pole residue at one is one, their double residues are
  Z^(-2-epsilon-delta)*W^(epsilon+delta)*E_J(kappa,kappa).
Their full-row inclusion signs are (-1)^(epsilon+delta). Therefore
  D_SF := Res_(s=kappa) Res_(t=kappa) F_SF(s,t)
    =sum_(epsilon,delta in {0,1})
       |Z|^(-2-epsilon-delta)*W^(epsilon+delta)*E_J(kappa,kappa)
    >0.                                                      (2.2)
There is NO cancellation of this double residue between occupancy modes.
Other modes may have single poles; they cannot cancel its double coefficient.

Locally the symmetric meromorphic germ can be written uniquely as
  F_SF(s,t)=D_SF/[(s-kappa)*(t-kappa)]
             +A(s)/(t-kappa)+A(t)/(s-kappa)+H(s,t),             (2.3)
with A and H holomorphic near the point. For example, subtract the double
part from the residue in t to define A(s); the remaining germ defines H.
The single-residue functions are part of the compensation obligation too.

The double-residue term in the unweighted double-Perron prefix is
  beta^2*D_SF*n^(2*kappa)/kappa^2.                             (2.4)
This is an exact local residue calculation if a contour is moved across
those two poles with the remaining integrals retained. It is NOT a proved
asymptotic for the actual sharp prefix: zeros elsewhere, single residues,
and the remaining contours have not been bounded. If it were the leading
term, its block contribution would be
  [beta^2*D_SF/kappa^2]
   *[(2^(2*kappa+1)-1)/(2*kappa+1)]*X^(2*kappa-1),
namely a positive constant times X^(1/3).

For gamma=1/6 the local poles are z1=z2=kappa-gamma=1/2.
Their residue term is instead
  beta^2*D_SF*n^(2*(kappa-gamma))/(kappa-gamma)^2
    =4*beta^2*D_SF*n.
Its putative leading block contribution is constant, not the
X^(-1/3+epsilon) product-local saving needed to undo this weight at balanced
products. Thus a bounded weighted endpoint is compatible with an unbounded
uncentered unweighted term.

The earlier low rectangle has real line c=1/log(2X-1). For sufficiently
large X it is left of z=1/2. The finite Euler bound there remains correct;
the high complement contains the rest of the finite Perron limit. One may
not silently identify this low rectangle with a pole-subtracted infinite
series or use it to erase (2.2).

3. What is refuted, and what the local pole does not yet prove
-------------------------------------------------------------
The isolated squarefree product-band proposal is uncentered and is not a
justified sufficient route to the COMPLETE target as previously motivated.
It asks for an extra saving against a genuine pole component. It should be
replaced by a joint centered statement, not installed as a proved lemma.

A rigorous simpler obstruction is available for a stronger rectangular
version. Let C_SF(x,y) be the sharp rectangular prefix of the coefficients
of F_SF. If for every epsilon>0 it satisfied
  |C_SF(x,y)|<=C_epsilon*(xy)^(1/2+epsilon), x,y>=1,
then two summations by parts would make
  F_SF(s,t)=s*t*integral_1^infinity integral_1^infinity
                C_SF(x,y)*x^(-s-1)*y^(-t-1) dy dx
holomorphic for Re(s),Re(t)>1/2, choosing epsilon below the distance to
the boundary. This contradicts (2.2). The raw rectangular version is FALSE.

The proposed same-X W_X-weighted product-band bound is not literally this
rectangular hypothesis. A local two-variable pole alone does not supply a
sharp diagonal asymptotic or an implication between these two kernel
families. Accordingly this memo does NOT assert an unconditional refutation
of that exact same-X inequality from (2.2) alone. Such a refutation requires
a diagonal Mellin/contour argument with all other singularities accounted
for, or an independently proved asymptotic. Assuming RH to dismiss those
terms and then presenting the result as unconditional would be circular.
What is already certain is the nonzero polar germ and the missing centering.

4. Exact full centering at every product cutoff, including the unit
------------------------------------------------------------------
Let A(n)=sum_(h<=n)a(h), A(0)=0, and let m_u be the old EXACT residue
coefficient. With R_u=kappa*m_u define
  c(h)=m_u*[h^kappa-(h-1)^kappa], h>=1,
  b(h)=a(h)-c(h).                                             (4.1)
In particular a(1)=0 but c(1)=m_u and b(1)=-m_u. The unit must be kept.
Then sum_(h<=n)c(h)=m_u*n^kappa exactly, and
  B(n)=sum_(h<=n)b(h)=A(n)-m_u*n^kappa.
No floor error or fractional-power derivative is discarded.

For any gamma>=0, X integer>=1, N=2X-1 and real T>=1, define
  J_gamma,X(T)=X^(-2)*sum_(h,k<=N, hk<=T)
       W_X(h,k)*(hk)^(-gamma)*b(h)*b(k).                       (4.2)
Its summand is EXACTLY
  a(h)*a(k)-a(h)*c(k)-c(h)*a(k)+c(h)*c(k).                     (4.3)
Split each of these three symmetric classes AA, AC+CA, CC by hk squarefree
or nonsquarefree. These are six sectors. Their sum is (4.2), at EVERY
cutoff and for EVERY retained product kernel. A restricted kernel does not
permit one to subtract a global scalar main term after the restriction.

At gamma=0,T=N^2,
  J_0,X(N^2)=X^(-2)*sum_(X<=n<2X)|A(n)-m_u*n^kappa|^2.        (4.4)
This is a positive full energy; its individual product sectors and proper
cutoffs generally are indefinite. The AA/SF sector is beta^2 times the
old occupancy projection. The AA/nonsquarefree sector includes repeated
valuations and shared primes; AC and CC include all unit and main crosses.

Analytic recovery of the center also checks exactly. If mathcal D(s) is
the old same-prime coefficient series, its complete local mass beta and
support q^e,e>=2 give absolute convergence for Re(s)>1/2. Thus
  mathcal A(s)=P(s)-mathcal D(s)
has residue R_u at s=kappa. Furthermore
  c(h)=R_u*h^(-u)+O(h^(-u-1)), h>=2,
so its Dirichlet series is
  mathcal C(s)=R_u*zeta(s+u)+an analytic remainder,
the remainder being holomorphic for Re(s)>-u. The exact h=1 term is
included in that remainder. Consequently mathcal A-mathcal C is
holomorphic near real kappa, and the UNRESTRICTED pair series
  [mathcal A(s)-mathcal C(s)]*[mathcal A(t)-mathcal C(t)]        (4.5)
has neither double nor single kappa poles. This is pole compensation,
not a zero-free theorem or a prefix moment estimate.

The AA/full double residue is R_u^2. Its AA/SF part is beta^2*D_SF and
its AA/nonsquarefree part is R_u^2-beta^2*D_SF. The AC+CA/full and CC/full
double residues are respectively -2*R_u^2 and R_u^2, so they cancel in
(4.5). These identities quantify the full-center recovery which an
isolated SF payment would lose.

Even centering INSIDE the squarefree sector is not automatically enough.
Coprime/squarefree conditioning changes the residue profiles. For example,
the leading CC/SF pair series has double residue
  R_u^2*prod_q (1-1/q)^2*(1+2/q),
rather than R_u^2. The product is strictly positive and absolutely
convergent after first-order cancellation. The O(h^(-u-1)) difference
in c cannot create this double pole: its residue sums after finite-prime
coprime conditioning converge normally. Analogous conditional profiles
occur in AC and AA. There is no algebraic reason for the centered SF
double coefficient to vanish separately. Its remaining residue, if any,
must cancel the centered nonsquarefree sector's residue because (4.5)
is locally holomorphic. No separate cofinal bound for centered SF is
claimed here.

For the exact reduced bank target U(n)=K-L0+Lu-main one has
  B(n)=U(n)+Lv(n).
If a cutoff compiler is to represent U itself, set
  ell_v(h)=Lv(h)-Lv(h-1), b_new(h)=b(h)-ell_v(h),
retaining all the resulting pair cross terms. Alternatively keep (4.2)
as the full centered object and use the reviewed paid Lv comparison in
both directions. The identity and triangle inequality are legitimate;
deleting the Lv cross term from a square is not. The fixed-source bank,
theta, and all earlier D/smaller-pole padding conventions remain unchanged.

5. A sensible sufficient product-local rule, not an equivalence
---------------------------------------------------------------
Use the COMPLETE b(h)b(k) summand of (4.2), including all six sectors.
For each dyadic product scale T0, demand a uniform bound over every partial
interval (T0,V], T0<V<=2T0, with the finite product support understood:
  |X^(-2)*sum_(T0<hk<=V) W_X(h,k)*(hk)^(-gamma)*b(h)*b(k)|
       <=C_epsilon*X^epsilon*T0^(-gamma).                     (5.1)
Keep the finite m=1 head as well. Difference of two partial intervals
gives arbitrary subintervals with twice the constant. Stieltjes Abel on
each band then removes its product weight at cost at most a fixed
gamma-dependent constant times C_epsilon*X^epsilon. Summing the O(log X)
bands gives the full cofinal energy, with epsilon rescaled to absorb the
logarithm. This is a sufficient joint centered candidate rule. It is not
a proved source lemma and not equivalent to the original endpoint goal.

The distinction is strict even for ONE fixed generic source with |b(h)|<=1.
Choose lacunary dyadic X_j=2^(2^j), and L_j=floor(X_j^(2/3)). On each
block put b=+1 on [X_j,X_j+L_j), b=-1 on the next L_j integers, zero
elsewhere. Ignore a finite initial head so 2L_j<X_j and the blocks are
separated. Each completed block has net zero. B(n) is a triangle of
height L_j and support 2L_j, hence its only nonzero dyadic block energy
is at most 2L_j^3/X_j^2<=2. Whole cofinal energy is therefore bounded.

Now take the SINGLE dyadic product band (X_j^2,2X_j^2] and its partial
endpoint V=X_j*(X_j+L_j)-1. Any pair with a negative factor from this
block has product at least X_j*(X_j+L_j)>V. Earlier pairs and pairs
between this block and earlier blocks have product below X_j^2, for
large j, since the preceding block lies below 2*sqrt(X_j).
The interval thus has only positive pairs from the new block. It contains
at least a constant times L_j^2 pairs with both indices between X_j and
X_j+floor(L_j/4), apart from the one pair at product X_j^2. These have
W_X>=3X_j/4 and product <=2X_j^2. Its weighted value is at least
  c_gamma*L_j^2*X_j^(-1-2gamma)
     >=c'_gamma*X_j^(1/3-2gamma).
Rule (5.1) would bound it by C_epsilon*X_j^(epsilon-2gamma), impossible
for epsilon<1/3. This is a logical countermodel to recovery-rule
equivalence, NOT a counterfeit claiming to satisfy actual-mu identities.
Sharp product cuts may destroy cancellation even after correct centering.

6. Normalized Abel is a genuinely bounded recovery operator
-----------------------------------------------------------
For the SAME centered coefficient sequence, use real staircase extension
  B(t)=sum_(h<=t)b(h),
  F_gamma(t)=t^gamma*sum_(h<=t)b(h)*h^(-gamma), t>=1.
Here B is constant on [n,n+1), while F_gamma(t)=(t/n)^gamma F_gamma(n)
there. One must not replace both by the same staircase convention.
Stieltjes integration by parts, including the h=1 atom, gives EXACTLY
  F_gamma(t)=B(t)+gamma*t^gamma*
                      integral_1^t B(u)*u^(-gamma-1)du,
  B(t)=F_gamma(t)-gamma*integral_1^t F_gamma(u)du/u.             (6.1)
These are not pointwise absolute-value inverse bounds between arbitrary
signed endpoint sums; they are complete causal operators with memory.

For sigma>0 define the truncated norm
  ||f||_(sigma,T)^2=integral_1^T |f(t)|^2*t^(-2sigma-1)dt.
Set H(t)=integral_1^t F_gamma(u)du/u. Then t*H'(t)=F_gamma(t)
almost everywhere, H(1)=0, and an exact integration by parts gives
  2 Re <F_gamma,H>_(sigma,T)
      =T^(-2sigma)*|H(T)|^2+2sigma*||H||_(sigma,T)^2.
Therefore
  ||B||_(sigma,T)^2
    =||F_gamma||_(sigma,T)^2
       -gamma*T^(-2sigma)*|H(T)|^2
       -gamma*(2sigma-gamma)*||H||_(sigma,T)^2.                 (6.2)
For 0<=gamma<=2sigma the inverse norm is AT MOST ONE, uniformly in T.
The formula works for complex coefficients and retains the endpoint term.

For 0<=gamma<sigma, the forward memory kernel on logarithmic coordinates
is exp(-(sigma-gamma)*x)*1_(x>=0), with L1 norm 1/(sigma-gamma).
Young's inequality, with zero extension past the finite interval, proves
  ||F_gamma||_(sigma,T)<=sigma/(sigma-gamma)*||B||_(sigma,T).   (6.3)
At sigma=1/2,gamma=1/6 the forward constant is 3/2 and inverse is one.
This bounded bridge needs no product-cutoff hypothesis. In particular,
cofinal integer complete moments in all epsilon imply finite norms for
every sigma>1/2, and (6.2)-(6.3) transfer that property both ways when
gamma<1/2. Conversely finite norms at every sigma>1/2 bound each dyadic
moment by a constant times X^(2sigma-1). For F_gamma the exact interpolation
above changes only finite comparison constants. Thus the cofinal property
is equivalent for B and F_gamma in this range, with constants enlarged
as specified. No such property is proved for the actual source here.

7. The normalized prime gain cancels, rather than closing the target
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Suppose a particular complete arithmetic transfer has weighted pullback
q^(-gamma)*S_gamma(t/q), S_gamma(y)=sum_(h<=y)b(h)*h^(-gamma).
Multiplying by t^gamma gives
  t^gamma*q^(-gamma)*S_gamma(t/q)=F_gamma(t/q).                 (7.1)
Thus the fractional gain disappears EXACTLY under the normalized state
used for bounded recovery. All original arithmetic weights, occupancy
unions, q-free restrictions, and exits remain. The formula does not assert
that centered b obeys the uncentered homogeneous transfer: its exact center
increments produce additional deterministic forcing, which must be kept.

If only integer state values at m=floor(t/q)>=1 are used, the same term is
  [t/(q*m)]^gamma*F_gamma(m).
The multiplier is not one and lies in [1,2^gamma); retain its exact value,
plus the below-one/unit convention. Continuous interpolation restores
(7.1). In two-leg quadratic states the analogous normalization is
t^(2gamma); the same issue is present on each transported leg.

In Mellin language, normalized F_gamma replaces the weighted prefix
denominator z by the physical denominator s-gamma, while the prime
arguments return to s. It does not move an actual source singularity to a
less demanding physical scale. The valid bounded inverse and the apparent
q^(-gamma) scalar improvement cannot be counted as independent credits.

8. Correct next obligation and stopping point
---------------------------------------------
Two mathematically sound routes remain distinct:
  (a) a joint centered product-local estimate such as (5.1), retaining
      all six sectors and all partial product cuts; this is strictly
      stronger than the whole endpoint goal and needs a new source lemma;
  (b) a normalized Abel auxiliary F_gamma with the proved bounded recovery
      (6.2), whose full arithmetic forcing and scalar transfer must be
      estimated after the cancellation (7.1), not before it.
Neither may separately pay an uncentered SF pole or discard a center
conditioned by the chosen kernel. The old low-contour theorem remains a
correct component result. Its high complement and the genuine shifted
polar germ have not been controlled by that theorem. Full centered
SF/nonsquarefree recombination removes the local kappa poles but does not
establish a uniform signed moment or a zero-free region.

The concrete correction to the search input is therefore exact kernel-
compatible centering plus explicit choice of a stronger sharp-cut rule
or a bounded-memory normalized auxiliary. Repeated valuations, moving
prime-bank cutoffs, lower-pole padding, and all center/mixed correlations
stay in the forcing ledger. Global and RH flags remain false.
