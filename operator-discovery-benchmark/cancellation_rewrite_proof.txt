CERTIFICATE FEEDBACK THROUGH EXACT PRIME-DILATION REWRITES
7 October 2026. Elementary consequences of classical Mobius Euler factors.
No new independent prime theorem or sublinear RH cancellation is proved.

1. REWRITE THE SAME ARITHMETIC TARGET

Let W be a rational polynomial on [0,1], W(0)=1,W(1)=0, extended by zero
beyond one. Let P be a nonempty product of distinct primes learned and
certified by the preceding row-law search. Define the piecewise polynomial

 F_(P,W)(u)=sum_(d|P)mu(d)W(d*u).

Then for every real X>=1,

 S_W(X)=sum_(n<=X,gcd(n,P)=1)mu(n)F_(P,W)(n/X).          (1)

To prove this, discard original terms with a squared prime factor from P:
their Mobius value is zero. Each remaining original index has a unique form
d*n, d|P and gcd(n,P)=1. The Euler-factor law gives mu(d*n)=mu(d)mu(n),
including when mu(n)=0. Regrouping the finite sum yields (1).

Thus this is an exact change in representation of the original signed
Mobius sum. Its Mellin transform W_hat(s)/zeta(s) is preserved, with the same
nonvanishing and ordinary-zero gates proved in estimate_proof.txt. The
search does not replace the target with a positive moment or an unrelated
sequence. Any better bound on the right of (1) bounds the original target.

2. GENERATE A POSITIVE PIECEWISE POLYNOMIAL ENVELOPE

The support changes only at u=1/d for d|P. On each interval [l,r] between
successive breakpoints, F is a polynomial of degree at most deg W=d0.
Write its Bernstein expansion after u=l+(r-l)*t:

 F(u)=sum_(k=0)^d0 b_k*binomial(d0,k)t^k(1-t)^(d0-k).

Replacing each b_k by |b_k| gives a nonnegative polynomial U(u)>=|F(u)|.
The construction uses exact rational arithmetic. The independent checker
expands that Bernstein polynomial to recover F and verifies the absolute
envelope coefficients, instead of repeating the production conversion.

Since W(1)=0, F is continuous at every support breakpoint. The two endpoint
Bernstein coefficients on adjacent cells are the corresponding actual
values of F, so their absolute values agree. Therefore U is continuous
across cells. At zero, sum_(d|P)mu(d)=0 gives F(0)=U(0)=0; at one,
W(1)=0 gives F(1)=U(1)=0. These endpoint conditions are checked exactly.

The basis integrals and derivative identity supply exact certificate costs:

 I_cell=(r-l)/(d0+1)*sum_k |b_k|,
 TV_cell <= sum_(k<d0) ||b_(k+1)|-|b_k||.

Let I be the sum of the cell integrals and V the sum of the variation bounds.
Then integral_0^1 U=I and TV(U)<=V, with no hidden breakpoint jumps.

3. AN ELEMENTARY ALL-SCALE ARITHMETIC COUNT CERTIFICATE

Let r0 be the number of prime factors of P and delta=product_(p|P)(1-1/p).
The exact finite-sieve count of positive integers coprime to P is

 A_P(x)=sum_(d|P)mu(d)*floor(x/d), x>=0.

It follows that D_P(x)=A_P(x)-delta*x is the signed sum of fractional parts.
Exactly 2^(r0-1) divisors have each sign, and each fractional part lies in
[0,1). Therefore |D_P(x)|<=2^(r0-1) for every real x>=0. This elementary
finite identity does not import a theorem on prime distribution.

Use (1), |mu(n)|<=1, the positive envelope and Stieltjes integration:

 |S_W(X)| <= sum_(n<=X,gcd(n,P)=1)U(n/X)
           =delta*X*integral_0^1 U(u)du + integral_0^1 U(u)dD_P(X*u)
           <=delta*I*X + 2^(r0-1)*V.                     (2)

The last inequality is integration by parts; U(0)=U(1)=0 removes the endpoint
terms and the discrepancy bound controls the variation integral. This proves
(2) for every real X>=1 and every declared finite prime set. Both parameter
costs are retained. Increasing the prime set can enlarge the boundary cost;
the search cannot hide that cost or conclude an unproved sublinear bound.

The executable grammar permits up to eight distinct prime multipliers and
polynomial degree up to eight, to bound certificate generation size. The
mathematical template itself applies to every finite prime set and degree.
The public finite evaluator checks the constant-one Dirichlet inverse law
on the complete supplied coefficient prefix. Arbitrary integer sequences
cannot inherit the Mobius-specific identity or bound.

4. AN AUTOMATICALLY RECOVERED SMOOTHING CALIBRATION

For W(u)=1-u and P=2, F(u)=u on [0,1/2] and 1-u on [1/2,1]. Its Bernstein
envelope is exact: I=1/4,V=1,delta=1/2 and the discrepancy cost is one.
The engine therefore recovers

 |sum_(n<=X)mu(n)(1-n/X)| <= X/8+1, EVERY real X>=1.      (3)

This is a known-framework smoothing consequence, not an RH-quality estimate.
The target, kernel and constants are generated from the composed row laws;
the formula (3) is checked after synthesis as a calibration target.

5. USE AFFINE CERTIFICATES TO PROVE OR REJECT LEARNED BOUNDS

Every unresolved estimate from the earlier search supplies a target
|S_W(X)|<=C*X^theta*(log X)^b for every integer X>=2. The engine searches
the generated rewrites for a way to close that exact obligation.

For theta>=1,b>=0, C*X^(theta-1)*(log X)^b is nondecreasing. If a rewrite
has bound a*X+e and an integer threshold X0 satisfies

 C*X0^(theta-1)*(log X0)^b >= a+e/X0,

then it bounds the requested target at every real X>=X0. Checking each
integer 2<=X<X0 with the exact arithmetic sum and certified inequality
completes an all-scale proof. The independent checker replays both the
monotone tail premise and every finite initial scale with new factorizations
and a different precision. A comparison that is undecidable with balls is
not called either a proof or a counterexample.

Every feedback goal first checks the small scales 2 through 15. This also
detects false hypotheses that the original training scales missed:
training begins at 16, while the target domain begins at 2. An initial-scale
counterexample is recorded as a disproof, even if all large held-out scales
passed. A repair is a new proposed constant, never a proof of the rejected
old constant. It is synthesized from a finite-data proposal plus the explicit
affine tail cost, and accepted only after both certificate parts are verified.

For theta<1 or b<0 this monotone-affine backend supplies no all-scale
certificate. It retains the exact coprime-row subgoal and all costs as an
open proof obligation. In particular no square-root bound is certified;
the same original ordinary-zeta root problem remains.

6. SCOPE

The search composes up to three rows from its first four certified learned
primes, creating fourteen conditioning sets. It generates and certifies
280 affine bounds for the twenty kernels, checks exact rewritten sums at
five changed integer scales, and feeds 360 previously unresolved estimate
goals through these certificates. Successful proofs cover linear or weaker
growth, with finite boundary checks. Sublinear signed cancellation, a new
prime-distribution theorem, historical novelty and RH remain unproved.

These elementary templates show that the detector can synthesize a bound
and its certificate rather than only evaluate a supplied formula. Their
completeness is limited. A new decomposition or recurrence proving the
remaining signed coprime-row cancellation would be genuine further work;
the engine records it as a subgoal instead of treating it as an assumption.
