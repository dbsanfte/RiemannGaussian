FRACTIONAL DISCRETE/CONTINUOUS ARITHMETIC REMAINDERS
Supplied mathematical backend; independently checked algorithms and reviewed
arguments, not a machine-formalized proof or autonomous theorem invention.

1. SEMANTICS AND EXACT ANCHORS

The input is u(n)=1, n>=1. Its Dirichlet inverse is the actual Mobius
function mu, obtained by inversion without a factor or prime-label oracle.
For every real 0<=beta<=1 set D_beta u(1)=0 and
D_beta u(n)=(log n)^beta for n>=2. At beta=0 the value at 1 stays ZERO.
Set T_beta=mu * D_beta u and

 J_beta(y)=integral_1^y (log t)^beta dt, y>=1,
 R_beta(x)=sum_(n<=x) T_beta(n)-sum_(d<=x)mu(d)J_beta(x/d).      (1)

All these sums include every integer, not a random sample. The producer
uses the triangular arithmetic-logarithm recursion. The checker computes
mu by factorization and takes the divisor convolution directly. Normalizers
are independently evaluated using Kummer's transformation of 1F1, with
additional native integral calibrations. Rational powers of positive real
logarithms have no semantic denominator bound. The pilot's denominator
budget is a resource choice, not a mathematical restriction.

T_0=delta_1-mu and T_1=Lambda. The exact floor identity gives
R_0(x)=1-x*sum_(d<=x)mu(d)/d for EVERY real x>=1. In particular
R_0(2)=0 and R_0(3)=1/2. The convention at index one is essential.

For fixed real rational vectors c,b, j=0,...,D, define

 V_a(x)=sum_j c_j sum_(n<=x)T_(ja)(n)
                -sum_j b_j sum_(d<=x)mu(d)J_(ja)(x/d),        (2)

on 0<=a<=1/max(1,D). A positive-order compensated family has b_j=c_j
for j>=1. Its b_0 can be independent. Coefficients do NOT vary with a.

2. AN ENTIRE NUMERATOR, NOT A CORRIDOR AROUND TWO ZEROS

Let A(s)=zeta(s)-1-1/(s-1), an entire function. For Re(s)>1,

 N_beta(s)=sum_(n>=2)(log n)^beta n^(-s)
                         -Gamma(beta+1)/(s-1)^(beta+1).

For 0<beta<1 the horizontal fractional derivative gives

 N_beta(s)= beta/Gamma(1-beta) integral_0^infty
                       [A(s)-A(s+u)]u^(-beta-1)du
          =-1/Gamma(1-beta) integral_0^infty A'(s+u)u^(-beta)du. (3)

Initially these equalities follow by absolute convergence in Re(s)>1;
the continuous term has the same fractional-derivative representation.
At zero, the first difference is O(u), the derivative is O(1). At infinity,
A(s+u)=-1/u+O(u^-2) and A'(s+u)=1/u^2+O(u^-3), locally uniformly
in complex s. The exponential zeta tail is uniform on every compact set.
Consequently both formulas define an ENTIRE function of s. They cross
s=1 analytically. N_0=A and N_1=-A'. No all-height nonvanishing assertion
about any particular N_beta is made.

Integration of (1) and termwise Dirichlet convolution gives, in Re(s)>1,

 s*zeta(s)*integral_1^infty R_beta(x)x^(-s-1)dx=N_beta(s).     (4)

For a compensated family the numerator on the right of the corresponding
identity is

 Q_a(s)=c_0(zeta(s)-1)-b_0/(s-1)+sum_(j>=1)c_j N_(ja)(s).     (5)

For an uncompensated positive order one must additionally retain
(c_j-b_j)Gamma(ja+1)/(s-1)^(ja+1). After division by zeta's simple
pole this produces a fractional pole/branch at s=1. Distinct positive
orders cannot cancel their highest uncancelled power. At the schedule
a_q=1/(max(1,D)*q), q>=2, every positive ja_q is noninteger and <=1/2.
A uniform integer bound with recovered growth exponent <1 is therefore
impossible for any uncompensated positive order. This is a rejection
certificate, distinct from a failure to prove a promising bound.

3. A PARAMETER POLE PRESERVES ALL ORDINARY ZETA ZEROS

For -1<Re(beta)<1 there is a meromorphic parameter continuation

 N_beta(s)=1/Gamma(-beta)*[
 integral_0^1 [A(s+u)-A(s)]u^(-beta-1)du
 +integral_1^infty A(s+u)u^(-beta-1)du -A(s)/beta].            (6)

The apparent singularity at beta=0 is removable and equals A(s).
Since A(s+u)=-1/u+O(u^-2), at beta=-1 the meromorphic continuation
has residue -1, INDEPENDENT OF s. To justify the boundary statement,
subtract -1/u from the tail. The remaining integral is holomorphic for
Re(beta)>-2, so the sole first boundary pole is -1/(beta+1), multiplied
by 1/Gamma(-beta), whose value at beta=-1 is one.

Consider any nonzero fixed compensated family with highest positive
index D and c_D!=0. Suppose, for EVERY q>=2, its Mellin transform is
holomorphic in Re(s)>eta, for some eta<1. If zeta(rho)=0 there, (5)
requires Q_(a_q)(rho)=0. The function a -> Q_a(rho) is holomorphic
on -1/D<Re(a)<1/D, including a=0. The infinitely many a_q tend to
zero. The identity theorem would therefore make Q_a(rho) identically
zero on that connected strip. But at a=-1/D it has residue -c_D/D:
the highest term has the pole from (6), every lower term is regular,
and the zero-order terms are constant in a. This nonzero residue is
a contradiction. ALL multiplicities and all heights are included.

For degree zero, Q(rho)=-c_0-b_0/(rho-1). With real c_0,b_0, not both
zero, any cancellation point is real. Ordinary zeta has no real zero
with real part >0 (the alternating eta series is positive on (0,1),
and the Euler product excludes zeros to the right of 1). Thus the
degree-zero scalar remainder also retains all ordinary zeros there.

In particular a bound |V_(a_q)(n)|<=C*a_q^kappa*n^(1/2)*
log(e+n)^ell, EVERY integer n>=1 AND EVERY integer q>=2, would imply
RH. Each individual q can even have its own finite constant for the
qualitative zero-exclusion argument. A SINGLE common C is required
by the stronger parameter-rate claims made by our estimate grammar.
A finite parameter grid, however dense or successful, DOES NOT
establish the identity-theorem premise. A fixed fractional numerator
can cancel a zero; it is not given the family's all-height theorem.

4. EXACT PARAMETER ORDER AND AN ANTI-AMPLITUDE OBSTRUCTION

For b=c, let r be the first k>=0 with sum_j c_j*j^k != 0.
It exists with r<=D by the Vandermonde matrix. Then V_a(x) has order
a^r for fixed x. If r=0, R_0(3)=1/2 witnesses a nonzero constant.
For r>=1, at x=2 the rth parameter derivative of R_beta is

 (log log 2)^r-integral_1^2 (log log t)^r dt.                 (7)

This is nonzero: log log t is negative and strictly increasing there;
its rth power is strictly increasing for odd r and decreasing for even r.
The leading Taylor coefficient in V is (sum c_j*j^r)/r! times (7).
If b_0!=c_0, then V_0(2)=c_0-b_0 is nonzero, so r=0.
Thus a proposed common-constant family bound with kappa>r is false
already at one fixed integer as q tends to infinity. Tiny observed
amplitudes cannot provide free asymptotic arithmetic control.

5. A NATIVE CAUCHY CERTIFICATE FROM ACTUAL PRIME STRUCTURE

For n>=2, write B=log(n/rad(n))>=0, m=omega(n), and
h_n(u)=exp(-Bu)*product_(p|n)(1-exp(-u log p)). For 0<Re(z)<1,

 T_z(n)=(-1)^(m+1)*z/Gamma(1-z)*integral_0^infty h_n(u)u^(-z-1)du.

The actual arithmetic structure gives |h''(u)|<=(log n)^2:
Leibniz's rule and factors bounded by one give the square of
B+sum_(p|n)log p=log n. We have h(0)=0,
h'(0)=log p if m=1 and zero otherwise, and h(infinity) is 1 for
squarefree n, 0 otherwise. Also

 |h(u)-h(infinity)| <=(log n/log 2)*exp(-u log 2).

Subtract h'(0)u on [0,1] and h(infinity) on [1,infinity]. This gives
the holomorphic representation on Re(z)<2

 (-1)^(m+1)*[z*rgamma(1-z)*(I_0+I_infinity)
                   +z*rgamma(2-z)*h'(0)+rgamma(1-z)*h(infinity)].

It equals the entire finite divisor sum, including at z=0 and z=1,
by continuation. On the rectangle Re(z) in [-rho,1+rho],
Im(z) in [-rho,rho], 0<rho<1,

 |I_0| <=(log n)^2/[2(1-rho)],
 |I_infinity| <= log n/[2(log 2)^2].

The second bound uses u^(rho-1)<=1 for u>=1. Let native interval
subboxes certify maxima M1=|z*rgamma(1-z)|, M2=|z*rgamma(2-z)|,
M3=|rgamma(1-z)| throughout that rectangle. The checked finite cover
is of the WHOLE rectangle, not merely sampled points. Set

 C0=M1/[2(1-rho)]+M1/[2(log 2)^2]+M2+M3.

Cauchy's inequality on every circle of radius rho around beta in [0,1]
then proves, for every integer r>=0 and every actual n>=2,

 |partial_beta^r T_beta(n)|<=r!*rho^(-r)*C0*log(e+n)^2.         (8)

No prime-distribution theorem is imported. The global proof uses
factorization structurally; production of coefficients uses no oracle.
The radius is selected automatically from a frozen finite set by the
resulting cost. That set is only a resource limit for the certificate.

6. PROVED ALL-SCALE FAMILY AND INTERPOLATION BOUNDS

For b=c and r>=1 set S_r=sum_j |c_j|j^r and L=log(e+x).
The rth derivative of the discrete prefix is bounded by
x*r!*rho^(-r)*C0*L^2. For the continuous part, exchanging the finite
divisor sum and the positive absolute-value integral gives

 |partial_beta^r G_beta(x)|
 <=x*integral_0^(log x) u^beta |log u|^r du
 <=x*[r!+L^(r+2)/(r+2)], beta in [0,1].                     (9)

On u<1 use u^beta<=1 and the exact log moment r!. On u>=1 use
u^beta<=u and (log u)^r<=u^r. Taylor's theorem and the exact zero
parameter moments below r prove, for EVERY real x>=1 and 0<=a<=1/D,

 |V_a(x)|<=W*a^r*x*L^(r+2),
 W=S_r*[rho^(-r)*C0+1+1/((r+2)r!)].                         (10)

This contains genuine signed cancellation in the parameter combination.
Its x-growth exponent is ONE; it is below the required RH strength.

For a unit interval N<=t<=N+1, N>=1, the discrete change contains
at most one T_beta(N+1). Split continuous divisors at d=N/2.
For d<=N/2 use log(t/d)>=log 2 and sum 1/d<=2*log(e+N+1).
The integral is bounded by 2*log(e+N+1)^(r+2).
For d>N/2, integer d>=(N+1)/2, so log(t/d)<=log 2 and
log(t/d)>=(t-d)/(N+1). The adjacent intervals t-d and 1/d<=2/N
bound the singular log moments by 4*r!, including N=1.
Applying the same parameter Taylor cancellation proves

 |V_a(t)-V_a(N)|<=I*a^r*log(e+N+1)^(r+2),
 I=S_r*[rho^(-r)*C0+2/r!+4].                                (11)

For r=0, or independent b, the real fractional sign integral gives
|T_beta(n)|<=(log n)^beta<=log(e+n). The same exchanges give the
valid conservative bounds

 |V_a(x)|<=(sum|c|+(3/2)sum|b|)*x*log(e+x)^2,
 |V_a(t)-V_a(N)|<=(sum|c|+6sum|b|)*log(e+N+1)^2.              (12)

Thus an integer family bound with growth theta has an all-real Mellin
continuation to Re(s)>max(theta,0). Its logarithmic losses are charged
by the exact upper moment majorant with m=ceil(ell),
log(e+x)<=log(e+1)+log x. Its parameter a^kappa is charged separately
from the increment's a^r. At a finite native complex query a nonzero
Q_a lower bound permits |1/zeta(s)|<=|s|*H_majorant/|Q_a|.
Every denominator, normalization and unit correction is included.
This finite-query cost is NOT an all-height bound. Shrinking Q and V
together cannot supply a free gain. Missing finite denominator coverage
does not disqualify an unfamiliar lead from the numerical archive.

7. NATIVE ENTIRE-NUMERATOR ENCLOSURES AND STATUS

The producer uses (3)'s difference integral. The independent checker
uses its derivative integral. Both enclose the small endpoint with a
Cauchy Taylor remainder on a full complex box; native integration of
u=exp(v) handles the middle; convergent rational geometric series and
upper incomplete gamma functions handle the infinite tail. The leftover
Dirichlet sums have explicit integral-test bounds, with all recorded
real parts beyond one. All finite probes are interval arithmetic.

The proof above supplies generic family-zero and weak-growth backends.
The detector can apply them to any enumerated rational coefficient
vector, including high-pass combinations whose a=0 limit vanishes.
It does NOT discover these proofs autonomously. Finite training,
validation and sealed fresh evaluation infer stronger estimates only.
No uniform square-root family bound is proved. RH remains unproved.
