1. General profile, floors and endpoints
----------------------------------------
Let w be any real or rational finitely supported profile on positive
integers. For computable interval formulas write

 w(n)=sum_(a=1..J) c_a 1_(A_a,B_a](n),
 0<=A_a<B_a<=X,                                  (1)

where X>=1 is real; coefficients may have either sign and intervals may
overlap. Every finite profile has such a representation, for example
using (n-1,n]. All interval arithmetic below is exact; rational endpoints
can have arbitrary denominators. Nothing assumes square-, cube- or other
restricted root denominators in source parameters or cutoff choices.
The zero profile or X<1 is an empty-support base. Put

 Z(w)=sum_(n>=1) mu(n)w(n).

Define pi(t)=#{primes p:p<=t}; thus pi(t)=pi(floor(t)) and pi(t)=0 for
t<2. Define the CLOSED-upper, OPEN-lower count

 Pi(L,U)=pi(U)-pi(L) if U>L, and 0 otherwise.      (2)

The zero branch is necessary: an inverted interval must not produce a
negative count. A p exactly at L is excluded and a p exactly at U included.
P+(1)=1; P+(m) is the largest prime factor for m>1. For the smallest-prime
formula below set P-(1)=infinity. Squareful cofactors may be retained:
their mu coefficient is zero and every identity remains exact.

Only the algebraic identities below are universal over arbitrary w.
A square-root bound uniform over all arbitrary finite profiles, even
with |w|<=1, is false: choosing w(n)=mu(n) through X gives the squarefree
count. Indeed squareful integers are covered by d^2 multiples for d>=2,
whose total count is at most X sum_(d>=2)d^(-2)<=3X/4; there are at least
X/4-1 squarefree integers through X. Any growth theorem must specify a
structured source family and
its coefficient/variation constants, rather than use (1) alone as an
admission theorem.

The actual necessary source is always

 w_k(n)=1_(8k,10k](n)-1_(16k,20k](n), X=20k,
 Z(k)=M(10k)-M(8k)-M(20k)+M(16k), k>=1 integer.   (3)

It is the admitted original-source slice, not an arbitrary inverse-filled
branch. Its exact joined remainder is

 J_1=Z(k)-2k H_mu(u)H_mu(v).                       (4)

A Z theorem transfers to J only with the entire071 conditional separable
price, its Mertens premises, parameter domains and invariant constants.
This review pays none of those additional obligations.

2. Exact largest-prime pooling, including the smooth complement
---------------------------------------------------------------
Fix a real y>=1 and put M=floor(X/y). Define

 S_y(w)=sum_(P+(n)<=y) mu(n)w(n),
 L_m=max(y,P+(m)),
 T_m(w;y)=sum_(prime p>L_m) w(pm)
         =sum_a c_a Pi(max(L_m,A_a/m),B_a/m).      (5)

Then, with no asymptotic assumption,

 Z(w)=S_y(w)-sum_(1<=m<=M) mu(m)T_m(w;y).          (6)

Proof. Nonzero nonsmooth terms have n squarefree and a unique largest
prime p=P+(n)>y. Write n=pm. Then p>P+(m), gcd(p,m)=1, and
mu(n)=-mu(m). Conversely a squarefree m and p>max(y,P+(m)) create
exactly one such n, without repetition. Since p>y and pm<=X, m<X/y;
including m=M even when X/y is integral simply includes a zero stencil.
Squareful n and m contribute zero. The unit is in S_y because y>=1.
Substituting (1) proves the precise count expression in (5).

Known prime anchors are exactly the m=1 stencil, with mu(1)=1:

 T_1=sum_a c_a Pi(max(y,A_a),B_a).

Thus every prime above y has its exact sign -1 before any norm. The
different prime anchors, composite cofactors, and smooth complement
are present in ONE signed state (6). Taking |T_1| separately, or taking
absolute values of all m stencils, changes its proof price and discards
the sought interaction. Formula (6) is outside the unsigned component
transport language excluded072 because pi can pool primes with distinct
outside-S parts.

If y>=sqrt(X), all contributing cofactors satisfy m<=X/y<=y, hence
P+(m)<=y. The factor gate disappears:

 T_m=sum_a c_a Pi(max(y,A_a/m),B_a/m).             (7)

Every n<=X has at most one prime factor >y, counted with multiplicity,
because a product of two such factors is >y^2>=X. This includes exclusion
of large prime squares. Choosing y=sqrt(X), or any exact algebraic cutoff
whose comparison with endpoints is certified, is a clean shorter-mu
radius decomposition. The identity does NOT say S_y is a shorter-radius
sum: its integers and source geometry still extend through X.

For (3), the exact stencil is

 T_m=Pi(max(L_m,8k/m),10k/m)
     -Pi(max(L_m,16k/m),20k/m).                   (8)

All prime endpoints, even when one of 8k/m,10k/m,16k/m,20k/m is itself
prime, are handled by (2). No boundary correction is implicit.

3. Smallest-prime alternative and its different complement
-----------------------------------------------------------
The dual decomposition is useful for checking that a changed factor gate
has not silently deleted composites. Define

 R_y(w)=sum_(n=1 OR P-(n)>y) mu(n)w(n).

For m>1 let q=P-(m), which is prime, and define

 Pi_<q(L,U)=Pi(L,min(U,q))-1[L<q<=U].             (9)

For m=1 set Pi_<infinity(L,U)=Pi(L,U). Formula (9) counts p in (L,U]
with p<q. Its subtraction is required when q is an included upper
endpoint. In particular Pi_<q(L,U)=0 when L>=q. Then

 V_m(w;y)=sum_a c_a Pi_<P-(m)(A_a/m,min(y,B_a/m)),
 Z(w)=R_y(w)-sum_(m<=floor(X/2)) mu(m)V_m(w;y).    (10)

Proof. A squarefree n with smallest prime p<=y has n=pm with
p<P-(m), hence mu(n)=-mu(m); the representation is unique. The p>=2
gate gives m<=X/2. The strict p<P-(m) gate excludes repeated factors.
R_y includes its unit and every rough composite; none can be discarded.

For y>=sqrt(X), R_y=w(1)-sum_(p>y)w(p), since no n<=X can have two
prime factors both >y. This makes the known large-prime anchors explicit
in R_y, but the other cofactors in (10) extend to X/2. Letting y>=X
also gives the all-largest-radius-prime stencil identity

 Z(w)=w(1)-sum_(m<=floor(X/2))mu(m)
              sum_a c_a Pi_<P-(m)(A_a/m,B_a/m).  (11)

It uses shorter cofactor signs and known prime signs throughout, but
still has current-radius prime-count geometry. It is finite arithmetic
reconstruction until a uniform price is established.

4. Centered signed prime stencils: exact leaves and quantifiers
--------------------------------------------------------------
Choose a deterministic cumulative function F on the real line, with
F(t)=0 for t<=1, fixed before arithmetic evaluations at the source scale.
It may be zero, an explicitly defined logarithmic-density integral, or
a separately certified prime-count approximant. No estimate for pi-F
is assumed here. Define its interval functional with the SAME emptiness
convention:

 F[L,U]=F(U)-F(L) if U>L, and 0 otherwise,
 Delta[L,U]=Pi(L,U)-F[L,U].

For each m put

 B_m=sum_a c_a F[max(L_m,A_a/m),B_a/m],
 E_m=sum_a c_a Delta[max(L_m,A_a/m),B_a/m],
 H_y=S_y(w)-sum_(m<=M)mu(m)B_m,
 D_y=sum_(m<=M)mu(m)E_m.

The exact signed state is

                       Z(w)=H_y-D_y.             (12)

Every prime-count, smooth, composite and boundary term is in (12).
There are two unpaid leaves: H_y and D_y, or a direct coupled theorem
for H_y-D_y. A prime-discrepancy estimate alone does not pay H_y. A bound
on S_y alone does not pay the potentially large centered main sum either.

A sufficient detector-search target for the actual source is: for every
epsilon>0 there exist explicitly parameterized constants C_H(epsilon),
C_D(epsilon) and a base K_epsilon such that for EVERY integer k>=K_epsilon,
using one declared y(k), one declared F, and all terms in (12),

 |H_y(k)|<=C_H(epsilon) k^(1/2+epsilon),
 |D_y(k)|<=C_D(epsilon) k^(1/2+epsilon).          (13)

All k<K_epsilon are paid bases. Constants may not depend on k, its mu
values, or a finite observed maximum. If a coupled theorem is used,
it must instead prove its complete price uniformly; separate large H,D
can cancel, so (13) is sufficient and not necessary.

Taking F=pi sets E=0 and H=Z: it is an exact tautological reconstruction,
not a zero-cost theorem. More generally allowing F, coefficients or a
claimed constant to encode the evaluated target has the same defect.
A admissible center must have a source-independent definition and a
separate uniform cost law. Even a perfectly legitimate deterministic
center leaves H unpaid; precommitment alone does not pay a bound.

For comparison, suppose as an explicit CONDITIONAL hypothesis that
|pi(t)-F(t)|<=C t^alpha L(X) for every 0<=t<=X, with 0<alpha<1.
Write A=sum_a|c_a|. Every nonempty interval in E_m has both endpoints
<=X/m, so

 |D_y|<=2 C A L(X) X^alpha sum_(m<=M)m^(-alpha)
       <=2 C A L(X) X^alpha M^(1-alpha)/(1-alpha). (14)

This includes the m=1 term and all interval endpoints. A zero lower
endpoint causes no error because F=pi=0 there. At alpha=1/2 and
y=X^theta, the price is of order X^(1-theta/2), up to constants and
L(X). Thus even a hypothetical square-root prime-count error, taken
termwise, does not deliver a square-root Mobius bound for theta<1.
Taking M bounded avoids that inflation but leaves an almost full-radius
smooth/composite complement. Equation (14) is not an imported prime
error theorem; it diagnoses the cost of THAT conditional triangle rule.

5. Common-prime Gram energy with all cofactor cross terms
---------------------------------------------------------
Partition (y,X] into finitely many disjoint open-left/closed-right blocks
B=(l_B,u_B]; dyadic prime blocks are permitted. Let

 a_m(p)=1[p>max(y,P+(m))] w(pm),
 A(p)=sum_(m<=M)mu(m)a_m(p),
 N_B=Pi(max(y,l_B),u_B),
 Q_B=sum_(p in B, p>y)A(p),
 E_B=sum_(p in B,p>y)A(p)^2.

Then Z=S_y-sum_B Q_B and, exactly,

 E_B=sum_(m,n<=M)mu(m)mu(n)G_B(m,n),             (15)
 G_B(m,n)=sum_(p in B,p>y)a_m(p)a_n(p).

The Gram matrix is positive semidefinite, because it is the Gram matrix
of the real vectors (a_m(p))_p. Its exact count-stencil formula is

 G_B(m,n)=sum_(a,b) c_a c_b
  Pi(max(l_B,y,P+(m),P+(n),A_a/m,A_b/n),
     min(u_B,B_a/m,B_b/n)).                      (16)

This formula applies to overlaps, coinciding endpoints and inverted
intervals. No independent mu copies are introduced. Ordered pairs m,n
are used, so the off-diagonal expression has both orientations:

 D_B=sum_m mu(m)^2 G_B(m,m),
 C_B=sum_(m!=n)mu(m)mu(n)G_B(m,n),
 E_B=D_B+C_B>=0.                                (17)

Discarding C_B, declaring it nonpositive without proof, or replacing
it by random-sign expectation is unsound. C_B can have either sign.

Cauchy gives the computable common-prime rule

 |Q_B|<=sqrt(N_B E_B),
 |sum_B Q_B|<=sum_B sqrt(N_B E_B).                (18)

For weighted deterministic prime functions b(p)>0 one may instead use
|Q_B|^2<=sum_(p in B)b(p) times sum_(p in B)A(p)^2/b(p).
The weighted Gram retains every cross term and every weight cost.
The unweighted diagonal has the elementary bound

 D_B<=A^2 sum_(p in B,p>y)floor(X/p),             (19)

because |w|<=A and mu(m)^2<=1. This is a genuine paid geometry bound,
but (19) and N_B normally lead to a much larger price than square root.
A useful detector must exploit C_B with a proved signed law or exploit
correlation between the prime aggregate and S_y. Merely computing a
smaller finite E_B has no all-scale consequence.

There is a sharp source-specific limitation. If y<=16k and B is the
top interval (16k,20k], then X/p<5/4 for every p in B, so the only
contributing cofactor is m=1. On this block A(p)=w_k(p)=-1. Consequently

 N_B=pi(20k)-pi(16k), Q_B=-N_B, E_B=D_B=N_B,
 C_B=0, sqrt(N_B E_B)=N_B.                       (20)

Even cofactor cross-term energy has nothing to cancel on that block.
If the remaining state is separately bounded by triangle, the existing
imported PNT lower bound on N_B again excludes a square-root price.
This is a limitation of separate-block prices, not a lower bound on
actual |Z|. Aggregating prime bands BEFORE a norm can permit their signed
interaction; interacting with S_y still requires a joint signed rule.

6. Centered common cells: an efficient exact correlation target
--------------------------------------------------------------
A common-cell representation exposes what a centered energy must retain.
Collect all distinct active interval endpoints in section4, including y,
P+(m) when active, and A_a/m,B_a/m. Sort them and retain cells
C=(l_C,u_C] within (y,X]. There are O(JM+M+1) endpoints, before removal
of duplicates. Every interval (L,U] is an exact disjoint union of cells
with the same endpoint conventions. Define

 delta_C=Pi(l_C,u_C)-F[l_C,u_C],
 h_(m,C)=sum_a c_a 1[C subset (max(L_m,A_a/m),B_a/m]],
 H_C=sum_m mu(m)h_(m,C).

Then, EXACTLY,

 E_m=sum_C h_(m,C)delta_C,
 D_y=sum_C H_C delta_C.                          (21)

For arbitrary declared weights rho_C>0,

 |D_y|^2<= (sum_C rho_C delta_C^2)
              (sum_C H_C^2/rho_C),              (22)

and the second factor equals

 sum_(m,n)mu(m)mu(n)
                 sum_C h_(m,C)h_(n,C)/rho_C.    (23)

This is a positive-semidefinite COMMON-CELL Gram energy, including every
ordered m,n cross term. It is not the common-PRIME Gram in (16): its
measure consists of geometry cells and weights, while the first factor
is a prime-count discrepancy energy. Conflating those measures would
lose the centering corrections. Cells of zero prime count can still
have nonzero delta_C and must not be deleted.

Equations (12),(21)-(23) give a concrete auditable correlation target:
prove a uniform bound for H_y and for the product of the two factors
in (22), or prove a joint bound keeping H_y and D_y correlated. All
rho, all endpoints, the number of cells, and the chosen F have costs.
Neither small individual discrepancies nor positive semidefiniteness
alone supplies the required product estimate. The exact equality
D_y^2=sum_(m,n)mu(m)mu(n)E_m E_n is merely a rank-one rewriting of
the same scalar target; it is not, by itself, an independently smaller
correlation obligation. The cell construction avoids falsely calling
that rank-one identity a general common-prime variance theorem.

For a full joined head, use the SAME ordinary mu values in every row
and apply these identities rowwise before any head norm. In particular
sum_j(S_j-sum_B Q_(j,B))^2 includes, within every row j, the smooth/prime
cross terms and all ordered prime-block cross pairs. There are no
cross-row pairs in this sum of row squares. If a different operator
squares a sum over rows, its cross-row terms must also be retained.
A theorem for the chosen complete energy must pay all those
terms; a theorem only for sum_j Q_(j,B)^2 is insufficient. Cauchy then
costs sqrt(number_of_rows times complete_energy). Scale-averaged energy
does not automatically give a pointwise k bound, either.

7. Exact smaller-radius recurrence and the missing contraction
--------------------------------------------------------------
Define the enlarged smooth state

 Phi(w,z)=sum_(P+(n)<=z)mu(n)w(n), z>=1,
 w_p(m)=w(pm),
 Phi(w,p^-)=sum_(n=1 OR P+(n)<p)mu(n)w(n).

The notation p^- is an exact strict cutoff, not a numerical perturbation.
For an arbitrary z, writing p<=z means primes through floor(z). Unique
largest-prime factorization proves

 Phi(w,z)=w(1)-sum_(p<=min(z,X))Phi(w_p,p^-).      (24)

Every child has support horizon X/p<=X/2. The boundary convention in
Phi(w_p,p^-) enforces p coprime to the cofactor and excludes p^2.
If no primes occur, Phi=w(1). Thus (24) is a genuinely well-founded
COMPUTATIONAL recurrence, with finite bases and no current-radius mu
query in a child. It also exactly evaluates S_y in (6).

It is not a closed bound recurrence for the original head family.
The child includes a smooth-factor mask, rescaled source endpoints and
a prime-dependent strict cutoff. Its state must be explicitly admitted
to the simultaneous invariant. Calling a masked child an ordinary
Mertens prefix or an original-source row changes the family and is
unlicensed. Prime enumeration in the parent still reaches min(z,X).

Even assuming the much stronger hypothetical invariant

 |Phi(w_p,p^-)|<=C A (X/p)^sigma,

with the same A and constant for all children, triangle transfer gives

 |Phi(w,z)|/(A X^sigma)
 <=|w(1)|/(A X^sigma)+C sum_(p<=min(z,X))p^(-sigma). (25)

For 0<sigma<=1, the contribution of just p=2,3,5,7 is at least
1/2+1/3+1/5+1/7=247/210>1. Thus this price has no scalar contraction
once those primes are present. A prime cutoff that decreases along a
branch does not turn (25) into a proved contraction at its parent.
For arbitrary w the hypothetical square-root invariant is itself false
by the profile counterexample in section1. Restricted source families
need new mask-dependent envelopes and signed sibling cancellation.

An auditable next target is a signed block transfer for the actual
children, for example a uniform price for

 sum_(p in (L,U]) Phi(w_p,p^-),                   (26)

with all child domains <X, together with a simultaneous smooth/prime-
discrepancy invariant having explicit coefficient dependence. The
unpaid difficulty is the signed sibling aggregation, not the smaller
support gate. The root price must satisfy its actual invariant
inequality, including powers of all auxiliary constants and every base.
Defining (26) as a new exact constant at each scale simply reconstructs
Phi; a uniform operator/correlation law is required.

Dyadic prime blocks can reduce representation costs and can guide a
search for (26). They do not make the remaining analytic obligation
strictly smaller rank: endpoints and primes still have radius X, and
the aggregate of all blocks is the current parent. Only the CHILD mu
arguments in (24) are strictly smaller. Rank must record at least source
geometry horizon, cofactor-sign horizon, prime-query horizon and admitted
mask/profile family. A proof claiming one rank must explicitly justify
why all its other horizons are licensed at that rank.

8. Computation, arithmetic access and certificate costs
------------------------------------------------------
These are theoretical evaluation bounds, not claimed runtimes or reruns.
Let M=floor(X/y), A=sum|c_a|, and H=number of squarefree y-smooth integers
<=X (including1). A conservative exact finite implementation can use:

 (a) Prime list through floor(X), with exact pi endpoint queries.
     Elementary Eratosthenes costs O(X log X) marking operations and
     O(X) flags: sum_(p<=X)floor(X/p)<=X sum_(n<=X)1/n. A prefix pi
     array costs O(X) words; a sorted prime list instead allows binary
     search in O(log X) comparisons per count endpoint. No PNT is needed
     for these conservative costs. The prime-query horizon remains X.

 (b) Ordinary mu and P+ through M, costing O(M log M) elementary sieve
     operations and O(M) words. This is actual arithmetic access, even
     though its radius is short. Formula (7) removes the P+ gate but
     does not remove the mu access. Exact rational stencils (5) take
     O(JM) interval queries and coefficient operations, in addition to
     count-query lookup cost.

 (c) S_y by enumerating increasing sequences of distinct primes
     <=min(y,X)
     whose product is <=X. Each squarefree smooth integer appears once,
     with sign (-1)^sequence_length; evaluate w at it with (1). Sorted
     primes allow a child loop to stop at its first overflowing product.
     Successful extensions give exactly H-1 edges and there is at most
     one failed comparison per node, so traversal uses
     O(H+pi(min(y,X))) such
     product/comparison steps and O(JH) direct profile checks. Memory
     is the prime list plus O(log X) recursion depth. Exact quotient
     comparisons avoid overflow. This computes current-radius signs
     by factor certificates instead of a full mu array; it is still
     source-scale reconstruction, and H can be of order X. It supplies
     no small growth price for S_y.

 (d) A full stored Gram matrix for b prime blocks using (16) takes
     O(b J^2 M^2) count queries and coefficient operations, with
     O(b M^2) storage if all matrices are kept. Ordered cross terms or
     an exactly doubled symmetric convention must be replayed. This
     quadratic cost is optional, not an inherent lower bound.

 (e) The signed energy itself can be evaluated without storing a Gram
     matrix. Loop over primes p>y and m<=floor(X/p), using the P+ gate,
     and sum mu(m)w(pm) into A(p). Let
       I=sum_(p>y,p<=X)floor(X/p).
     Direct work is O(JI), plus O(pi(X)) block/energy accumulation and
     the preceding arithmetic tables; I<=X(1+log(max(1,X/y))) is a
     conservative all-integer harmonic bound. Streaming primes and
     cofactors computes E_B,Q_B,D_B and hence C_B exactly with small
     extra storage. It keeps every cross term because it squares the
     complete A(p); it must not square each m separately. Evaluating
     these quantities is finite target/energy reconstruction, not a
     uniform estimate.

 (f) The common cells in section6 need O(JM+M) endpoint records and
     O((JM+M)log(JM+M)) exact sorting comparisons. An event sweep
     updates H_C when an interval starts/ends, using the same mu(m)
     signs, and evaluates (21),(22) in O(JM+M) subsequent operations.
     Direct explicit Gram storage in (23) is unnecessary. Endpoint
     multiplicities must be merged, with open-left/closed-right event
     incidence handled as stated. All delta_C require pi and F queries,
     including cells with no primes.

Every displayed operation count suppresses bit-operation factors rather
than claiming unit-cost unbounded rationals. For J input rationals of
height at most h, a common denominator can have O(Jh) bits; sums add
O(log X+log H+log M) further count-size bits, and Gram squares multiply
coefficient heights. Endpoint ratios add O(log M) bits. Algebraic cutoffs
need exact comparison certificates; analytic F needs an explicit exact
definition or certified enclosures with all enclosure errors added to
the final price. Weights rho and growing block/cell counts also have
representation costs. A statement with y=y(k), J=J(k), or a growing
prime dictionary must charge all of these dependencies, not reuse a
fixed-scope constant.

An identity certificate should expose the admitted w, its original-source
binding, y, floor/cutoff gates, interval lists, smooth-state obligation,
prime stencils, chosen center, complete cross terms and final price
dependencies. A checker can independently reconstruct (5),(9),(16),
(21) and (24) from these data without trusting finite target scores.
Synthesis should not inspect current-scale mu/prime discrepancies to
choose a purported uniform price unless the adaptive selection has its
own proved theorem. Finite values may validate algebra or pay specified
bases, but cannot select an unexplained all-scale envelope.

9. Review of the root's proposed normalized quadratic candidate search
----------------------------------------------------------------------
After the initial review the root described a proposed genuine candidate
discovery search. This section reviews that EXACT conditional grammar;
it inspects no candidate outcomes and does not certify any recurrence.
Let x(k) be a fixed-dimensional vector of exact signed source states,
with x_0(k)=Z(k). Its proposed additional coordinates include the complete
squarefree sectors omega(n)=1,2,3,>=4 and S_(X^theta)(w_k), with rational
theta and X=20k. Theta denominators need no special restriction. The
>=4 sector keeps its actual ordinary-mu signs; its sign is not constant
across the sector. Every coordinate retains the difference of both
source bands. The sectors are disjoint, while smooth states overlap them.

On this source the unit does not contribute, so exactly

 Z=U_1+U_2+U_3+U_(>=4),
 U_r=sum_(squarefree n in sector r)mu(n)w_k(n).   (27)

The sign mu(n)=(-1)^r is known within a fixed omega=r sector. This fact
does not permit deleting its signed source-band imbalance, nor assigning
one sign to the >=4 aggregate. Smooth thresholds at rational powers
require exact algebraic comparisons or certified enclosures with a rule
that settles EVERY boundary. They are not rounded floating cutoffs.

Freeze rational directions v_j, nonnegative rational lambda_j, and a
rational q>0 independent of k. Define

 P(k)=Z(k)^2+sum_j lambda_j (v_j.x(k))^2,
 H=e_0 e_0^T+sum_j lambda_j v_j v_j^T,
 F(k)=P(k)/k^q.                                 (28)

H is positive semidefinite and H-e_0e_0^T is positive semidefinite.
Consequently P(k)>=Z(k)^2 for actual arithmetic and indeed every real x.
This is the complete universal domination rule. Expanding a direction
square retains the signed cross terms 2 lambda_j v_(j,a)v_(j,b)x_a x_b;
individual off-diagonal entries can be negative although the whole added
square is nonnegative. Replacing the expansion by only its diagonal
would change the candidate. The signs of x remain the actual shared
ordinary-mu signs, not free signs or independent copies.

Suppose the following still-UNPAID hypothesis is proved:

 F(k)-F(floor(k/2))<=C for EVERY integer k>=2,    (29)

with one finite constant C independent of k, and the actual states (28).
Let B=F(1), which is a paid finite base, not an unspecified fitting
intercept. The chain k_i=floor(k/2^i) reaches 1 after exactly
h=floor(log_2 k) transitions. Every preceding k_i is at least2, so
telescoping (29) and using domination proves

 0<=F(k)<=B+C floor(log_2 k),
 |Z(k)|<=k^(q/2) sqrt(B+C floor(log_2 k)).        (30)

This checks the root's supplied conditional transfer. Bounds only at
dyadic k do NOT imply (30) for other k; every integer in (29) is needed.
If all-scale (29) held with C<0, taking k=2^h would contradict F>=0 for
large h. Thus C>=0 is necessary for a genuine uniform theorem (or use a
verified nonnegative upper bound). B>=0 is exact. The k=1 base costs all
coordinate evaluation and every lambda/direction coefficient; its size
cannot be suppressed while optimizing C. Rescaling auxiliary directions
does not remove their lambda-weighted base and recurrence costs.

If the recurrence is instead proved only from k>=K_0, pay

 B_0=max_(1<=t<K_0)F(t).

The chain eventually reaches a t<K_0 and gives the safe bound
F(k)<=B_0+C floor(log_2 k), for C>=0. A sharper bound may count only
transitions while k_i>=K_0. Without the complete finite base set, the
restricted recurrence has a gap. Certified rational/algebraic enclosures
for F and B_0 are sufficient if every enclosure error is charged.

For q=1, (30) gives sqrt(k) times a paid sqrt(log k) factor. It therefore
implies O_epsilon(k^(1/2+epsilon)) for every epsilon>0: for example
log k<=k^(2epsilon)/(2epsilon) for k>=1, so a sufficient constant is
sqrt(B+C/(2epsilon log 2)). This is conditional on (29), not a proof
of it and not a proof of the whole RH dependency graph.

For one q>1 and C>0, (30) supplies only exponent q/2 with the stated
logarithmic factor; it gives a power bound k^(1/2+epsilon) when
epsilon>(q-1)/2. At equality the logarithmic factor remains. If C=0,
the equality case is allowed with constant sqrt(B). The proposed
q=18/17 and q=258/257 are individual positive-loss candidates. They
do not establish the every-epsilon target. A cofinal theorem requires
that for EVERY epsilon>0 a q_epsilon with 1<=q_epsilon<1+2epsilon
has a proved recurrence, paid finite base, finite constants and a
complete original-source/head transfer. Candidate-specific constants
may depend on epsilon but not on k. A finite set of q values is not
cofinal. A q=1 proof would avoid that particular cofinal-q obligation.

All coordinates described above satisfy |x_a(k)|<=6k by the total
source-band mass. Thus the elementary universal cost is

 P(k)<=36 k^2 [1+sum_j lambda_j ||v_j||_1^2],
 F(k)-F(floor(k/2))
 <=36 [1+sum_j lambda_j ||v_j||_1^2] k^(2-q).    (31)

For q<2 this does not pay a constant C. For q>=2 it gives a trivial
constant recurrence, whose resulting growth bound has no exponent gain
over linear. Therefore (29) at the proposed near-critical q needs new
signed cross-scale information, not merely domination, known sector
signs, squarefreeness, or total mass. One must prove cancellation in
the difference of the COMPLETE quadratic states at the two scales.
This difference is generally indefinite and has every within-scale
cross term; it is not a free-sign robust quadratic form.

The child source radius is 20 floor(k/2)<20k, so the scale graph in
(29) is well-founded. However its left side still contains the unknown
current-radius state x(k). A verified inequality (29) would be exactly
the new analytic transfer needed; a finite search that proposes H and C
has not paid that inequality. Prime/cofactor decompositions (6),(24)
can expose the correlations in it, but their exact identities do not
automatically prove an upper bound for its indefinite quadratic form.
Smooth masks also change with scale: y(k)=(20k)^theta differs from
y(floor(k/2)), and the crossing primes/composites are genuine terms.

An exact null direction from (27), such as Z-U_1-U_2-U_3-U_(>=4), has
zero square at every scale and cannot improve (28) or (29). Such
directions should be recorded as identities and quotiented out, or
flagged as vacuous rather than presented as a cancellation mechanism.
Other overlaps among smooth/sector states are actual correlations and
must not be silently treated as independent coordinates.

An LP over frozen direction-square features can propose nonnegative
lambda and a finite C. Adding rational approximations to negative

Neither operation establishes a uniform recurrence. The exact checker
must reconstruct the fixed H, replay every tested inequality including
non-dyadic k, and distinguish exact finite validation from (29). q has
arbitrary rational denominator, so k^q needs exact algebraic comparison
or correctly rounded certified bounds. A numerical eigenvector becomes
a rational candidate direction, with no algebraic or theorem status
inherited from the floating eigensolver. Freezing larger-scale tests
before evaluation prevents one form of selection leakage; surviving
them still leaves all untested integers and all-scale (29) open.

Computing the proposed states by factoring/sieving through 20k costs
