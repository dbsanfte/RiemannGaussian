Complete fractional branch centers, retained arithmetic, and cofinal recovery

Scope. Classical complex analysis, Euler summation, the Gamma reflection
formula, analytic continuation of ordinary zeta with its simple pole at one,
and its functional equation are foundational dependencies. No prime number
theorem, zero-density estimate, RH assumption, or new prime-distribution
estimate is used in a proved arithmetic bound. Numerical fits are finite
evidence only. These arguments are independently audited, not formalized in
a proof assistant. The supplied all-scale power is ONE, not one half.

1. Actual coefficients and the Bernstein bound.
For 0<a<1 put c_a=a/Gamma(1-a)>0, L_a(1)=0, and L_a(n)=(log n)^a
for n>1. Let lambda_a=mu*L_a and T_a(x)=sum_{n<=x}lambda_a(n).
The producer computes the divisor inverse of the constant-one sequence, then
the complete finite convolution. It has no prime or factorization oracle.
Independent factorization is used only by the evaluator.

t^a=c_a integral_0^infinity (1-exp(-ut))u^(-a-1)du. Integration by
parts proves this formula; both endpoints vanish. For
n=product p_j^e_j, r=omega(n)>0, b=sum(e_j-1)log p_j, the exact divisor
difference gives
lambda_a(n)=(-1)^(r-1)c_a integral_0^infinity
 exp(-bu) product_j(1-p_j^(-u)) u^(-a-1)du.
All integrands have their asserted sign. At zero the product is O(u^r),
and at infinity u^(-a-1) is integrable even when b=0. Bounding all factors
except the smallest-prime factor by one proves
|lambda_a(n)| <= (log p_min)^a <= (log n)^a.
Thus |T_a(x)| <= x*(log x)^a <= x*(1+log x), for EVERY real x>=1.
This supplies all-index signed structure; it is not a cancellation bound
of RH strength or a prime-distribution estimate.

2. Whole continued numerator, including the exponent parameter.
F_a(s)=sum_{n>=2}(log n)^a*n^(-s), initially Re(s)>1.
There D_a(s)=F_a(s)/(s*zeta(s)) is the Mellin transform of T_a.
Euler summation at any integer N>=2 gives the COMPLETE continuation
F_a(s)=sum_{n=2}^N f(n)+Integral_N^infinity f(t)dt - f(N)/2
       -sum_{k=1}^M B_{2k} f^(2k-1)(N)/(2k)! + R,
where f(t)=t^(-s)(log t)^a. The integral denotes its analytic continuation
(s-1)^(-a-1)*Gamma(a+1,(s-1)log N) off the cut s<=1 on the real axis.
The periodic Bernoulli remainder has modulus at most
2*zeta(2M)/(2*pi)^(2M)*Integral_N^infinity |f^(2M)(t)|dt.
If f^(r)(t)=t^(-s-r)sum_j d_{r,j}(log t)^(a-j),
d_{0,0}=1 and d_{r+1,j}=-(s+r)d_{r,j}+(a-j+1)d_{r,j-1}.
For real a>0 an explicit remainder bound is
[2*zeta(2M)/(2*pi)^(2M)] *
Gamma(a+1,(Re(s)+2M-1)log N)/(Re(s)+2M-1)^(a+1) *
sum_j |d_{2M,j}|/(log N)^j.
The incomplete Gamma term has a positive lower integration endpoint; it is
ENTIRE in its order. On compact sets of complex a the periodic remainder
converges locally uniformly for Re(s)>0 once 2M+Re(s)>1. Consequently,
for each fixed nonreal s with Re(s)>0, F_a(s) is ENTIRE in complex a.
F_0(s)=zeta(s)-1, and F_k(s)=(-1)^k zeta^(k)(s), k>=1.

An independent numerical evaluation uses
F_a(s)=c_a integral_0^infinity [zeta(s)-zeta(s+t)]t^(-a-1)dt
for nonreal s, Re(s)>0, 0<a<1. The identity holds by absolute summation
on Re(s)>1 and then analytic continuation. A Taylor endpoint, a native
finite integral, and the exact extracted tail (zeta(s)-1)/a give a whole
continuation. The remaining tail beyond B is bounded by
2^(-Re(s)-B)*(1+2/(Re(s)+B-1))*B^(-a-1)/log 2.
Neither evaluation replaces F by a finite Dirichlet prefix.

3. Complete real cut center.
For 0<u<1 the paired integral for eta(u) is positive and >=1/2:
each integral over [2k-1,2k] of u*t^(-u-1) is at least the next one,
so their total is at least half the full integral over [1,infinity).
Since zeta(u)=eta(u)/(1-2^(1-u)), zeta(u)<0 and
|1/zeta(u)|<=2*(2^(1-u)-1)<=4*log(2)*(1-u).
Z(u)=(u-1)zeta(u) has the removable value Z(1)=1, Z(0)=1/2,
and Z(u)>=1/(4*log 2) throughout [0,1].

A_a(x)=c_a integral_0^1 (x^u-1)/(u*Z(u))*(1-u)^(-a)du,
x>=1, is the FULL branch center, not just a leading asymptotic term.
The apparent singularity at u=0 is removable. The singularity at u=1
is integrable because a<1. A_a(1)=0 and
0<=A'_a(x)<=K_a=4*log(2)*c_a/(1-a), for every real x>=1.
Its Mellin transform is
M_a(s)=c_a/s integral_0^1 (1-u)^(-a)/[Z(u)*(s-u)]du,
s outside [0,1], initially Re(s)>1. In particular M_a is holomorphic
at EVERY nonreal ordinary zeta zero and at all positive ordinary heights.

Euler summation writes F_a(s)=Gamma(a+1)(s-1)^(-a-1)+G_a(s),
where G_a is holomorphic on Re(s)>0. Thus the sole branch contribution
to D_a is Gamma(a+1)(s-1)^(-a)/[s*Z(s)]. Gamma reflection gives
c_a=Gamma(a+1)sin(pi*a)/pi. Across 0<s<1, the upper-minus-lower jump
of this contribution is -2*pi*i*c_a*(1-s)^(-a)/[s*Z(s)], exactly
the Cauchy-transform jump of M_a. Local Cauchy deformation also cancels
the integrable endpoint singularity at one, with no residual pole.
Therefore D_a-M_a continues meromorphically to Re(s)>0, with no
positive-real cut and with ordinary nonreal zeta zeros retained as possible
poles. The subtraction never divides out or erases those ordinary zeros.

For native evaluation write v=1-u and
H_x(v)=(x^(1-v)-1)/[(1-v)Z(1-v)]; its apparent singularity at v=1
is removable. Z is ENTIRE. Its first 64 deflated-zeta coefficients and
a native upper bound on |v|<=2 bound |Z(1-v)-1| strictly below one
on |v|<=5/4. The omitted Z coefficients have a geometric Cauchy bound.
Rouche therefore gives a uniform positive lower modulus on that disk,
without assuming any ordinary zero-free region. On its boundary,
|H_x(v)| <= (x^(1+R)+1)/[(R-1)*Z_lower], R=5/4.
If H_x=sum h_j v^j, then
A_a(x)=c_a sum_{j=0}^{L-1} h_j/(j+1-a) + error,
|error|<=c_a H_upper R^(-L)/[(L+1-a)*(1-1/R)].
The producer uses 448..1024 terms with an explicit accuracy/resource check.
The independent checker uses formal coefficient division at higher native
precision, 640 terms, and a separately certified disk of radius 6/5.
Every source center must CONTAIN that sharper independent enclosure.
The smallest and largest protected final roots also receive direct
original-variable quadrature anchors at x=32, with a separate Taylor
endpoint, an exponential/sinc integrand, and bounded omitted tail.
No point sample substitutes for either analytic remainder bound. Resource
exhaustion is a missing observation, not countergrowth.

4. Finite signed parameter families and normalization.
Take ANY finite nonzero weights w_i in Q, with distinct b_i in Q cap(0,1).
Let p_m=sum_i w_i*b_i^m. A Vandermonde matrix shows some p_m!=0
with m<=number_of_atoms-1. Let m0 be the first such index, and define
h(q)=p_m0/q^m0 !=0 for every positive integer q.
E_a=T_a-A_a; R_q(x)=sum_i w_i E_{b_i/q}(x)/h(q).
The normalization exposes the first nonvanishing parameter response. It
is part of the operator, and ALL its arithmetic and observer costs are
charged. A high-pass combination cannot gain credit just by tending to
zero with increasing q. Signs of h are kept in arithmetic semantics.

For every fixed q the following TRUE all-real-scale bound holds:
|R_q(x)| <= C_q*x*(1+log x),
C_q >= sum_i |w_i|*(1+K_{b_i/q})/|h(q)|.
The certified ordinary power is one. The bound constant may grow with q.
On n<=x<n+1, T_a(x)=T_a(n). Therefore an integer estimate extends to
reals with the additional derivative guard
K_q=sum_i |w_i|K_{b_i/q}/|h(q)|. No uncharged interpolation is allowed.

5. Cofinal RH recovery, INCLUDING sum_i w_i=0 combinations.
Suppose a family as in paragraph 4 satisfies
for ALL q>=1, there exists a finite C_q such that for EVERY real x>=1,
|R_q(x)|<=C_q*x^(1/2+eps/q^nu)*log(e+x)^d,
with eps>0, nu>0 and d>=0 fixed. No bound on the dependence of C_q
on q is required. A cofinal subsequence of q is also sufficient.

If an ordinary zero rho has beta=Re(rho)>1/2, then for all sufficiently
large q the centered Mellin integral is holomorphic at rho. Since M_a
is holomorphic there and s!=0, this forces sum_i w_i F_{b_i/q}(rho)=0,
regardless of multiplicity. Define the ENTIRE parameter function
J(t)=sum_i w_i F_{b_i*t}(rho). Its zeros at t=1/q accumulate at zero,
so J is identically zero. Its Taylor coefficients imply
p_m*(partial_a^m F_a(rho) at a=0)=0 for every m.

Let b_* be the largest base, with w_*!=0. An exact rational certificate
finds M with |w_*|b_*^M>sum_{b_i<b_*}|w_i|b_i^M. Because all ratios
b_i/b_* lie in (0,1), the inequality holds for EVERY m>=M. Thus
p_m!=0 for every m>=M, and the entire function a -> F_a(rho) is a
polynomial of degree <M (zero polynomial allowed). At positive integers
k, |zeta^(k)(rho)|=|F_k(rho)| is at most polynomial growth in k.
Hence the Taylor series of zeta at rho has INFINITE radius. Identity
continuation would remove zeta's actual simple pole at one, a contradiction.
There are no zeros with beta>1/2. The functional equation then gives RH.
This is a conditional implication, not a proof of its signed premise.
Finite sampled q and finite sampled x do not supply that premise.

6. Charged point observers.
At a query s=sigma+it, sigma>theta_q, t!=0, put
F_q=sum w_i F_{b_i/q}/h(q), M_q=sum w_i M_{b_i/q}/h(q).
|M_q(s)|<=K_q/(|s|*|t|). If an integer signed premise with constant
C_q is supplied, its full Mellin norm is at most C_q I(sigma-theta_q,d)
+K_q/sigma, where I=Integral_1^infinity x^(-gap-1)log(e+x)^d dx.
Therefore, when the COMPLETE F_q enclosure excludes zero,
|1/zeta(s)| <= |s|*(K_q/(|s|*|t|)+C_q I+K_q/sigma)/|F_q(s)|.
To bound I for a rational d, take D=ceil(d), log(e+x)<=log(e+1)+log x,
and sum binomial(D,j)*log(e+1)^(D-j)*j!/gap^(j+1).
All small parameter factors, centers, and interpolation costs are included.
The observer lower modulus is recomputed from the SERIALIZED complex ball,
then rounded downward on a 160-bit dyadic grid with an additional grid unit
of guard. The checker verifies the strict margin against that exact printed
enclosure; a floating point-endpoint reconstruction is not used as equality.
An unresolved denominator is missing evidence. This conditional cost is
not itself a new global estimate.

7. Negative control: leading-only centering is mathematically false.
Subtracting only a*x*(log x)^(a-1) (with any compact initial patch) has
Mellin leading term Gamma(a+1)(s-1)^(-a). But
1/[s*Z(s)]=1-(1+EulerGamma)*(s-1)+O((s-1)^2).
The remaining branch coefficient is
-Gamma(a+1)*(1+EulerGamma)*(s-1)^(1-a), which is nonzero and not
holomorphic at one. No power strictly below one can bound the resulting
error over all scales, even if RH is assumed. For distinct parameter
bases the largest base gives the unique smallest residual exponent;
finite signed combinations cannot remove it. Full-center candidates are
not rejected by this obstruction. A finite fit may miss this false control.

8. Discovery and promotion scope.
The generator sees only declared arithmetic constructions; the coefficient
fit sees anonymous real response columns without operator names, prime
labels, analytic constants, or expected answers. Features and budgets
are supplied assumptions. An inferred combination remains a numerical
proposal until independent proof admission. A frozen rule can annihilate
its first nonzero parameter moment: P(t)-2^m0 P(t/2). This creates a
NEW signed family; it is not a semantics-preserving abbreviation. The
universal recovery argument is rechecked on the new family and all q
normalization costs change accordingly. Equal random slots are evaluated
with the same discovery and validation resources. Adaptive batches are
frozen before validation; private final cases are generated after sealing.

No historical novelty, new distribution theorem, uniform critical bound,
or RH proof follows merely from this expanded language or its finite tests.
