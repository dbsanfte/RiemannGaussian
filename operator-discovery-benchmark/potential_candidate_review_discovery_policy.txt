Semantic policy for a next structural potential generation
Read-only analytical review of selected pilot001 ASTs, 2026-10-09.
Owned addition only; frozen source and old artifacts are unchanged.
Read selected program records and generator/selector source, not final response
scores. No tests, new coefficient tables, fresh arithmetic scores or hashes.

Purpose and conclusion
The pilot genuinely changes parameterized state expressions. That capability
should now be guided by semantic classification, rather than treating every
new AST as a new signed-control mechanism. A different function remains an
unproved candidate. Source-sensitive masks, lag energies, bounded root padding
and dormant moving banks deserve explicit classes alongside mixed interactions.
The proposed policy nominates next structural mutations and proof obligations;
it establishes no complete innovation estimate or RH result.

Selected ASTs, without using performance scores
All have the complete anchored E_mu and weight1; ID0 has V=0. Write
M(y)=sum_{1<=n<=y}mu(n), with M(0)=0. The selected state descriptions are:
  ID4: M(floor(t/2)).
  ID16: sum_{i<=t, i=0 mod max(2,floor(X^(1/6)))}mu(i).
  ID61: coprime(t,max(2,floor(sqrt(X)))) times
        [M(t)-M(floor(t/3))].
  ID25: M(floor(t/2))*mu(floor(sqrt(t))).
  ID26: M(floor(t/2))*mu(floor(t^(1/3))).
  ID73: ID26 state multiplied bymu(t).
  ID67: sum_{d<=floor(X^(1/6))}mu(d)[M(t)-M(floor(t/d))]
        -M(floor(t^(1/6))).
  ID42: sum_{p prime,p<=floor(X^(1/3))}M(floor(t/p)).
In every case V(X)=X^-2 sum_{t=X}^{2X-1}U(X,t)^2. This list describes
semantics/provenance, not validated improvements over baseline.

Proof-backed square semantics
For actual mu, mu(g)^2=1_(g squarefree). Multiplying a WHOLE state U by
mu(g), then squaring, therefore changes its potential to a squarefree-masked
U^2. The added mu(g) sign disappears, even when U itself depends on mu(g).
IDs25/26 are masked lag energies; ID73 adds another squarefree mask. They
are different potential functions, but this particular mutation adds no
new coefficient-sign factor to the squared expression.

This does NOT mean that those potentials are wholly unsigned or that masks
are useless. M(floor(t/2))^2 already contains all signed prefix cross terms.
A mask can change their weighting and the signed parent/child increment.
It still needs an actual arithmetic estimate; removing the extra factor's
sign is only a precise algebraic classification.

Pointwise potential domination does not order signed innovations: subtracting
the parent term can reverse that comparison. Thus a smaller squarefree-masked
V is not automatically a better innovation bound or a stronger mechanism.

Likewise guard(P,U)^2=1_P*U^2. ID61's guard changes where its signed square
is counted, while the square retains the mixed term
  -2*M(t)*M(floor(t/3)).
The mask is not itself a new signed identity. Multiplying by a SUM inside
a square differs from multiplying by a single point coefficient: its square
can introduce new mixed products. In particular ID42 retains all prime-bank
cross terms. A blanket label of sign-insensitive for every product mutation
would be false.

Generic next-generation rule: track added sign dependence RELATIVE TO THE
parent. Classify whole-state point-coefficient multiplication as norm/mask
reweighting when a checked norm law applies. Reserve separate mutation slots
for placing factors within different signed summands before aggregation, or
forming coupled state sums before squaring. Expand or symbolically describe
their complete mixed terms, then apply only justified arithmetic reductions.
Their mere presence remains no theorem about cancellation.

Lag and alias semantics
For dyadic X>=2, every n in[X/2,X-1] occurs twice as floor(t/2) when
t runs[X,2X-1]. Thus ID4 has the exact identity
  V(X)=2*A_mu(X/2)/X^2=E_mu(X/2)/2.
Its W increment is Delta E_mu(X)+Delta E_mu(X/2)/2. At X=1 its potential
iszero; the initial case must remain explicit. ID4 is a positive lag/smoothing
channel. Its square contains the original signed arithmetic, but its smallness
cannot be assumed independently of the unknown energy bound it samples.
It is not the same function as baseline V=0.

Next classification should distinguish proved full-function identities,
proved identities only on dyadic/even domains, and finite-response aliases.
Only the first two can justify a theorem-scope alias, with initial/domain
exceptions retained. Equal sampled outputs do not justify universal merging.
Global state sign changes U->-U give the same potential exactly; alpha-renamed
binders and ordinary ring simplifications can also be grouped with a checked
rewrite record. Preserve all ordinal IDs, original payloads and provenance.

Effective cutoff exposure
Moving syntax need not move effectively in discovery. ID16's modulus is
max(2,floor(X^(1/6))). Throughout discovery, including its largest child
argument X=512, it is exactly2 because512<3^6=729. That evidence therefore
concerns a parity mask, not several moving-modulus regimes. The frozen final
arguments cross the next regime; no final response score is needed to see
this coverage distinction.

ID67's d=1 summand vanishes identically because floor(t/1)=t. When its
bank cutoff is1, the bank iszero and only the subtracted root-prefix remains.
At later cutoff values the bank genuinely changes. Such exact dormant terms
should be simplified before counting effective bank size or coupling degree.

Sixth-root t selectors have seen no index4 when the complete maximum t is4095:
4^6=4096. Likewise the largest own-scale X=2048 stays below that threshold.
This is an exact AST/domain coverage statement, not an arithmetic score or
an assertion that later regimes behave similarly. Integer-root transitions
alone do not guarantee a potential change: an outer max may hide them, and
a squared point-coefficient factor may retain the same norm across them.

Generic exposure metadata should record, conservatively and with exact integer
inequality witnesses: effective selector ranges on parent and child domains;
whether max/min masks suppress root transitions; guaranteed empty banks;
identically zero summands; and which new regimes remain untested. A branch
coverage count is not a lower bound on signed information. General nonlinear
index maps may require conservative bounds or an explicit unknown label.

Predeclare transition-sensitive evaluations from AST semantics before seeing
new responses. Include nearby irregular cases as diagnostics and dyadic cases
on both sides where the resource ledger permits. Recompute all child selectors
and retain newly entered primes/divisors and all boundary terms. If the next
effective regime is beyond budget, record unexposed rather than treating many
samples within one regime as confirmation of the moving family. Do not add
unbudgeted coefficient lookahead or use final failures to refit this old run.

Bounded padding versus mixed interaction
For a standalone U(t)=M(floor(t^theta)) with0<=theta<=1/2, the elementary
|mu(n)|<=1 bound gives
  V(X)<=X^-2*X*(2X)^(2theta)=2^(2theta)*X^(2theta-1).
This is uniformly bounded and even decays when theta<1/2. It is a checked
component envelope, not critical control of E_mu. For any nonnegative V
uniformly bounded by B, |V(2X)-V(X)|<=B. Therefore the existential cofinal
innovation claim for E_mu+V is equivalent to the same claim for E_mu by
adding B to each constant, with initial storage retained. Such padding can
alter finite constants or simplify a proof presentation, but supplies no
independent discharge of the missing arithmetic estimate.

Do not apply this rule to root_mixture(U,B) merely because B's own normalized
square is bounded. The potential (U-B)^2 includes -2UB. A bound for B^2
does not bound that mixed term independently of the unknown U energy. ID67
must retain its complete bank/root mixed interaction. A correct classifier
can mark its root-square component bounded and its mixed component unresolved.

Proposed evidence and selection policy
Keep three separate annotations for each candidate:
  (a) checked semantic identities/reductions and their source/domain scope;
  (b) proved component envelopes, initial charges and unresolved mixed terms;
  (c) finite evidence, effective-regime coverage and missing computations.
Neither (a) nor (b) may be replaced by a response fingerprint or fitted slope.
Actual-mu norm reductions must not silently be applied to another control source.

The next archive should retain baseline plus bounded calibration slots for
lag aliases, masks and bounded padding, while reserving explicit slots for
distinct mixed interactions and unexposed moving regimes. Group diversity
by effective mechanism as well as generation provenance: IDs25/26/73 are
three different masks in one mechanism class, not three automatically new
signed-control directions. Do not reject every mask or every unproved lead.
Classification can be unknown, with a dedicated bounded exploration slot.

Report baseline-relative whole innovation and anchored costs, completeness,
AST/work cost and exposure alongside the class label. Preserve the complete
signed quantities; do not improve a candidate score by deleting negative
cross terms, complement regions, child bank entries or initial W1. Constants
remain frozen from training, and new validation/final responses remain separate.
Finite rank is a nomination rule, never a universal arithmetic estimate.

Evidence-driven structural feedback should state why a mutation is nominated:
for example a checked semantic collapse identifies an ineffective placement
of a point factor; an unexposed selector requires regime coverage; or an
unresolved mixed expression supplies a concrete lemma obligation. New mutations
must change actual retained AST structure. There is no coefficient optimizer,
preferred named formula, universal response alias or automatic novelty claim.

Proof priority and stopping discipline
Highest analytical priority goes to an explicit complete expression for which
a universal source/domain binding and induction-relevant signed innovation
lemma can be stated. A new operator/function may still be only a reformulation
of the unknown bound. Ask whether its proposed lemma controls a signed mixed
term with all moving-boundary and scalar costs, or merely assumes an equivalent
energy bound. Uniform envelopes/aliases can resolve that question negatively
for a mechanism without refuting every possible future candidate.

The all-scale conclusion still requires cofinal q, finite C_q independent of
dyadic X, the entire W(2X)-W(X), and finite initial W(1). Passing finite cases,
cheap root padding, control-source contrast or a new syntactic function proves
none of that premise. If a search round yields only masks/aliases/padding,
record that result and change generic structural placement or semantic coverage;
do not substitute another gradient method or claim the finite template exhausts
signed arithmetic discovery.

Minimal changes to the existing next-generation hooks
No new optimizer, general theorem engine or parallel infrastructure is needed.
At generate_initial/generate_mutations, annotate the few recognized identities
above and retain unknown for other expressions. Reallocate some whole-state
source_product attempts to factor placement within distinct summands before
coupling/squaring, so their direct sign need not disappear in one norm gate.
At choose, retain baseline and calibration representatives, but limit redundant
mask/lag representatives and reserve slots for genuinely distinct unresolved
mixed mechanisms. Count mechanism diversity separately from inherited family
labels. Before freezing a new scale plan, check effective selector transitions
of the nominated parents and declare which affordable regimes will be exposed.
These three changes alter what expressions are generated and selected while
keeping exact complete evaluation and the existing proof gate unchanged.
