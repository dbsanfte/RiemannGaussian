FRACTIONAL COEFFICIENT OPERATORS WITH A VERIFIED ORDINARY-ZERO BRIDGE

This is a general construction search over a declared fractional coefficient
and logarithmic-kernel grammar. It reuses the ordinary zeta and Dirichlet
power semantics. It is not claimed independent of those representation choices
or historically novel. The parameter values and fitted estimates are selected
by the frozen numerical protocol, not a language-model choice after outcomes.

1. For 0<a<=1 define nu_a=mu^{*a}, the normalized Dirichlet power of the
constant-one inverse. The producer uses factor-free inversion and binomial
Dirichlet powers. The evaluator independently uses the prime-power recursion
c_0=1, c_k=c_(k-1)*(k-1-a)/k and multiplicativity. For 0<a<1 every c_k,
k>=1, is negative and has absolute value <=1. Hence |nu_a(n)|<=1 for all n;
nu_a(p)=-a, and its sign is (-1)^omega(n). At a=1 it is ordinary mu.
This is a universal coefficient theorem, not a new estimate of prime counts.
Its absolutely convergent series is zeta(s)^(-a) for Re(s)>1, with the
Euler-product logarithm branch; absolute series <=zeta(sigma)^a.

2. For 0<b<=1 and real kappa put
K_b,kappa(u)=Gamma(b)^(-1) integral_0^u exp(kappa*v)*v^(b-1) dv,
u>=0, and G_a,b,kappa(x)=sum_(n<=x) nu_a(n) K_b,kappa(log(x/n)).
For b=0 use K=1 and G=sum nu_a. No coefficient beyond floor(x) contributes.
K is positive, increasing, continuous for b>0 and K(0)=0. Its numerical
formula is u^b/Gamma(b+1)*1F1(b;b+1;kappa*u). Independent evaluation uses
regularized incomplete gamma, with auxiliary entire-integrand quadratures
after u=v^d for rational b=p/d. No sampled quadrature replaces a sum over n.

Fubini on Re(s)>max(1,kappa) gives the exact Mellin identity
Mellin G(s)=zeta(s)^(-a)*(s-kappa)^(-b)/s.
Indeed the Mellin transform of the kernel is (s-kappa)^(-b)/s. Absolute
convergence follows from the coefficient majorant and the positive kernel.
The coefficient tail above N is bounded by N^(-h) zeta(sigma-h)^a for
0<h<sigma-1, with the complete Mellin tail multiplied by
|s-kappa|^(-b)/|s|. Numerical checks of this identity retain those tails.

3. At zeta's pole one the transform, after multiplying by s, has local
order a-b if kappa=1 and b>0, otherwise a. It can be holomorphic there
only if this order is a nonnegative integer. At a shifted point kappa!=1,
where zeta is finite and nonzero, a positive b adds order -b. Such a point
inside a proposed holomorphic domain is another obstruction. The pilot
uses real shifts -1/2,0,1/2,1,3/2, where the needed real zeta facts are
checked natively when the shifted point is inside a claimed domain.
Matching a=b at kappa=1 removes the pole locally: the transform is
[((s-1)zeta(s))]^(-a)/s. This supplies a new arithmetic observable,
not the pointwise signed root of M. The shift and power grids also retain
unmatched cases with their explicit failed continuation obligations.

4. Integer bounds must cover real x. Let N>=1 and N<=t<N+1. For b>0,
the kernel derivative is (t/n)^kappa*log(t/n)^(b-1)/(t Gamma(b)).
For kappa<=1 it is <=log(t/n)^(b-1)/(n Gamma(b)). Split n<=N/2,
N/2<n<N and n=N. Using log(t/n)>=(N-n)/N, the first two sums are
bounded by [2(1+log N)+2+2/b]/Gamma(b); the endpoint term integrates
to at most 2/Gamma(b+1). Thus the whole unit increment is at most
[4+2 log N+4/b]/Gamma(b) <= (4+4/b)(1+log N)/Gamma(b).
For kappa>1 multiply this by (N+1)^(kappa-1) <= (2t)^(kappa-1).
This holds for every arithmetic input with coefficient magnitudes <=1.
For b=0 the cumulative sum is constant between consecutive integers.

If every integer N satisfies |G(N)-A|<=C*N^theta, theta>=0, the real
function therefore satisfies |G(t)-A|<=C*t^theta+E*t^lambda*(1+log t),
lambda=max(kappa-1,0), E=2^lambda*(4+4/b)/Gamma(b); for b=0 E=0.
Its Mellin transform is holomorphic on Re(s)>eta=max(theta,lambda,0).
For sigma>eta its absolute value is bounded by
C/(sigma-theta)+E*[1/(sigma-lambda)+1/(sigma-lambda)^2].

5. Let m clear the denominators of a and b. In the initial domain,
zeta(s)^(a*m)*(s-kappa)^(b*m)*[s*Mellin(G-A)(s)+A]^m=1.
The left side is meromorphic with integral powers, so this identity extends
through any claimed Mellin holomorphic domain by the identity theorem.
At EVERY nontrivial ordinary zero, whatever its multiplicity, zeta^(a*m)
vanishes and the other factors are finite; it would yield 0=1. Consequently
a PROVED uniform integer bound with eta<=1/2 and no local divisor
obstruction would imply RH. A previously certified actual critical zero
rules out eta<1/2. Local orders also reject numerically plausible bounds
that cannot cross the pole at one or a shifted kernel singularity.

The quantitative inverse cost is
|1/zeta(s)| <= (|s|*B_sigma+|A|)^(1/a)*|s-kappa|^(b/a).
Small a is therefore charged its full inverse-power and height costs.
No exponent division by a is introduced into eta: this is a fractional
DIRICHLET inverse, unlike a pointwise root of a fixed residual. Its signed
coefficient sums are different observables. Small finite amplitudes are
not global arithmetic cancellation proofs.

6. All-positive coefficients and coefficients eventually equal to one have
Dirichlet series with a genuine pole at one. Their kernel-weighted sums
cannot have the same subcritical-half-plane control, since the transformed
series has negative local order there. Finite point counterexamples are
computed with complete sums. Claims outside that analytic contradiction
are marked unresolved rather than incorrectly ruled out.

The signed estimates across every integer or dyadic scale remain open.
The operator identity, coefficient theorem, increment bound and conditional
ordinary-zero implication are proved; they do not prove their arithmetic
premise. RH remains unproved.
