1. Primal bound is legitimate, with the positive Gram sign as proposed

Group every squarefree n<=N by the complete tuple of source-delay values
f_i(n) and geometry profiles C_j(n). Include unit and all squarefree
coordinates, not only observed nonzero source values. Let c_g be the actual
squarefree group population and z_g=c_g^(-1) sum_(n in g)mu(n), so |z_g|<=1.
Empty groups are omitted. Actual source features are sum_g c_g f_i(g)z_g.

For K>=1, C_K(n)=floor(K/n)-1[n=1] gives
sum_n mu(n)C_K(n)=1-mu(1)=0; for K=0 set C0 identically0. The sum needs only
n<=K, so min(N,floor(scale*u^eta)) stays inside the original arithmetic
horizon. Unit subtraction is essential. Geometry, source floors and profile
floors must be grouped exactly before counts or coefficients are used.

Write A0_gh=c_g c_h f(g)^T T f(h). The symmetric Z_j,i has entries
c_g c_h[C_j(g)f_i(h)+f_i(g)C_j(h)], and actual z^T Z z=0. A positive Gram
addition w v v^T, w>=0, makes actual z^T A z at least the original drift.
It therefore has the correct sign for seeking an upper bound by diagonal
completion. The feature-normalization convention must be exact and frozen;
normalizing a feature changes its admissible w-box and is not just notation.

Let d_g=max(0,A_gg+sum_(h!=g)|A_gh|). Then diag(d)-A is symmetric diagonally
dominant with nonnegative diagonal and hence PSD. One direct proof expands
pair terms |A_gh|(z_g-sign(A_gh)z_h)^2 plus the remaining nonnegative diagonal.
Thus original drift<=z^T A z<=sum_g d_g z_g^2<=B=sum_g d_g. This prices the
FULL drift; no unlinked arithmetic remainder is omitted. It is nevertheless
an upper price in a relaxation, and finiteB is not a uniform source bound.

The group count uses mu(n)^2 only. It must be independently checked against
squarefree counts, while LP selection must never use mu signs, actual group
sums z, source values or savedU. Such values can be reported after selection.

2. Exact finite box dual

For arbitrary rational0<=lambda_g<=1 and |nu_gh|<=lambda_g+lambda_h, g<h,
L(A)=sum_g lambda_g A_gg+sum_(g<h)nu_gh A_gh is at mostB(A). This follows
from max(0,x)>=lambda*x and from each offdiagonal absolute value being
counted once underlambda_g and once underlambda_h. There is no extra factor2
in this definition ofL; ifL is obtained from z^T A z, use nu_gh=2z_gz_h.

For independenttheta boxes[-16,16] and nonnegative Gramweights[0,16],
B(A)>=L(A0)-16sum_j |L(Z_j)|+16sum_alpha min(0,L(v_alpha v_alpha^T)).
This follows by minimizing the linearL over each actual parameter box.
The formula is exact for these boxes and normalized features only. It is a
lower bound for this diagonal-completion price cone, not an arithmetic
counterexample, a lower bound on actual drift, or unrestricted SDP optimum.

Rounded LP duals need exact feasibility. Clamp lambda into[0,1] rationally;
then clamp eachnu against the NEWlambda_g+lambda_h. Recompute all dot
products and the box penalty exactly. Record rational denominators and
coefficient bit budgets. A finite dual certifies only the evaluated count
geometry. Extending the conclusion to allu requires a separately proved
construction of feasible duals and a uniform lower estimate of their value.

3. Automatic uniform obstruction for every sublinear profile schedule

Suppose all registeredK_j(u)<=floor(u/5) eventually. This holds for every
fixed finite positive scale list with eta_j<1. On the entire interval
I=(floor(u/5),floor(u/4)], the original unfolded G and G+.433G(u//3)
source-delay vector is[-1,0,...] and everyC_j is0. Therefore all squarefree
coordinates inI belong to one canonical group g (possibly with additional
coordinates sharing that same tuple). Its sizec_g is at least Q(u/4)-Q(u/5),
where Q(x)=sum_(n<=x)mu(n)^2. Here H00>=1 and A0_gg=H00*c_g^2.

Take lambda_g=1, all otherlambda=0, allnu=0. EveryL(Z)=0 because C_j(g)=0.
EveryL(vv^T)=v_g^2>=0, so the nonnegative Gram box penalty vanishes. The
exact dual lower bound is thereforeH00*c_g^2, regardless of theta boxes,
Gram normalization and positive Gram roster. This is not just a large-box
artifact; even unrestrictedtheta cannot alter that diagonal through these
zero profiles, and positive Gram cannot lower it.

Elementary square-divisor inversion proves, for realx>=0,
Q(x)=x/zeta(2)+O(sqrt(x)), with an explicit admissible error3sqrt(x) forx>=1
and harmless small-x handling. Hence the outer interval contains at least
u/[20*zeta(2)]-O(sqrtu) squarefree integers. For an elementary rational lower
coefficient, zeta(2)<2 by sum_(n>=2)1/n^2<integral_1^infinity dt/t^2=1, so
Q(u/4)-Q(u/5)>=u/40-3(sqrt(u/4)+sqrt(u/5)); an exact rational upper bound
on square roots gives a machine-checkable finite starting scale. Eventually
c_g>=u/80, and B>=H00*u^2/6400. Consequently no finiteC can make this price
B(u)<=C*u^q uniformly for ANYq<2.

This obstruction uses no PNT, no signed Mertens estimate and no numerical
arithmetic. It is about the certificate grammar: actual mu may cancel inside
the large group, but the interval relaxation|z_g|<=1 discards that cancellation.
Adding squarefree group counts exactly does not repair the loss. A pilot
restricted to sublinearK is uniformly doomed and should be admitted as an
obstruction ablation rather than offered as a plausible positive route.
At least one full-radius/linearK profile reaching into the outer interval is
necessary to evade this PARTICULAR zero-profile dual. It is not sufficient.

4. A more general route to uniform dual obstructions

For any formal group vectorz with |z_g|<=1, setlambda=z_g^2 andnu_gh=2z_gz_h.
The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives dual feasibility. If all
incidence moments sum_g c_g C_j(g)z_g vanish, thenL(Z)=z^TZz=0; positive
Gram terms obeyL(vv^T)=(v^Tz)^2>=0. If additionally all delayed source forms
except currentf0 vanish onz, thenL(A0)=H00[sum_g c_g f0(g)z_g]^2.
Thus an exact formal covariance with orderu current-source mass, zero
profile moments and zero delayed-source moments obstructs everyq<2 price.
It need not equal actual mu. This is a genuine finite-grammar obstruction.

For a uniform theorem the formalz and zero moments must be constructed for
every sufficiently largeu, not fitted once. One implementable sufficient
certificate would use a finite collection of fixed rational fractional
intervals on which all floor profiles are constant, solve a rational moment
system using their exact squarefree counts, and prove the solution stays
inside[-1,1] with an orderu source moment by explicit count-error margins.
For linearK profiles, choose intervals away from all profile floor thresholds
with bounded quotient values. The limiting moment matrix must have a proven
nonzero minor and strict interior feasibility; count errors are thenO(sqrtu)
and an explicit inverse-matrix perturbation price supplies the starting
scale. Every selected interval's prime/squarefree population is geometric,
not a signed source estimate. This is an analytic construction opportunity,
not a theorem for arbitrary profile lists. If the limiting matrix is singular
or f0 lies in the constrained span, this argument does not apply.

Finite LP witnesses may suggest which intervals and moments to use. They
must not silently assume group patterns persist, suppress floor-entry changes,
or treat finite conditioning as a uniform bound. A certified obstruction
should print its exact limiting rational interval/moment matrix, a minor,
strict feasibility margins and the elementary squarefree error budget.

5. Pilot disposition

The finite dense completion pilot is worthwhile if it includes linear/full
radius profiles and exact box-dual admissions, and explicitly carries the
sublinear automatic obstruction. It directly prices the entire signed drift
instead of081's sparse packet. It still relaxes all arithmetic not represented
by its finitely many incidence moments. Feasible finiteB/u and poor box-duals
are neither RH evidence nor payment of uniformB. Positive completion at all
scales needs an actual symbolic construction and geometry-paid bound;
negative uniform results need a proved dual family such as section3 or4.

No source bound, uniform drift bound or RH result is proved in this review.
The proved new mathematical statement is the sublinear-profile obstruction
for the proposed completion price, plus exact finite primal/dual validity.
