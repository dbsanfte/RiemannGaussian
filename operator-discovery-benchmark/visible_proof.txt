RESIDUE-VISIBLE PRIME-POWER PRODUCT DECODER

Status: universal mathematical proofs with exact and Arb ball checks.
These are written proofs, not theorem-prover certificates. Historical
originality is unresolved; no new RH estimate is asserted. r is an integer
at least 3 unless explicitly stated otherwise. The trusted numerical
kernel is FLINT/Arb; the independent checker imports no producer code.

1. Definition, learned grammar, and complete-fibre scope

Let PP={p^e: p prime, e>=1}, g(p^e)=1/e, and g(n)=0 otherwise.
Thus g is the formal Dirichlet logarithm of u(n)=1. Define the bilinear
index coupling K_r(a,b) by summing a(x)b(y) at

                  Phi_r(x,y)=xy(x+y)^r, x,y>=2.

The inputs in the decoder bridge are supported on PP. An unordered
PP pair {p^e,q^f} is admissible if e,f are nonzero modulo r when p!=q,
or if e+f is nonzero modulo r when p=q. Let I_r be the image of these
pairs. Section 2 proves each member of I_r has a UNIQUE unordered PP
fibre among ALL PP pairs, not merely among the admissible inputs.

For an arbitrary coefficient sequence h, define the linear operator

 (V_r h)(m)=sum_{x<=y, x,y in PP, xy=m, pair admissible} h(Phi_r(x,y)).

Every row is finite. This operator includes the earlier two-visible-prime
coordinate decoder and adds same-prime rows. At a prime-power product
p^k with k nonzero modulo r, the row has floor(k/2) coordinates. V_r
therefore aggregates distinct encoded coordinates; it is not a partial
coordinate permutation, isometry, or two-sided inverse.

The searched grammar uses full prime valuations, their residues modulo r,
integer power comparisons, residual r-th roots obtained from factorization,
and affine integer reconstruction. For r=3, nine comparisons U^d>N
leave three training fits and the unique held-out exponent d=4. Twenty-five
reconstruction programs leave five training fits and the unique held-out
divisor r+2, with the special two-prime diagonal shift r. Held-out examples
include very unequal exponents and integers beyond a machine word.
The source sums duplicate factor entries before using valuations.

2. Universal unique-fibre theorem on the accepted sectors

Call a prime visible in N when its FULL valuation v_p(N) is nonzero
modulo r; do not replace that valuation by its residue.

For distinct-base PP inputs x=p^e,y=q^f, gcd(x,y)=1 and the sum x+y
is coprime to pq. Hence the only possible visible primes of Phi are
p,q, with their exact input exponents. If both are visible, those full
prime-power factors reconstruct x and y. No same-prime pair can produce
two visible primes. This proves uniqueness for the earlier sector.

Now let x=p^e,y=p^f,e<=f, k=e+f nonzero modulo r. Put d=f-e.
If d>0, N=p^v(1+p^d)^r with v=(r+2)e+d. If d=0 and p is odd,
N=p^((r+2)e)2^r. If d=0 and p=2, N=2^(r+(r+2)e).
In every case p is the only visible prime, and its residue is k mod r.
Write U=p^v, using the FULL valuation v of p in N.

For d>0, U>1+p^d, so U^(r+1)>N. For odd-prime diagonal pairs,
U>2 gives the same inequality. For the two-prime diagonal, N=U>1.
By contrast, a distinct-base pair with exactly one visible prime has
that prime-power factor U as one input, so N=U*y*(U+y)^r>U^(r+1).
The strict oversize comparison therefore excludes every distinct-base
PP fibre for these same-prime images.

Any alternative same-prime fibre must have the same visible prime p.
Let R=(N/p^v)^(1/r), an integer obtained by dividing all other prime
valuations by r. Except for p=2,R=1, the equation R-1=p^d determines
d uniquely, and then e=(v-d)/(r+2),f=e+d. R=2 includes odd-prime
diagonals with d=0. In the exceptional case p=2,R=1, the pair must
be diagonal and e=f=(v-r)/(r+2). Thus it too is unique. Reconstruction
is accepted only after checking positive integer exponents and Phi=N.

This is NOT a claim of global PP injectivity or unrestricted integer
injectivity. Images with no visible prime are unclassified. Other
single-visible images are excluded by the oversize inequality. Known
unrestricted integer collisions from the earlier stage remain valid.

3. Generic bilinear bridge, raw-zeta calibration, and harmonic law

For ANY finitely supported PP inputs a,b, unique complete fibres give

 (V_r K_r(a,b))(m)=sum_{xy=m, admissible} a(x)b(y).

For x!=y the unordered encoded coefficient is a(x)b(y)+a(y)b(x);
for x=y it is a(x)b(x). The formula is a filtered ordinary Dirichlet
product, without the earlier subtraction of every same-prime diagonal.
It extends coefficientwise to formal PP sequences because each output
row has finitely many inputs. It is tested on unrelated signed rational
inputs; it was not defined by fitting the zeta scalar identity.

At r=3, delta_16000-delta_8192 is a nonzero kernel vector: the unique
pairs are (2,8) and (4,4), both with decoded product 16. Also V_r
annihilates delta_1. On raw u(n)=1, it gives one on the admissible
two-prime sector and floor(k/2) on p^k with k nonzero modulo r.
These are known factorization/partition masks, not new primitive counts.

Put kappa_r=K_r(g,g), and v_r=V_r kappa_r. Its nonzero coefficients are

 v_r(p^k)=2 H_(k-1)/k,                  if k is nonzero modulo r;
 v_r(p^e q^f)=2/(ef), p!=q,            if e,f are nonzero modulo r.

The first formula follows from
sum_{e=1}^{k-1} 1/[e(k-e)]=(2/k)sum_{e=1}^{k-1}1/e.
It is a classical Stirling/harmonic logarithm-square law. In particular
v_r agrees with g*_D g through n<2^r and first differs at n=2^r by
-2H_(r-1)/r. For r>Omega(n), every PP factorization of n is admissible,
so the generic filtered product and the zeta coefficients recover the
unfiltered ordinary Dirichlet product at that coefficient.

Six source profiles r=3,4,5,7,11,13 decode through 8192. The source
computes the entire finite K_r(g,g) before selecting image coordinates.
Inputs through 4096 cover every PP pair with xy<=8192. The independent
checker enumerates EVERY possible integer sum S with S^r dividing N,
solves the discriminant of X^2-SX+N/S^r, and then tests PP membership.
All 20,416 complete PP-fibre queries, including 478 same-prime queries,
pass. This is evidence for the implementation, not the universal proof.

4. Local cancellation and the scalar zeta identity

On |x|<1 define
 h(x)=-LOG(1-x), h_r(x)=h(x)+LOG(1-x^r)/r,
 J_r(x)=h(x)^2-(1/r)sum_{j=0}^{r-1} h(omega_r^j x)^2,
 c_r(x)=J_r(x)-h_r(x)^2=sum_{k>=0} c_(r,k)x^k.

The root-of-unity average deletes total degrees divisible by r.
h_r deletes individual logarithm degrees divisible by r. These are
different filters; their difference restores the admissible same-prime
sector while keeping the earlier cross-prime filter.

Let b_k=2H_(k-1)/k (b_0=b_1=0). Both the J_r coefficient and the
h_r^2 coefficient lie in [0,b_k]. Induction gives H_(k-1)<=k/2 for
k>=2: the step uses 1/k<=1/2. Consequently |c_(r,k)|<=1 universally.
For k<r the two local filters coincide, so c_(r,k)=0. At k=r,
the total-degree filter deletes the unfiltered coefficient, but no
individual exponent e<r has yet been deleted. Thus

                   c_(r,r)=-2H_(r-1)/r.

The cancellation-order grammar scores 35 affine programs and the leading
harmonic-coefficient grammar scores 54 programs; their held-out survivors
are respectively degree r and this negative harmonic law. The classical
logarithm-square coefficient is rediscovered, not declared new mathematics.

Write L(s)=the Euler logarithm of zeta(s) on Re(s)>1 and P(s)=sum_p p^-s.
The actual decoded Dirichlet series satisfies, on Re(s)>1,

 U_r(s)=sum_n v_r(n)n^-s
       =[L(s)-L(rs)/r]^2 + R_r(s),
 R_r(s)=sum_p c_r(p^-s)=sum_{k>=r} c_(r,k)P(ks).

The square accounts for distinct-prime products plus the individually
filtered same-prime square. R_r replaces that square by J_r. This
proves the identity by absolutely convergent coefficient rearrangements.

0<=v_r(n)<=2. The prime-power bound uses b_k<=1 and the cross-prime
bound uses 2/(ef)<=2. Thus U_r converges absolutely for Re(s)>1.
At n=2q, q an odd prime, its coefficient is 2; sum_q 1/q diverges by
the prime number theorem. Hence its absolute abscissa is exactly 1,
and it diverges at that endpoint. Below 1 the scalar identity is a
branch-valued continuation, not convergence of the full ordinary series.

By contrast, |c_r(p^-s)|<=p^(-r*sigma)/(1-p^-sigma). On every compact
subset of Re(s)>1/r this is summable and uniform. R_r is therefore
single-valued holomorphic throughout Re(s)>1/r. For r=3 this crosses
the entire critical line and reaches Re(s)>1/3, cancelling the earlier
separate diagonal term's order-two obstruction. R_r is an ADDITIVE
correction here; it is not asserted to be a nonzero multiplicative factor.

5. Actual zero obstruction, quantitative anomaly, and parameter limit

On the half-plane Re(s)>1/r, L(rs) has its single-valued Euler branch.
For a zero rho of zeta of multiplicity m, a local branch of L(s) changes
by 2*pi*i*m around rho. Set G_r=L(s)-L(rs)/r. The decoded continuation
therefore has monodromy

             Delta U_r=4*pi*i*m*G_r - 4*pi^2*m^2.

R_r has no monodromy in this domain. At a selected basepoint the modulus
of this jump is at least 4*pi*m*|Re G_r|, because the constant term is
real and Im(4*pi*i*m*G_r)=4*pi*m*Re G_r. This bound is independent of
the initial LOG zeta(s) sheet. The native and independent Cauchy checks
reuse the earlier certified simple-zero disks near heights 14.134725
and 21.022040, for r=3,4,5,7. All eight jumps have strictly positive
lower bounds. R_r is rigorously bounded on each whole disk. Unlike the
earlier decoder, no assertion that zeta(2s) is nonzero on the disk is
needed for this cancellation. No new zero location is claimed.

Relative to L(s)^2, the monodromy differs by exactly

      Delta U_r-Delta(L^2)=-(4*pi*i*m/r)L(rs).

Its modulus is bounded, uniformly in height at real part sigma>1/r,
by (4*pi*m/r)L(r*sigma), which decays exponentially as r increases.
This estimate compares existing local obstructions. It does not estimate
zeros in an untested region or prove their absence.

More generally let K lie in Re(s)>=sigma>0 and have a fixed holomorphic
branch L on a zero-free, pole-free neighborhood, with M=sup_K |L|.
For r*sigma>1,

 |R_r| <= [zeta(r*sigma)-1]/[1-2^-sigma],
 |L(rs)| <= L(r*sigma) <= zeta(r*sigma)-1,
 |U_r-L^2| <= 2M L(r*sigma)/r + L(r*sigma)^2/r^2
                 + [zeta(r*sigma)-1]/[1-2^-sigma].

The elementary bound zeta(a)-1<=2^-a(1+2/(a-1)), a>1, proves
locally uniform exponential convergence U_r->L^2 on this chosen branch.
The branch/zero-free condition is essential. It must not be promoted
to unconditional continuation through a possible zeta zero.

To remove the known pole at s=1 let F(s)=(s-1)zeta(s), with its removable
value there. The germ

 [LOG F(s)-L(rs)/r]^2+R_r(s)

extends single-valuedly holomorphically to Re(s)>1/2 if and only if
zeta has no zeros there. If F is nonzero, the simply connected half-plane
has a holomorphic logarithm. Conversely, any zero supplies the above
quadratic logarithmic monodromy, which is nonzero as a function of the
basepoint and cannot be cancelled by the holomorphic terms. By the
classical functional-equation symmetry this is an RH-equivalent criterion.
It is a reformulation of the missing global continuation estimate.

6. Sharp weighted absolute and Hilbert operator bounds

Define A_sigma by ||h||=sum |h(n)| n^-sigma, and H_sigma by
||h||^2=sum |h(n)|^2 n^(-2*sigma). Image coordinates for different
decoded rows are disjoint by the unique-fibre theorem. A_tau membership
gives absolute Dirichlet convergence on Re(s)>=tau and holomorphy strictly
to its right. H_tau is a coefficient norm: by Cauchy--Schwarz it gives
Dirichlet holomorphy only on Re(s)>tau+1/2 in general. No Hilbert weight is
silently identified with a pointwise analytic domain. For sigma>0,
the absolute norm of V_r:A_sigma->A_tau is

 sup_{admissible x<=y} [xy(x+y)^r]^sigma/(xy)^tau.

Put m=xy. This ratio equals
m^((r+1)*sigma-tau)(1/x+1/y)^(r*sigma).
Since m>=4 and 1/x+1/y<=1, for tau>=(r+1)*sigma the supremum is
attained at x=y=2 and is EXACTLY 4^((r+1)*sigma-tau). If tau is
smaller, the pairs (2,q), q prime tending to infinity, make it infinite.

For Hilbert spaces, the squared norm is the supremum of squared row
norms. At tau_0=(r+1)*sigma a same-prime row p^k has squared norm

 sum_{e=1}^{floor(k/2)} (p^-e+p^(-(k-e)))^(2*r*sigma), k mod r!=0.

For every sigma>0 this is at most
2^(2*r*sigma)/(2^(2*r*sigma)-1), a convergent geometric bound. Cross-prime
rows have one coordinate. Thus the Hilbert map is bounded iff
tau>=(r+1)*sigma. Necessity uses the same (2,q) rows.

If r*sigma>=1, the Hilbert norm is also EXACTLY
4^((r+1)*sigma-tau). At tau_0, the k=2,p=2 row has norm squared 1.
For k>=3 the first term at exponent 2 is at most (3/4)^2. The remaining
terms are bounded by sum_{e>=2}(2*2^-e)^2=1/3, so the row is at most
43/48<1. Higher exponents only decrease the terms. Cross-prime rows
have reciprocal sum at most 5/6. Increasing tau adds a row factor
m^(-2*(tau-tau_0))<=4^(-2*(tau-tau_0)); the (2,2) row attains equality.
Without r*sigma>=1 the claimed norm-one boundary is FALSE, as the exact
low-weight row controls show.

At sigma=0, H_0->H_tau is bounded iff tau>0. The prime-power rows have
floor(k/2) coordinates; damping by p^(-2*k*tau) bounds them for tau>0,
but their cardinalities are unbounded at tau=0. In particular V_r is
not an unweighted Hilbert partial isometry. A nonzero kernel also excludes
a two-sided inverse. These restrictions concern this operator, not all
possible arithmetic operators or all approaches to RH.

7. Full prime-power encoded endpoint, including a uniform family bound

The earlier prime-only result extends to the FULL source kappa_r, rather
than just its prime-pair projection. Let

 W(X)=sum_{p^e<=X}1/e.

The prime number theorem implies W(X)=O(X/log X): the e=1 term is
pi(X), and the e>=2 contribution is O(sqrt(X)log X). Partial summation
then gives, for each fixed 0<sigma<1,

 sum_{a<=X} g(a)a^-sigma=O_sigma(X^(1-sigma)/log X).

Put sigma_0=2/(r+2), r>=1. On a<=b, a+b>=b; consequently the full
positive scalar sum obeys

 Gamma_r(sigma_0)=sum_{a,b in PP}g(a)g(b)[ab(a+b)^r]^-sigma_0
 <=2 sum_b g(b)b^(-(r+1)*sigma_0) sum_{a<=b}g(a)a^-sigma_0
 <=C sum_b g(b)/(b log b)<infinity.

The last convergence follows by partial summation of W: its integral
is dominated by integral_2^infinity dt/(t log^2 t). Balanced prime pairs
in [X,2X] give a lower contribution of order
X^(2-(r+2)*sigma)/(log X)^2, so every sigma<sigma_0 diverges.
The full encoded absolute abscissa is therefore EXACTLY sigma_0,
with ABSOLUTE CONVERGENCE AT THAT ENDPOINT. No analyticity across this
boundary is claimed. This sharpens the old sufficient strict-half-plane
bound without altering the old numerical certificates.

For r>=3, r*sigma_0>=1. Section 6 therefore gives exact norm 1 for
V_r:H_sigma_0->H_{2(r+1)/(r+2)}, also with absolute norm 1 at those weights.
Moreover

 Phi_r(a,b)^(-2/(r+2))
  =(a+b)^-2 [((a+b)^2)/(ab)]^(2/(r+2)).

The ratio in brackets is at least 4, so each term decreases with r.
Dominated convergence using Gamma_3(2/5)<infinity proves

 Gamma_r(2/(r+2)) decreases to sum_{a,b}g(a)g(b)/(a+b)^2,
 ||kappa_r||_{H_sigma_0}<=||kappa_r||_{A_sigma_0}
                           <=Gamma_3(2/5)<infinity.

This is a uniform finite family estimate, not a numeric evaluation of
Gamma_3(2/5). The bounded output weights are 2(r+1)/(r+2), at least
8/5, and tend to 2; they do not approach the critical line 1/2.

For the earlier pure-power regularization multiplying output by n^(-lambda), at the full endpoint
and target tau=1/2, the boundedness inequality becomes
lambda >= (r+1)*sigma_0-1/2 = (3r+2)/(2(r+2)).
At r=3 equality lambda=11/10 is now permitted by endpoint convergence;
the critical-line obstruction is still shifted to real part at most -3/5.
The old strict inequality was correct using only its earlier open-half-plane
certificate. This endpoint refinement does not eliminate that domain loss.

8. Numerical error certificates and independent references

For a>1 the Mobius representation P(s)=sum_{m>=1}mu(m)LOG zeta(ms)/m
has tail at cutoff M bounded by

 2^(-(M+1)*a)(1+2/((M+1)*a-1))/[(M+1)(1-2^-a)].

Using |c_(r,k)|<=1, truncating the local R series at degree K leaves
tail at most
2^(-(K+1)*sigma)(1+2/((K+1)*sigma-1))/(1-2^-sigma).
Complex error boxes include every Mobius and local-degree tail.
All native logarithms used for P have a>=9/8 and L(9/8)<pi; the
Euler logarithm is therefore the principal value at these arguments.

The source uses K=64,M=24 and the exact decoded prefix through 8192 at
six fresh points with sigma=5/3,2,7/3. The full decoded series tail is
at most 2*N^(1-sigma)/(sigma-1). Perturbing coefficient four by 1/2
is rejected. The independent checker uses coefficients from its own
harmonic/factorization formula through 12288, a prime-local root-of-unity
reference through 384, and smaller perturbation 1/3. Its prime tail is
at most N^(1-r*sigma)/[(r*sigma-1)(1-(N+1)^-sigma)]. All six pass.

Six additional REmainder-only points have sigma=3/8,2/5,5/12. The source
and checker compare the degree/Mobius form with the root-of-unity form,
subtracting c_(r,r)p^(-r*s) before the finite prime sum and restoring
c_(r,r)P(rs) with a separately bounded Mobius computation. The remaining
prime tail is at most

 N^(1-(r+1)*sigma)/[((r+1)*sigma-1)(1-(N+1)^-sigma)].

The source uses prime cutoff 2048, leading M=32; the checker uses 4096,
leading M=48, independent harmonic degree 80 and M=36. Perturbing
coefficient eight by respectively 1/2 and 1/3 is rejected. This certifies
the holomorphic REMAINDER identity below 1/2. It does not claim convergence
of the whole decoded series there or test an RH-equivalent continuation
without a chosen LOG zeta branch.

Eight actual-zero controls use the existing disks and a native R enclosure
over each whole disk. The checker obtains a basepoint modulus upper bound
from a two-coefficient zeta jet and a Taylor/Cauchy remainder at radius
1/48, without reusing the production basepoint zeta value. Real Euler
majorants independently bound L(rs) and R_r on the disks. Six parameter
values also pass limit-majorant controls at different source/checker points.

9. Historical audit, calibration, and what remains open

Unique factorization, valuation residue filters, coordinate aggregation,
root-of-unity filters, Mobius prime-zeta inversion, harmonic/Stirling laws,
the prime number theorem, and logarithmic monodromy at zeros are classical.
Primary references reviewed in this stage include
https://dlmf.nist.gov/26.8.E8 (Stirling logarithm generating function),
https://dlmf.nist.gov/26.8.E15 (two-cycle harmonic law),
https://dlmf.nist.gov/27.12 (prime number theorem), and the previously
reviewed zeta and factorization references in the candidate registry.
Exact-expression searches have not established the publication history of
this particular arithmetic decoder and its combined domain/norm theorem.
Failure to find a matching paper is not proof of originality.

The stage improves the arithmetic sector, the correction's analytic domain,
the parameter-limit comparison, and the full encoded endpoint theorem.
It also retains explicit actual-zeta zero sensitivity. It supplies no bound
that rules out zeros with Re(s)>1/2, no critical-line bounded decoder, and
no universal impossibility theorem for other grammars. The verified-new
operator list remains empty; the research objective remains open.
