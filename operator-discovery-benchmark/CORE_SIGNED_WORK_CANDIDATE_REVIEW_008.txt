4. Unconditional coefficient diagonal: a cubic logarithm is unavoidable
------------------------------------------------------------------------
This section supplies an actual-source estimate not assumed from the
cofinal target. Let t=p^(-u), z=p^(-s). The exact nu0 local coefficients are
 nu0(1)=1, nu0(p)=t-2,
 nu0(p^e)=(1-t)^2*t^(e-2), e>=2.
Therefore their square-coefficient Euler factor is
 C_p(s)=1+(2-t)^2*z+(1-t)^4*z^2/(1-t^2*z).
The complete nonnegative coefficient series factors, for Re(s)>1, as
 C(s)=sum nu0(n)^2*n^(-s)=zeta(s)^4*E(s),
 E(s)=product_p (1-p^(-s))^4*C_p(s).                       (4.1)
Its local factor is1+O(p^(-Re(s)-u)+p^(-2Re(s))). All higher-degree
absolute local tails satisfy the same summable majorant on compact
half-planes Re(s)>2/3. Thus E has a Dirichlet coefficient expansion
E(s)=sum e(n)n^-s absolutely convergent there. At s1 every factor is
positive and the deviations from1 are absolutely summable, so
                         E(1)>0.                         (4.2)
This uses the complete actual-prime local coefficients, not truncation.

An elementary convolution proof gives its unsigned summatory asymptotic.
The repeated divisor sum obeys
 sum_(n<=x)d4(n)=x*(log x)^3/6+O(x*(1+log x)^2).
To see this without a Tauberian assumption, begin with the harmonic-sum
formula for d2 and induct via sum_n D_(k-1)(x/n); monotone integral
comparison of (log(x/t))^j/t gives its next leading coefficient1/(j+1).
Choose any sigma0 in(2/3,1), for example3/4. Complete convolution with
e(n), whose sum |e(n)|n^-sigma0 is finite, is justified absolutely. Expand
(log(x/n))^3, paying all log(n) moments at weight1/n; the tail beyond x
costs O(x^sigma0*(1+log x)^3). Hence
 sum_(n<=x)nu0(n)^2=E(1)*x*(log x)^3/6
                                      +O(x*(1+log x)^2). (4.3)
All constants are finite exact source/Euler constants; none is fitted.

Write f=beta*nu0+r. The other three coefficient modes have modulus<=1,
so |r(n)|<=L=2beta+R_u. Since |nu0(n)|<=d2(n),
 sum_(n<=x)|nu0(n)r(n)|<=L*x*(1+log x),
 sum_(n<=x)r(n)^2<=L^2*x.
Therefore the COMPLETE coefficient square has
 S_(2,0)(x)=sum_(n<=x)f(n)^2=c_0*x*(log x)^3
                                      +O(x*(1+log x)^2),
                      c_0=beta^2*E(1)/6>0.              (4.4)
This is coefficient second moment, not prefix second moment. Its bounded
coefficient companions have been included; their PREFIX correlations
are not thereby small and cannot be discarded.

For critical a=sqrt2 replace nu0 by the multiplicative function

It changes ONLY the prime2 Euler factor. If v_e=nu0(2^e), v_0=1, put

There is no same-prime sector omission or assumption that a delay removes

                       [S2(x)-eta*U2(x)/log x]/x^2 dx.    (5.2)

By(4.4)/(4.5), writing l=log2 and Lx=log X,
 integral_X^(2X)S2(x)/x^2 dx=c_a*l*Lx^3+O(Lx^2),
 integral_X^(2X)U2(x)/(x^2 log x)dx=c_a*l*Lx^2+O(Lx).
Thus for EVERY fixed selected eta,
 D_eta,a(X)=c_a*l*(log X)^3+O((log X)^2),

              =(c_a*l/2)*(log X)^3+O((log X)^2).           (5.3)
In particular eta1 or eta2 cannot eliminate the dominant diagonal by

Consequently separately paying D by O(log^2 X) is analytically FALSE,
even though the complete proposed W step might be true. The selected

Its right-hand side has NEGATIVE cubic leading term

This is a mandatory cancellation frontier. Equivalently the compensated

This is logically equivalent to the original full step, not a solved
