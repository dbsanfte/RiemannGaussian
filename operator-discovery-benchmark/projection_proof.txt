CANONICAL CARRY PROJECTION, OPERATOR INTERACTION, AND ACTUAL ZETA ZEROS

These are written universal proofs with independent exact and Arb checks,
not theorem-prover certificates. Historical originality is unresolved.
The main operator is linear; the logarithmic maps derived from it are
consequences, not additional discoveries created by naming compositions.
An RH-equivalent continuation criterion is not a proof or simplification
of RH. The full commutator's half-plane analytic bridge is still open.

1. DOMAINS AND PRODUCTS

Let f be a rational-valued arithmetic function on positive integers.
delta=delta_1 is the unit, and * denotes ordinary Dirichlet convolution.
Put e_p(n)=v_p(n), Omega(n)=sum_p e_p(n). For integer b>=2 let s_b(k)
be the sum of the base-b digits of k. Define two zero/one kernels:
  W_A,b(i,j)=1[e_p(i)+e_p(j) has no base-b carry for every p],
  W_G,b(i,j)=1[Omega(i)+Omega(j) has no base-b carry].
Their weighted divisor products are called A_b and G_b. At b=2 these
are respectively primewise infinitary convolution A and the global
total-degree product G already investigated by this detector.

For any b, V_b(k)=sum_{r>=1}floor(k/b^r)=(k-s_b(k))/(b-1).
The defect V_b(a+c)-V_b(a)-V_b(c) is nonnegative and zero exactly when
the sum has no base-b carries. The corresponding defect for a product
telescopes on triples. A product of zero/one weights is one exactly when
the total defect is zero; hence both A_b and G_b are associative.
They are commutative, unital, and coefficient-local. For prime b,
V_b(k)=v_b(k!), but primality is not needed for the carry products.

Define
  D_b(n)=sum_p s_b(e_p(n))-s_b(Omega(n)) >=0,
  chi_b(n)=1[D_b(n)=0],
  M_b(f)(n)=chi_b(n)*f(n).
Write M=M_2 and chi=chi_2. All these diagonal maps act on every arithmetic
function. LOG and EXP below act only on f(1)=1 or h(1)=0 respectively.
Only finitely many powers contribute at a fixed n: k nonunit factors
force n>=2^k. Formal LOG/EXP identities thus need no analytic convergence.

2. CANONICAL ALGEBRA INTERTWINER, INCLUDING UNIQUENESS

The basis identity is
  chi_b(ij)*W_A,b(i,j)=chi_b(i)*chi_b(j)*W_G,b(i,j).             (1)

If W_A,b(i,j)=1, primewise digit sums add, and
  D_b(ij)=D_b(i)+D_b(j)
          +s_b(Omega(i))+s_b(Omega(j))-s_b(Omega(ij)).
Each summand is nonnegative. It vanishes exactly when chi_b(i), chi_b(j),
and W_G,b(i,j) all equal one, proving (1) in this case.

If the right side of (1) equals one, then at each digit position all
exponents of i together use fewer than b units, as do those of j, and
their total-degree sum also uses fewer than b. Therefore the sum of all
prime-exponent digit contributions from i and j is less than b at that
position. In particular each individual prime's two exponent digits add
without a carry. Thus W_A,b(i,j)=1. This proves (1) also when its left
kernel is zero. By bilinearity and finite divisor sums,
  M_b(f A_b g)=M_b(f) G_b M_b(g),                              (2)
and M_b is a unital linear algebra homomorphism A_b -> G_b.
Its diagonal mask also gives M_b^2=M_b and M_b M_c=M_c M_b.

Suppose another diagonal A_b -> G_b homomorphism multiplies coefficient n
by m(n) and preserves delta and every prime-power impulse. Express
  delta_n = A_b-product_{p^e||n} delta_(p^e).
There are no same-prime carries in this factorization. Its image must be
the corresponding G_b product. The telescoping total-degree defect is
zero exactly when sum_p s_b(e)=s_b(sum_p e). The image is therefore
chi_b(n)*delta_n, forcing m(n)=chi_b(n) for every n. This proves uniqueness
without restricting m to a zero/one or numerical search grammar.

The domain/codomain in (2) matters. M is not a G -> G endomorphism:
delta_2 G delta_6=delta_12, M(delta_12)=delta_12, while
M(delta_2) G M(delta_6)=0. It is not an ordinary convolution endomorphism
either: M(delta_2*delta_3)=0 but M(delta_2)*M(delta_3)=delta_6.

3. INTEGER PARITY, DIRICHLET INVERSE, AND DIGIT LAYERS

Let
  W(n)=Omega(n)!/product_p(e_p(n)!).
This is the integer number of orderings of the multiset of prime factors.
It is classical (OEIS A008480); its definition is not claimed new here.
For each prime ell, Legendre's formula gives
  v_ell(W(n))=[sum_p s_ell(e_p(n))-s_ell(Omega(n))]/(ell-1).
Hence chi_ell(n)=1[ell does not divide W(n)]. In particular
  chi(n)=W(n) mod 2.                                         (3)

The exact integer identity
  W(ij)*product_p binomial(e_p(i)+e_p(j),e_p(i))
    = W(i)*W(j)*binomial(Omega(i)+Omega(j),Omega(i))
also proves (1) at b=2 by reduction modulo two: each binomial is odd
precisely when its two lower summands add without binary carries. This
parity fact follows directly from (1+x)^(2^r)=1+x^(2^r) in F_2[x].
The independent verifier uses integer factorials/binomials rather than
the production mask's digit-sum implementation.

Let pi(n)=1 for primes and zero otherwise. The formal ordinary inverse
  (delta+pi)^(-1)=sum_{k>=0}(-1)^k*pi^{*k}
has coefficient (-1)^Omega(n)*W(n), since only k=Omega(n) contributes.
Its coefficient parity is therefore chi. This inverse is also established
prior art (OEIS A355939); recovering it is not historical novelty.

For j>=0 let pi_j be the indicator of integers p^(2^j), p prime. In
characteristic two Frobenius identifies pi^{*2^j} with pi_j. The finite
geometric identity for product_j(delta+pi_j) tends coefficientwise to
the inverse of delta+pi. Equivalently, over the ordinary integers,
  chi = ordinary-product_{j>=0}(delta+pi_j).                  (4)
Here coefficients actually are zero or one: at each binary digit j,
choose no prime or exactly one prime. The resulting prime exponents
have pairwise disjoint bit positions; each admissible n occurs uniquely.
The independent verifier also computes (4) directly through n=4096.

4. LOGARITHMIC PROJECTION AND FACTORIZATION OF THE OLD TRANSFERS

For f(1)=1 set
  P_b(f)=EXP_*(M_b(LOG_*(f))),
  T(f)=EXP_A(LOG_*(f)), S(f)=EXP_G(LOG_*(f)).
Since M_b is linear, idempotent, and the M_b commute, ordinary LOG/EXP give
  P_b(f*g)=P_b(f)*P_b(g), P_b^2=P_b, P_b P_c=P_c P_b.           (5)
Each f has the unique ordinary product decomposition
  f=EXP_*(M_b h)*EXP_*((I-M_b)h), h=LOG_*f.
The first factor is fixed by P_b; the second projects to delta. Uniqueness
follows from the direct linear splitting of h into the two mask supports.

Equation (2) sends the coefficient-local A-exponential to the G-exponential.
With b=2 this proves the universal typed factorization
  M(T(f))=EXP_G(M(LOG_*f))=S(P_2(f)).                         (6)
Injectivity of EXP_G shows
  S(f)=M(T(f)) iff M(LOG_*f)=LOG_*f.                         (7)
This class includes every multiplicative f, whose ordinary logarithm is
supported on prime powers, but it is larger. For h=delta_12 the exponential
f=EXP_*h is fixed by P_2 and is nonmultiplicative: f(12)=1, f(4)=f(3)=0.
For h=delta_6 its exponential projects to delta; (7) fails. Thus (6) cannot
be simplified to S=M T on arbitrary normalized inputs. Since LOG_*u has
coefficient 1/e at prime powers and zero elsewhere, P_b(u)=u and (6)
recovers S(u)=M(T(u)). P_b itself has trivial action on the zeta input u;
it is not counted as an independent new zeta operator.

5. PRIME-BASE HIERARCHY AND CLASSICAL MULTIPLICATIVE COMPLETION

For an integer with at least two distinct prime divisors, W(n)>1 and some
prime ell divides it. For 1 and prime powers, W(n)=1. Consequently
  product_{all prime ell} chi_ell(n)=1[n=1 or n is a prime power].
Each coefficient stabilizes after finitely many projections. The commuting
P_ell therefore converge coefficientwise to EXP_*(the prime-power part of
LOG_*f). On a prime-power divisor chain, LOG_*f uses only the same local
coefficients f(p^e). Exponentiating separately over primes gives the limit
  C_mult(f)(n)=product_{p^e||n}f(p^e), with C_mult(f)(1)=1.     (8)
This is classical multiplicative completion, not a claimed new operator.

No fixed finite collection of prime bases implements (8) universally:
choose a prime ell outside the collection and n=2^(ell-1)*3, giving
W(n)=ell. For bases 2,3,5,7 take n=3072=2^10*3. Then W(n)=11, so all four masks
preserve delta_n, while the base-11 mask removes it. The input
EXP_*(delta_3072) survives those four projections and is removed by P_11.
The finite checks through 4096 agree with (8) after bases through 11,
but the universal claim needs all prime bases and the proof above.

6. NONCOMMUTING TRANSFORMS AND THE FIRST MIXED-PRIME COEFFICIENT

Let D(f)=T(S(f))-S(T(f)); subtraction is coefficientwise. At prime powers,
all divisor indices are powers of one prime, and A and G coincide. Since
both transfers use only divisors, their compositions agree at these indices.
At a squarefree index, all local exponents are 0 or 1, so A and ordinary
convolution agree throughout its divisor subalgebra. T(f) fixes every
squarefree coefficient for all f; applying the same fact to S(f) and using
divisor-locality of S proves D(f)=0 at squarefree indices too.

For distinct primes p,q abbreviate
  a=f(p), b=f(q), c=f(p^2), d=f(p*q), e=f(p^2*q).
Ordinary logarithmic coefficients on this six-element divisor grid are
  h(p)=a, h(q)=b, h(p^2)=c-a^2/2, h(p*q)=d-a*b,
  h(p^2*q)=e-a*d-b*c+a^2*b.
Enumerating allowed A/G exponential terms gives
  T(f)(p^2)=S(f)(p^2)=c-a^2/2,
  T(f)(p*q)=d, S(f)(p*q)=d-a*b,
  T(f)(p^2*q)=e-a*d+a^2*b/2,
  S(f)(p^2*q)=e-a^2*b/2.
The prime coefficients remain a,b. Substitution in these same formulas gives
  T(S(f))(p^2*q)=e-a*d+a^2*b,
  S(T(f))(p^2*q)=e-a*d,
  D(f)(p^2*q)=f(p)^2*f(q).                                  (9)
An independent Sympy computation using generic formal products proves the
symbolic identity, including cancellation at every other grid divisor.
For u(n)=1 the first discrepancy is n=12: T S u(12)=1, S T u(12)=0.
The detector learns the cubic from eight rational-input examples and
retains it on eight fresh inputs/prime pairs; the universal proof is (9).

A Dirichlet series for the entire commutator has a proved initial domain
Re(s)>=2: T(u) and S(u) have coefficients in [0,1], hence their nonunit
weighted l1 norms are <=zeta(2)-1<1. The ordinary logarithm is absolutely
summable, and the A/G exponentials are bounded by exp(||LOG_*f||).
This proves the composed series converge there. It does not prove an
analytic bridge for the commutator throughout Re(s)>1/2. This operator
interaction is recorded as a verified formal consequence with that limit.

7. NONTRIVIAL RAW ZETA ACTION OF THE LINEAR PROJECTION

For u(n)=1, M(u)=chi. Equation (4) and absolute convergence give
  F_chi(s)=sum_n chi(n)*n^(-s)
          =product_{j>=0}(1+P(2^j*s)), Re(s)>1,               (10)
where P(z)=sum_p p^(-z). Define, on H={Re(s)>1/2},
  C(s)=product_{j>=1}(1+P(2^j*s)),
  Q(s)=sum_{k>=2}P(k*s)/k.
Both converge locally uniformly and are holomorphic on H: their first
arguments have real part >1 and the higher arguments grow without bound.
C is not identically zero (it is >=1 on real s>1/2). It is nonzero for
Re(s)>1, since |P(2^j*s)|<=P(2)<sum_{n>=2}n^(-2)<1, and the product's
deviations from one are summable. Nonvanishing on all H is not asserted.

The normalized Euler logarithm gives P(s)=log(zeta(s))-Q(s) on Re(s)>1.
Thus the second independent representation is
  F_chi(s)=C(s)*log(zeta(s))+C(s)*(1-Q(s)).                  (11)
This is a logarithmic connection to zeta, not preservation of its zeros.

Let Z(s)=(s-1)*zeta(s), including its removable value Z(1)=1. Pole cancellation
gives the initially defined function
  Phi_chi=F_chi+C*log(s-1)=C*log(Z)+C*(1-Q).                 (12)
RH implies Z is zero-free on simply connected H, so its normalized logarithm
is holomorphic there and (12) continues single-valuedly and holomorphically.
Conversely a zero rho in H of finite positive order m changes (12) around
a small loop by 2*pi*i*m*C(basepoint). Since C is not identically zero,
one can choose a basepoint with C!=0. Even C(rho)=0 cannot remove the
monodromy on its neighborhood. Such a zero obstructs the required extension.
Functional reflection and the known critical-strip location of nontrivial
zeros identify absence of zeros in H with RH. Therefore
  RH iff Phi_chi has a single-valued holomorphic extension to H.           (13)
The global continuation in (13) remains unproved; finite tests do not
decide it. This is a reformulation with an explicit arithmetic input.

8. CERTIFIED ACTUAL-ZETA COMPARISONS AND INFINITE TAILS

For real a>1 put D(a)=2^(-a)+2^(1-a)/(a-1). The integral bound gives
  |P(z)|<=D(Re(z)).
For sigma=Re(s)>0 with a=2^(J+1)*sigma>1, omitted binary layers satisfy
  B_J=sum_{j>J}|P(2^j*s)|<=D(a)/(1-2^(-sigma)).
Indeed 2^(J+1+r)*sigma>=a+r*sigma, and replacing its integral denominator
by a-1 bounds the summands by D(a)*2^(-r*sigma). If C_J is the finite
product, its omitted-product error is at most
  |C-C_J|<=|C_J|*(exp(B_J)-1).                               (14)
The Q tail after k=K is bounded similarly by
  tau_Q<=D((K+1)*sigma)/[(K+1)*(1-2^(-sigma))].
For chi coefficients in {0,1}, the arithmetic Dirichlet tail after N is
  tau_DS<=N^(1-sigma)/(sigma-1), sigma>1.

The prime sums in the independent analytic route use actual Arb zeta
values and Mobius inversion, not the searched arithmetic coefficients:
  P(z)=sum_{m>=1}mu(m)*log(zeta(m*z))/m.
The infinite tail beyond m=M, with a=M+1 and t=Re(z)>1, is bounded by
  [(1+2/(a*t-1))*2^(-a*t)]
  /[a*(1-2^(-a*t))*(1-2^(-t))].
This follows from |log zeta(m*z)|<=D(m*t)/(1-2^(-m*t)) and a geometric
majorant. On the initial tested domain log(zeta(t))<pi, ensuring the
principal complex logarithm equals the normalized Euler branch. Every
finite prime-zeta value includes a complex error box for its omitted tail.

If epsilon_C denotes (14), finite product reference (1+P)*C_J has error
<=|1+P|*epsilon_C, while finite affine reference C_J*(log zeta+1-Q_K)
has error <=|C_J|*tau_Q+epsilon_C*(|log zeta+1-Q_K|+tau_Q).
Ball arithmetic retains finite-rounding and prime-zeta errors separately.
Adding tau_DS bounds each independent numerical comparison.
projection_analytic_probe.py tests six fresh points with N=512, J=8, K=64.
At each point both representations pass these proved error bounds. Adding
1/7 to the coefficient at n=6 is rejected by each representation using
the very same bounds; tolerances are not fitted to the observed errors.

9. A CERTIFIED ACTUAL ZERO AND ITS LOGARITHMIC OBSTRUCTION

Take c=1/2+14.13472514173469379046*i and r=1/10000. Let R be the rectangle
with both component radii r. Arb Taylor coefficients at c and over R give
  |zeta(s)-zeta'(c)*(s-c)|
    <=|zeta(c)|+sup_R|zeta''/2|*r^2
    < r*|zeta'(c)| on |s-c|=r.
Taylor's remainder estimate follows by integration along the segment in R.
Rouche therefore counts exactly one zero, with multiplicity one, in the
disk. The reflection s->1-conjugate(s) preserves the disk; the functional
equation forces its unique zero onto the critical line. No global rank
(such as being the first zero) is inferred from this local certificate.

To test (12) on the WHOLE disk, C must continue through its small portion
with Re(s)<1/2. For j>=2 its prime sums still have real argument >1.9996.
For j=1 the variable w=2s has real part at least 0.9998, so an initial
Euler-domain principal-branch assumption would be invalid. Instead ten
adjacent Arb rectangles cover
  0.9998<=Re(w)<=1.5,
  |Im(w)-28.26945028346938758092|<=0.0002.
Each image has Re(zeta(w))>0. The principal log throughout this corridor
is thus holomorphic and connected to the normalized Euler logarithm at
Re(w)=1.5, where log(zeta(1.5))<pi is independently certified.

Möbius inversion with that anchored m=1 logarithm, normalized m>=2 logs,
and the same convergent tail continues P(w) holomorphically through the
disk. Products through j=8 and the tail (14) enclose the local continuation
of C there. Its whole-rectangle modulus lower bound after the infinite
tail is positive. This proves C holomorphic and nonzero on the whole disk.
Q need not be continued to this entire disk to test logarithmic monodromy:
every term P(k*s) for k>=2 has a holomorphic local continuation there by
the same k=2 corridor or, for k>=3, its absolutely convergent prime series.
Their tail is locally uniform. Thus C*(1-Q) is also single-valued locally.

The actual Z has winding one by the certified simple zero; therefore
Phi_chi has monodromy 2*pi*i*C(basepoint), whose modulus is bounded below
by 2*pi times the certified whole-disk C lower bound. A 129-point phase
mesh agrees but supplies no proof beyond the Arb/analytic certificate.
The circle crosses Re(s)=1/2, so this branching is fully compatible with
RH. It tests sensitivity to a real zeta zero, not an artificial model or
a statement about zeros elsewhere in the critical strip.

10. SEARCH, INDEPENDENT VERIFICATION, AND PUBLICATION HISTORY

The learner enumerates 10,260 two-feature rational/digital programs and
accepts the basis equation m(ij)*W_A=m(i)*m(j)*W_G plus m(1)=m(p^e)=1.
No target mask labels are supplied. Twelve programs fit the 119 training
basis pairs through 32. Through 4096, nine fail and three equivalent
expressions for the same unique mask survive all 34,720 basis pairs.
These three syntactic survivors count as one operator.

check_projection.py independently uses integer multinomial parity,
binomial parity, prime-indicator inverse recursion, ordinary digit-layer
products, triangular Omega-derivation LOG/EXP recurrences, and symbolic
commutator algebra. It reconstructs all held-out decisions/counterexamples,
generic rational-input laws, prime-base hierarchy and the cubic law.
This supports the universal proofs rather than replacing them with
finite tests or counting shared production functions as independence.

The mask has known multinomial-parity ingredients and a known inverse
sequence. Its particular canonical A->G characterization, typed nonlinear
factorization, full prime-base projection hierarchy, cubic transfer
interaction, and raw-zeta product must be compared with further primary
literature. No missing search hit certifies originality. The exact verified
historically-new list remains empty until publication history is resolved.
See novelty_audit.txt; numerical and exact evidence is in projection-results/.

11. CERTIFIED CREATED ZERO: M DOES NOT PRESERVE ZETA'S ZERO SET

A diagnostic scan of 1+P(s) at Re(s)=1.1, 0<=Im(s)<=2000, followed by
Newton iteration suggests a root near
  c=1.153396876020314+14.016611700317199*i.
These diagnostics alone establish no zero. A fresh certificate uses radius
r=1/1000000, the same enclosing rectangle, and the finite analytic function
  F_64(s)=1+sum_{m=1..64}mu(m)*log(zeta(m*s))/m.
For minimum sigma=Re(c)-r>1, log(zeta(sigma))<pi proves all normalized
logarithms agree with their principal branches on the disk. The omitted
Mobius sum has the explicit uniform bound tau from section 8. Arb Taylor
coefficients at c and over the rectangle give on the circle
  |(1+P(s))-F_64'(c)*(s-c)|
    <=|F_64(c)|+sup_rectangle|F_64''/2|*r^2+tau
    <r*|F_64'(c)|.
Rouche therefore proves exactly one simple zero of 1+P in the disk,
including the infinite Mobius tail. C is nonzero throughout Re(s)>1 by
section 7; a separate finite-product plus infinite-layer enclosure confirms
a positive modulus lower bound on this same disk. Thus F_chi=C*(1+P)
has exactly one simple zero there too.

An independent actual-zeta Arb enclosure on the entire rectangle excludes
zero. Hence this is a rigorously certified counterexample to the proposed
automatic identity of zero sets for M(u) and zeta, in their original
convergence half-plane. It is a created zero of the transformed function,
not a zeta zero or an RH counterexample. The valid RH criterion is the
single-valued logarithmic continuation in section 7; it does not say that
the zeros of F_chi are the zeros of zeta.

check_projection_analytic.py independently repeats this zero count using
M=32 and a Cauchy estimate on the larger disk of radius R=1/100 around c.
For the finite normalized function F_32, an Arb rectangle encloses its
modulus on the larger circle by M_R. Cauchy's coefficient bounds imply
  |F_32(s)-F_32(c)-F_32'(c)*(s-c)|
     <=M_R*(r/R)^2/(1-r/R), |s-c|=r.
Adding its own integral/geometric Mobius tail and the constant gives a
second strict Rouche inequality. The independent tail uses the sharper
elementary |log zeta(m*s)|<=zeta(m*sigma)-1<=D(m*sigma), hence
  tail<=D((M+1)*sigma)/[(M+1)*(1-2^(-sigma))].
A deliberately incorrect logarithm shifted by 2*pi*i fails this same
certificate. The checker also recomputes every critical-zero branch
corridor, C product and actual-zero count with different prime-sum and
product cutoffs, importing no production helper. FLINT/Arb is the shared
trusted numerical kernel; independence does not mean an independent
implementation of the special-function library itself.
