1. Actual source, exact centers, and paid same-prime recovery

  p(n)=beta*(B(n)-U(n)-V(n)+delta(n)),
  a(1)=a(q^e)=0,
and a=p at all other n. No higher-prime-power or nonsquarefree term is
removed from U,V,B or from the off-prime-power a source.

At prime q set t=q^(-u), so q^(-v)=t^2. The nonunit local U,V rows are
  U(q^e)=-(1-t)*t^(e-1),
  V(q^e)=-(1-t^2)*t^(2e-2), e>=1.                    (1.1)

The same-prime p channel is their nonunit convolution multiplied by beta.
Every d(q^e)>=0, it vanishes for e<2, and
  sum_{e>=2} d(q^e)=beta.                            (1.2)

  0<=D(x):=sum_{n<=x}d(n)<=beta*pi(sqrt(x))<=beta*sqrt(x).
In particular
  X^(-2)*sum_{X<=n<2X}|D(n)|^2<=2beta^2.             (1.3)
This elementary cofinal payment is sufficient; no unproved prime estimate
is being used. It is not, by itself, a finite critical-integral bound.

2. All-valuation four-color transfer and actual parity
------------------------------------------------------
Use colors hu,hv,ku,kv, with the first two on the h leg and the last two
on k. At a processed prime q let alpha_i>=0 be the exponent assigned
to each color. A zero exponent has local weight one; a positive exponent
has the U or V weight (1.1). Thus the exact local coefficient is
  c_q(alpha)=prod_i ell_i(alpha_i),
  sign c_q(alpha)=(-1)^number_of_occupied_local_colors.          (2.1)
Its leg exponents are
  A_q=alpha_hu+alpha_hv, B_q=alpha_ku+alpha_kv.
The actual indices become q^(A_q)H and q^(B_q)K. This includes primes
shared between both legs, between both colors of one leg, and every
higher valuation. No condition gcd(H,K)=1 appears.

For a subsequent increment in color i, its exact local ratio is
  alpha_i=0: -(1-t_i), activating that local color;
  alpha_i>=1: t_i, preserving its occupancy and sign,
where t_i=t for u and t_i=t^2 for v. A repeated exponent is therefore
not another squarefree parity flip. Two colors occupied at one prime
give positive sign even when the product valuation is two; one color
with exponent two still gives negative sign. The parity rule follows
the actual color factors, not mu of a nonsquarefree product (which is zero).

Here is a complete rational transfer. Let E_i be commuting idempotent
marks: E_i takes a global occupied mask S to S union {i}, and E_i^2=E_i.
Let z_h,z_k track the two leg exponents. Then the new-prime operator is
  T_q(z_h,z_k)=prod_i [1-E_i*(1-t_i)*z_leg(i)
                                             /(1-t_i*z_leg(i))].       (2.2)
Expanding it assigns every alpha exactly once, with weight (2.1), and
updates S to S union supp(alpha). This is an exact 16-state union-mask
matrix with rational exponent-generating entries. A q is new relative
to the processed base, but ALL its valuations are admitted at that step.
If a language instead revisits the same q, global masks alone do not
say whether q already occupied a color. Its local occupied subset and
valuation-dependent coordinate shifts must then be recorded explicitly.

Birth is the part supp(alpha) not already in S; global occupancy and
local q occupancy are different. A color occupied at an earlier prime
still receives its FIRST local negative q coefficient when this q is
adjoined. Confusing that coefficient with a positive repeated-q ratio
would change the arithmetic source.

For exact finite a admission, occupied colors alone do not recognize
whether a leg is supported on just ONE distinct prime. Add a per-leg
prime-support count truncated to 0,1,>=2, or retain the full p/d recovery
in section 1. Counts increase once per processed q on that leg, however
many local colors/exponents q occupies. The count-one final contribution
is d and must be subtracted; all units and prime-power zero conventions
are then correct. Paying d cofinally is legitimate for a theorem
reduction, but an artifact purporting to equal a pointwise must still
perform that exact subtraction or use the count state.

4. Complete repeated-valuation chain and a paid bulk/exit reduction

The defect is ONLY at e=0: for e>=1 and H>1, q^e H is not a prime

All three series in (4.1) vanish at z=1. Their local absolute masses

For e>=3 the chain obeys the exact two-root recurrence

5. A genuinely paid full repeated-valuation region
--------------------------------------------------
There is a nontrivial complete repeated-prime sector which CAN be peeled
without any signed-correlation hypothesis. Fix an integer j>=2 and a
cutoff exponent rho>0. At integer n select ALL pairs h,k<=n for which
some prime q>n^rho satisfies q^j|h AND q^j|k. This selection retains every
higher valuation and every remaining common factor. It is not limited
to coprime bases or particular color assignments.

For the actual full a coefficients,
  |a(q^j H)|<=beta*d2(q^j H)<=beta*(j+1)*d2(H),       (5.1)
even if q divides H. Sum both bases through n/q^j and enlarge primes
to integers. The absolute pointwise selected quadratic sum is at most
  beta^2*(j+1)^2*n^2*(1+log n)^2
                         *sum_{integer q>n^rho}q^(-2j)
  <=C_j*n^(2-(2j-1)rho)*(1+log n)^2,
  C_j=beta^2*(j+1)^2*(1+1/(2j-1)).                  (5.2)
The divisor-sum estimate is used only on nonempty ranges >=1.
Repeatedly counted pairs are safe in this ABSOLUTE upper bound. An
exact subtraction uses the union indicator once, or a canonical
qualifying q; it must not subtract overlapping q sectors repeatedly.

Therefore the FULL normalized selected quadratic contribution obeys
  C_j*2^max(2-(2j-1)rho,0)*X^(1-(2j-1)rho)
                                           *(1+log(2X))^2.     (5.3)
At rho=1/(2j-1) it is cofinal padding. At larger rho it decays until
rho>=1/j, where the strict prime cutoff makes the sector empty.
In particular j=2,rho=1/3 gives the explicit cost
  24beta^2*(1+log(2X))^2.                            (5.4)
This rho is a NEW shared-valuation cutoff; it is not the bank theta=3/4.
The j=2 region is nonempty in principle between n^(1/3) and sqrt(n).
It can contain products of size comparable to n^2 and primitive heights
of positive-power size; it is not merely the previously paid low-product
or fixed/polylog primitive-height peel.

Its exact moving entry/exit includes n<q^(1/rho) and q^j|h,k, together
with h,k<=n. For rational rho use the corresponding exact integer
threshold. This full quadratic component need not be positive energy,
but its absolute payment is valid in the complete centered-square
decomposition (1.5). The main cross terms remain; no positive storage
or whole source bound is inferred from (5.3).

One should NOT generalize this to a free q^(-u) factor for all repeated
source valuations. B(q^2) tends to one as q grows because both local
colors can be occupied once. Hence a(q^2 H) can have order-one amplitude
for a fixed nonunit H. The saving in (5.3) is a full shared-divisibility

6. Rational finite differences, complete boundaries and inverse cost
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Another useful exact kernel form comes from the numerators in (4.1).
For q-free H,K let T_h W shift H to qH, and T_k W shift K to qK.
On one color the source transfer is
  (I-T_leg)/(I-t_i T_leg);
on the B leg it is
  (I-T_leg)^2/[(I-t T_leg)(I-t^2 T_leg)].             (6.1)
The geometric denominators have nonnegative coefficients and convergent
finite-q masses. Thus complete parity can be moved to FIRST or SECOND
prime-scale differences of the ACTUAL kernel, with all the exponent
and lower-color/unit terms in (2.2) retained. It is not necessary to
pretend the full source is squarefree to perform this transformation.

For example the mixed first difference is exactly
  (I-T_h)(I-T_k)W_X(H,K)
   =sum_{X<=n<2X}[1_{H<=n}-1_{qH<=n}]
                       *[1_{K<=n}-1_{qK<=n}]>=0.               (6.2)
It counts SIMULTANEOUS exits. A second difference on the h indicator is
  1_{H<=n}-2*1_{qH<=n}+1_{q^2H<=n}
  =-1 on n/q^2<H<=n/q,
   +1 on n/q<H<=n,
    0 elsewhere.                                      (6.3)
The joint second differences retain all four annular quadrants. Their
fresh/fresh quadrant can have both source coordinates near n. This
frontier is not a smaller-scale forcing merely because another quadrant
has smaller indices. Deleting it would reinstate the old past-only flaw.

There is an explicit exact inverse in the idempotent occupancy algebra.
For one color,
  [1-E_i*(1-t_i)z/(1-t_i z)]^(-1)
      =1+E_i*(1-t_i)z/(1-z).                         (6.4)
All positive inverse exponents have coefficient 1-t_i, with NO geometric
decay. The four-color inverse is the product of these four factors;
this records every repeated valuation and its recovery cost.

To quantify a LEG prefix cost, for sigma>0 define
  ||F||_(sigma,T)^2=int_1^T |F(x)|^2 x^(-2sigma-1) dx,
with the actual step extension F(x)=F(floor x), zero below one.
Prime dilation F(x/q^e) has norm at most q^(-sigma e)||F||.
For a finite occupancy vector use the sum of its scalar prefix norms;
union operators E_i have norm at most one by the triangle inequality.
The per-color absolute inverse bound is then
  1+(1-t_i)/(q^sigma-1),
and the TWO-color per-leg bound is
  K_sigma(q)=[1+(1-t)/(q^sigma-1)]
                               *[1+(1-t^2)/(q^sigma-1)].       (6.5)
For a finite processed prime set the honest bound is product_q K_sigma(q).
At sigma=1/2 this infinite absolute product diverges: its factors are
1+O(q^(-1/2)) with a positive comparable leading term, and the prime
sum q^(-1/2) diverges (already sum_q 1/q diverges by the elementary
Euler/harmonic argument). To recall that argument, convergence of
sum_q 1/q would bound all products prod_{q<=Y}(1-1/q)^(-1), whereas
each product contains the entire harmonic sum through Y in its positive
Euler expansion. The harmonic sums are unbounded. Thus this does not
supply a uniform all-prime critical-norm recovery certificate.
Divergence is not a proof that
signed source-specific recovery or a cofinal finite-horizon estimate
is impossible. The finite product, any horizon growth and all companion
costs must be recorded rather than treated as one bounded operator.

The two-leg rational algebra is exact, but its product-state norm is
not automatically the diagonal n-prefix moment in (1.5). A coupled
prefix-vector argument, with the same centers and all mixed entries,
must supply that step. Subtracting real pole functions also changes
the below-one and unit forcing: (6.1)-(6.5) act on uncentered actual
prefixes, not on pole profiles silently set to zero. The centering
feedback in the new input explicitly keeps those deterministic terms.
