ORDERED PRIME PAIRING: EXACT STENCILS, PAID AUXILIARIES, AND RECOVERY GAPS

Scope and conclusion
--------------------
This is a new source-only analytical review of PRIME_BANK_HANDOFF.txt and
candidate10_ordered_prime_covariance_obligation.json. The older covariance
memo and all frozen files remain unchanged. I also read the earlier prime-pair
forcing handoff/proof/certificate review, and the current producer and independent
checker for prime-slide stencil discovery. No arithmetic study, response
calculation, numerical experiment, test, hash, or parameter search was performed.

There is an actual proved new auxiliary estimate: exact same-prime power
stencils with a sufficiently high local zero have a cofinal bound on the FULL
ordered smooth/bank kernel, including the common B channel. A cumulative
prime-power stencil gives an explicit example. This is stronger than discovering
a local commutator identity, but it is not a bound for the original covariance:
its complete lifted outside-domain terms remain unestimated, and quantitative
inversion loses the same prime powers that supplied suppression.

Distinct-prime slides have the expected multiplicative small factor in the
u/v tensor component. Repeated use of the same prime has a different, order-one
defect. The common B/u and B/v components have no such small slide factor.
The cofactor-diagonal and fixed/polylog primitive-cofactor portions of the
repeated-prime defect can be paid. The remaining large primitive sector, all
outside-domain terms and the full smooth/bank/main covariance stay open.
No closed all-scale recurrence, complete signed moment, or RH is proved.

1. Source channels and original ordered support
-----------------------------------------------
Use actual mu, u=1/3, v=2/3, beta=9/2, and
  a_b=mu*(n^(-b)), B=a_u*a_v,
  p=((a_u-delta)/u)*((a_v-delta)/v), a=p-d,
where d is the COMPLETE same-prime prime-power overlap. For n>1,
  p(n)=beta*[B(n)-a_u(n)-a_v(n)].
The unit correction is separate: p(1)=0. Every a(p^e)=0, including the
unit, because p(p^e)=d(p^e). At other n>1, a=p. Thus the smooth coefficient
a(h) has no prime-power support, although its tensor-channel expansion
uses channels which do have such support. This mask must survive expansion.

For cofactor k>=2,
  z_0=beta*(2B-a_u-a_v),
  z_u=beta*(B-a_u), z_v=beta*(B-a_v).
All transfer sequences are zero at k=1. Let theta>=1/2, theta<1, and define
  tau_theta(q)=ceil(q^(1/theta)),
  alpha_theta(h)=max(h,tau_theta(Pplus(h))).
The exact ordered overlap count on an integer dyadic block [X,2X) is
  I(h;q,k)=max(0,min(2X,tau_theta(q))
                            -max(X,alpha_theta(h),q*k)),        (1.1)
with h>=2, k>=2 and q prime. The high-smooth variant also puts h^2 in
the upper minimum, because h>sqrt(n) means n<h^2 strictly. Rational powers
are tested by exact integer comparisons, not floating endpoint rounding.

A positive count supplies an actual integer n with
  Pplus(h)<=n^theta<q, k<=n/q<n^(1-theta)<=n^theta<q.
Consequently q is coprime to BOTH h and k. It gives no condition on
gcd(h,k). All shared-prime/valuation sectors of h,k remain. For the
high-smooth kernel, k<sqrt(n)<h and qh>n. Every q-adjunction to h therefore
leaves the original pointwise coefficient prefix. An outside source value
may appear in a valid exact identity; its support cannot then be truncated.

The complete target remains
  R_new=K-L_0+L_u-m_u*x^(2/3),
with the previously paid L_v and low-smooth components. Every bank-bank,
smooth-smooth and main covariance remains alongside the ordered cross.
Bounding a new auxiliary is progress only after its distinct scope and
its unresolved recovery terms are stated.

2. Exact prime-adjunction calculus
----------------------------------
Set t=q^(-1/3). The channels S=(B,a_u,a_v) have local prime multipliers
  lambda_B=t+t^2-2,
  lambda_u=t-1,
  lambda_v=t^2-1.                                (2.1)
For q coprime to the relevant base integer, S_i(qh)=lambda_i(q)S_i(h).
For a tensor F_ij(h,k)=S_i(h)S_j(k), define T_q^h and T_q^k to adjoin q
to the indicated coordinate. On the domain q coprime to h*k,
  (T_q^h-T_q^k)F_ij=(lambda_i-lambda_j)F_ij.       (2.2)
In particular
  (T_q^h-T_q^k)(a_u(h)a_v(k))
     =(t-t^2)a_u(h)a_v(k).
For distinct primes q,r, both coprime to h*k, the full four-term double
slide is exactly
  S_i(qrh)S_j(k)-S_i(qh)S_j(rk)
        -S_i(rh)S_j(qk)+S_i(h)S_j(qrk)
    =(lambda_i(q)-lambda_j(q))
       *(lambda_i(r)-lambda_j(r))*S_i(h)S_j(k).   (2.3)
This retains every lifted term. For u/v its multiplier is
(q^(-u)-q^(-v))(r^(-u)-r^(-v)), bounded by q^(-u)r^(-u).

The common channels behave differently:
  lambda_B-lambda_u=lambda_v,
  lambda_B-lambda_v=lambda_u.                     (2.4)
Their factors tend to -1 rather than zero. The original a(h)z_0(k)
projection contains nonzero B/u and B/v tensor coefficients. Hence a
u/v-only commutator cannot control the whole common bank. Same-channel
distinct-prime slides vanish exactly, but eliminating those tensors is
not domination of the original quadratic projection either.

3. The repeated-prime exception is not a squared prime eigenvalue
----------------------------------------------------------------
For a_b at a fresh base,
  a_b(q^2)-a_b(q)^2=a_b(q).
For B,
  B(q^2)-B(q)^2=lambda_B-lambda_u*lambda_v.
Write these three defects as
  delta_u=lambda_u, delta_v=lambda_v,
  delta_B=lambda_B-lambda_u*lambda_v.             (3.1)
The exact repeated-prime double slide is therefore
  (T_q^h-T_q^k)^2 F_ij
    =[(lambda_i-lambda_j)^2+delta_i+delta_j]F_ij.  (3.2)
For the u/v tensor its multiplier is
  (t-t^2)^2+(t-1)+(t^2-1),
which tends to -2 as q grows. It is NOT a q^(-2/3) term. Even for i=j,
the true expression is 2*delta_i, not zero: after the first adjunction,
the base coordinate is divisible by q, so (2.2) cannot be reapplied with
its original coprime guard. Prime-power rows, not powers of a prime row,
are required. Uniformly |delta_u|,|delta_v|<=1 and |delta_B|<=3, so
|delta_i+delta_j|<=6.

If r is chosen as Pplus(h), it is already a source factor. One must
write h=r^e H and k=r^f K, with r coprime to H*K, and use the actual
rows S_i(r^(e+a))S_j(r^(f+b)). Neither f=0 nor e=1 may be assumed.
This includes the h/k shared-prime sector and all higher powers. A
symbolic grammar limited to fresh primes cannot silently use r=Pplus(h).

4. Ordered swaps do not cancel within the original support
----------------------------------------------------------
On high-smooth support, exchanging h and k makes the reversed bank
cofactor h: qh>n, so the corresponding I(k;q,h) is zero at that n.
Similarly replacing a smooth largest prime r=Pplus(h) by its bank prime
q>r reverses the ordering. A would-be bank at r cannot be above the new
largest smooth prime q. Thus the reversed term is in a complement, not
in a second interior signed pair of equal weight.

A source antisymmetry and a kernel antisymmetry must both be accounted
for. Writing a tensor as symmetric and antisymmetric parts under h,k
exchange, and the kernel as its symmetric and antisymmetric parts, gives
an exact decomposition. On this ordered domain the kernel's antisymmetric
part is nonzero; its source part is not integrated against an automatically
canceling symmetric domain. All complements remain.

There are useful local signed identities, but they do not change this
support fact. For q>r both coprime to k,
  a_u(qk)a_v(rk)-a_u(rk)a_v(qk)
    =(1-q^(-u))(1-r^(-u))(r^(-u)-q^(-u))
                                  *a_u(k)a_v(k)>=0.             (4.1)
The last product is positive since both channels have the same radical
Mobius sign at k. This is an exact ordered-prime determinant with a
common cofactor; it is not a sign assertion for h!=k or for the full
common B tensors. Removing its extra factors to infer positivity of an
unrelated bilinear target would repeat the invalid cycle reasoning warned
against in the earlier prime-pair certificate review.

5. Counting independent prime labels correctly
----------------------------------------------
The bank-bank intersection count has two independently summed prime
labels q,r. Positive support implies both are greater than n^theta and
both are coprime to both cofactors. For theta>=1/2, qr>n (strictly also
at the endpoint). The lifted terms in (2.3) are generally outside the
original coefficient prefix.

The distinct u/v slide gain has ONE 1/3 power for each prime. It is not
a single-prime 2/3 bank. For example its absolute pointwise bank-bank
sum is bounded by
  [n*(1+log n)]^2
       *[sum_(q>n^theta)q^(-1-u)]^2,
which yields normalized block exponent 1-2u*theta. This is positive for
every theta<1. The Chebyshev input can add prime-count logarithmic savings
but does not change that power. Fixed cofactor columns already explain
why a power gain on two independent prime labels must not be collapsed
into the old one-branch threshold.

More generally, positive weights b,c on the TWO free labels give power
  1-theta*(b+c)
for the complete bank-bank auxiliary, while weight b on the ONE free
prime of a smooth-bank auxiliary gives 1-theta*b. The geometric domains
are respectively h<=n/q,k<=n/r and h<=n,k<=n/q. This distinction is
explicit in the current producer and independent checker.

6. A paid part of the repeated-prime defect, with gcd overlap retained
---------------------------------------------------------------------
The order-one defect in (3.2) is not all paid by a small prime weight.
Nevertheless its low primitive-cofactor sector IS cofinal padding. This
is a candidate10 tensor/kernel specialization of the earlier primitive-
height counting idea, not a new global cancellation theorem.

At integer n write h=gH,k=gK, gcd(H,K)=1. Retain every g, including
overlap of g with H or K. Let the primitive height max(H,K)<=B, B>=1.
For any channels i,j,
  |S_i(gH)S_j(gK)|
    <=d_2(gH)d_2(gK)
    <=d_2(g)^2*d_2(H)d_2(K)
    <=d_4(g)*d_2(H)d_2(K).
Thus, for q>n^theta and h,k<=n/q, the full g sum is at most
  [n/(q*max(H,K))]*(1+(1-theta)log n)^3
                                      *d_2(H)d_2(K).
Dropping only coprimality in an UPPER bound,
  sum_(H,K<=B)d_2(H)d_2(K)/max(H,K)
    <=[sum_(H<=B)d_2(H)/H]*[sum_(K<=B)d_2(K)]
    <=B*(1+log B)^3.                             (6.1)
All repeated-prime valuations and h/k overlap are present in this bound.

Let C_psi be the proved all-real psi(t)<=C_psi*t constant from
prime_discrepancy_proof.txt section 1, or the self-contained 4 log 2
fallback in PRIME_BANK_COVARIANCE_REVIEW_001.txt section 2. For a nonempty
bank define
  D_theta=(1-theta)*(1+1/log 2)/theta.
Finite partial summation of sum log(q)/q, followed by log q>=theta log n,
gives
  sum_(n^theta<q<=n/2)1/q<=C_psi*D_theta.          (6.2)
The bank is empty through n=2^(1/(1-theta)); otherwise
1/log n<=(1-theta)/log 2, which supplies the uniform constant. No PNT
or RH-strength prime-error bound is used.

With defect multiplier at most six, its COMPLETE normalized primitive-
prefix contribution on [X,2X) is bounded in absolute value by
  9*C_psi*D_theta*B*(1+log B)^3
                         *(1+(1-theta)log(2X))^3.               (6.3)
Use sum_(X<=n<2X)n<3X^2/2. B=1 is exactly the cofactor diagonal h=k.
Fixed B, or B a fixed power of 1+log X, gives explicit cofinal log
padding. A positive-power B=X^eta leaves that positive exponent under
this rule and is not promoted. If a child changes B, its complete
primitive-sector boundary remains. The complementary large primitive
height repeated-prime defect is still unresolved.

7. Exact polynomial stencils give a genuine complete-kernel auxiliary bound
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Consider a same-prime program
  O=sum_(e,f)c_ef T_(q^e)^h T_(q^f)^k,
on S_i(h)S_j(k), with q coprime to h*k. Its multiplier is the EXACT
polynomial
  M_ij(t)=sum_(e,f)c_ef S_i(q^e)S_j(q^f),
where local prime-power rows are polynomials in t=q^(-1/3). Suppose its
first nonzero degree is at least m>0 and its coefficient l1 norm is C.
Then for every actual prime q,
  |M_ij(q^(-1/3))|<=C*q^(-b), b=m/3.              (7.1)
This works for EVERY b>0, including b>=1. There is no need to take a
b->1 limit of the earlier weighted-prime branch theorem.

Define the whole smooth-bank auxiliary by applying O under (1.1),
with all source shifts kept. Write Q_O,ij(X) for X^(-2) times the sum
over integer X<=n<2X of that pointwise signed auxiliary; equivalently
sum the same tensor against I(h;q,k) and multiply by X^(-2).
At each n its absolute sum is at most
  C*[sum_(h<=n)d_2(h)]
       *sum_(q>n^theta)q^(-b)*sum_(2<=k<=n/q)d_2(k)
    <=C*n^2*(1+log n)^2*sum_(q>n^theta)q^(-1-b)
    <=C*(1+1/b)*n^(2-theta*b)*(1+log n)^2.        (7.2)
The last bound enlarges primes to integers. For Y>=1,
sum_(integer l>Y)l^(-1-b)<=Y^(-b)*(1+1/b), by its first term plus the
tail integral. All smooth masks, high-smooth restrictions, and unit
exclusions only reduce this absolute bound; they are not removed from
the defining identity.

Consequently the normalized complete auxiliary obeys, for ALL integer
dyadic X>=1,
  |Q_O,ij(X)|
    <=C*(1+1/b)*2^max(2-theta*b,0)
                   *X^(1-theta*b)*(1+log(2X))^2.                (7.3)
The max is necessary if 2-theta*b is negative. For several independent
free primes in the declared bank-bank geometry, the identical proof
gives product_j(1+1/b_j), power 1-theta*sum_j b_j, and the same max
with that sum. Distinctness is imposed in the identity; enlarging it in
the absolute upper bound is safe. Local repeated-prime rows must still
be used for any binding which actually identifies those labels.

These are bounds for SIGNED BILINEAR AUXILIARIES, not squared energies
of prefix outputs. A negative power is summable with the displayed logs;
zero power is cofinal log padding; positive power is not cofinal under
this proof. For an original tensor projection, add the absolute fixed
projection coefficients times the individual polynomial l1 bounds.
No numerical coefficient fit enters this estimate.

8. Explicit cumulative stencil, including the common B channel
--------------------------------------------------------------
For q coprime to the base coordinate define
  C_(q,m)^h=sum_(e=0)^m T_(q^e)^h.
For m>=1, exact finite Euler sums give
  sum_(e=0)^m a_u(q^e)=t^m,
  sum_(e=0)^m a_v(q^e)=t^(2m),
  sum_(e=0)^m B(q^e)=-t^(m-1)+t^(2m-1)+t^(2m).    (8.1)
For the last identity, B's local series is
  (1-z)^2/[(1-tz)(1-t^2 z)].
Its cumulative coefficient is that of (1-z)/[(1-tz)(1-t^2z)]; subtracting
the two successive finite geometric sums proves (8.1). At m=0 every
cumulative multiplier is one; the displayed B formula is not used there.

Thus m=5 gives local orders 4,5,10 in channels B,u,v and l1 norms 3,1,1.
Every tensor S_i(h)S_j(k) with the stencil on its h coordinate has at
least a 4/3 prime weight. At theta=3/4 the WHOLE smooth-bank auxiliary
is cofinal by (7.3), including every B/u and B/v common-channel component.
For theta>3/4 it even has a negative block exponent, with the full logs
retained. This is an actual unconditional bound, distinct from the weaker
u/v-only two-prime slide mechanism.

The finite sparse/rectangular grammar may discover this and other local
zeros by exact polynomial enumeration. The current producer recurrence
and independent literal-convolution checker agree with the mathematical
local rows; neither may replace B(q^e) by B(q)^e. Coordinatewise polynomial
valuations supply one weight per independent FREE prime, not one weight
per tensor factor. A zero component is an exact identity, not an automatically
recovered target. All whole-target/RH flags correctly remain false.

9. Outside-domain recovery and its quantitative cost remain open
---------------------------------------------------------------
On the original a(h) support, h is not a prime power. Adjoining a fresh q
preserves that property, so the cumulative projected identity is
  sum_(e=0)^5 a(q^e h)
    =beta*[F_B,5(t)B(h)-t^5*a_u(h)-t^10*a_v(h)],
  F_B,5=-t^4+t^9+t^10.                            (9.1)
If base prime powers are allowed instead, an additional -d(h) defect
appears because only e=0 loses the same-prime branch. The h=1 convention
is separate again. Keeping the original non-prime-power mask avoids
both errors, but does not solve recovery.

For any original covariance tensor whose stencil includes the baseline
e=f=0 term, its exact transformed auxiliary is the baseline covariance
plus ALL nontrivial lifted terms. In (9.1), high-smooth h has q^e h>n
for every e>=1. Those terms are genuine outside-domain source terms;
bounding the small cumulative auxiliary does not bound the baseline
unless their whole weighted contribution is separately controlled.
Dropping, clipping, or treating them as unseen zero coefficients breaks
the identity. Recovering through a recurrence at those larger indices
is not a strictly decreasing-scale induction.

The suppression also has an explicit inverse loss. For m=5 and q>=2,
  |F_B,5(t)|=t^4*(1-t^5-t^6),
  1-t^5-t^6>=1-2^(-5/3)-2^(-2)>0.
Thus B recovery by this local scalar filter costs order q^(4/3), while
the separate u and v filters cost q^(5/3) and q^(10/3). There is no
uniform q-independent coercivity for these filtered channels. One scalar
filtered projection also does not invert all three differently scaled
channels without companions or further exact identities. Multiplying
by these inverse factors can undo the very powers used in (7.3).

This does not refute an argument combining several stencils and a signed
outside-domain compensation. It identifies its explicit residual:
  prove a complete bound or closed decreasing-scale recurrence for the
  lifted-support sum which expresses the original K/L0/Lu projection
  through the proved cofinal auxiliary, with the original full K, all
  h/k overlap, bank-bank/main terms and initial costs retained.
No such bound has been supplied here. It cannot be assumed to follow
from a separate RH-strength companion estimate or from the original
target itself.

10. Adversarial audit and next admissible constructions
------------------------------------------------------
The previous prime-removal study's two-prime saturation rules out its
own strict entrywise absolute contraction; it concerns different sources.
Here the exact polynomial cancellation orders are genuine candidate10
channel identities. The earlier finite-prime counterfeit examples still
warn that finitely many local laws and positive cycles cannot prove a
global signed moment. The current auxiliary bound instead includes the
complete growing-prime kernel, but its recovery is explicitly not closed.

Concrete safe constructions now available are:
  (a) component u/v slides with distinct-prime guards, complete outside
      terms, and their actual two-label support exponent;
  (b) cumulative/rectangular same-prime power stencils with exact local
      rows, all-channel cancellation order, and the full-kernel bound;
  (c) a primitive-height partition of repeated-prime defects, with the
      fixed/polylog prefix paid by (6.3) and the complement left open;
  (d) source-valuation partitions when a proposed second prime already
      divides h or k, retaining all shifted prime-power rows and masks.
These are operator+auxiliary+argument productions, not a claim of finite
score improvement. Their next proof input is the outside-domain recovery
and remaining large-primitive/fresh covariance, not another fitted drift
constant. Normalizing a new auxiliary by its term count may make it cheap,
but cannot silently normalize the original target or create coercivity.

------------

leakage; ordered-support failure of interior swapping; complete primitive-
