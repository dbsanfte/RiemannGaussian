JOINT INTEGER-SIZE PRODUCTS: CORRELATED BASES AND A NATURAL BOUNDARY

Research record, 7 October 2026. Universal written proofs are accompanied
by exact arithmetic and actual-zeta enclosures. Historical originality
is unresolved. No new primitive or RH estimate is asserted. This stage
extends the grammar and identifies a stronger obstruction introduced
by the selected products themselves.

1. BOOLEAN COMPOSITION OF ASSOCIATIVE KERNELS

For b>=2 let K_b(a,d) be the floor no-carry weight
  1[floor(log_b(ad))=floor(log_b(a))+floor(log_b(d))].
Its associative, commutative and unital divisor product was proved in
size_carry_proof.txt. If K and J are two such kernels, their pointwise
product K(a,d)*J(a,d) is again associative: multiply the two identities
  K(a,d)*K(ad,e)=K(d,e)*K(a,de)
and the corresponding identity for J. Commutativity and the unit persist.
Thus the selected intersection K_(b,c)=K_b AND K_c is universally valid.
The same argument applies to any floor/ceiling choices and to other
certified kernels. This closure construction is not a new primitive.

The 320-program Boolean grammar tests all truth tables on five base
pairs and four orientation pairs. Unit normalization rejects half of
the tables. Training through 4 has 128 fits; held-out integers through
24 and large exact powers leave 80. Their truth tables are precisely
AND, the first kernel, the second kernel, and the constant-one kernel.
All four rules have universal proofs; the 80 instances are not 80 new
operators. OR and implications require their own associativity checks.
The complement of an intersection need not be associative, even though
each single size kernel has an associative carry-only complement.
check_joint_size.py records an explicit complement counterexample.

For normalized arithmetic functions, coefficient-local LOG/EXP yield
  V_(b,c)(f)=EXP_Kjoint(LOG_D(f)),
with inverse EXP_D LOG_Kjoint and the normalized group-transfer law.
This is the established abstract LOG/EXP construction. Its finite root
lift L_q is the q-fold joint product of the ordinary qth root. Its
coefficient limit as q->infinity is V. On u(n)=1, all coefficients of
finite lifts and V lie in [0,1], since positive ordinary products dominate
the filtered products. Hence all series converge absolutely on Re(s)>1
and the root-order limit is locally uniform on that half-plane.

The independent arithmetic checker uses Euler-degree triangular LOG/EXP,
rising-factor roots, and a binomial evaluation of finite lifts. For sparse
large integers it uses bivariate prime-cycle polynomials: replace each
prime-power log term x^j/j by z^{h_b(p^j)}*w^{h_c(p^j)}*x^j/j, multiply
ordinary local exponentials, and extract degrees (h_b(n),h_c(n)). Equality
in both height inequalities means exactly that both carry totals vanish.
The 27 sparse profiles include rational parameters and do not zero-pad
unavailable input coefficients. Factorial degree clears the predicted
rational denominators as in the one-base cycle proof.

2. TWO INDEPENDENT ANGLE COORDINATES

Write theta_b(n)={log_b(n)}, tau_b=2*pi/log(b). Suppose b and c are
multiplicatively independent, meaning b^r*c^s=1 with integers r,s only
when r=s=0. This is equivalent to log(b)/log(c) being irrational.
For any nonzero integer pair (m,n),
  t_(m,n)=m*tau_b+n*tau_c !=0:
if it were zero, m*log(c)+n*log(b)=0 would give c^m*b^n=1.
Sharing prime factors is allowed, for example b=2,c=6.

For a>0 the nonnegative ordinary root coefficients d_a define a positive
measure on the two-dimensional angle torus:
  mu_(epsilon,a)=epsilon^a*sum_j d_a(j)*j^(-1-epsilon)
                         *delta_(theta_b(j),theta_c(j)).
Its mass tends to 1. Its (m,n) Fourier coefficient is
  epsilon^a*zeta(1+epsilon+i*t_(m,n))^a.
At every fixed nonzero mode, the zeta value is bounded near the indicated
point, since its only pole is at 1. The modulus of the normalized real
power is |zeta|^a, so the coefficient tends to zero. Positivity, bounded
mass and density of finite trigonometric polynomials prove weak convergence
to uniform torus measure. The two coordinates in that limit are independent.

For any fixed q, the q-fold product measures also converge. The angle cut
and the two simplex boundaries have measure zero under the uniform limit,
so their bounded indicator-phase functions have convergent integrals.
The q-fold joint floor product retains a factorization exactly when
  sum_j theta_b(n_j)<1 AND sum_j theta_c(n_j)<1.
Applying the product-measure lemma with a=alpha/q proves, for every
alpha>0 and positive integer q,
  lim_(epsilon down to 0) epsilon^alpha
      L_(b,c,q)(u^{*alpha})(1+epsilon+i*t_(m,n))
       =A_q(m)*A_q(n),
where
  A_q(0)=1/q!,
  A_q(m)=1/(q-1)!*integral_0^1 v^(q-1)*exp(-2*pi*i*m*v) dv
        =-sum_(r=1)^(q-1)(1/(2*pi*i*m))^r/(q-r)!, m!=0.
The one-base proof shows A_q(m) is nonzero for every integer m when
q>=2. For q=1, all nonconstant coefficients vanish; the natural-boundary
conclusion below therefore expressly excludes q=1.

3. RE(s)=1 IS A NATURAL BOUNDARY FOR EVERY FINITE q>=2

The additive group {m*tau_b+n*tau_c} is dense in the real line. One
elementary proof uses pigeonhole approximation of the irrational ratio:
the group contains nonzero elements Delta of arbitrarily small modulus.
Integer multiples of each Delta approximate any given real number to
within |Delta|/2, and remain elements of the group.

At every frequency in this dense group, the preceding Abelian coefficient
is nonzero for q>=2. The original half-plane series is therefore unbounded
when approaching that boundary point from the right, at rate epsilon^-alpha.
Consider any putative holomorphic continuation across any point of the
line Re(s)=1. A small disk on which it is holomorphic overlaps the original
right half-plane, where the continuation agrees with the series. Inside
a smaller disk it is bounded. A group-frequency boundary point lies in
that smaller disk; approaching it from the right contradicts boundedness.
Thus no such continuation exists across any point of the line.

Even local meromorphic continuation is impossible. Each of the dense
unbounded boundary points would have to be a pole of the continuation.
Poles of a meromorphic function cannot accumulate inside its domain.
This proves a natural boundary, rather than merely listing a finite set
of suspected singularities. Arbitrarily small coefficients at high-index
modes do not evade the argument: each fixed nonzero coefficient gives
unbounded growth as epsilon->0.

This is unconditional and is caused by the joint filter. Actual zeta is
classically zero-free on Re(s)=1, and at nonzero group frequencies it is
holomorphic. Ordinary pole cancellation at 1 cannot remove a dense family
of other singularities. These joint finite lifts consequently cannot
provide the proposed individual RH-half-plane holomorphy test. The earlier
single-base paired-root identity is a different composition and is intact.

Only TWO reciprocal logarithms are used. Multiplicative independence
of three or more integer bases does not by itself prove rational linear
independence of their reciprocal logarithms; no such extrapolation is made.

4. CORRELATED BASES: THE INDEPENDENCE SHORTCUT FAILS

If c=b^k for an integer k>=1, write an angle in base c as
  theta_c=(j+u)/k, j=0,...,k-1, 0<=u<1.
Then theta_b=u. Uniform circle measure for theta_c makes j uniform on
these k digits and u uniform on [0,1), independently. In q factors the
two no-carry conditions become
  sum u_i<1, sum j_i+sum u_i<k.
Apart from a null boundary, this is equivalent to sum u_i<1 and
sum j_i<=k-1. The number of ordered digit tuples satisfying the latter
condition is binomial(k+q-1,q), by stars and bars; their individual upper
bound j_i<=k-1 is automatic. Consequently the real-pole coefficient is
  binomial(k+q-1,q)/(k^q*q!).
For k=2 it is (q+1)/(2^q*q!), which at q=2 is 3/8. The independent-base
coefficient would be 1/(q!)^2=1/4, so that shortcut is exactly false.

The geometry stage obtains simplex-box counts by cumulative sums and
correlated digit counts by a separate integer recurrence. A 405-program
grammar of binomials, factorials and powers fits the correlated formula;
one program survives fresh q and k values. The independent checker
recomputes box counts with binomial formulas. This confirms implementation;
the universal law follows from the angle decomposition just proved.

The actual-zeta torus control also distinguishes dependence. For (b,b^2),
the nonconstant torus mode (1,-2) has IDENTICALLY zero scalar frequency.
Its Fourier moment equals the total mass and tends to 1. For independent
bases every fixed nonconstant mode tends to zero. Neither a finite set
of samples nor near-cancellation of floating logs is used to decide the
universal rational-dependence question.

No natural-boundary theorem is asserted here for dependent bases. Their
frequency group is discrete, and a separate continuation analysis would
be necessary. The independent-base proof cannot be applied to them.

5. AN EXACT ARITHMETIC IDENTITY FOR THE q=2 ZETA COMPARISONS

Let A and B be coefficient multipliers by theta_b and theta_c; C=AB
is their commuting pointwise composition. Write *=ordinary convolution,
g=d_beta, P=g*g=d_(2beta), F=zeta^beta. Expanding
  (1-theta_b(i)-theta_b(j)+theta_b(ij))
  *(1-theta_c(i)-theta_c(j)+theta_c(ij))
proves the following exact coefficient identity for g Kjoint g:
  P-2*g*(Ag+Bg)+AP+BP+2*g*(Cg)+2*(Ag)*(Bg)
       -2*A(g*(Bg))-2*B(g*(Ag))+CP.
It holds before taking any Dirichlet series. On Re(s)>1 all terms converge
absolutely for the tested beta=1/2, since their coefficients are bounded
by ordinary products of nonnegative root coefficients.

Let R_b keep indices b^k. Write S_b for the Fourier sawtooth multiplier
with midpoint value 1/2 at integer angles, so A=S_b-R_b/2. A finite
Fejer version of S_b acts on a Dirichlet series H by
  S_b H(s)=H(s)/2-sum_(0<|m|<=M)
                (1-|m|/(M+1))*H(s-i*m*tau_b)/(2*pi*i*m).
Using A and B as commuting diagonal multipliers gives
  Cg=S_b*S_c(g)-S_b(R_c g)/2-S_c(R_b g)/2+R_b R_c g/4,
and likewise for P. The outer multiplier terms A(g*Bg) and B(g*Ag)
require R_b(g*Bg) and R_c(g*Ag). Their coefficients are evaluated from
actual divisor sums; no false composite-base convolution-homomorphism
assumption is made.

The common-power projection R_b R_c is delta-only for independent bases.
For (2,4) it is R_4. Replacing it by delta-only in the exact identity
changes the n=4 lift coefficient from 3/4 to 5/16. This is an exact
counterexample to the shortcut, independently of spectral tail size.

For a prime-power base b=p^e the root resonance series has coefficients
d_beta(p^(e*k)). The production code uses its native hypergeometric
representation. The independent checker uses elementary root-of-unity
averaging of (1-p^-s)^(-beta); for e=2 this is the average of the plus
and minus arguments. At |p^-s|<1 these are normalized analytic branches.
The filtered-convolution resonance series is computed from prime-power
divisor coefficients, with an explicit geometric tail.

6. TWO DIFFERENT PROVED SPECTRAL ERROR BOUNDS

The production code uses the corrected Fejer multiplier theta_tilde,
which lies in [0,1], including exact zero at powers of its base. Its
one-base weighted error is bounded by E_b(sigma,M) from
size_carry_proof.txt, for sigma>2 and root
coefficients d_beta,d_(2beta)<=1. The approximate no-carry kernel
1-theta_tilde(i)-theta_tilde(j)+theta_tilde(ij) lies in [-1,2].
For two kernels the pointwise product error is <=one kernel error plus
twice the other, in either order. Summing positive g_i*g_j weights gives
  joint_error <=(2*zeta(sigma)^beta+1)
                     *min(E_b+2*E_c,E_c+2*E_b).
The individual three-term kernel error is bounded by
(2*zeta(sigma)^beta+1)*E_b because g*g=d_(2beta).
Add the geometric tails of R_b(g*Bg) and R_c(g*Ag), each bounded by
b^(-(K+1)*sigma)/(1-b^-sigma) when its multiplier lies in [0,1].

The independent checker uses ordinary Fourier partial sums, without
Fejer weights. Their corrected sawtooth multiplier has absolute bound
  D_M=1/2+H_M/pi.
Hence the approximate kernel has absolute bound B_M=1+3*D_M. The
one-base error is, for sigma>2,
  E_b^ordinary=sqrt(b)*log(b)/(2*pi*(M+1))
               *(zeta(sigma-1)-1/(1-b^(1-sigma))).
To obtain this bound, Abel summation bounds the omitted sawtooth sine
tail by 1/(pi*(M+1)*|sin(pi*theta)|). At a nonresonant integer index n,
the distance of log_b(n) to its nearest integer is at least
1/(sqrt(b)*n*log(b)): the corresponding integer power differs from n
by at least one and is at most sqrt(b)*n. The inequality
sin(pi*distance)>=2*distance then gives the displayed bound after
summing n^(1-sigma). Exact power indices have zero multiplier error
and are subtracted as the geometric series in the formula.
The independent joint error uses
  (2*zeta(sigma)^beta+1)
       *min(E_b+B_M*E_c,E_c+B_M*E_b).
Filtered resonance tails additionally carry the bound D_M. The direct
arithmetic truncation tail is N^(1-sigma)/(sigma-1), since actual q=2
joint-lift coefficients lie in [0,1]. Every omitted infinite component
has a separately stated bound.

The source uses M=48, resonance cutoff 48 and arithmetic cutoff 512;
the independent checker uses M=32, resonance cutoff 40 and arithmetic
cutoff 768, and calculates fresh arithmetic
coefficients directly from divisor sums. Six fresh actual-zeta points
at Re(s)=3,4,6 include independent bases and the correlated pair (2,4).
They reject a controlled coefficient-2 perturbation: +2 in the source
and a smaller +1 in the independent checker. The moment controls
use genuine root Fourier moduli at Re(s)=1+epsilon. They support the
implementation and the independence counterexample; the natural boundary
is established by sections 2 and 3, not numerical extrapolation.

7. HISTORICAL AND RESEARCH SCOPE

The general divisor-kernel framework, its associative cocycle condition,
and LOG/EXP unit-group transfers are classical, as documented in
novelty_audit.txt and the earlier proofs. Boolean intersection is a
closure rule, not a newly counted primitive. The exact joint zeta
applications and their boundary characterization have not been matched
to a reviewed publication; this is insufficient to certify originality.
Primary classical zeta context: NIST DLMF 25.10 (the zero-free line)
and 25.4 (functional equation); the actual computations use Arb:
  https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.10
  https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.4
The previous proofs list the reviewed primary convolution literature.

These families are now rejected as unmodified individual RH-domain
diagnostics. Their formal arithmetic laws remain valid. The next search
must seek a stronger arithmetic consequence with a genuine domain
through the critical strip, rather than attributing filter-created
barriers to zeta itself. The historically-new list remains open.
