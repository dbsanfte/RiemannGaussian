DYADIC COEFFICIENT EXTRACTION AND FRACTIONAL ZETA TRANSFERS

Scope: written universal mathematics with independently recomputed rational
witnesses and Arb implementation checks. No theorem-prover formalization,
historical originality, global analytic continuation, or RH proof is claimed.
This extends the candidate T; it is not counted as a third independent new
operator merely by naming a parameterized composition of known operations.

1. THE TYPED FAMILY

Let delta be the arithmetic identity concentrated at 1. Ordinary Dirichlet
convolution is denoted *. Infinitary convolution is

 (a A b)(n)=sum_{ij=n} a(i)b(j) prod_p 1[v_p(i)&v_p(j)=0],

where & is binary AND. Its associativity follows from the no-carry condition:
an iterated product is allowed exactly when the exponent bit sets in all
factors are mutually disjoint at each prime. Both algebras are commutative
and filtered by integer index. For h(1)=0, every k-fold product vanishes at
n whenever 2^k>n, so formal EXP and LOG terminate coefficientwise.

For f(1)=1 and rational beta define

 T_beta(f)=EXP_A(beta LOG_*(f)).

The definition also has coefficientwise complex parameter semantics. The
usual finite-at-each-coefficient formal identities give

 T_beta(f*g)=T_beta(f) A T_beta(g).

For beta!=0 the explicit normalized-group inverse is
EXP_*(beta^(-1) LOG_A(f)). At beta=0 the output is delta.
None of these abstract group identities is asserted to be historically new.

2. CLASSIFYING THE INDEX OPERATION

For an integer m>=1 define E_m(f)(n)=f(n^m). This operation needs lookahead:
an output cutoff N needs input values through N^m. Finite implementations
must either provide those coefficients or reduce their output cutoff.

Claim: E_m is an endomorphism of the entire infinitary convolution algebra
if and only if m is a power of 2, including m=1.

Suppose m=2^k. If ij=n^m contributes to the infinitary product, put a=v_p(i)
and b=v_p(j). Their addition has no binary carries and a+b=m*v_p(n).
Every one of the lowest k binary digits of a+b is zero. Hence those digits
of a and b are zero individually, so both are divisible by m. Thus i=r^m,
j=t^m with rt=n. Conversely, an allowed pair r,t gives an allowed pair
r^m,t^m because shifting all exponent bits preserves disjointness. This
bijection proves E_m(a A b)=E_m(a) A E_m(b), on ALL arithmetic inputs.
It preserves delta, so also commutes coefficientwise with EXP_A and LOG_A.

If m is not a power of 2, split its set bits into two nonempty disjoint
parts: m=a+b with a&b=0 and 0<a,b<m. For any prime p,
delta_(p^a) A delta_(p^b)=delta_(p^m). Applying E_m gives delta_p, whereas
both separately extracted impulses are zero. This disproves the law.
The same operation already fails for ordinary convolution when m=2:
E_2(delta_p*delta_p)=delta_p but E_2(delta_p)*E_2(delta_p)=0.

The implementation checks unrelated rational local and integer-index
inputs, including nonmultiplicative inputs. It supplies every non-dyadic
impulse counterexample among 2<=m<=32. These are checks of implementations;
the bit argument proves the classification for every m>=1.

3. THE DISCOVERED PARAMETER LAW

Let u(n)=1. The ordinary logarithm has coefficients

 LOG_*(u)(p^e)=1/e, e>=1,

and is zero at 1 and at integers with multiple distinct prime factors.
This follows coefficientwise from -log(1-x)=sum_{e>=1}x^e/e at each prime.
Consequently E_m(LOG_*(u))=(1/m) LOG_*(u) for every positive integer m.
Combining this fact with section 2, for EVERY k>=0 and every parameter beta,

 T_beta(u)(n^(2^k))=T_(beta/2^k)(u)(n), n>=1.              (1)

The search supplied all integer multipliers 2 through 32, with an exact
scalar c fitted only from exponent 1 at beta=1. Each model therefore fits
that training datum. Exponents 2 through 12 and parameters -1/2,2/3,3/2,5/7
were withheld. Only m=2,4,8,16,32 survive, with c=1/m. Input lookahead is
prime exponent 384; no unavailable coefficients were treated as zero.
An independent partition computation reproduces all 26 false fits and all
five survivors. This is an explicitly enlarged, factor-aware secondary
grammar, not a claim that its primitives were inferred from raw samples.

The parameter-halving law is special to u, not all normalized inputs. For
f=delta_1+delta_2, E_2(T_1(f))(2)=-1/2 while T_(1/2)(f)(2)=+1/2.
LOG_*(f)(2^e)=(-1)^(e+1)/e and repeated factors of delta_2 cannot contribute
under A, giving this exact counterexample. The implementation checks it.
The E_m endomorphism classification itself still holds on ALL inputs.

For reference, the local coefficient h_e(beta)=T_beta(u)(p^e) is

 h_e(beta)=sum_{partitions pi of the set bits of e}
                 beta^(number of blocks) prod_{B in pi} 1/(sum_{b in B}b).

Here bit values are 1,2,4,...; h_0=1. Ordered partitions appear in EXP_A and
the factorial divides out their order. This proves the formula independently
of the production local convolution polynomial generator. Examples:
h_1=beta, h_2=beta/2, h_3=beta/3+beta^2/2, h_4=beta/4.

4. A REAL PARAMETER THEOREM, NOT ONLY SAMPLED ROOT ORDERS

For every real beta in [0,1], all h_e are nonnegative. Term by term the
no-carry exponential is bounded by the ordinary exponential of the same
nonnegative source. Its ordinary coefficient is (beta)_e/e!<=1. Thus

 0<=h_e(beta)<=1 for ALL e and beta in [0,1].

Set H_beta(x)=sum h_e(beta)x^e, analytic for |x|<1, and

 R_beta(x)=(1-x)^beta H_beta(x),                          (2)

using the analytic logarithm of 1-x that vanishes at x=0. Its constant
coefficient is 1 and its linear coefficient is zero. If g_e denotes the
coefficient of (1-x)^beta, then g_e<=0 for e>=1 and sum_{e>=1}|g_e|<=1.
The latter assertion follows by letting real x increase to 1 in the binomial
series; at beta=0 every nonconstant coefficient vanishes. Therefore each
coefficient r_e of R_beta obeys |r_e|<=2, uniformly in e and real beta.

For e<=48 the r_e are rational polynomials in beta, recomputed in two ways:
generic no-carry exponential versus independent bit-set partitions, and
FLINT polynomial multiplication versus standard-library rational arrays.
For each r_e the exact Bernstein coefficients on [0,1] bound its absolute
value by their maximum absolute value C_e. The convex hull property of the
Bernstein basis proves that bound throughout the interval.

At the exact radius r=71/100, the stored rational witness gives

 sum_{e=2}^{48} C_e r^e + 2*r^49/(1-r)
                  = 0.658980751510... < 1.              (3)

The decimal is only a display. Every coefficient, Bernstein bound, head
sum, tail and strict inequality is independently checked as a Fraction.
The tail uses the universal coefficient bound, not extrapolation from the
first 48 polynomials. Consequently |R_beta(x)-1|<1 on |x|<=71/100 for EVERY
real beta in [0,1]. In particular R_beta has no zeros in that closed disk.
This parameter beta differs from alpha=1/q in the finite root-lift detector;
the present theorem covers the full continuous real interval [0,1].

5. THE ANALYTIC ZETA BRIDGE

The arithmetic coefficients of T_beta(u) are multiplicative: EXP_A of a
prime-power-supported source factors over primes. They lie in [0,1], so

 mathcal H_beta(s)=sum_{n>=1}T_beta(u)(n)n^(-s)

converges absolutely for Re(s)>1. The product of (2) over primes gives

 mathcal H_beta(s)=zeta(s)^beta mathcal R_beta(s),         (4)

initially Re(s)>1, with the zeta logarithm normalized by its absolutely
convergent Euler expansion. The correction product is

 mathcal R_beta(s)=prod_p R_beta(p^(-s)).

Since r_1=0 and |r_e|<=2,
|R_beta(p^(-s))-1|<=2*p^(-2sigma)/(1-p^(-sigma)), sigma=Re(s)>1/2.
This is summable locally uniformly in that half-plane. Every individual
factor is nonzero there by (3), since p^(-sigma)<2^(-1/2)<71/100. Standard
convergent-product estimates, or locally summing the normalized factor
logarithms after a finite number of primes, show that mathcal R_beta is
holomorphic and nowhere zero on Re(s)>1/2. This statement holds for every
real beta in [0,1]. It does not assert that zeta^beta is single-valued there.

6. REPEATED EXTRACTION AND THE RH OBSTRUCTION

Put Z(s)=(s-1)zeta(s), holomorphic on Re(s)>1/2, with Z(1)=1. Its zeros in
that half-plane are exactly zeta's zeros there. Let beta_k=1/(2^k), k>=1,
and initially on Re(s)>1 define the pole-cancelled function

 Phi_k(s)=(s-1)^beta_k mathcal H_beta_k(s)
         =Z(s)^beta_k mathcal R_beta_k(s).                (5)

The branch in this initial half-plane is log Z=log(s-1)+log zeta, with both
logs normalized to their real values at real s>1. Equation (1) realizes
the coefficient family by k repeated square-index extractions from T_1(u).

Claim: RH holds if and only if EVERY Phi_k, k>=1, admits a single-valued
holomorphic extension to Re(s)>1/2.

If RH holds, Z has no zeros there. The half-plane is simply connected, so
Z has a holomorphic logarithm matching the initial normalization. Equation
(5) then supplies every extension because mathcal R_beta_k is holomorphic.

Conversely, suppose all those extensions exist. Divide each by the nowhere
zero correction to obtain a holomorphic G_k throughout the half-plane.
The identity theorem applied to the initial half-plane gives G_k^(2^k)=Z
everywhere. If Z has a zero of finite positive order m, then m must be
divisible by 2^k for EVERY k. Choosing 2^k>m is a contradiction. Hence Z
is zero-free in Re(s)>1/2. The classical functional reflection of the
nontrivial zeros makes that equivalent to RH.

A SINGLE root order cannot make this deduction: a double zero is compatible
with a holomorphic square root locally, and a quadruple zero is compatible
with both square and fourth roots. The next sufficiently large dyadic root
detects their branching. This criterion is equivalent to RH; there is no
proof here that the required extensions exist. Nor is coefficient extraction
asserted to preserve analytic continuation for an arbitrary input series.

Classical zeros/reflection references: https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.10 and
https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.4. The index classification and rational correction
witness are the distinct arithmetic content of this investigation.

7. INDEPENDENT NUMERICAL BRIDGES AND BRANCH CONTROLS

dyadic_analytic_probe.py computes T_beta(u) through 512 using generic
ordinary LOG and infinitary EXP for beta=1/2,1/4,1/8. The independent
partition route agrees coefficient by coefficient. Twelve fresh actual
Arb zeta comparisons use sigma=3,4,6, local degree 48 and primes <=257.
For these sigma, |log zeta(s)|<=log zeta(sigma)<pi, so the principal Arb
log is the normalized Euler logarithm used in (4).

The Dirichlet tail is bounded by N^(1-sigma)/(sigma-1). For the Euler
truncations put a=2/(1-2^(-sigma)), P=257, M=48. Then valid bounds are

 E=exp(a*(2^(-2sigma)+2^(1-2sigma)/(2sigma-1))),
 B=a*P^(1-2sigma)/(2sigma-1),
 K=a*(2^(-sigma(M+1))+2^(1-sigma(M+1))/(sigma(M+1)-1)).

The product error is <=E*(exp(B)-1+K); multiply by |zeta^beta| and add the
Dirichlet tail. These follow from |r_e|<=2, telescoping finite products,
and bounding omitted primes by the corresponding positive integer sums.
All comparisons use outward-rounded Arb balls. Adding 1/7 to coefficient
4 is rejected in all twelve comparisons by a lower error bound larger than
the proved permissible error.

For the root criterion, Arb encloses actual zeta on the rectangle
Re(s)=0.75+/-0.01, Im(s)=1+/-0.01 and certifies its modulus bounded away
from zero. Thus Z is nonzero on the disk centered at 3/4+i, radius 1/200.
The controlled analytic model (s-(3/4+i))^m Z(s) has exactly a zero of order
m there. The nonzero correction cannot change root monodromy. A circuit
multiplies its order-2^k root by exp(2*pi*i*m/2^k), which equals 1 exactly
when 2^k divides m. Arb certifies every nonzero jump factor; exact integer
arithmetic establishes the no-jump cases. Independently sampled, unwrapped
phases agree for m=0,1,2,4,8 and k=1,2,3,4. Sampling is diagnostic; the
whole-region zeta enclosure and explicit model factor justify the winding.

These models test the DERIVED root-continuation criterion. They are not
asserted to be T_beta of altered arithmetic inputs, and give no conclusion
about actual off-line zeta zeros outside that certified test disk.

8. A CONTROL USING AN ACTUAL CRITICAL-LINE ZETA ZERO

The actual-zero control does not assume RH or merely use a numerical root.
Its rational-decimal centre is c=1/2+14.13472514173469379046*i, radius
r=1/10000. Arb supplies zeta(c), zeta'(c), and an enclosure for zeta''/2
throughout the enclosing rectangle c+[-r,r]+i[-r,r]. The rectangle excludes
the pole. Complex Taylor's integral remainder gives, on the whole circle,

 |zeta(s)-zeta'(c)(s-c)| <= |zeta(c)|+r^2 sup_rectangle |zeta''/2|.

This upper bound is strictly smaller than r*|zeta'(c)| by Arb's outward
rounded bounds. Rouche's theorem therefore certifies exactly ONE zero,
counting multiplicity, in the disk; it is simple. The disk is invariant
under s->1-conjugate(s), which reflects nontrivial zeta zeros. Uniqueness
then forces that zero's real part to be exactly 1/2. Z=(s-1)zeta has the
same single zero there. The claim does not require a global count proving
that this is the first zero; it is an actual certified zero at this height.

The correction from section 5 is initially defined to the right of the
critical line. To justify its role throughout this crossing disk, cancel
its quadratic coefficient rather than assuming extension:

 r_2(beta)=-beta^2/2, a=beta^2/2,
 U_beta(x)=R_beta(x)*(1-x^2)^(-a),
 mathcal R_beta(s)=zeta(2s)^(-a) prod_p U_beta(p^(-s)).    (6)

The coefficients of U at degrees 1 and 2 vanish. For 0<=beta<=1, a<=1/2,
the coefficients of (1-x^2)^(-a) are nonnegative and <=1 at even degrees.
Using |r_e|<=2 therefore bounds |[x^e]U| by 2*(floor(e/2)+1)<=e+2. In
particular |U_beta(x)-1|<=5*|x|^3/(1-|x|)^2. The prime product in (6)
converges locally uniformly for Re(s)>1/3. Each factor is nonzero wherever
|p^(-s)|<71/100, by section 4 and the nonzero factor (1-x^2)^(-a).
Thus the product is holomorphic and nonzero for sigma greater than both
1/3 and -log(71/100)/log(2), approximately 0.4941.

The actual control certifies 2^(-0.4999)<71/100. Arb also certifies zeta(2s)
nonzero on the entire enclosing rectangle. Its logarithm therefore extends
to this simply connected disk from the overlapping Re(s)>1/2 part. Formula
(6) proves that EVERY real-beta correction extends holomorphically and
without zeros to the full test disk. This is a local extension theorem;
it does not assume global zero-freeness of zeta(2s) to the left of Re(s)=1/2.

Consequently the actual simple zero makes Phi_k in (5) acquire the factor
exp(2*pi*i/2^k) after a circuit, for k=1,2,3,4. Arb certifies each nonzero
root-of-unity jump, and independent unwrapped phase samples show winding
one and the expected endpoint jumps. The rigorous zero count and correction
extension justify the obstruction; phase sampling is only a diagnostic.

An additional 13 cases use ACTUAL arithmetic inputs u^{*m}, m=1,2,4,8,
whose Dirichlet series are zeta(s)^m. Generic LOG/EXP computes
T_(1/q)(u^{*m})=T_(m/q)(u) through n=128, for q=2,4,8,16 with m<=q.
The identity holds universally because LOG_*(u^{*m})=m LOG_*(u).
Here the effective parameter m/q lies in the entire certified interval.
The pole-cancelled transformed function is Z(s)^(m/q) R_(m/q)(s).
Our certified simple zero of Z is a zero of multiplicity m in Z^m, so its
order-q root branches exactly when q does not divide m. Exact root-of-unity
decisions, generic arithmetic coefficient routes, and sampled winding m
all agree. In particular the square-root transfer of zeta^2 has no branch
jump at this zero, while its fourth-root transfer does; the fourth-root
transfer of zeta^4 has no jump, while its eighth-root transfer does.
These particular controls are arithmetic transfers of the stated actual
zeta-power inputs, rather than models of the derived criterion alone.

This circle crosses the critical line. Branching at the certified boundary
zero is compatible with RH: the RH criterion asks for extension on the
OPEN half-plane Re(s)>1/2. The check confirms sensitivity to actual zeta
zeros without implying an off-line zero or a resolution of RH.
