1. Domain, trust base, and the present leaf

Products of arithmetic sequences below mean Dirichlet convolution. The unit is
delta(1)=1. For EVERY integer U>=1 set
 A_U(n)=mu(n) 1_(n<=U), c_U=sum_(n<=U)mu(n)/n,
 T_U=c_U delta-A_U, E_U=delta+One*T_U.
Use the actual mu*One=delta identity and the established |c_U|<=2. In the
half-plane of absolute convergence, and then by meromorphic continuation,
 E_U(s)=1+zeta(s)T_U(s), T_U(1)=0.
Consequently E_U is analytic at 1. Keep this harmonic-null pair atomic.

Write u=1/3, theta=1-u=2/3, K(n)=n^-u, and, for fixed nonnegative integers p,m,
 P_U=A_U^p*E_U^m,
 F_U(s)=zeta(s+u)A_U(s)^p E_U(s)^m,
 M_U=A_U(theta)^p E_U(theta)^m.
Its complete analytically centered prefix, for every real x>0, is
 C_U(x)=sum_(n<=x)(K*P_U)(n)-(M_U/theta)x^theta.
The sum is zero below 1; the second term is NOT zero there. At integers this
is the prefix of K*P_U-(M_U/theta)J_theta, J_theta(n)=n^theta-(n-1)^theta.
This defines ANALYTIC-residue centering. Ordinary convergence of the coefficient
moment at theta is not inferred, particularly for m>=3.

Review020 supplies the Gaussian Perron/Mellin and integer-cell-average recovery,
including the x<1 tail, complete earlier prefixes, and discrete averaging error.
It also supplies the precise phase-uniform weighted-height provider under its
short-polynomial hypothesis, ultimately using the primary twisted fourth moment
recorded in the frozen handoff. These are inherited analytic primitives; this
memo does not supply a new external moment theorem or cross an unproved pole.

The present p=2,m=3 plan has sigma=9/20, kappa=2/9, epsilon=1/120,
 tau=45/2, H_U=2 U^tau. With measure dt/(2pi |sigma+it|^2), put
 L_U=integral_(|t|<H_U)|F_U(s)|^2,
 I_U=integral_(|t|>=H_U)|F_U(s)|^2.
The inherited theorem proves I_U<=C_high U^-1/16. The uniform leaf is
 L_U<=C_low U^b, b=12/5, EVERY integer U>=1.                OPEN
The required constant cannot depend on U,X,N or on a tested cutoff.

2. Exact shell transition, including unit and residue

For integers V>=U define D=mu 1_(U<n<=V), delta_c=D(1), where D(1) here denotes
the Dirichlet SERIES value sum mu(n)/n, not the coefficient at the integer 1.
To avoid that notational ambiguity an implementation should call it eval_at_1.
Put Q=delta_c delta-D. Then exactly
 A_V=A_U+D, c_V=c_U+delta_c, T_V=T_U+Q,
 E_V=E_U+One*Q, Q(1)=0.                                  (2.1)
At an integer n>=0,
 prefix(E_V-E_U)(n)=sum_(U<q<=V)mu(q){n/q}.                (2.2)
For real x the staircase extension uses n=floor x in (2.2). In particular the
unit term delta_c is indispensable: the increment is generally nonzero below
the shell's first integer. The support of D is >U; that of One*Q is not >U.

The binomial compiler can produce the exact finite polynomial
 Delta F=zeta(s+u) sum_(0<=i<=p,0<=j<=m,(i,j)!=(0,0))
   binom(p,i)binom(m,j) A_U^(p-i)D^i E_U^(m-j)[zeta(s)Q]^j. (2.3)
All terms and their signed crosses are retained. Because Q(1)=0, zeta Q is
analytic at 1, as are E_U and E_V. Expanding delta_c and D into separately
estimated states would reintroduce real poles at 1. Such an expansion is legal
only with every extra compensation and its opposite drift explicitly restored.

The residue transition is Delta M=(Delta F/zeta(s+u)) evaluated at theta.
Thus Delta C(x)=prefix(K*(P_V-P_U))(x)-(Delta M/theta)x^theta for ALL x>0.
The unit-cell J drift and the lower tail are included. No coefficient-tail
convergence at theta is needed for this finite-polynomial identity.

3. A finite arithmetic head is uniformly payable in physical space

The actual inverse identity gives, on 1<=n<=U,
 P_U(n)=c_U^m d_(m-p)(n) if m>=p,
 P_U(n)=c_U^m mu^(p-m)(n) if m<p.                         (3.1)
Here d_j=One^j, d_0=delta, and all powers mean convolution. This is the already
known source constraint, not a new prime-distribution theorem. It is suitable
for automatic matching and head extraction, rather than manually nominating
a coefficient pattern.

For the open case let h_U=c_U^3 One 1_(n<=U). Its finite analytic moment is
 M_head=c_U^3 sum_(d<=U)d^-theta. Subtracting the FULL centered K*h_U leaves
the centered remainder with moment M_U-M_head, not M_U. For every y>0,
 |sum_(k<=y)k^(theta-1)-y^theta/theta|<=1+1/theta.          (3.2)
For y>=1 this follows by upper/lower integral comparisons for the decreasing
positive power; for y<1 the left side is y^theta/theta. Therefore
 |prefix(K*h_U)(x)-(M_head/theta)x^theta|
 <=|c_U|^3 (1+1/theta) U <=20 U                           (3.3)
for ALL real x>0. This includes d>x, whose prefix sum is empty but whose
centering contribution is not. It also includes the unit.

Normalized polynomial-history channels of this centered prefix are O(U), with
explicit constants from the coefficient l1 norms of the polynomial and its
derivative and, if present, the lower bound log(2n)>=log 2. Discrete Abel summation
retains the entire history: the normalized polynomial derivative has total
variation at most its coefficient derivative norm on [0,1]. Consequently the
head's channel energy is O(U^2/X), paid for U=floor((4X-1)^(1/24)). Its positive
norm can be paid by triangle/Cauchy with the complete remainder and then kept
in the full source recovery. There is no claim of an orthogonal head/tail split.

There is also an unconditional GLOBAL weighted-norm payment for this head,
stronger than merely its physical-block envelope. Put
 D_theta(y)=sum_(k<=y)k^(theta-1)-y^theta/theta.
For y>=1, decreasing-function integral comparisons give -1/theta<=D_theta(y)<=0;
for 0<y<1 it equals -y^theta/theta. Therefore, for 0<sigma<theta,
 integral_0^infinity |D_theta(y)|^2 y^(-2sigma-1)dy
 <=B_(theta,sigma)=[1/(theta-sigma)+1/sigma]/(2theta^2).    (3.4)
The complete centered prefix for a FINITE h is sum_d h(d)D_theta(x/d), so
Minkowski and change of variable give its weighted norm at most
 sqrt(B_(theta,sigma)) sum_d |h(d)|d^-sigma.               (3.5)
This includes every d>x and the entire lower tail. At theta=2/3,sigma=9/20,
B=100/13. The current head has sum |h(d)|d^-sigma<=8 U^(1-sigma)/(1-sigma).
By the inherited Mellin isometry its FULL spectral squared norm is at most
 (100/13)(8/(1-sigma))^2 U^(11/10).                       (3.6)
Consequently the raw and head-corrected low goals U^(12/5) imply one another,
up to uniform paid constants, using the same height and complete analytic
centering. This does not identify their signed cutoff work, nor prove either
goal. A shell for the corrected target can be paid by twice the raw shell plus
twice the full CHILD head norm. Its coarse exponent is 11/10, not -1/16;
block recurrence admission remains valid against b=12/5. Do not report this
as the unchanged raw high-tail saving.

For m>=p and m-p>=1 the same proof pays the matched finite d_(m-p) head with
O(U(1+log U)^(m-p-1)) prefix envelope; m=p has only the finite unit. For m<p
the compiler must use the complete signed inverse-divisor head, not substitute
One. A zero raw coefficient head does not mean a zero centered head: its residue
creates an opposite power drift at all small x.

4. Single-cutoff signed work: exact three-valued arithmetic

Let q=U+1 and e=mu(q). Then D=e q^-s, Q=e(q^-1-q^-s). The identity
 q^-1-q^-s=((s-1)/q) integral_1^q y^-s dy                 (4.1)
proves harmonic nullness without discarding the pole compensation. For a fixed
sigma<1 it does not reduce the low-height size below order q^-sigma.
Expand (2.3) in e, then use e^3=e and e^4=e^2. There are exact functions J_o,J_e
depending on the old state and q such that
 F_q-F_U=e J_o+e^2 J_e.                                  (4.2)
For raw P, J_o is the sum of terms i+j positive odd and J_e the positive even
terms, with the powers of e removed. This reduction retains all repeated-prime
and composite effects; it uses actual mu(q) in {-1,0,1} rather than a random sign.

On ANY common integration interval, the exact squared-norm difference is
 e [2 Re(F_U conjugate(J_o))+2 Re(J_o conjugate(J_e))]
 +e^2 [2 Re(F_U conjugate(J_e))+|J_o|^2+|J_e|^2].          (4.3)
The first bracket is a signed arithmetic correlation. The second is not known
nonnegative, because its cross with F_U is retained. The two squares themselves
are nonnegative. Neither bracket has a justified mean-zero/negative expectation.
mu(q) is correlated with the previous actual-Mobius state. Harmonic nullness,
negative local coefficients, or the three-valued alphabet cannot supply such
an expectation or a martingale filtration.

Head correction has an additional zero-sign term. For the current P'_U=P_U-h_U
define its series F'_U=zeta(s+u)[P_U(s)-c_U^3 D_U(s)],
 D_U(s)=sum_(n<=U)n^-s. Then
 F'_q-F'_U=J_0+e J'_o+e^2 J'_e,
 J_0=-zeta(s+u)c_U^3 q^-s,
 J'_o=J_o-zeta(s+u)(3c_U^2/q+q^-3)D_q(s),
 J'_e=J_e-zeta(s+u)(3c_U/q^2)D_q(s).                     (4.4)
In particular e=0 does NOT give a zero corrected increment. The finite head
advances at q even when the raw A and E stay unchanged.

With P denoting the OLD corrected series F'_U, its squared-norm increment is
 C_0+e C_1+e^2 C_2, where
 C_0=2 Re(P conjugate(J_0))+|J_0|^2,
 C_1=2 Re((P+J_0)conjugate(J'_o)+J'_o conjugate(J'_e)),
 C_2=2 Re((P+J_0)conjugate(J'_e))+|J'_o|^2+|J'_e|^2.      (4.5)
The same formulas hold pointwise before integration. Residues update by
evaluation of each complete term after removing zeta(s+u), at theta. An automatic
checker must compare the entire polynomial, including C_0 and all head drifts.

More generally, a matched c_U^m d_(m-p) restricted head with m>=p has new-cell
term -c_U^m d_(m-p)(q)delta_q, plus the complete change of c_U^m times the new
head. Both can be expanded and reduced in e. For m<p the signed convolution
coefficient at q requires its own exact source evaluation/binding; (4.4) must
not be copied verbatim.

5. The moving height shell is a real cost, but an inherited paid one

Define Delta F=F_V-F_U, V>=U. Exactly
 L_V-L_U=W_(U,V)+S_(U,V),
 W_(U,V)=integral_(|t|<H_U)[2 Re(F_U conjugate(Delta F))+|Delta F|^2],
 S_(U,V)=integral_(H_U<=|t|<H_V)|F_V|^2 >=0.              (5.1)
All integrals use the measure in section 1. The shell contains the CHILD, so
an old-F high-tail bound alone cannot bound it. An equivalent useful identity is
 L_V-L_U=integral_(H_U<=|t|<H_V)|F_U|^2
       +integral_(|t|<H_V)[2 Re(F_U conjugate(Delta F))+|Delta F|^2]. (5.2)
Here the old shell is paid, but the new signed work on that shell is retained.
Equations (5.1) and (5.2) are alternative exact rules, not two costs to add.

For fixed 1<=V/U<=d, the inherited weighted theorem actually pays S_(U,V).
The child's polynomial lengths are at most V^(p+j)<=d^(p+j)U^(p+j). The old
height gives the length premise up to that fixed factor. Choose once a kappa'
strictly between kappa and 1/4. Above a fixed height threshold the factor is
absorbed in Y^(kappa'-kappa); below it there are only finitely bounded U and
plain coefficient bounds pay a finite uniform constant. For example a threshold
of the form 2 C^(1/(kappa'-kappa)) absorbs a fixed length factor C. The moment
theorem permits an arbitrary fixed epsilon, so keep the already allocated
epsilon and use the SAME height exponents from Review020.

Consequently, with r_j=p+j and
 d_0=-sigma-u, d_1=1-u-3sigma,
 d_j=j-u-(j+3)sigma for j>=2,
 S_(U,V)<=C_d sum_(j=0)^m U^[r_j(1-2sigma)+tau(d_j+epsilon)]. (5.3)
Constants depend on fixed d,p,m,sigma,kappa,epsilon and the imported theorem,
but not on U,V,X,N or observed signs. For the current plan this is
 S_(U,V)<=C_d U^-1/16.                                   (5.4)
This pays a moving boundary; it does NOT pay W_(U,V). The corrected-head target
requires its own complete tail bookkeeping, or physical-space removal by (3.3).
One must not automatically reuse (5.4) for an arbitrarily changed series.

6. Executable recurrence and forcing rules, with exact coverage

The raw goal L_U=O(U^b) suggests normalized J_U=U^-b L_U. For V=dU, integer d>=2,
 J_V=d^-b J_U+V^-b W_(U,V)+V^-b S_(U,V).                 (6.1)
A uniform upper bound W_(U,dU)<=C U^b, together with (5.3), proves a dyadic
or fixed-ratio cutoff bound. The factor d^-b<1 is supplied by NORMALIZATION,
not a newly proved arithmetic contraction. Conversely L_U=O(U^b) already implies
W_(U,V)<=L_V=O(V^b). Thus an endpoint-work upper bound of this order is an
equivalent presentation up to constants, although its exact odd/even or shell
structure can make it a useful new arithmetic-search interface.

The detector may enumerate a genuinely stronger signed recurrence
 J_(dU)<=rho J_U+C(1+log_d U)^k, rho in [0,1], C>=0,
 with an explicit finite base and a uniform OPEN premise.                  (6.2)
For rho<1 the elementary iteration gives log degree k; for rho=1 it gives
k+1; if C=0 it gives a bounded base. No sign is imposed on W. A polynomial
auxiliary potential must be coercive for J (or have separately proved recovery),
and its exact signed work and moving shell must be included. Changing the root
norm instead of the squared norm does not itself prove a smaller forcing.

Dyadic cutoff endpoints do NOT imply the stated EVERY-integer leaf. One sound
bridge is a uniform one-sided bound on W_(U,V) for EVERY U<=V<=2U, with U dyadic,
plus (5.3). A maximum-partial-shell work certificate gives that bridge without
pretending L_U is monotone: its integrand, as well as its height, changes with U.
Alternatively sum exact single-q work for every integer cutoff.

For the current b=12/5, the stronger single-step hypothesis
 W_(q-1,q)<=C q^(b-1)=C q^(7/5), q>=2                    (6.3)
and (5.4) imply the all-integer leaf, because sums of q^(7/5) are O(U^(12/5))
and sums of q^-1/16 are O(U^(15/16)). Keep the finite U=1 base. In a generic
plan a shell exponent h sums to U^(h+1), log U, or a bounded constant according
as h>-1, h=-1, or h<-1. Its cost must be compared with the target b explicitly.
This single-step bound is STRONGER than L_U=O(U^b): a nonnegative sequence can
alternate between zero and U^b while obeying the latter but violating an
increment bound of order U^(b-1). Do not force the search to prove (6.3) if a
joined block or auxiliary cancellation can prove the endpoint goal instead.

For the head-corrected target, its shell bound from (3.6) is O(q^(11/10)) per
consecutive cutoff. Summing these coarse shell bounds costs O(U^(21/10)), still
strictly below U^(12/5). Thus (6.3), with the COMPLETE corrected work (4.5), also
gives its all-integer goal. Constants can be enlarged uniformly to absorb this
paid shell. This is not a sign bound on C_0 or on the corrected work itself.

The physical cutoffs floor((4X-1)^alpha) do not license silently sampling only
the dyadic-U leaf. A different cutoff choice can be used in the finite inverse
identity, but then all its source terms, analytic drifts and recovery costs must
be recompiled. It cannot be treated as the same F_U with an endpoint proof.

7. What an automatic auxiliary grammar can honestly certify

For a fixed list of monomials in A,E,D,zeta Q, binomial translation gives an
exact triangular state transition. A detector can search rational Hermitian
quadratic potentials and linear combinations of their transition work. A PSD
matrix domination P>=v v* is a sufficient algebraic recovery of the nominated
linear target v*Z. A more specialized indefinite potential needs its OWN valid
coercive lower bound and finite-head term; cancellation in its coefficients
alone is not recovery. The compiler can solve exact polynomial identities and
nullspaces before any sign observations, then leave the resulting signed Gram
or work inequality OPEN unless proved by an accepted theorem.

Useful enumerable primitives are: complete cutoff shell; consecutive cutoff;
matched arithmetic head removal; harmonic-null atomic Q; three-valued reduction;
exact Gram polarization; positive square and rational PSD recovery; joined
maximum partial work; fixed-ratio recurrence; explicit shell and head payment.
Forces may be full odd/even work, a complete polarized auxiliary work, or a sum
of work over a block. The grammar must allow all crosses to stay joined. This
is construction and proof-route search, not a claim that enumeration proves
the arithmetic premise.

The old unsigned argument gives no exponent improvement: the coefficient sizes
of A and T on sigma are O(U^(1-sigma)). Even replacing the triangle telescope
by unproved orthogonality of consecutive increments would not settle the
current target. Such orthogonality is not true merely because q changes: the
finite-head Mellin phases overlap, with deterministic actual coefficients.
There is no justified probabilistic martingale measure on those cutoffs.

8. A fixed generic-sequence obstruction to automatic closure

The previous generic sharpness theorem is not an actual-Mobius disproof. The
following fixed integer-valued example additionally shows why cutoff compatibility
and the alphabet {-1,0,1} do not cure the gap. Put a(1)=1 and
 a(n)=(-1)^k for 2^k<=n<2^(k+1), k>=1.
The harmonic mass of these blocks decreases with k: pair a block's terms as
1/(2n)+1/(2n+1)<1/n to compare successive blocks. Therefore every partial cutoff
has 1-5/6<=c_U<=1, because the first negative block has mass 1/2+1/3=5/6 and
the following alternating blocks decrease. Thus |c_U|<=2 for ALL U, not only
at selected endpoints. The tail signs obey the same alphabet as mu, but not
the full actual inverse relation mu*One=delta.

For U=2^(K+1)-1, on a fixed compact small-t interval and any fixed 0<sigma<theta,
 sum_(2^k<=n<2^(k+1)) n^-s
 =[(2^(1-s)-1)/(1-s)] 2^[k(1-s)]+O_s(2^-k sigma).
The alternating geometric series has a nonzero leading term of order
U^(1-sigma); its errors sum to O_s(1). The leading coefficient is nonzero near
real s=sigma. On a sufficiently small interval, zeta(s) and zeta(s+u) are
nonzero (and away from their poles), so for this sequence
 integral_small_t |zeta(s+u)A_U(s)^p[1+zeta(s)(c_U-A_U(s))]^m/s|^2
 >=c U^[2(p+m)(1-sigma)] along those cutoffs.              (8.1)
In the present case this is U^(11/2), exceeding U^(12/5).

Hence coefficient bounds, the bounded harmonic sum, atomic harmonic-null
cutoff transitions, integer signs, and generic Gram/PSD algebra cannot imply
the missing improved estimate. An automatic closure rule using only these
hypotheses would be false. This does not exclude using mu*One=delta together
with a new complete signed divisor/cofactor correlation. The payable source
head is one such actual-source consequence, but not the missing tail estimate.

9. Common finite horizon versus global cutoff algebra

The truncated-inverse decomposition binds the ACTUAL core on integers n<=N,
using one common N=4X-1 for both parent and child. It may discard monomials only
after proving their coefficient support exceeds N. Changing U changes the
decomposition and its components; the COMPLETE joined prefix stays the same
on that common domain. Its cutoff transition there can be identically zero.
That is bookkeeping invariance, not a bound for its value or for an expanded
component. The discarded coefficient tail and all analytic compensation remain
outside any attempted global Mellin identity for that finite-domain join.

Do not promote finite inverse equality to global meromorphic equality. In
particular it cannot cross zeros of zeta below 1/2 for the full original source.
Global finite-polynomial F_U and its analytic center are valid objects, but their
source recoverability is the previously certified finite-horizon rule with
opposite drifts, complete remaining terms and all channel bindings retained.
A coupled proof may be better than paying each component. The complete core
should therefore have its own joined work/potential lane, not be replaced by
a compulsory list of independent positive component bounds.

10. Concrete next certificate interface and conclusions

Each generated rule should record: source binding and common finite horizon;
raw or exact matched-head-corrected word; sigma and contour trust premises;
all cutoff endpoints; complete residue transition; old or child integration
domain; explicit moving-shell theorem application; exact J_0,J_o,J_e or shell
polynomial; every contracted Gram coefficient; recovery matrix and finite base;
required ALL-integer or maximum-partial coverage; normalized exponent and log
degree; and a precise uniform OPEN forcing inequality.

Finite samples, compact-height quadrature and a sampled Gaussian Gram can check
algebra or suggest work patterns. They cannot certify the complete |t|<H_U
integral, its uniform U exponent, or an all-scale recurrence constant. Record
them as diagnostics, never as theorem payment. In particular no observations
made by a future bounded study retroactively prove (6.3).

Proved here: exact full transition and residue rules; exact odd/even and head
zero-sign work; payable complete arithmetic head; fixed-ratio moving-shell
payment using inherited uniform imports; sound recurrence/all-integer recovery;
and a scoped generic obstruction. Open: actual signed forcing for the joined
tail or full core. The new head primitive removes a rigorously payable physical
piece, while the signed work grammar gives a falsifiable arithmetic interface.
It does not establish its missing uniform bound or RH.
