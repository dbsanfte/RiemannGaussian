1. PRIMEWISE ROOT-LIMIT TRANSFER

  T(f)=EXP_A(LOG_*(f)),
  (a A b)(n)=sum_{ij=n}1[v_p(i)&v_p(j)=0 for every prime p]*a(i)*b(j).

The product A is classical infinitary convolution, expressed by binary
no-carry exponent addition. T is nonlinear and has the exact inverse
EXP_*(LOG_A(f)). It satisfies T(f*g)=T(f) A T(g) on all normalized inputs.
It is the coefficientwise limit of a qth ordinary root followed by q-fold
A convolution, as integer q->infinity. This limit also has a proved analytic
meaning for the zeta applications.

set bits of e: multiply reciprocal sums of the bit values in each block,
then sum over partitions. Its Dirichlet series satisfies H=zeta*R, where R
is holomorphic and nonzero on Re(s)>1/2. One exact Bernstein certificate
proves that statement for EVERY integer q>=2 and the infinite-order limit:
|R_q(x)-1|<=0.6945190291<1 on |x|<=0.71 for each local correction.

variables, rather than a finite-data extrapolation.

and p^e*q and write F=F_0+y F_1, y^2=0. Then
  T(F)=t+y(t A k), S(F)=t+y B(t,k),

4 AND 5. FLOOR AND CEILING INTEGER-SIZE TRANSFERS

Fix any integer b>=2. Compute h(n)=floor(log_b(n)) and H(n)=ceil(log_b(n))
by exact integer comparisons. The products are
  (a Kminus d)(n)=sum_{ij=n}1[h(ij)=h(i)+h(j)]*a(i)*d(j),
  (a Kplus d)(n)=sum_{ij=n}1[H(ij)=H(i)+H(j)]*a(i)*d(j),
and the transfers are U_-=EXP_Kminus LOG_*, U_+=EXP_Kplus LOG_*.
Both products are commutative, associative and unital. Their complementary
carry-only products are associative without a unit; each pencil K_0+t*C
is associative for every scalar t. Nonzero t gives an established scalar
weight transport. The transfers have explicit inverses, preserve normalized
unit groups, and are coefficientwise limits of q-fold K products of ordinary
qth roots. Every zeta-input lift and its limit has coefficients in [0,1].
This proves locally uniform root-limit convergence on Re(s)>1.

Actual integer values change the rule: in base 2, the floor product allows
(2,3) but rejects (5,7). The floor zeta-transfer coefficients at 6 and 35
are 1 and 0; the ceiling coefficients are 1 and 1. This distinguishes actual
prime relabeling, while still extending to monoids with assigned sizes.
The independent cycle-height calculation verifies 27 sparse large-integer
profiles without substituting zeros for unavailable coefficients.

The additive gcd condition gcd(i+j,ij)=1 rediscovers unitary convolution;

Floor-quotient kernels collapse to ordinary LOG above n=2*m+2.

6. PRIME-BINARY PARTITION REGULARIZER

Reindex additive degree as n-1, and let C split its occupied binary bits
without carry. Let k(n)=popcount(n-1) and M(f)(n)=f(n)/k(n)!. The selected
operation is

  N_beta(f)=M EXP_C(beta LOG_Dirichlet(f)), f(1)=1.

It has an exact inverse at beta=1 and is a unit-group isomorphism from
ordinary Dirichlet convolution D to the factorial-transported subset
product B. Its parameter law is N_alpha(f) B N_beta(f)=N_(alpha+beta)(f).
The correct raw additive unit is delta_0; explicit reindexing gives
delta_1. Additive LOG needs an n-1 factor bound, not D's floor(log2 n).
The wrong bound misses the exact coefficient 1/15 at n=16 for delta_1+delta_2.

For zeta input u(n)=1, partition the occupied bits of n-1 into blocks.
A block contributes beta/r if its numeric value plus one equals p^r,
and zero otherwise. Sum the block products over partitions and divide
by k(n)! to obtain F_beta(n). Actual prime labels matter: F_1(7)=1 and
F_1(11)=3/4, although both inputs are prime with the same bit count.

The 384-program recurrence grammar retains 12 training fits and one
fresh survivor. The universal partition proof gives, for even n,
  F_beta(n)=sum_(r=1)^v2(n) beta/r*(k(n)-r)!/k(n)!*F_beta(n-2^r+1).
The block containing bit zero must represent an even prime power, 2^r.
The independent anchored-partition verifier checks all search decisions
and four parameter profiles through 1024, separately from the production
Euler recursion. All ten independent exact gates pass.

An entire Bell-polynomial majorant proves absolute/local-uniform Dirichlet
convergence on Re(s)>0 for every complex beta, jointly entire in beta.
For beta in [0,1], normalized carry-free q-fold lifts of the ordinary
zeta^(beta/q) coefficients converge locally uniformly there. The raw
ordinary-additive EXP_A LOG_D calibration instead has no convergent
Dirichlet half-plane for any positive parameter. These are proved domain
statements, not extrapolations from finite coefficient growth.

Six fresh native-zeta cases recover exact input LOG(zeta) coefficients
through 64, assuming their classical finite value set but not supplying
prime-power locations. Independently, a different real part and precision
recover through 128, with full arithmetic profiles doubled to 2048 and
different infinite Bell tails. This is explicitly a coefficient-origin
bridge for N, not a closed scalar expression for N(s) in zeta(s).
The reverse calibration V=EXP_D LOG_A also passes direct native-zeta
comparisons via two distinct representations:
  V(s)=exp(sum_(k>=1)(zeta(s+k)-1)), initially Re(s)>0.
All four independent analytic gates and smaller perturbation controls
pass. That stage brought the cumulative comparison-case count to 96.

Subset convolution, formal LOG/EXP, diagonal transport and Bell partitions

7. ORDINARY-ROOT-SENSITIVE QUADRATIC DIFFERENCE TRANSFER

On augmentation sequences h(1)=0, let E_a push coefficient h(n) to
n(n+a), a>=0. Form the classical finite-difference combination
  A_m=sum_(a=0)^m (-1)^(m-a)*binomial(m,a)*E_a,
and define
  P_(m,k,beta)(f)=EXP_D(beta*(I+A_m^k)*LOG_D(f)), m,k>=1.

This nonlinear normalized unit-group automorphism at beta=1 has a
coefficient-local inverse. Its root laws use ordinary D convolution:
P_beta(f)=P_1(f)^{*beta}, and P_alpha*P_beta=P_(alpha+beta).
The ordinary-zeta root relation therefore survives the type gate that
rejected the earlier subset regularizer's root interpretation.
The zeta-input coefficients at 20 and 45 are -1 and 1 at beta=1; swapping
primes 2 and 3 sends one index to the other. This shows actual-prime-label
dependence. The coefficient at 20 also rejects multiplicativity of the
output, since its coefficients at the coprime indices 4 and 5 are both 1.

The weight grammar scores 1120 primitive vectors, with six training fits
and one fresh survivor: the classical third-forward-difference weights
(-1,3,-3,1). This is a positive known-operator calibration. A separate
1890-program grammar learns the nonlinear-iteration cancellation law
  r(m,k)=m*(2^k-1), image degree d=2^k.
The 80-program leading grammar learns
  c_(k+1)(s)=(-1)^m*(2^k*s+r_k)_m*c_k(s), c_0=1.
Both leave one survivor after fresh checks. Universal moment arguments
prove the hierarchy for all m,k; exact order excludes the roots of the
leading polynomial. Thirty-nine exact Taylor profiles and seven independent
exact gates pass. A sparse inverse check through 10^8 rejects a shortcut
whose first wrong coefficient is -2/7 at n=65536.

For zeta input,
  H_(m,k,beta)(s)=zeta(s)^beta*exp(beta*Delta_(m,k)(s)), initially Re(s)>1.
The grouped correction is holomorphic on
  Re(s)>-m+(m+1)/2^k,
and its exponential never vanishes. The selected m=2,k=2 instance has
correction domain Re(s)>-5/4. At beta=1 the function preserves every
zeta zero and its multiplicity in that domain. Fractional parameters
retain ordinary-zeta monodromy. This yields an RH-equivalent dyadic-root
criterion, without a new estimate proving the needed continuation.

The negative domain is a grouped analytic continuation. It is not
convergence of the ordinary coefficient series: at p^(2^k), prime p>m,
