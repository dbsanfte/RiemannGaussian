1. A PRIME-EXTRACTION CALIBRATION WITH A UNIVERSAL INVERSE

For h(1)=0 define U_k h(n)=h(m) when n=m^k with integer m>=2, and zero
otherwise; set U_k h(1)=0. Each coefficient uses finitely many k. The
operators obey U_a U_b=U_(ab). Put

 V=sum_(k>=1) U_k/k,   W=sum_(k>=1) mu(k)*U_k/k.

Coefficient-local rearrangement gives W V=V W=identity, since the
coefficient of U_r in either product is (1/r)sum_(d|r)mu(d), equal to
one for r=1 and zero otherwise. Thus

 P(f)=W LOG_D f,   E(g)=EXP_D(V g)

are inverse maps between normalized ordinary Dirichlet units and
arithmetic functions g with g(1)=0. P is nonlinear in f but linear in
LOG coordinates. It obeys P(f^beta_D)=beta P(f), rather than a
normalized-unit output-root law. It is the established Euler-product /
plethystic logarithm framework, used here as a known-operator recovery.

For zeta's ordinary input u(n)=1, LOG_D u(p^e)=1/e and is zero at other
n>1. At n=p^e,
 P(u)(p^e)=(1/e)sum_(d|e)mu(d)=1[e=1].
It vanishes on every integer with at least two distinct prime factors.
Consequently P(u) is EXACTLY the prime indicator for every positive
integer, not merely on the observed prefix. Summatory evaluation and
a residue mask therefore give, for every real X and integer q>=1,

 sum_(n<=X)P(u)(n)=pi(X),
 sum_(n<=X,n=a mod q)P(u)(n)=pi(X;q,a).

The empirical weight grammar scores 42 programs formed from six
arithmetic atoms and powers k^-3,...,k^3. Exponents e=1..8 train the
annihilation law; e=9..32 hold it out. It uniquely recovers mu(k)/k.
Independent coefficient-root tests extend to exponent 64. The complete
LOG/input-output prefix through 4096, generic rational inverse controls,
and changed implementations are checked exactly. These are finite
implementation checks; the preceding proof supplies all-integer scope.
This extraction alone does not improve any prime-distribution bound.

Multiplying LOG_D u(n) by log n gives the classical von Mangoldt
coefficient Lambda(p^e)=log p, zero elsewhere. Hence on Re(s)>1,
 sum_n Lambda(n)n^-s=-zeta'(s)/zeta(s).
Lambda includes prime POWERS. A statistic weighted by Lambda must not
be called an unweighted prime count without explicitly removing them.

2. SEARCHED FLOOR FILTERS WITH CERTIFICATES VALID AT EVERY SCALE

For integer a=1..A and integers c_a with sum_a a*c_a=0 define

 w_c(t)=sum_a c_a floor(a*t),
 T_c(X)=sum_n Lambda(n) w_c(X/n),   X>=0.

The sum is finite, since floor(aX/n)=0 for n>A X. The balance condition
makes w_c(t+1)=w_c(t). On [0,1) it is constant on half-open intervals
whose endpoints are k/a. Checking its value at EVERY such left endpoint
is a universal positivity certificate: w_c(t)>=0 for ALL real t>=0.
This is not an extrapolation from a numerical grid. Endpoint conventions
are included because floor is right-continuous.

The implemented grammar exhausts 5^7=78125 coefficient vectors with
c_a in [-2,2], A=7, height -sum c_a in {1,2}, and coefficient gcd one.
There are 543 balanced primitive candidates; 341 pass the coarse
quarter grid, and precisely 67 have the complete positivity certificate.
Fifteen have height one. All 52 height-two survivors in THIS BOUNDED
GRAMMAR are sums of two of those height-one survivors. This is not a
decomposition theorem for every possible factorial-ratio kernel.

The independent checker replaces rational breakpoints with the complete
integer grid of denominator lcm(1,...,7)=420. That grid contains every
jump and certifies every constant interval. It re-enumerates the full
bounded grammar and verifies the same 67 vectors and decompositions.

A genuine coarse-grid false positive is c=(-2,-2,0,1,2,1,-2). It passes

zeta trace is -2 log 2, with factorial ratio 1/4. Thus a sampled positive
kernel can even fail on an exact small-prime input at a missed scale.

The divisor identity sum_(d|n)Lambda(d)=log n proves, by finite swapping,

 T_c(X)=sum_a c_a log(floor(aX)!).                         (1)

Equivalently, the exponent of p on either side's multiplicative version
is sum_(k>=1) w_c(X/p^k). This is the familiar factorial-ratio / Legendre
valuation construction. Positivity of w_c proves that the ratio in (1)
is an integer for EVERY real X>=0. It also gives nonnegative weighted

but is an established framework, not a new estimate on actual primes.

The selected smallest-support kernel is c_1=-2,c_2=1:

 (M T_c)(X)=I_c(X):=sum_a c_a psi(aX),

Indeed T_c(X)=sum_(m>=1) I_c(X/m); swapping the finite m,d sums in its

At X=5, T(5)=log 252>0 but
 I(5)=psi(10)-2psi(5)=log(7/10)<0.                        (3)
This exact example rejects inheritance of positivity under decoding.

4. WHY THESE EXACT ALL-SCALE TRACES ERASE ZETA-ZERO INFORMATION

Use the Mellin convention int_0^infinity F(X)X^(-s-1)dX and let
A_c(s)=sum_a c_a*a^s. Absolute convergence for Re(s)>1 justifies

 Mellin(w_c)=zeta(s)*A_c(s)/s,
 Mellin(T_c)=-zeta'(s)*A_c(s)/s,
 Mellin(I_c)=-(zeta'(s)/zeta(s))*A_c(s)/s.                 (5)

For the first formula, floor(aX)=sum_(j>=1)1[X>=j/a] and direct
integration gives a^s*zeta(s)/s. Multiplication by the Lambda Dirichlet
series gives the other formulas. Balance makes w_c bounded, so its
Mellin integral itself converges on Re(s)>0 and the displayed symbol
continues there. The zeta factor in w_c cancels EVERY zeta-zero pole
of the logarithmic derivative in T_c. No choice of a finite floor
combination removes this structural cancellation.

For the central kernel A(s)=2^s-2. It has zeros only at
1+2*pi*i*j/log 2. In the nontrivial-zero strip Re(s)<1,
 |A(s)|>=2-2^Re(s)>0.
At a zeta zero rho of multiplicity m, Mellin(I) therefore has a simple
pole with residue -m*A(rho)/rho. Mellin(T) is holomorphic there. Native
zeta derivative and symbol bounds verify this distinction on two reused
certified simple-zero disks near heights 14.134725 and 21.022040.
Their disks contain no newly located zero. This distinguishes an
all-scale prime identity from retention of ordinary-zeta root information.

At s=1, A has a simple zero. Mellin(I)'s main simple pole is cancelled,
whereas Mellin(T) retains a simple pole with residue C=2 log 2.
Stirling's formula gives T(X)=C X+O(log X) for X>=1, also for real X
with the factorial arguments floored. Subtracting that main term does
not bypass decoding. The exact identity is

 I(X)=sum_(m<=X)mu(m)[T(X/m)-C X/m]
                  + C X sum_(m<=X)mu(m)/m.              (6)

Both terms still require cancellation of actual arithmetic coefficients.
An unconditional smooth trace and its asymptotic alone give no new
critical-half-plane estimate on I. A bound |I(X)|<=C_epsilon*X^(1/2+epsilon)
for EVERY X>=1 and EVERY epsilon>0 would make its Mellin integral
holomorphic on Re(s)>1/2 and exclude right-half nontrivial zeros by (5).
No such bound is proved or inferred from this experiment. The holomorphy
of the continued central Mellin(I) in 1/2<Re(s)<1 is RH-equivalent.

5. THE SHARP GENERIC DECODER NORM BARRIER

Let B_sigma consist of functions F(Y)=0 for Y<1, with norm
sup_(Y>=1)|F(Y)|/Y^sigma. For real sigma>1,

 ||M||_(B_sigma->B_sigma)=sum_(m>=1)|mu(m)|m^-sigma
                        =zeta(sigma)/zeta(2sigma).       (7)

The upper bound is the triangle inequality. At a chosen integer X=N,
set F(N/m)=mu(m)*(N/m)^sigma for m<=N and zero elsewhere. Its norm
is at most one and (M F)(N)/N^sigma=sum_(m<=N)|mu(m)|m^-sigma.
Taking N to infinity proves the matching lower bound. The Euler product
prod_p(1+p^-sigma)=zeta(sigma)/zeta(2sigma) proves the last equality.

The norm is infinite for sigma<=1. An elementary all-scale argument
uses the union bound for divisibility by squares: among 1,...,N,
the number Q(N) of squarefree integers is at least
delta*N, delta=2-zeta(2)>0. In each block (N,4N],
Q(4N)-Q(N)>=(4delta-1)N, so its contribution to sum |mu(m)|/m is
at least delta-1/4>0. Disjoint power-four blocks prove divergence.
For sigma<1 the weights only increase. No RH, PNT or probabilistic
independence assumption is used. Exact saturation controls and native
positive bounds for delta and the block constant verify implementations.

This is a GENERIC operator norm statement. It does not rule out a new,
strong cancellation estimate on the specific arithmetic trace inputs.
That additional prime-specific estimate is the missing high-priority
search target; moving or renaming a generic norm does not provide it.
