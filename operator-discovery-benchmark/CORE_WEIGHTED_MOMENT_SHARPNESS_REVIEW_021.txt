CORE WEIGHTED MOMENT SHARPNESS REVIEW 021
Independent analytic review; 2026-10-10
Scope: proposed reusable signed finite-weight moment grammar. No source
observations, numerical experiments, tests, or changes to frozen files.

1. Conclusion and exact scope

The squared U cost U^[2(p+m)(1-sigma)] cannot be uniformly decreased over
the generic class of real finite weights |a_n|<=1, a_1=1, c=A(1), |c|<=2.
Exact harmonic cancellation T(1)=0, where T=c-A, does not change this
conclusion. An elementary rational two-block construction gives c=1 and
saturates that exponent already on one fixed small t interval. It applies
to the FULL expression

 F_U(s)=zeta(s+u) A_U(s)^p [1+zeta(s)(c-A_U(s))]^m,
 theta=1-u, 0<sigma<min(1/2,theta), p>=0, m>=1.

The fixed parameters p,m,sigma,theta remain independent of U. This is a
generic weight-class obstruction to a smaller global vertical norm cost,
not an obstruction for actual Mobius weights. It does not prove a lower
bound on a specified block X<=n<2X with U=floor(N^alpha), nor on the joined
signed core after cancellations with its other terms. Actual arithmetic
identities, source-specific Gram estimates, localization in x, and complete
signed recurrences can evade this generic-class obstruction.

The result is compatible with the previous unconditional centered A^2 E^2
component payment: that payment uses the present U exponent and selects a
cutoff for which its X exponent is nonpositive. No new core/RH claim follows.

2. Exact paired-dilation construction

Let Q>=2 be an integer and U=4Q. Set

 a_1=1;
 a_n=1/2 for Q<n<=2Q;
 a_(2n)=-1 for Q<n<=2Q;
 all other a_k=0, including odd k in the upper block.

The lower and upper supports are disjoint. All weights are exact rationals
in [-1,1], including the unit. Each pair has harmonic sum

 (1/2)/n - 1/(2n)=0.

Consequently the complete tail harmonic sum is exactly zero, A_U(1)=1,
and c=1. This is not a numerical cancellation or an asymptotic definition
of c. With S_Q(s)=sum_(Q<n<=2Q) n^(-s), exactly

 A_U(s)=1+d(s) S_Q(s), d(s)=1/2-2^(-s),
 T_U(s)=c-A_U(s)=-d(s) S_Q(s),
 E_U(s)=1+zeta(s) T_U(s).

In particular T_U(1)=0. Thus the apparent pole of E_U at s=1 is removable,
and the full F_U has only the possible simple pole at theta on the positive
strip. Expanding E_U and bounding its pieces before restoring that pole
cancellation is not a license to cross s=1.

For the fixed sigma in section 1, define delta_sigma=2^(-sigma)-1/2>0.
Reverse triangle inequality gives |d(sigma+it)|>=delta_sigma for every t.
Rotate S_Q by (sqrt(2)*Q)^(it). Since n/(sqrt(2)*Q) lies between 1/sqrt(2)
and sqrt(2), for |t|<=1/(2 log 2) every rotated summand has argument with
absolute value at most 1/4. Its real part is at least half its magnitude.
Therefore, uniformly on that whole interval,

 |S_Q(sigma+it)| >= (1/2) sum_(Q<n<=2Q)n^(-sigma)
                   >= 2^(-sigma-1) Q^(1-sigma),
 |T_U(sigma+it)| >= k_sigma Q^(1-sigma),
 k_sigma=delta_sigma*2^(-sigma-1)>0.

Both zeta(sigma) and zeta(sigma+u) are nonzero. Indeed for a real 0<a<1,
the positive alternating eta series divided by 1-2^(1-a)<0 gives zeta(a)<0.
This fact requires no zero-free strip or RH premise. Continuity therefore
provides a fixed t0>0, t0<=1/(2 log 2), and positive z0,z1 such that

 |zeta(sigma+it)|>=z0,
 |zeta(sigma+u+it)|>=z1, for |t|<=t0.

These constants depend on the fixed lines, not Q. For all sufficiently
large Q, the unit terms cannot cancel the large tails:

 |A_U(sigma+it)| >= (k_sigma/2) Q^(1-sigma),
 |E_U(sigma+it)| >= (z0*k_sigma/2) Q^(1-sigma).

It follows, with D=sqrt(sigma^2+t0^2), that

 |F_U(sigma+it)/(sigma+it)|
 >= (z1/D)(k_sigma/2)^p(z0*k_sigma/2)^m
       Q^[(p+m)(1-sigma)], for |t|<=t0.

Integrating the square gives a positive fixed constant times
U^[2(p+m)(1-sigma)]. The same lower bound holds with any fixed positive
Gaussian window exp(-t^2/H^2), H>0. Thus neither harmonic-nullness nor the
exact dependence c=A(1) removes the generic low-height obstruction.

The proof also works for arbitrarily large nonmultiples of four by taking
Q=floor(U/4) and leaving all unused indices zero. A subsequence U=4Q alone
already refutes a uniform smaller U exponent, even with fixed log losses.

3. A genuine constant-on-each-block variant

If a benchmark requires both complete dyadic intervals, rather than a sparse
dilated upper interval, let

 L_Q=sum_(Q<n<=2Q)1/n, H_Q=sum_(2Q<n<=4Q)1/n,
 lambda_Q=L_Q/H_Q,
 a_1=1, a_n=1 on (Q,2Q], a_n=-lambda_Q on (2Q,4Q].

Exactly H_Q>L_Q>0: pair the upper indices 2k-1,2k for Q<k<=2Q and note
1/(2k-1)+1/(2k)>1/k. Thus 0<lambda_Q<1, all weights satisfy the bound,
and the complete tail harmonic sum L_Q-lambda_Q H_Q vanishes exactly.
Again c=1. These are rational finite harmonic sums, not fitted coefficients.

Elementary integral estimates imply lambda_Q=1+O(1/Q). Uniformly for t in
a fixed compact interval and s=sigma+it,

 Q^(s-1)(A_U(s)-1)
  -> I(s)=(1-2^(1-s)) integral_1^2 x^(-s) dx.

The error is O(1/Q) on the compact s set; it follows, for example, by bounding
the derivative of x^(-s) in the Riemann sums and using the harmonic sum
integral remainder. At t=0, I(sigma)<0 when sigma<1. Continuity gives a
fixed small interval on which its magnitude is bounded below. Consequently
this variant has the same low-height sharpness, including the full E and F.
The paired-dilation family has the simpler all-Q certificate; the contiguous
family has the simpler block description. Either may be found by a detector.

4. Full centering and the integer staircase

Let h have Dirichlet function A^p E^m and let M=A(theta)^p E(theta)^m.
Write P(x)=sum_(n<=x)(K*h)(n), K(n)=n^(-u), and
C(x)=P(x)-(M/theta)x^theta for ALL x>0. In particular the interval 0<x<1
has C(x)=-(M/theta)x^theta. The Gaussian Perron/Mellin argument in review018
identifies the centered transform on Re s=sigma with F_U(s)/s itself.
One must not subtract a residue rational function a second time from that
transform. The pole crossing already supplied the physical centering term.

Whenever the complete vertical L2 norm is finite, that reviewed bridge gives

 integral_0^infinity |C(x)|^2 x^(-2sigma-1) dx
  =(1/(2pi)) integral_R |F_U(sigma+it)/(sigma+it)|^2 dt.

The compact-height lower bound therefore survives FULL centering. If the
high-height integral diverges, it cannot support a proposed finite global
bound in the first place. A finite Gaussian-window norm and its Gaussian
smoothed physical counterpart still exhibit the head obstruction. No
ordinary convergence statement for M is inferred from analytic continuation:
the supplied generic Em prefix route gives rho=1-1/m, and its Abel moment
is licensed only when rho<theta. For m=2, theta=2/3 this condition holds.

For the witness, |M|<=C U^[(p+m)(1-theta)], using the elementary finite mass
bound on the theta line and the fixed zeta(theta). This squared exponent is
strictly lower than 2(p+m)(1-sigma), because sigma<theta.
Put C_n=P(n)-(M/theta)n^theta, including n=1. On each [n,n+1),
C(x)=C_n-(M/theta)(x^theta-n^theta). Bounding the square by twice the two
squares and integrating yields

 integral_0^infinity |C(x)|^2 x^(-2sigma-1) dx
 <=2 sum_(n>=1)|C_n|^2 n^(-2sigma-1)+C_(sigma,theta)|M|^2.

Here the finite constant includes 0<x<1 and the convergent cell-error
integral of x^(2theta-2sigma-3). Consequently the complete integer weighted
global norm has the same asymptotic lower U exponent whenever the bridge is
available; the full J_theta staircase correction cannot remove it. This
argument makes no lower-bound claim for arbitrary polynomial channels or
projected cells: some channels can kill a particular witness. It also makes
no claim about the actual source's complete signed join, other drifts, or -e.

5. Exact Gaussian Fourier/Mellin Gram identity

For ANY finite complex coefficient array b_n, real sigma, and H>0, define
B(s)=sum_n b_n n^(-s). Finite expansion and the Gaussian Fourier integral
give exactly

 integral_R exp(-t^2/H^2)|B(sigma+it)|^2 dt
 =sqrt(pi)*H sum_(r,n) b_r conjugate(b_n)(rn)^(-sigma)
        exp[-(H^2/4)(log(r/n))^2].

For real coefficients this is the requested signed b_r b_n identity.
The diagonal terms plus twice each r<n term must be retained with their
actual signs. It is not equal to the same sum of absolute coefficient
products, and it is not equal to its diagonal except in a limiting regime.

For completeness, the scalar identity follows without a contour shift.
For J(w)=integral_R exp(-t^2/H^2)exp(-iwt)dt, differentiation and integration
by parts give J'(w)=-(H^2 w/2)J(w); J(0)=sqrt(pi)H. Thus
J(w)=sqrt(pi)H exp(-H^2 w^2/4). Substitution w=log(r/n) proves the identity.

The centered Gaussian exp[-(t-T)^2/H^2] introduces the additional phase
exp[-iT log(r/n)]. For real weights, pairing symmetric terms gives cosine
cos[T log(r/n)]. This supplies a sign-sensitive low-, medium-, or high-height
finite-polynomial identity with no unmentioned polynomial-length threshold.

The Gram matrix is positive definite for distinct occupied integers and
H>0: a zero quadratic form would make the finite exponential polynomial
zero almost everywhere, hence identically zero and all its coefficients
zero. At fixed finite support, as H tends to infinity, the matrix divided
by sqrt(pi)H tends to the diagonal. As H tends to zero the normalized matrix
tends to the rank-one matrix (rn)^(-sigma). Those are limits, not exact
diagonal licenses at finite H. Harmonic cancellation only forces B(1)=0;
it does not force B(sigma+it)=0 on an interval for sigma<1/2.

6. A detector-discoverable construction grammar

The reusable input grammar can be stated before any benchmark observations:

(a) Enumerate disjoint integer intervals at rational fractions of U and
    integer dilations d in a bounded declared list. Include constant-on-block
    templates and sparse dilation images. Include the unit as a fixed atom.
(b) Introduce rational amplitudes, solve the exact harmonic linear equation
    for the tail, and normalize nonzero nullspace directions to infinity
    norm <=1. Reject overlapping assignments, lost units, or |c|>2.
(c) For dilation pairs the equation is x+y/d=0. Its exact nullspace solver
    generates (1,-d) and normalization generates (1/d,-1). These coefficients
    are outputs, not nominated candidate answers. For two full blocks the
    equation is L_Q*x+H_Q*y=0; the exact solver produces the harmonic ratio.
(d) Compile the exact finite Mellin polynomial, including c, then compute
    its asymptotic block profile and prove a nonzero value at a fixed real
    sigma. An interval-stability lemma generates a fixed low-height window.
(e) Independently check the coefficient/nullspace identity, magnitude
    bounds, exact c, profile, remainder, and full F centering scope.

Enumerating this grammar does not amount to selecting a particular Mobius
cutoff or discovering an actual arithmetic bound. It is an adversarial
generic calibration. A finite grammar may fail to find a witness without
the generic class being better behaved; report only its declared coverage.

A useful exact generic benchmark should provide rational source arrays and
the unit, require recomputation of c rather than accepting a declared c,
and ask for the FULL signed Gaussian Gram. Cases should include harmonic-null
tails, both signs of cross terms, dilations, duplicate frequencies merged by
exact convolution, a changed sigma/H/window center, and an unbalanced tail
that must be rejected. A later numerical benchmark can compare direct
certified Gaussian integration with the independently expanded Gram, using
outward-rounded log/exp intervals and a certified tail. No such experiment
was run in this review.

7. A useful actual-weight bound language, and its limitations

The finite Gram identity can be compiled for A, T=c-A, and every finite
Dirichlet convolution B_j=A^p T^j. Its length is at most U^(p+j), not U.
The complete source polynomial is exactly

 F_U(s)=zeta(s+u) sum_(j=0)^m binom(m,j) zeta(s)^j B_j(s).

To retain joint cancellations, a more capable moment grammar must estimate
the Hermitian matrix with entries

 integral w(t)|zeta(s+u)/s|^2 zeta(s)^j conjugate(zeta(s)^k)
             B_j(s) conjugate(B_k(s)) dt,

and contract it with BOTH binomial coefficient vectors. It must not pretend
that the finite-polynomial Gaussian Gram already supplies this zeta-weighted
matrix. Certified compact-line zeta enclosures can bound the low-height head;
twisted moment rules or other uniform analytic providers can bound high
height regimes. All uncovered intermediate and low-height ranges must be
paid separately. The full E combination still removes its pole at 1 before
the contour proof. Expanding individual branches never discards their joint
principal-part cancellation or the complete J_theta compensator.

Off-critical twisted-polynomial rules must carry the coefficient shift
n^(1/2-sigma) and the resulting normalization exponent. A theorem restricted
to polynomial length R<=T^delta cannot be applied at fixed T as U grows.
Absolute/Hilbert fallback costs in that missing head can restore the generic
U exponent just proved sharp. Thus a promising high-height gain alone does
not pay a higher-degree actual component. Source-specific coefficient
information or a different complete signed target is needed to beat that
head, rather than relabeling it as negligible.

Numerically small signed Gram values at several U and H are observations,
not a uniform bound for every source cutoff. Executable discovery can propose
and rank arithmetic inequalities from exact signed arrays, but a paid theorem
must identify a verified all-cutoff rule, all-height coverage, complete
centering, ordinary moment convergence where required, and a fixed uniform
constant. If these are open, the output remains a missing-bound task. This
review supplies no proof of a uniform Mobius Gram gain and no RH closure.

8. Provenance and final status

The sharpness construction, Gaussian Gram proof, finite harmonic nullspace,
and comparison of integer cells are elementary derivations written above.
The full Gaussian Perron/Mellin centering bridge is the explicitly reviewed
rule in JOINT_REMAINDER_MOMENT_REVIEW_018.txt, not a newly assumed identity.
The real-line zeta sign uses the primary NIST DLMF alternating representation
at https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.2.E3; its meromorphic continuation and sole pole
are stated at https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.2. These pages were consulted, without
any source-data observation. No numerical value of a zeta lower bound is
needed; continuity gives fixed symbolic constants, and a future numerical
implementation would certify any chosen interval endpoints.

Backend020 and this review agree that a high-height twisted rule cannot erase
the uncovered low-height polynomial head. Stronger generic conclusions from
the same |a_n|<=1, |c|<=2 and harmonic-null hypotheses are ruled out for the
global norm exponent. Stronger actual Mobius conclusions remain open and
can be legitimate next search tasks. Edits stopped after this review.
