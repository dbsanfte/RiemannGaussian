TRUNCATED INVERSE REPRESENTATIONS AND SIGNED NONNEGATIVE POTENTIALS

This stage separates three claims: response-inferred reconstruction weights,
an admitted universal representation identity, and a proposed actual signed
energy inequality. The first two do not prove the third. No global critical
signed estimate or RH conclusion is asserted from finite numerical passes.

1. UNIVERSAL REPRESENTATION AND EXACT ERROR.

Let convolution mean (f*g)(n)=sum_(d|n) f(d)g(n/d), with unit delta at n=1.
For ANY arithmetic reference h with h(1)=1, let a*h=delta. Multiplicativity
is unnecessary. Set a_u(n)=a(n) for n<=u and zero otherwise, u>=0 integer.
All powers below are convolution powers, with zeroth power delta; k is ANY
positive integer. Rational references suffice for exact response learning.

Let b=a_u*h and R=delta-b=(a-a_u)*h. The finite geometric identity gives

 a=a_u*sum_(j=0)^(k-1) R^j+a*R^k.

Consequently the universal k-column identity is

 a=sum_(r=1)^k c(k,r) a_u^r*h^(r-1)
                         +(a-a_u)^k*h^(k-1),

where, AFTER analytical admission, c(k,r)=(-1)^(r-1) binom(k,r).
One direct proof expands the last term: its r=0 term is a, while its
r>=1 term is (-1)^r binom(k,r) a_u^r*h^(r-1), since a*h=delta.
This is a finite formal identity at every index, not an infinite-series
convergence argument. Summing coefficients through x gives the same exact
prefix identity, with the full error prefix retained.

The tail a-a_u is supported at indices >=u+1. Thus the error vanishes at
EVERY index n<(u+1)^k. Reconstruction through integer x>=1 is guaranteed
by the STRICT condition x<(u+1)^k. At n=(u+1)^k the error coefficient is
exactly a(u+1)^k; equality at the cutoff is generally unsafe. If the first
nonzero tail coefficient is at v>u, the first nonzero error is at v^k with
coefficient a(v)^k. Later error coefficients may cancel; no fuller support
set is assumed. For u=0 the identity still holds but no positive response
index satisfies the universal safe cutoff.

For x>=1, u=floor(x^(1/k)), computed by exact integer comparisons, gives
u^k<=x<(u+1)^k. At u=1 a safe choice is k=floor(log2 x)+1. The shortcut
k=ceil(log2 x), u=1 fails at x=2^k: for actual h(n)=1 the prefix error there
is (-1)^k. Using the true floor root gives u=2 at that equality and restores
safety. At x=1 the admitted arity is at least one, not ceil(log2 1)=0.
A finite evaluation or bit budget is not a semantic upper bound on k.

2. ADMISSION OF THE LEARNED COEFFICIENT RECURRENCE.

The learner receives numeric prefix responses from unrelated h and numeric
columns for a_u^r*h^(r-1), together with the declared broader dictionary.
It is not supplied the binomial coefficients or the expected recurrence.
Sparse exact fitting selects coefficient vectors and then a recurrence
program from coefficient-response tables. These outputs are frozen before
separate analytical admission and protected checks. A finite table alone
cannot certify continuation to all arities.

The root model uses c(k,0)=0 and c(k,r)=0 outside 1<=r<=k. The analytical
backend checks seed c(1,1)=1, the declared boundaries, consistency of stored
rows with the rule, and whether the DETECTED recurrence algebraically equals

 c(k+1,r)=c(k,r)-c(k,r-1)+1_(r=1),  k>=1.

This is symbolic rule admission, not a requirement that discovery match a
supplied coefficient vector. Write P_k(z)=sum_(r=1)^k c(k,r)z^r. The admitted
rule gives P_1(z)=z and P_(k+1)(z)=(1-z)P_k(z)+z. Therefore its error
polynomial satisfies 1-P_(k+1)(z)=(1-z)(1-P_k(z)); induction proves
1-P_k(z)=(1-z)^k for EVERY positive integer k. Substitute z=a_u*h and
multiply by a to obtain the representation and error above. The binomial
expression follows as a theorem AFTER admission and is then available for
cost accounting. If the detected rule, seed or boundaries fail admission,
numerical agreement at finitely many arities is not a universal certificate.

Identifiability also needs evidence. For h=delta all columns coincide, so
only the sum of weights is identified. At u=1 write h=delta+z, with z
supported at indices >=2. Through horizon N, h^(r-1) involves only powers
z^j with j<=floor(log2 N); hence column rank is at most floor(log2 N)+1.
Learning k independent weights in this setting requires N>=2^(k-1).
Distinct spike references h=delta+t*delta_2 at x=2^(k-1) give a Vandermonde
rank witness. Rank, sparse selection and protected replay must be recorded;
observed training coefficients are not a theorem established by tests.

3. ACTUAL INFORMATION AND CHARGED REPRESENTATION COSTS.

Generic inverse coefficients come from complete finite divisor inversion:
a(1)=1 and a(n)=-sum_(d|n,d<n)a(d)h(n/d). Generic h does not imply |a(n)|<=1.
For h(n)=1 this inversion is actual Mobius, using complete divisor rows,
not a prime or factor oracle supplied to the learner. An independent checker
may compare it with prime-derived arithmetic. A generic representation does
not by itself preserve the ordinary-zeta interpretation of an arbitrary h.

Only the low inverse prefix a_u is used in the columns, but the reference h
through the evaluation horizon and the complete divisibility geometry are
also inputs. For actual h=1, put tau_m=1^m, tau_0=delta and
D_m(t)=sum_(n<=t)tau_m(n). A column prefix is exactly

 sum_(n_1,...,n_r<=u) mu(n_1)...mu(n_r)
                              D_(r-1)(x/(n_1...n_r)).

Here D_m counts ordered positive-factor tuples, including unit factors. It
is an evaluated arithmetic object, not a free primitive. At a fixed root
cutoff u^k<=x, tuple enumeration uses up to sum_(r<=k)u^r<=k*x terms before
product aggregation, and every D_m evaluation has its own cost. Fixed-order
divisor asymptotics are not automatically uniform when the order grows.

The admitted coefficient norm is sum_r |c(k,r)|=2^k-1. For u=1 the columns
are D_(r-1)(x). If x>=2^(k-1), each is >=2^(r-1), by independently choosing
its factors from {1,2}. The fully charged unsigned prefix sum is therefore
at least (3^k-1)/2. On a dyadic block X=2^m, safe u=1 arity k=m+1 has this
large unsigned cost at every index in [X,2X), despite exact signed recovery.
Its squared unsigned block majorant grows at least on the scale
X^(1+2*log2 3), not at critical energy strength. Moving to the finite
nonunit-factor basis replaces large binomial weights by signs but retains
ordered-factorization terms; at n=2^m there are 2^(m-1) such terms although
mu(2^m)=0 for m>=2.

These are representation and unsigned-proof costs, NOT an unavoidable
exponential algorithmic lower bound. Dynamic convolution can share powers:
compute b=a_u*h and f_(r+1)=f_r*b starting at f_1=a_u. A basic dense divisor
implementation costs O(k*x*log x) arithmetic operations plus reference,
inverse-prefix, storage and exact bit costs. Binomial magnitudes require
O(k) bits, not 2^k bits. For actual h=1, tau_m(n)<=m^Omega(n)<=m^(log2 n);
analogous bounds with 2r-1 factors bound absolute column coefficients.
For k=O(log x), coefficient bit lengths can be O(log x*log log x), with
prefix summation and weights adding their costs. Arbitrary rational h can
have arbitrarily large heights and denominators. Neither a short inverse
prefix nor a high-arity identity is an RH gain with those costs suppressed.

4. THE SEPARATE NONNEGATIVE POTENTIAL TARGET.

For actual M(n)=sum_(d<=n)mu(d), define
A(X)=sum_(n=X)^(2X-1)M(n)^2 and E(X)=A(X)/X^2, at dyadic integers X>=1.
A separate learner fits a finite potential

 V(X)=sum_t lambda_t * A_(F_t)(X)/ceil(X^(p_t)),

where lambda_t>=0 are rational, p_t>=0 are rational, and F_t are declared
component-prefix functions from specified arities and cutoff rules.
Every square, term and normalizer is retained; the normalizer is the ceiling
of the FULL rational power, not a separately rounded fractional factor.
Exact comparisons or certified enclosures must determine each ceiling.
Unavailable arithmetic is missing, not a successful case or a refutation.

V is a finite nonnegative scalar at every scale where its defined arithmetic
is completed. Its rules and coefficients must be fixed independently of
X; explicit declared scale-dependent cutoffs are permitted as part of those
rules. For each component comparison that uses a common reconstruction of
both blocks, choose (u+1)^k>4X-1. If a potential rule changes the cutoff
between X and 2X, V(2X) is evaluated from that rule at 2X: it must not silently
be identified with a child component energy computed using the cutoff at X.
The scalar telescope below is valid for any well-defined nonnegative V;
component-level cancellations need their own exact treatment.

The still-unproved target is: there exists a finite integer q0 such that
for EVERY integer q>=q0 there exists a finite C_q>=0, independent of X,
with, for EVERY dyadic integer X>=1,

 E(2X)+V(2X)-E(X)-V(X)<=C_q*ceil(X^(1/q)).

If a candidate additionally freezes a specific C_q law, that exact law must
satisfy these quantifiers. Fitted constants at finitely many q do not supply
unobserved constants or prove any all-scale assertion. Individual low-q
failures do not refute an unknown q0; persistent or unbounded-q failures
require separate justification.

5. CONDITIONAL RH BRIDGE AND THE MISSING ARITHMETIC LEMMA.

ASSUME the preceding all-scale target and V(X)>=0, finite at each dyadic X.
For fixed q>=q0 telescope at X=2^J. Since ceil(2^(j/q))<=2*2^(j/q),

 E(X)<=E(1)+V(1)
             +2*C_q*(X^(1/q)-1)/(2^(1/q)-1).

Thus A(X)=O_q(X^(2+1/q)). No uniform q-growth bound on C_q is needed for this
cofinal inference; each q is fixed before taking X to infinity. A cofinal
sequence of admitted positive exponents also suffices. Dyadic Cauchy then
gives absolute convergence of the unfiltered Mellin integral of M for
Re(s)>1/2+1/(2q). Its exact identity, initially Re(s)>1, is

 Integral_1^infinity M(t)t^(-s-1)dt=1/(s*zeta(s)).

Analytic continuation rules out ordinary zeros in those half-planes.
Letting q tend to infinity and using ordinary functional symmetry gives RH,
conditionally. These are the same unfiltered recovery dependencies used in
critical_defect_proof.txt; arbitrary generic references do not acquire them.

The representation theorem proves neither nonnegativity of the signed
innovation nor its upper bound. All cross terms between component prefixes
remain in their energies. Full divisor inversion is an identity, not a new
signed-cancellation inequality. Even bounded generic inverses cannot give
such a universal critical statement: take a(n)=1 and h(n)=mu(n), so a*h=delta;
its prefix is floor(x) and its full signed defect is (28X^3-X)/3. This is a
counterexample to generic transfer, not to the actual h=1 target.

The missing lemma is the COMPLETE actual signed innovation bound, with the
frozen nonnegative potential, exact rational ceilings, specified component
cutoffs and a finite scale-independent constant at every sufficiently large
q. A learned identity, a resource-limited finite pass, an unsigned expansion,
or a successful fit to finitely many innovations does not establish it.
Protected evaluations and an analytical proof attempt after the queue is
frozen keep discovery, finite evidence and global arithmetic conclusions
separate. No global critical signed bound or RH proof is claimed here.

6. SUPPLIED ACTUAL COMPONENT ENVELOPES, NOT SIGNED INNOVATION CONTROL.

For actual h(n)=1 and ANY truncation u>=0, |a_u(n)|<=1 because a=mu.
The rth component has coefficient function f_r=a_u^r*1^(r-1), r>=1.
Taking absolute values in convolution therefore gives

 |f_r(n)|<=d_(2r-1)(n),  d_m=1^m.

For m>=1, its summatory function obeys

 sum_(j<=n)d_m(j)<=n*H_n^(m-1)<=n*(1+log n)^(m-1),

where H_n=sum_(j<=n)1/j and log is natural. To prove the first inequality,
fix the first m-1 factors in an ordered m-tuple and bound the number of
last factors by n divided by their product. Extending each of the first
factor sums to 1,...,n gives H_n^(m-1). For m=1 there are no first factors
and the bound is exactly n. Thus the component prefix F_r satisfies

 |F_r(n)|<=n*(1+log n)^(2r-2).

In particular r=1 is valid: |F_1(n)|<=min(u,n)<=n. No lower bound on u or
arity-dependent cutoff assumption is needed for these envelopes. Squaring
and summing over n=X,...,2X-1 yields, for EVERY integer X>=1,

 A_(F_r)(X)<=4*X^3*(1+log(2X))^(4r-4).

Since ceil(X^p)>=X^p for rational p>=0, a potential feature obeys

 A_(F_r)(X)/ceil(X^p)
              <=4*X^(3-p)*(1+log(2X))^(4r-4).

For FIXED r and p>=3 this is globally subpower: for every epsilon>0 a
finite constant K_(r,p,epsilon), independent of X and u, bounds the feature
by K_(r,p,epsilon)*X^epsilon for all X>=1. Indeed each fixed logarithmic
power times X^(-epsilon) is bounded on [1,infinity). A finite nonnegative
combination of such features has the same property. This asserts neither
a uniform constant bound at p=3 nor a particular epsilon-dependent constant
law; r=1 is the harmless zero-log-degree boundary case.

If r or the number or weights of potential terms increases with X, the
logarithmic degree and every other growing cost must be charged. The fixed-r
subpower statement is not uniform in an undeclared growing arity. These are
supplied universal norm estimates, not critical estimates inferred by the
learner. Even a nonnegative subpower potential does not bound the COMPLETE
actual signed innovation E(2X)+V(2X)-E(X)-V(X); that arithmetic premise remains
unproved.
