Residual observer: conditional bridge and structural review

Scope
Pure analytical deductions. No responses, source tables, tests, hashes or
frozen files accessed or changed. No signed observer moment bound is proved.
The reciprocal-zeta conclusion below is conditional on a fixed all-scale
cofinal moment/drift estimate. NIST DLMF was consulted only for the standard
functional equation/zero symmetry used at the final analytic step.

1. Exact operator hypotheses and contraction
Let d_j be a fixed increasing sequence of DISTINCT INTEGERS>=2, so d_j>=j+1.
N(n) is a source-free finite nonnegative prefix count. Coefficients a(n,d_j)
are fixed functions with modulus<=1; they may query the frozen actual Mobius
source. Set Af(n)=N(n)^-1 sum_{j<=N(n)}a(n,d_j)f(floor(n/d_j)), empty0.
Extend every sequence by f(x)=f(floor(x)), f=0 below1; likewise freeze N and
coefficients at floor(x). Integer denominators ensure
floor(x/d)=floor(floor(x)/d), so this extension agrees with the continuous
quotient operator. Evaluating roots/counts at noninteger x instead would define
a different operator. A divisor set depending on n is not automatically a
prefix of one fixed denominator sequence; masks inside the fixed prefix are
allowed, renormalizing a variable selected subset requires another proof.

Write H_sigma,T(f)=integral_1^T |f(x)|^2 x^(-2sigma-1)dx. Jensen and substitution
u=x/d_j give, for sigma>=1/2,
 H(Af)<=sum_j d_j^(-2sigma) integral |f(u)|^2 u^(-2sigma-1)
                 *1_{N(d_j u)>=j}/N(d_j u) du.
All upper endpoints after substitution are<=T; no infinite-energy premise is
needed. For j<=m the coefficient is<=1/(j*d_j); for j>m it is<=1/[j(j+1)].
Thus H(Af)<=c_m H(f),
 c_m=sum_{j<=m}1/(j*d_j)+1/(m+1).
If some d_j>j+1 with j<=m, c_m<1. Bounded signs/masks or dependence on frozen
source coefficients do not invalidate this norm proof. Fixed denominator and
count/domain hypotheses matter for the interpretation of the grammar and inverse.

For r=(I-A)f,
 ||f||_T<=(1-sqrt(c_m))^-1 ||r||_T<=2/(1-c_m)||r||_T.
The displayed recovery factor is for the UNSQUARED norm; the squared H factor
is (1-sqrt(c_m))^-2, at most4/(1-c_m)^2. The norm argument takes place on each
finite interval and can then pass to infinity monotonically. A fixed finite
composition of admissible residual maps multiplies these norm constants; it
must be inverted in reverse order using the same coefficient functions.

An outer source mask is different: mu(n)r(n) or a coprime gate times r(n) can
erase coordinates and is not covered by recovery. Put such masks inside A,
or retain complementary channels with a proved lower frame bound. Likewise
the full original E anchor can be kept as additional nonnegative storage; no
observer simplification licenses silently replacing the actual target by a
filtered source. Every function must be defined on all positive integers.

For dense d_j=j+1 this particular critical bound is1, not a contraction.
At each sigma>1/2 the refined sum sum_j1/[j*d_j^(2sigma)] is strictly below1,
so a sigma-dependent bound can still suffice for the analytic bridge. Lack of
a gap is not an impossibility theorem; the simple uniform critical certificate
does not admit it. Finite triangular solvability alone is also not a uniform
infinite-energy recovery estimate.

2. Exact discrete-to-integral bridge
For a step-extended observer r define
 E_r(X)=X^-2 sum_{X<=n<2X}|r(n)|^2, dyadic X.
On its real block the exact bounds are
 2^(-2sigma-1) X^(1-2sigma)E_r(X)
 <=integral_X^(2X)|r(x)|^2 x^(-2sigma-1)dx
 <=X^(1-2sigma)E_r(X).
Hence E_r(X)<=B_delta X^delta implies H_sigma,infinity(r)<infinity whenever
0<delta<2sigma-1, by a convergent geometric sum over dyadic blocks. Conversely
finite H gives a weighted moment statement; no pointwise Mertens bound is
implicitly inferred from it.

A sufficient conditional premise is ONE fixed candidate with W_r=E_r+V,
V>=0, finite W_r(1), and some integer q0 such that for EVERY integer q>=q0,
 W_r(2X)-W_r(X)<=Cq ceil(X^(1/q)) for all dyadic X>=1,
with finite Cq independent of X. Taking Cq nonnegative is harmless. Telescoping
gives E_r(X)<=W_r(1)+2Cq(X^(1/q)-1)/(2^(1/q)-1).
For any sigma=1/2+epsilon>1/2 choose q>=q0 with1/q<2epsilon; then the global
H_sigma(r) is finite. No control on how Cq grows is needed for this qualitative
bridge. The fixed candidate cannot change with q, epsilon or X. A finite set
q=2,3,6,12 does not meet the premise. A fixed finite observer composition has
finite sigma-dependent recovery constants, so H_sigma(M) is then finite.
If W retains the original E_M anchor, this moment conclusion already follows
from that anchor; recovery remains a consistency check rather than extra proof.

3. Ordinary reciprocal-zeta and conditional RH consequence
For any complex s with Re(s)>1/2 choose1/2<sigma<Re(s). Cauchy-Schwarz gives
 integral_1^infinity |M(x)|x^(-Re(s)-1)dx
 <=sqrt(H_sigma(M))/sqrt(2(Re(s)-sigma)).
Thus F(s)=s integral_1^infinity M(x)x^(-s-1)dx is holomorphic on Re(s)>1/2,
with locally uniform convergence and logarithmic derivative domination. For
Re(s)>1, ordinary absolute Dirichlet convergence and summation by parts give
 F(s)=sum_n mu(n)n^(-s)=1/zeta(s).
Here actual Mobius inversion is essential; a control source does not represent
this reciprocal.

For clarity the needed zeta continuation can be obtained directly from
 zeta(s)=s/(s-1)-s integral_1^infinity {x}x^(-s-1)dx,
initially Re(s)>1 and then Re(s)>0 except its simple pole at1. The holomorphic
identity (s-1)zeta(s)F(s)=s-1 extends from Re(s)>1 to Re(s)>1/2. A zeta zero
there, different from its pole, contradicts that identity. The standard
functional equation then reflects a nontrivial zero to1-s, so absence to the
right forces all nontrivial zeros onto the critical line. Standard equation
and symmetry references: https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.4.E3 and
https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.10.i . This is the ordinary Riemann zeta function.

The proof does NOT directly establish ordinary convergence of the Mobius
Dirichlet series throughout Re(s)>1/2. The integral continuation suffices;
ordinary partial summation there also requires M(N)N^(-s)->0, a pointwise
boundary assertion not supplied immediately by these dyadic L2 moments.
No such stronger assertion is needed for this forward RH implication.

4. Growing-prefix degeneracy is stronger than norm equivalence
Suppose N(n)->infinity as n->infinity. If output n is in a region with N(n)>=K,
the same proof bounds its coefficient by1/max(K,j), yielding
 b_K<=K^-1 sum_{j<=K}1/(j+1)+1/(K+1), b_K->0.
For an input supported above L and L sufficiently large, every nonzero output
is at least2L, hence ||A f_high||_T<=sqrt(b_K)||f_high||_T, uniformly in T and
sigma>=1/2. High-input components see an asymptotically small operator; the
residual is asymptotically the identity on those components.

Split a fixed M into its finite low-input piece and high-input piece. The low
piece has finite H and A maps it to finite H by the uniform bounded-operator
estimate. If H_sigma,T(M) diverges as T->infinity, its fixed low-piece norm is
negligible. The preceding high-piece bound and arbitrary K then imply
 ||AM||_T/||M||_T->0,
 H_sigma,T((I-A)M)/H_sigma,T(M)->1.
This applies also at critical sigma=1/2; it is a conditional statement about
divergent truncated energy, not a claim that actual critical energy diverges.
It does not imply a pointwise ratio near zeros of M. Fixed bounded signs inside
the average cannot undo the b_K estimate. Composing finitely many such residuals
has the same high-energy near-identity limitation.

Thus normalization provides certified recoverability but growing-count averaging
alone does not produce a new critical-norm cancellation. Equivalent observers
can nevertheless have an analytically useful exact representation. Such a
benefit must come from an arithmetic identity or a closed signed estimate, not
the normalization theorem itself. Fixed small banks avoid this asymptotic
identity effect, but do not automatically make their moments easier.

5. Full divisor inverse shows the normalization tradeoff exactly
Mobius inversion gives, for every integer n>=1,
 M(n)+sum_{d=2}^n M(floor(n/d))=1.
The raw signed residual is identically1, but its unnormalized quotient operator
does not have the required critical-norm recovery bound. It cannot be declared
a successful observer merely because the residual is bounded.

With N=n-1 and positive normalized coefficients, for n>=2,
 AM(n)=(1-M(n))/(n-1),
 (I-A)M(n)=n*M(n)/(n-1)-1/(n-1).
With negative coefficients the residual instead equals
 (n-2)*M(n)/(n-1)+1/(n-1).
Normalization has turned the exact bounded raw forcing into a near-identity
rescaling of the target. This is an explicit analytic degeneracy, not a finite
alias inferred from scores. The initial n=1 case is handled by the empty bank.
Truncating the divisor domain preserves its complete omitted quotient tail.

Similarly the actual logarithmic identity, with Lambda(p^k)=log(p), is
 sum_{d<=n}Lambda(d)M(floor(n/d))
 =-M(n)log(n)+integral_1^n M(x)dx/x.
Its raw log-normalized residual has a useful smoothing representation, but its
weights are not the proposed bounded-coefficient prefix average: expressing
them with a large prefix count multiplies coefficients beyond1. All prime
powers and domain complements are essential. This cannot simultaneously be
treated as bounded forcing and as a certified contraction without a new proof.

6. Which source laws or signed factors can genuinely simplify a proof
For each prime p, complete nonmultiple prefixes C_p(n)=sum_{k<=n,p not dividing
k}mu(k) satisfy M(n)=C_p(n)-C_p(floor(n/p)). This supplies fresh source access
and an exact prime-multiple compensation. A normalized mixture gives M=A-B
and the nonnegative square of A+B diagonalizes E+V=2||A||^2+2||B||^2. Fresh
C_p energy remains uncontrolled; the law is not a signed moment bound.

On a prime domain mu(p)=-1 is a constant sign. Whole common point factors may
be norm masks; they do not certify independent variation. mu(floor(n/d)) or
mu(n) inside distinct summands can retain actual mixed dependencies, but only
exact divisor and coprimality hypotheses allow multiplicative simplifications.
Divisor-supported masks can facilitate exact inversion; variable renormalized
selected sets must not be confused with the proved fixed-prefix operator.
Complementary parity/coprime channels are preferable to discarding coordinates
with an outer gate. A source-dependent phase must remain fixed through the
entire recovery argument, and a nonunit mask must not be called an isometry.

For quotient-only observers the fresh-block obstruction persists: old source
and child observation cannot constrain a coherent new prefix solely through
norm bounds. Near-t translates introduce fresh point interactions, but pure
bounded point banks are bounded padding; a latest multiple can reduce exactly
to an old quotient and a mask. Complete prime/nonmultiple source laws provide
a stronger compensation mechanism, still requiring a genuine bound on the
fresh correlated source component. No such bound was found here.

Final disposition
The conditional recovery-to-ordinary-zeta/RH bridge is valid with the hypotheses,
integer step extension and norm/squared-norm constants above. Normalized growing
prefixes are asymptotically target-like in critical norm, and full inverse
identities expose the bounded-forcing versus recoverability tradeoff. This is
a theorem about admission and obstruction, not a proof of the missing cofinal
signed moment estimate. Reviewer edits to this memo stop on completion.
