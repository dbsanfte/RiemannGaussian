1. Complete four-state source and prime-log recurrence
------------------------------------------------------
Use u=1/3,v=2/3,kappa=2/3,beta=9/2 and the old exact R_u>0. Define
  nu0=k_u*(mu*mu), nu1=k_u*(mu*mu_v), nu2=k_u*mu_v, nu3=mu,
  c=(beta,-beta,-R_u,-beta), f=sum_i c_i*nu_i,
  A_i(x)=sum_(n<=x)nu_i(n), F(x)=sum_i c_i A_i(x).
Here k_alpha(n)=n^(-alpha), mu_v(n)=mu(n)n^(-v), and * is COMPLETE
Dirichlet convolution. All prefix values below one are zero. The unit is
  f(1)=-beta-R_u.
The paid soft-divisor inverse and complete eta prefix from the verified
handoff remain the route back to the original centered target, including
all unit/main/prime-power crosses. They are not discarded at sharp cuts.

For every prime power r>=2 let
  D(r)=diag(r^(-u)-2, r^(-u)-1-r^(-v), r^(-u)-r^(-v), -1).
The exact coefficient derivation is
  log(n)*nu_i(n)=sum_(r|n,r>=2)Lambda(r)*D_ii(r)*nu_i(n/r).
Lambda includes ALL prime powers. Every nonzero argument n/r is smaller.
Summation by parts gives exactly, for real x>=1,
  F(x)*log x=Z(x)+integral_1^x F(y)dy/y,
  Z(x)=sum_(r<=x)Lambda(r)*sum_i c_i D_ii(r)A_i(x/r).          (1.1)
The x=1 equation is 0=0, not a recovery of the unit by dividing by zero.
The recurrence does not sum a Dirichlet coefficient series at kappa.
All arguments in Z are <=x/2, but they are FOUR source channels, not
automatically the one controlled scalar F at that smaller scale.

2. Actual signed polarization fails the naive positivity test
--------------------------------------------------------------
Set P=c c^T. A proposed pointwise whole-target dissipation certificate
from the generator would involve
  P D(r)+D(r) P.
For a real state vector z its quadratic form is
  2*(c dot z)*((D(r)c) dot z).                               (2.1)
D(r)c is not proportional to c: the four diagonal entries of D(r)
are distinct in the required ways and every c_i is nonzero. The two
linear forms in (2.1) are independent; choose their signs equal or opposite
to obtain positive or negative values. Thus the symmetric matrix is
INDEFINITE, despite several negative entries of D(r).
This already rejects a universal pointwise positive-form proof for the
rank-one TARGET storage. Transported prefix/cofactor vectors are generally
different, so their bilinear signs cannot be inferred from this failed
same-point proxy either.

A full positive coercive metric does not repair entrywise dissipation:
D_22(r)=r^(-u)-r^(-v)>0. Its positive eigenvalue prevents
D^T M+M D<=-delta M for positive definite M and delta>0. Also the
common nu0 eigenvalue tends to -2; smaller arguments alone are not a
certified prefix-moment contraction. This statement concerns a generic
matrix inequality, not every possible arithmetic norm or coupled forcing.

There is a SOURCE-SPECIFIC obstruction to a natural square completion.
The exact kappa main-prefix coefficients of A_i are
  m0=1/[kappa*zeta(kappa)^2],
  m1=1/[kappa*zeta(kappa)*zeta(kappa+v)],
  m2=1/[kappa*zeta(kappa+v)], m3=0.
Then m0,m2>0,m1<0, c dot m=0, but
  (D(r)c) dot m
    =beta*[-2m0+m1+m0*r^(-v)]<0, every r>=2.                 (2.2)
Adding ANY multiple of c leaves this pole coefficient unchanged.
Consequently a raw auxiliary with row D(r)c+eta*c, for fixed r and eta,
has a genuine uncanceled pole at s=kappa. It CANNOT have complete
all-epsilon cofinal squared moments: such moments give finite weighted
prefix norms for every sigma>1/2, and Mellin/Cauchy then makes its
Dirichlet series holomorphic in Re(s)>1/2, contradicting that real pole.
This contradiction uses only local nonzero real zeta values, not RH or
a zero-free half-plane elsewhere.

Thus completing (2.1) by a nonnegative square of that raw row is not an
independently payable defect. Centering that square is allowed, but its
deterministic main forcing must return. A zero-pole target projection is
not an invariant subspace of D(r). The common mode and pole channel
cannot be hidden in an alleged positive storage or a tail at kappa.

3. Negative delay coefficients are not a coercivity theorem
-----------------------------------------------------------
On ||G||_sigma^2=integral_1^infinity |G(x)|^2*x^(-2sigma-1)dx,
S_q G(x)=G(x/q) has norm q^(-sigma). A single-prime nu0 generator is
  L_(0,q)=log q*sum_(e>=1)(q^(-eu)-2)S_(q^e).
After logarithmic coordinates, its Fourier multiplier at phase theta is
  log q*[a*t*exp(-i theta)/(1-a*t*exp(-i theta))
                      -2a*exp(-i theta)/(1-a*exp(-i theta))],
  a=q^(-sigma), t=q^(-u).
Its real value at theta=0 is negative; at theta=pi it is positive,
  log q*[-a*t/(1+a*t)+2a/(1+a)]>0.                           (3.1)
Long logarithmic wave packets approximate these two signs. Thus even
this finite, absolutely convergent one-prime operator has no universal
one-sign quadratic form on arbitrary prefix Hilbert-space signals.
Actual arithmetic F is fixed, not an arbitrary wave packet; (3.1) rules
out a source-independent inference from negative coefficients, not a
future source-specific signed covariance lemma.

There is a useful VALID semidefinite rule. For any contraction T,
  Re <G,(I plus-or-minus T)G>
    =1/2*||G plus-or-minus TG||^2
             +1/2*(||G||^2-||TG||^2)>=0.                   (3.2)
It has no uniform coercive gap when ||T||=1: phases can approach the
opposite sign. With ||T||<=r<1, the gap is >=1-r. A detector may compose
this rule when it has proved a PREFIX operator-norm certificate for T.
The coefficient identity sum_(d|n)Lambda(d)=log n does not supply that
certificate for the lifted prefix bank: its weights and supports differ.
Using a coefficient probability or a coefficient Hilbert norm here would
replace the complete prefix-square covariance by a different problem.

4. Exact joint log-covariance absorption certificate
---------------------------------------------------
Fix L=64, sigma>1/2 and H>=64. Let w_sigma(x)=x^(-2sigma-1),
  C_head=integral_1^64 F(y)dy/y,
  V(x)=integral_64^x F(y)dy/y,
  Q_(sigma,H)=Re integral_64^H conjugate(F(x))*Z(x)w_sigma(x)dx.
From (1.1), EXACTLY
  Q=integral_64^H log x*|F(x)|^2*w_sigma(x)dx
               -Re <F,V> -Re(C_head*<F,1>),                 (4.1)
with complex conjugation in the conventional inner product. F is real
for this source, so no conjugation ambiguity changes the expression.
Also
  2 Re <F,V>=H^(-2sigma)|V(H)|^2+2sigma*||V||^2,
  ||V||<=||F||/sigma.
Consequently, writing k_sigma=log64-1/sigma>0 and
b_sigma=|C_head|*64^(-sigma)/sqrt(2sigma),
  Q>=k_sigma*||F||_(sigma,[64,H])^2
                         -b_sigma*||F||_(sigma,[64,H]).      (4.2)
The finite head is explicit: |f(n)|<=(3beta+R_u)d2(n) gives
  |C_head|<=(3beta+R_u)*64*log64.

A precise CLOSING certificate input would be constants delta_sigma>0,
A_sigma>=0, independent of H, such that for EVERY H>=64,
  Q_(sigma,H)<=(k_sigma-delta_sigma)*||F||_(sigma,[64,H])^2
                                                          +A_sigma. (4.3)
Then Young's inequality applied to (4.2) gives
  ||F||_(sigma,[64,H])^2
       <=2A_sigma/delta_sigma+b_sigma^2/delta_sigma^2.        (4.4)
If (4.3) were proved for every sigma>1/2, the complete core cofinal bound
would follow, and the verified original recovery would apply. All prime
powers, proper arguments, channel crosses and finite head are explicit.

Attempted proof: replacing the signed covariance by negative generator
entries fails (2.1),(3.1); completing squares gives the unpaid pole row
(2.2); separately centered channels reintroduce the macroscopic bank
of _004 section7. Those attempts therefore do not prove (4.3).
Equation (4.3) is a falsifiable ACTUAL-SOURCE correlation lemma, not an
automatic matrix theorem. In the all-sigma cofinal class it is a closing
certificate form, largely equivalent to the missing bound, and must not
be counted as an independent gain merely because Q has a new name.
A useful generated argument must prove it from narrower source pairings
or an acyclic coupled estimate without assuming RH-strength companions.

5. Generic delayed fractional filters with full recovery classification
----------------------------------------------------------------------
For ANY prefix signal with zero extension below one, fixed integer q>=2
and real or complex a, define
  G(x)=F(x)-a*F(x/q).
The exact finite inverse at x is
  F(x)=sum_(k=0)^floor(log_q x) a^k*G(x/q^k).                (5.1)
Every term has a smaller argument except the k=0 observed residual; units
and all floors remain. On truncated weighted norm the r=|a|q^(-sigma)
classification is
  r<1: ||F||<=||G||/(1-r), ||G||<=(1+r)||F||;
  r=1: inverse cost <=1+floor(log_q H);
  r>1: inverse cost <=sum_(k=0)^floor(log_q H)r^k.           (5.2)
At the critical scale |a|<sqrt q gives constant recovery; equality gives
polylog recovery and preserves the cofinal class. Above sqrt q a generic
power loss remains. In the fractional family a=q^gamma this is the gate
gamma<1/2, gamma=1/2, or gamma>1/2 respectively.

The boundary is admissible, not automatically rejected. For every fixed
sigma>1/2 it has r<1, and the original cofinal property is equivalent to
that of G. Negative a does not improve this SOURCE-INDEPENDENT norm gate;
an improvement would require an actual signed source correlation.
Oversized recovery cannot be inferred from a finite fit: the prefix
F(x)=a^floor(log_q x) has G supported only on [1,q), but its energy has
a positive power when |a|>sqrt q. It is a legitimate generic staircase
prefix for integer q, not a counterexample claiming actual mu identities.

Raw main cancellation with a=q^(2/3) is above the critical gate. A future
inverse can be written only with an independently justified future boundary
F(q^k x)/a^k ->0 and its complete tail cost. Neither local analytic pole
cancellation nor finite source responses prove that boundary. Current-scale
cycles cannot be replaced by an assumed future condition.

These recovery rules are generic and detector-composable. They classify
operator+auxiliary routes; they do not demonstrate that any G is cofinal.
Within the admitted range, simply replacing F by G is an equivalent
presentation. It becomes a useful argument only if a new source-specific
inequality for G or its full covariance is independently proved.

6. Dyadic covariance, strict-gap induction, and bounded signed drift
-------------------------------------------------------------------
For X=2^j define the COMPLETE energy and delayed covariance
  E(X)=X^(-2)*sum_(X<=n<2X)|F(n)|^2,
  C(X)=X^(-2)*sum_(X<=n<2X)Re[F(n)*conjugate(F(floor(n/2)))].
For X>=2 each lower-block value occurs exactly twice, so
  E_(S_2 F)(X)=E(X/2)/2,
  E_G(X)=E(X)+|a|^2*E(X/2)/2
                    -2 Re[a*C_complex(X)],                 (6.1)
where C_complex uses conjugate(F(n))*F(floor(n/2)); for the real source
and real a this is simply -2a*C(X). The two definitions agree in real part.
These identities keep every source square and cross, not just a filtered norm.

For real a and r^2=a^2/2<1, Young gives the conditional recurrence
  E(X)<=(1+eta)*E_G(X)+(1+1/eta)*r^2*E(X/2), eta>0.
Choosing eta=2r^2/(1-r^2) when r>0 gives
  E(X)<=rho*E(X/2)+K_a*E_G(X),
  rho=(1+r^2)/2<1, K_a=(1+r^2)/(1-r^2).                    (6.2)
For a=0 use E=E_G directly. This is an AUTOMATIC certificate if the
source inequality for E_G is supplied; it proves no such inequality.
At the boundary |a|=sqrt2 the exact dyadic inverse gives
  sqrt(E(X))<=sum_(k=0)^j sqrt(E_G(X/2^k)).                  (6.3)
Bounded E_G would give E<=K(j+1)^2. For r<=1 and
E_G(Y)<=K_epsilon Y^epsilon, the more general inverse yields
  E(X)<=K_epsilon X^epsilon/(1-r*2^(-epsilon/2))^2.

A different reusable route is a SIGNED increment. For any rho>=0,
  Delta_rho(X)=E(2X)-rho*E(X)
    =E_G(2X)+2a*C(2X)-(rho+a^2/2)*E(X).                    (6.4)
It is an exact observable, including every prime/composite and main cross.
No sign of C is inferred from a or from a prime-log coefficient.

Conditional theorem: if rho<1 and EVERY dyadic X satisfies
  Delta_rho(X)<=K*(1+log X)^p, p>=0,
then, with j=log2 X and exact head E(1)=(beta+R_u)^2,
  E(X)<=rho^j*E(1)+K*(1+log X)^p/(1-rho).                  (6.5)
If rho=1, the same hypothesis gives
  E(X)<=E(1)+K*j*(1+log X)^p.                              (6.6)
For a constant defect this yields bounded energy with a strict gap, or
logarithmic energy with rho=1. Only a ONE-SIDED increment bound is needed.
If the defect is instead <=K_epsilon X^epsilon, the geometric telescoping
constant is 1/(1-rho*2^(-epsilon)) for rho<=1, up to the finite head.
For rho>1 a bounded increment does not close cofinal growth: E_j=rho^j
has Delta=0. This is another automatic rejection gate.

An all-epsilon subpower increment bound with rho<=1 is essentially an
equivalent encoding of cofinality, since Delta_rho<=E(2X) already. A FIXED
polylog or bounded increment is a genuinely stronger intermediate lemma.
For example E(2^j)=exp(sqrt j) is cofinal subpower but its increments for
rho<=1 exceed every fixed polynomial in j; a staircase prefix with these
block energies realizes the logical distinction. This is not an actual
source counterexample. Neither type of actual-source increment bound has
been proved here. Raw bounded increments are testable hypotheses at finite
scales, not evidence of all-scale control by themselves.

8. Composable positive auxiliary storage: coarse proof and pole gates
---------------------------------------------------------------------
The proposed automatic grammar has
  A(X)=E_G(X)+w*E_H(X), w>=0,
  H(n)=n^(-alpha)*sum_i v_i[A_i(n)-b*A_i(floor(n/2))],
with alpha>=0, 0<=b<=2 and a delay |a|<=2, or the log-Abel target.
All signed crosses INSIDE G and H remain. E_H is nonnegative; dropping
it from A is a valid recovery inequality, not a signed cancellation.

Here is an unconditional coarse certificate that needs no core solution.
The exact local bounds |nu0(n)|<=d2(n), |nu_i(n)|<=1 for i=1,2,3 imply
  |f(n)|<=K*d2(n), K=3beta+ceil(R_u),
  |F(n)|<=K*n*(1+log n),
since sum_(j<=n)d2(j)<=n*(1+log n). Thus
  |G(n)|<=Ct*n*(1+log n), Ct=2K for a delay.
For the log-Abel choice put
  P_F(n)=F(n), n<64;
  P_F(n)=sum_(j<=n)f(j)log j/log n, n>=64.
Then |P_F(n)|<=K*n*(1+log n), so Ct=K. This target remains recoverable:
on real x>=64, P_F=F-(1/log x)*integral_1^x F(y)dy/y, with staircase
coefficients and the complete fixed head. The tail memory operator has
norm <=1/(sigma*log64)<1 for sigma>1/2; the finite head is paid as in
section4. For the saved integer convention, its constant-cell extension
differs from the continuous Z(x)/log x by at most 2K for x>=64: bound
|Z(n)|<=K*n*(1+log n)*log n and use
|1/log n-1/log x|<=1/(n*(log n)^2) on [n,n+1).
That bounded error has finite critical tail norm and is explicitly paid.
Below64 the target is exactly F. Thus cofinal target recovery
is a proved generic conditional rule, not a claim that P_F is bounded.

With Ch=2*||v||_1, the same elementary estimates give
  |H(n)|<=Ch*n*(1+log n).
For n in [2X,4X-1], 1+log n<=2*(1+log(2X)). There are 2X terms.
Consequently, for EVERY dyadic X>=1,
  A(2X)<=32*(Ct^2+w*Ch^2)*X*(1+log(2X))^2.                (8.1)
The proposed reference constant72 is therefore valid with margin. Since
A(X)>=0, (8.1) certifies a recurrence with any rho>=0, gamma=1, k=2.
This is a coarse positive-power reference, not an RH-strength estimate.

Any proved recurrence
  A(2X)<=rho*A(X)+C*X^gamma*(1+log(2X))^k,
0<=rho<=1, gamma>=0, k>=0, can be telescoped exactly. Gamma=0 gives a
fixed polylog bound by section6 and hence cofinal core recovery. Gamma>0
gives O(X^gamma*(1+log X)^k), including its explicit geometric constant;
it does NOT imply an all-epsilon bound. Training an upward-rounded C and
checking finitely many blocks does not certify the universal premise.

There is an exact pole rejection rule for auxiliary storage with w>0.
Let B_v(s)=sum_i v_i times the Dirichlet series of nu_i, so its pole at
kappa has residue kappa*(v dot m). For the continuous zero-below-one
version of H the Mellin identity, initially in an absolutely convergent
right half-plane, is
  integral_1^infinity x^(-alpha)*[v dot A(x)-b*v dot A(x/2)]
                         *x^(-s-1)dx
     =[1-b*2^(-(s+alpha))]*B_v(s+alpha)/(s+alpha).           (8.2)
If v dot m!=0 and b!=2^kappa, it has a genuine pole at s=kappa-alpha.
The integer staircase convention introduces no extra floor cancellation:
A(x) and A(x/2) are constant on each integer unit cell. Replacing x^-alpha
there by floor(x)^-alpha changes H by O(x^-alpha*(1+log x)), using the
coarse prefix bound, so its Mellin error is analytic for Re(s)>-alpha.
This cannot cancel the pole in (8.2). No asymptotic for A_i is presumed.

Hence alpha<1/6, v dot m!=0, b!=2^(2/3), w>0 rejects a cofinal bound
for A: its nonnegative H square has an uncanceled supercritical pole.
More precisely a recurrence forcing O(X^gamma polylog X) is impossible
if gamma<1/3-2alpha, by Mellin/Cauchy holomorphy to the right of
(1+gamma)/2. If gamma meets or exceeds this threshold, that pole alone
does not reject the positive-power recurrence. Equality alpha=1/6 is a
critical boundary and is NOT rejected by this test. Neither a zero pole
coefficient nor exact b=2^kappa establishes a bound: other arithmetic
forcing and recovery obligations remain. If w=0 the unused auxiliary
cannot obstruct A and should not trigger this gate.

These are reusable theorem/certificate rules on generated expressions.
They justify reference bounds, recovery and rejection; none supplies the
missing stronger actual-source inequality or independent negative credit.
