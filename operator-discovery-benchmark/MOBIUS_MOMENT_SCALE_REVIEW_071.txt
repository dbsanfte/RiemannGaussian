FINITE MOMENT EXPOSURE, CONDITIONAL HEAD COST, AND SIGNED STATE 071
================================================================

Scope and disposition
---------------------
This independent review reads MOBIUS_DIVISOR_CORRECTION_HANDOFF.txt,
MOBIUS_DIVISOR_CORRECTION_REVIEW_070.txt and relevant source definitions
in MOBIUS_CONVOLUTION_SCALE_REVIEW_069.txt. It runs no scientific array,
optimizer, runner or historical test and writes only this prose file.
All prior artifacts are preserved. There is no claim to have proved RH,
an all-scale Mertens invariant, or complete signed head control.

Three results are proved here. The proposed exposure frontier is an exact
optimum of the stated affine exponent model, with explicit rational
primal and dual certificates. Its exponent has a complete same-source
conditional aggregate cost, including floor/ceiling constants and rank
bases. A separate disjoint-interval argument gives a uniform conditional
price for the two short linear terms, using a fractional source-step norm.
The remaining signed floor state is identified exactly with a current-rank
joined Mertens functional after a paid separable subtraction. In particular,
the moment frontier is not a frontier for the entire source or for RH.

1. Finite moment operator and explicit integer ranks
---------------------------------------------------
Let 0<=sigma<1, beta=1-sigma, C>=1, and

 A_sigma=(2-sigma)/(1-sigma).

The finite divisor argument of070 gives, for integer T>=u>=1,

 |H_mu(u)| <= (u+1)/T + C A_sigma u^(-beta),
 H_mu(u)=sum_(n<=u)mu(n)/n,                           (1)

provided |M(t)|<=C t^sigma for every integer 1<=t<=T. This is a conditional
finite lemma. Its proof uses the exact complete divisor identity through
T, pointwise |mu|<=1, and those Mertens premises. It does not use an
infinite reciprocal tail. Every added premise remains unpaid unless a
separate uniform theorem or an already closed induction licenses it.

For integer R>=1 and rational parameters put

 u=floor(R^theta), v=floor(R^(1-theta)),
 T_u=ceil(R^gamma_u), T_v=ceil(R^gamma_v),
 0<theta<1,
 theta<=gamma_u<=rho, 1-theta<=gamma_v<=rho<1.        (2)

These capacities are feasible precisely when rho>=1/2 and
1-rho<=theta<=rho. The formulas below do not describe a feasible
program when rho<1/2. The cuts satisfy (u+1)(v+1)>R exactly, because
each incremented floor is strictly larger than its underlying power.
Thus the original long-long support term vanishes with the required
strict gate. Also T_u>=u and T_v>=v.

The elementary bounds floor(x)>=x/2 for x>=1 and ceil(x)>=x give

 |H_mu(u)| <= 2 R^(theta-gamma_u)
              +2^beta C A_sigma R^(-beta theta),
 |H_mu(v)| <= 2 R^(1-theta-gamma_v)
              +2^beta C A_sigma R^(-beta(1-theta)).  (3)

Consequently, if kappa_u and kappa_v obey the four indicated lower
exponent constraints, a common rational upper constant is

 B_sigma(C)=2+2 C A_sigma,
 |H_mu(u)H_mu(v)|<=B_sigma(C)^2 R^(alpha-1),
 alpha=1+kappa_u+kappa_v.                           (4)

Here kappa_u>=theta-gamma_u and kappa_u>=-beta theta,
and the analogous two inequalities hold for v. In particular every term
of the expanded price remains present:

 B_sigma(C)^2=4+8 A_sigma C+4 A_sigma^2 C^2.

There is an explicit uniform rank base. Choose an integer B>=3 satisfying
B^(1-rho)>=2. For every R>=B,

 T_u,T_v <= ceil(R^rho) <= R/2+1 < R.                (5)

For rho=a/b in lowest terms, the base inequality can be checked without
floating powers by B^(b-a)>=2^b. For a rational exponent a/b, a floor
cut is certified by u^b<=R^a<(u+1)^b; a ceiling domain is certified by
(T-1)^b<R^a<=T^b. Bases R<B must be retained explicitly. Their declared
arithmetic scope is not automatically smaller rank merely because the
formula still returns a number.

2. Exact optimum of the affine exponent model
---------------------------------------------
The objective is to minimize alpha=1+kappa_u+kappa_v subject to the
four lower exponent constraints and the capacities in(2). Summing the
two moment constraints gives the first dual certificate

                 alpha>=1-beta=sigma.              (6)

Summing the two exposure constraints and using gamma_u,gamma_v<=rho
gives the second certificate

                 alpha>=2-2rho.                    (7)

These are exact linear combinations with coefficient1. More explicitly,
the sum of the exposure facets

 kappa_u-theta+gamma_u>=0,
 kappa_v+theta+gamma_v-1>=0,
 rho-gamma_u>=0, rho-gamma_v>=0

is kappa_u+kappa_v-1+2rho>=0. No approximate dual stationarity is used.

A matching rational feasible point is

 theta=1/2, gamma_u=gamma_v=rho,
 kappa_u=kappa_v=max(1/2-rho,-beta/2).

It proves, rather than assumes, the exact model optimum

              alpha_*=max(sigma,2-2rho),            (8)

for 1/2<=rho<1 and 0<=sigma<1. A rational linear solver can discover
the primal point; the two displayed sums independently certify its
optimality. A checker should derive the objective from its actual input
facets, rather than be given(8) as its expected answer.

Balance is sufficient but need not be the unique optimum. If
rho>=1-sigma/2, both moment facets can be active throughout the interval

 [1-rho/(2-sigma),rho/(2-sigma)] intersect [1-rho,rho].

If rho<=1-sigma/2, both exposure facets can be active throughout

 [rho/(2-sigma),1-rho/(2-sigma)] intersect [1-rho,rho].

With gamma_u=gamma_v=rho these intervals describe the respective
optimal plateaus. The threshold rho=1-sigma/2 is exactly where the two
frontier values meet. At sigma=1/2 a price with exponent1/2 needs
rho>=3/4 within this model. This is an auxiliary arithmetic exposure
cost, not a new proof that the premises at that exposure hold.

There is also an exact secondary optimum if the program minimizes
z=max(gamma_u,gamma_v) after fixing alpha=alpha_*. Add the facets
z-gamma_u>=0 and z-gamma_v>=0. Their sum with the two exposure
facets proves alpha>=2-2z, hence z>=(2-alpha_*)/2. When
alpha_*=2-2rho this lower bound is rho and is attained at the balanced
primary point. When alpha_*=sigma it is1-sigma/2 and is attained at
theta=1/2,gamma_u=gamma_v=1-sigma/2. Therefore the lexicographic
secondary optimum is

                 z_*=min(rho,1-sigma/2).            (8a)

This certificate uses only rational facet sums. In the moment-led case,
secondary minimal exposure forces theta=1/2: primary optimality makes
both moment facets equalities, and the exposure constraints require
gamma_u>=(2-sigma)theta and gamma_v>=(2-sigma)(1-theta).
In the exposure-led case the primary optimal theta plateau can persist.

The certificates prove optimality only for the separable pricing model
based on(1). They are not lower bounds on actual ordinary-mu values,
not a barrier to another coupled moment identity, and not a frontier for
the full signed remainder or another detector grammar.

3. Complete aggregate separable cost for the original source
-----------------------------------------------------------
Keep the original joined source and its coalesced step coefficients

 g(d)=sum_T a_T 1[d<=T], D=floor(4N/p),
 M1=sum_T |a_T|T,
 b_j=sum_T a_T[floor(T/j)-floor(T/(j+1))].

For any 0<alpha<=1, exact quotient counting gives

 sum_(j>=1) j^(1-alpha)
             [floor(T/j)-floor(T/(j+1))]
 =sum_(n<=T)floor(T/n)^(1-alpha)
 <=T^(1-alpha) sum_(n<=T)n^(alpha-1)
 <=T/alpha.                                        (9)

For alpha=1 the same statement is the exact identity T. Absolute a_T
are taken only after the source join. For any head subset,

            sum_j j^(1-alpha)|b_j|<=M1/alpha.       (10)

Let R_j=floor(D/j). On nonbase rows R_j>=B, use the cuts(2) with that
radius. Since R_j>=D/(2j) for j<=D and alpha<=1, equations(4),(10)
prove

 sum_nonbase |b_j H_mu(u_j)H_mu(v_j)|
 <= [2^(1-alpha)/alpha] B_sigma(C)^2
                         D^(alpha-1) M1.           (11)

A completely rational, slightly weaker coefficient is
2 B_sigma(C)^2/alpha. The optimum(8) always lies in(0,1]: positivity
comes from rho<1 even when sigma=0. Thus(11) covers that entire frontier.
It is incorrect to use the earlier factor1/sigma when sigma=0; the
quotient exponent actually being paid here is alpha>0.

The same admitted six-floor source has the unconditional bound

 M1<=2D+12N(1/p+r/q)(1+log(max(1,D))).               (12)

Substitution in(11) gives the explicit parameter dependence. For fixed
p,q and bounded r, D has order N, yielding

 O_(sigma,rho,p,q)((1+C)^2 N^alpha log(2N))          (13)

for the separable part, conditional on every finite Mertens premise
through its T_u,T_v. If p or q vary with N, the D and parameter factors
in(11),(12) must be retained; replacing D by N with a universal constant
is not licensed. The source norm is M1, not core_inverse or an empirical
coefficient norm.

The auxiliary queries are generally larger than the polynomial cuts.
For example theta=1/2, sigma=1/2, rho=3/4 uses radius about R^(3/4)
to price factors querying about R^(1/2). A domain-T implementation does
not inherit the result if it declares only T=u or max(u,v).

There is a distinction between rank bases and finitely many N bases.
Rows with R_j<B can occur at every large N if the head extends too far
in j. A safe condition removing all such rows from a head j<=H is
floor(D/H)>=B. For a fixed-p square-root head this holds for every
sufficiently large N, so finitely many N bases suffice. With arbitrary
parameter-dependent p or a different head, it must be checked explicitly.
Otherwise add the complete base-row price to(11), for example the exact
base moment values times |b_j|. Declaring a bounded row radius is not
itself a bound on the total number or mass of those rows.

4. A new uniform law for the short signed branches
-------------------------------------------------
The two short sums are L_j(w)=sum_(n<=w)mu(n)kappa_j(n). Their naive
head price is of order D^(1/2)H^(1/2) at balanced cuts. There is a
different conditional law that uses cancellation in the short branches,
without invoking the unpaid signed floor remainder.

Let w_j be any sequence of nonnegative integer cuts on consecutive
rows j>=1. In particular both w_j=floor(R_j^theta) and
w_j=floor(R_j^(1-theta)) are admitted for R_j=floor(D/j).
Assume |M(t)|<=C t^sigma at all their endpoint arguments, which are at
most W=max_j w_j. For a fixed source step T put

 A_j=floor(T/j),
 U_j=min(w_j,A_j), V_j=min(w_j,A_(j+1)).

Its contribution to L_j is a_T[M(U_j)-M(V_j)]. The interval(V_j,U_j]
is either empty or contained in the ordinary quotient interval
(A_(j+1),A_j]. These quotient intervals are pairwise disjoint, so the
nonempty capped intervals are disjoint even if the cuts fluctuate.
For nonincreasing cuts one also has V_j>=U_(j+1), but that stronger
inequality is unnecessary. The disjointness remains true across integer
floor changes, empty intervals and arbitrary truncation of the head.

For an integer splitting point J>=1, the first J intervals therefore
cost at most

 2 C T^sigma sum_(j<=J)j^(-sigma)
 <= [2C/(1-sigma)] T^sigma J^(1-sigma).              (14)

All later intervals have total length at most floor(T/(J+1))<=T/J.
Pointwise |mu|<=1 prices their total absolute contribution by that length.
Consequently the source-step head variation is at most the sum of(14)
and T/J. It is also at most T by disjointness alone.

Put delta=1/(2-sigma), so 1/2<=delta<1, and
J_*=T^((1-sigma)/(2-sigma)) C^(-delta). If J_*>=1 take
J=ceil(J_*)<=2J_*; if J_*<1 use the bound T instead. In both cases,

 sum_j |M(U_j)-M(V_j)|
 <=K_sigma C^delta T^delta,
 K_sigma=1+4/(1-sigma).                             (15)

This rational K is a conservative upper coefficient; the direct sharper
coefficient in the first case is 1+2^(2-sigma)/(1-sigma). In the second
case J_*<1 implies C>T^(1-sigma), so T<C^delta T^delta exactly.

Define the fractional source-step norm

                  M_delta=sum_T |a_T|T^delta.

Joining the source first, then applying(15), proves

 sum_j |L_j(w_j)| <= min(M1,K_sigma C^delta M_delta).
                                                               (16)

Thus the two short branches cost at most twice this common bound.
The Mertens arguments in this theorem are at most W, not T or the
source-step horizon. They are licensed by the moment domains when
those domains dominate the cuts. The law is uniform in all source steps,
cut choices and head lengths.

This is a genuine new conditional price for a previously unpaid part,
but its source norm must be charged. For delta<1 one has
T^delta>=D^(delta-1)T when T<=D. Hence it would be an error to infer
M_delta<=D^(delta-1)M1. The true inequality goes in the opposite
direction. An arbitrary source may place substantial mass at small T.

There is a direct source-specific envelope. For integer 0<=y<D,
the tail cutoff contributes at most2 y^delta, with 0^delta=0. For a
single floor atom floor(Z/d), its steps above y satisfy

 sum_(T>y) T^delta[floor(Z/T)-floor(Z/(T+1))]
 =sum_(n<=floor(Z/(y+1)))floor(Z/n)^delta
 <=Z^delta sum_(n<=floor(Z/(y+1)))n^(-delta)
 <=[Z/(1-delta)](y+1)^(delta-1).                    (17)

The exact equality is another quotient count, including endpoint floors.
Summing the six atoms with their signed-source coefficient magnitudes
only after the join gives

 M_delta<=2 y^delta
       +[12N(1/p+r/q)/(1-delta)](y+1)^(delta-1).    (18)

For y>=D, g is identically zero and the norm is zero. Equation(18)
is log-free and uniform, including y=0. For fixed parameters and y
comparable with N it is O(N^delta), so(16) yields a conditional
O_sigma(C^delta N^delta) short-branch price. At sigma=1/2 the exponent
delta is2/3, not1/2. Moreover for a square-root head and such large y
the short branches already vanish. There is no global square-root
short-branch theorem hidden in this estimate: at smaller y the second
term in(18) can dominate, and the unconditional cut-length bound may
be better. Retain the minimum of these sound prices.

A further exact localization is useful for unequal cuts. A branch
vanishes on row j whenever (j+1)w_j<=y. On a head j<=H, the sufficient
conditions are respectively

 y>=2D^theta H^(1-theta),
 y>=2D^(1-theta)H^theta.                            (19)

Both conditions follow from (j+1)j^(-theta)<=2j^(1-theta).
Only source steps with T<(j+1)w_j can contribute to that short branch.
Neither branch nor this localization is allowed to remove any term from
the independent floor remainder. Equations(16),(18),(19) are additional
pricing choices; they do not alter the original joined identity.

5. What is still in the signed floor state
-----------------------------------------
Keep exactly the original kernel and signed state

 K_j(t)=sum_A h_(j,A)floor(A/t)=b_j/t+e_j(t),
 E_j(u,v)=sum_(a<=u,b<=v)mu(a)mu(b)e_j(ab),
 J_j=L_j(u)+L_j(v)-E_j(u,v),
 c_j=d_j-b_j H_mu(u)H_mu(v)+J_j.                    (20)

In particular all common-prime factors, repeated factors, units and
pairs with ab>R_j remain. At such products the zero original kernel
is exactly the sum of its reciprocal term and floor residual. Their
separate deletion would change(20).

There is an exact diagnostic reduction which specifies the current
rank carried by J_j. From kappa_j(n)=sum_A h_(j,A)1[n<=A],

 c_j-d_j=sum_A h_(j,A)M(A),
 J_j=sum_A h_(j,A)M(A)+b_j H_mu(u)H_mu(v).           (21)

The M(A) arguments can reach R_j, even though every arithmetic factor
used to evaluate the bilinear polynomial is below max(u,v). Equations
(11) and(21) imply that uniform head control of J and uniform control
of the original joined Mertens functional are equivalent up to the
proved separable price. This is not a bound for J; it records precisely
what subtraction has and has not removed.

For the actual admitted source N=15k,y=16k,p=3,q=5,r=1,j=1,

 kappa(n)=1_(8k,10k](n)-1_(16k,20k](n), b=-2k,d=0.

For fixed feasible exponents(2), both cuts are below8k for sufficiently
large k, so both short branches vanish. The full remaining signed state
is then exactly

 J_1=-E_1
 =M(10k)-M(8k)-M(20k)+M(16k)
                        -2k H_mu(u)H_mu(v).        (22)

All four nonzero Mertens endpoints eventually exceed the auxiliary
domains T_u,T_v<=ceil((20k)^rho), since rho<1. Thus(22) cannot be
priced using those finite Mertens premises by direct substitution.
Under the paid separable rule, an O(k^alpha) remainder theorem is
equivalent to an O(k^alpha) estimate for this current-rank interval
combination. Boundedness |e_1|<=4 only yields the linear absolute
price4uv. That fact leaves the desired signed cancellation untouched.

This exposes a concrete failure of a proposed rank transfer: replacing
J by a smaller-rank Mertens leaf because its two factor indices are
small is unsound. It does not prove that an independent signed lemma
for J is impossible. It tells such a lemma the current-rank oscillation
it must control. The source is actual and joined, not an arbitrary
surrogate single floor atom.

The new short-branch theorem does not solve this obstruction: in(22)
the short branches are zero already. On that source, all improvement
after the separable frontier must come from signed floor cancellation,
a coupled energy, or another arithmetic identity. Full local ideal
correction of E to its exact value would again be finite target
reconstruction, unless that value's growth is separately bounded.

6. Credible proof-program use and the remaining invariant
--------------------------------------------------------
A uniform detector can correctly use this work as a conditional backend:
it chooses rational theta,gamma_u,gamma_v,kappa_u,kappa_v within declared
capacities; an independent checker verifies the literal affine facets,
the exact cuts and domains, the support gate, the base test and the
quotient/source prices. It must retain every required Mertens family,
the complete C polynomial in(4), the signed J node and any base-row
price. The displayed optimum is a theorem about this backend, not a
value that should be supplied to the search as a target.

The actual proof state now has useful paid conditional operators:
finite reciprocal moments, aggregate separable cost, and the optional
disjoint-interval short-branch cost. They are meaningful mathematical
reductions, although the lemmas were supplied by the reviewer rather
than discovered from numerical finite optimization.

For a simultaneous all-scale proof, every M(t) premise through T must
belong to an explicit state family and have smaller common rank or a
paid base. The original source geometry, the arithmetic query radius,
and the auxiliary pricing radius are three distinct quantities. A
strict row-rank statement T<R_j needs reconciliation with the common
rank used for the complete N,y,p,q,r head family, particularly if these
parameters vary. The current-rank geometric endpoints in(21),(22)
must remain visible.

The normalization and invariant constants cannot be chosen after
finite data. Even the separable part carries a positive quadratic term
in C. Enlarging C worsens that term rather than automatically closing
an induction. The short-branch price adds C^delta and its own fractional
source norm; a rational upper envelope may be used if a polynomial
invariant is required, but its cost must be stated. Signed J or coupled
energy control, Mertens closure, filled-core transfer and the ordinary
zeta/RH transfer remain separate obligations.

The frontier(8) says how cheaply one supplied moment decomposition can
be paid when the relevant finite Mertens premises are available. It
provides no mechanism that establishes those premises. The whole
all-scale signed goal therefore remains OPEN after the stronger partial
cost laws proved in this review.
