RENEWAL SHIFT WORDS: EXACT STRUCTURE, RECOVERY, AND THE SIGNED REMAINDER

Scope and conclusion
--------------------
This is a new analytical review. It preserves all frozen studies and earlier
reviews, including both selected shifted-quotient candidate reviews. No new
arithmetic evaluation, numerical study, test, parameter search, rescore, or
hash was performed. The current producer renewal_shift_candidates.py was
read as source only. Its actual-mu local logarithm, merged stencil, positive
shift normalization, and simple-real-pole centering agree with the formulas
below. No numerical outcome is a premise of any theorem in this memo.

The proposed local positivity and mass-one conjecture is TRUE. The words
give exact renewal distributions on prime exponents, a nonnegative survival
kernel, and an actual-mu signed random-stretch interpretation. This is a
structural change from multiplying old zero-unit increments: there is no
factor-access lag, and the fixed-index small-shift limit is a logarithmically
weighted von Mangoldt coefficient, not a convolution power of Lambda.

These facts do not prove the complete signed moment. The survival kernel
still acts on the original Mertens prefix by proper-divisor dilations. Its
fresh/past decomposition retains exactly the original fresh source. The
stochastic representation has alternating subset covariances; independence
of exponent draws does not make the actual Mobius signs independent. One
genuine uniform component estimate is available: the random fluctuation of
the largest-prime singleton component has finite critical integral, while
its negative mean prime-count bulk still requires the entire composite
complement. A second complete component peel removes the higher powers of
single primes, with no discarded remainder.

For previous candidate 10, a positive renewal equation can be proved
exactly. Its positive kernel has x log x mass and is unbounded on critical
weighted L2, even on a compactly supported positive input. Thus the natural
absolute-value renewal contraction is a specifically invalid route to its
remaining full signed bound. This obstruction does not exclude a genuinely
signed correlation proof. Neither that bound nor a new word's cofinal
moment, and therefore neither RH, is proved here.

1. Word semantics and exact aliases
----------------------------------
Let B=(b_1,...,b_k), each b_i>0, and let beta=product_i b_i. Start from
Q_empty(s)=1/zeta(s), and append b by Q_new(s)=Q(s)/Q(s+b).
For a subset S of positions, write t_S=sum_(i in S)b_i. Then
  Q_B(s)=product_S zeta(s+t_S)^((-1)^(|S|+1))
        =product_t zeta(s+t)^(e_t),
  e_t=sum_(S:t_S=t)(-1)^(|S|+1).                     (1.1)
The finite stencil is merged BEFORE classifying poles. In particular e_0=-1
and sum_t e_t=0 for every nonempty word. Appending shifts commutes. Sorting
the word removes the permutation aliases.

Example: B=(b,c,b+c) merges to
  Q_B= zeta(s+b)zeta(s+c)zeta(s+2b+2c)
        /[zeta(s)zeta(s+2b+c)zeta(s+b+2c)].           (1.2)
The shift b+c cancels. Provided b!=c, the surviving positive exponents are
one. If 2(b+c)<1, this is compatible with the proposed pilot restrictions.
Conversely, B=(b,b) gives
  Q_B=zeta(s+b)^2/[zeta(s)zeta(s+2b)],
and requires a DOUBLE pole normalizer with an x^(1-b) log x term. Excluding
that word from a simple-pole pilot is a schema restriction, not a failure
of its general algebra or local positivity.

For rational words, equality of the formal merged stencils is exactly
equality of the unordered multisets of shifts. Choose a common denominator
L and put m_i=L b_i. The stencil polynomial is
  sum_t e_t z^(Lt)=-product_i(1-z^(m_i)).            (1.3)
In its cyclotomic factorization, the exponent of Phi_d counts how many m_i
are divisible by d. Starting at the largest m_i and descending recovers
each multiplicity. Hence a merged stencil is a complete exact word key;
it is not merely a heuristic alias test. Equality of the Euler products
also forces this polynomial equality: their prime coefficients evaluate
the same finite polynomial at p^(-1/L), for infinitely many p tending to
infinity. The polynomial identity follows from the accumulation at zero.
An allowed b=0 would erase a whole word, since Q(s)/Q(s)=1; positive shifts
are essential.

The new increment is
  H_B(s)=(Q_B(s)-1)/beta.
Its coefficient at a prime is nonzero. It is not the product of the old
increments H_b. In particular the old degree-k zero-unit convolution lag
does not occur here. The fixed comparator mu-delta has the same unit-zero
convention and the same first coefficient access; the old degree-k lagged
comparator would be inappropriate for these new words.

2. Proof of local positivity, mass, and stochastic monotonicity
-------------------------------------------------------------
At a fixed prime p, use z=p^(-s) and q=p^(-b) in (0,1). Suppose the current
local factor is 1-P(z), with P(0)=0, nonnegative coefficients, and P(1)=1.
Then
  Q_new,p(z)=(1-P(z))/(1-P(qz))=1-P_new(z),
  P_new(z)=[P(z)-P(qz)]/[1-P(qz)].                  (2.1)
Every coefficient of the numerator is P_j(1-q^j)>=0. The denominator's
inverse is sum_(r>=0)P(qz)^r, also coefficientwise nonnegative. Because
the distribution is supported on positive integers, P(q)<=q<1. Therefore
  P_new(1)=(1-P(q))/(1-P(q))=1.                   (2.2)
The base is P_empty(z)=z. After the first positive shift all positive-degree
coefficients are strictly positive; the numerator term alone preserves
strict positivity at every subsequent step. Formal coefficient operations
and evaluation at one are justified by these nonnegative convergent sums.

Consequently the actual coefficient sequence a_B of Q_B is multiplicative,
  a_B(1)=1,
  a_B(p^e)=-Pr(N_p=e), e>=1,
  a_B(n)=(-1)^omega(n) product_(p^e||n)Pr(N_p=e).   (2.3)
Every |a_B(n)|<=1. The sign is mu(rad(n)), not mu(n): higher prime powers
remain present. Each prime has COMPLETE nonconstant absolute mass one.
This is an actual Euler-coefficient statement, not a random sign premise.

There is a useful construction of the new distribution. Draw independent
copies N_1,N_2,... of the old positive exponent. After drawing N_i, declare
success with probability 1-q^(N_i), independently conditional on the draw;
stop at the first success and sum all exponents drawn. The failures have
defective generating function P(qz), and the success has generating
function P(z)-P(qz). Their sum is (2.1). Under this coupling the new sum is
at least N_1. Thus adding a shift stochastically increases the prime
exponent; it does more than reduce the prime coefficient in isolation.
For a finite mean, differentiation at one gives exactly
  E N_new = E N_old/[1-P(q)].                      (2.4)
The finite rational Euler factors have a radius beyond one at each fixed
p, so the means here are finite. They need not be uniformly small across
primes or words.

In particular
  Pr(N_p=1)=kappa_p:=product_i(1-p^(-b_i)).         (2.5)
At fixed positive shifts kappa_p tends to one as p tends to infinity.
For squarefree n, a_B(n)=mu(n) product_(p|n)kappa_p. The normalized
coefficient tends to mu(n)/beta when a fixed number of its distinct primes
all grow. Thus raw small-prime attenuation is genuine, but it is not a
uniform attenuation of the normalized comparator at all prime locations.

3. Logarithms, the small-shift limit, and a signed renewal identity
-----------------------------------------------------------------
For Re(s)>1, absolute Euler expansions yield
  log Q_B(s)=-sum_(p,e>=1) ell_B(p^e)p^(-es),
  ell_B(p^e)=product_i(1-p^(-e b_i))/e >=0,         (3.1)
with ell zero off prime powers. Thus a_B=exp_*(-ell_B), whereas
a_B^(-1)=exp_*(ell_B) has nonnegative coefficients. These two exact facts
must not be confused: a positive inverse is not a signed remainder bound.

Since 1-exp(-b log n)<=b log n,
  ell_B(n)/beta <= Lambda(n)(log n)^(k-1)          (3.2)
on its prime-power support. At each FIXED integer n, taking all shifts to
zero with fixed word length gives
  (a_B(n)-delta(n))/beta
       -> -Lambda(n)(log n)^(k-1).                (3.3)
To see this, ell_B(n)/beta has this limit, while every convolution term of
degree at least two in exp_*(-ell_B) is O(beta^2) at that fixed n and has
only finitely many divisor tuples. This is a pointwise finite-divisor
argument. It is not uniform in x or in moment integrals. The limit is not
(-1)^k Lambda^{*k}, which belongs to products of old increments.

Let
  L_B(n)=Lambda(n) product_i(1-n^(-b_i)) >=0
on prime powers and zero elsewhere. Logarithmic differentiation gives
  a_B(n) log n = -(a_B*L_B)(n),
  A_B(x) log x - integral_1^x A_B(t)/t dt
       = -sum_(d<=x)L_B(d)A_B(x/d),               (3.4)
where A_B(x)=sum_(n<=x)a_B(n), with inclusive real endpoints and prefix
zero below one. This identity retains every quotient prefix and boundary.
For a complete center g_B(x), replacing A_B by A_B-g_B moves the ENTIRE
same expression for g_B to the right; no smooth term may be omitted.

The existing factorial-trace lemma supplies
sum_(n<=x)Lambda(n)/n=log x+O(1), with the inclusive endpoint adjustment
also bounded. Expanding the product in L_B, every nonempty shifted term
has absolutely convergent sum Lambda(n)/n^(1+t_S). Therefore
  sum_(n<=x)L_B(n)/n=log x+O_B(1).                (3.5)
This is an unconditional all-scale harmonic component estimate. It uses
the earlier proved actual-prime trace, not an RH or Mertens premise.
It does not turn (3.4) into a critical-norm contraction. The full signed
quotient prefixes in (3.4), after centering, remain the unknown quantity.

4. Exact positive survival kernel and the fresh/past obstruction
--------------------------------------------------------------
Write
  v_B(p^e)=Pr(N_p>e), e>=0.
Then v_B(p^0)=1, 0<=v_B(p^e)<=1, and
  [1-P_p(z)]/(1-z)=sum_(e>=0)v_B(p^e)z^e.
Extending v_B multiplicatively gives the exact identities
  a_B=mu*v_B,
  K_B(s):=sum_n v_B(n)n^(-s)=zeta(s)Q_B(s),
  A_B(x)=sum_(d<=x)v_B(d)M(x/d).                  (4.1)
The survival kernel is nonnegative. Its values depend on the actual prime
index and the fixed word, not on unknown signs of M. It acts on the full
original prefix; no source-domain complement is lost.

Let g_B(x)=1+sum_t C_t x^(1-t) be the UNNORMALIZED complete pole center,
so the numerical word output is F_B=(A_B-g_B)/beta. On an integer doubling
block X<=n<2X define
  H_B,past(n)=sum_(2<=d<=n)v_B(d)M(n/d)-g_B(n).
Every quotient n/d in this sum is strictly below X. Exactly
  beta F_B(n)=M(n)+H_B,past(n),
  beta^2 E_F(X)=E_M(X)+E_Hpast(X)
                 +2 X^(-2)sum_(X<=n<2X)M(n)H_B,past(n).            (4.2)
This is the complete signed-square gate. The new grammar creates no
fresh/fresh source interaction within that block: the entire fresh source
occurs in M(n) with coefficient 1/beta. A proof may succeed through its
correlation with the past compensation, but a norm bound on the past alone
does not establish that correlation. The observation is a structural
limitation and a guide to the proof obligation, not a rejection of the
grammar or a claim that the desired correlation is impossible.

5. Random stretched actual primes: justified expectation and covariance
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Independently draw N_p with the distributions of section 2. For each finite
x, only actual primes p<=x can appear. Define
  Z_x=sum_(S subset primes<=x)(-1)^|S|
                       1_{product_(p in S)p^(N_p)<=x}.
Then, with no infinite product or limit exchange,
  A_B(x)=E Z_x.                                    (5.1)
Each distinct prime is selected at most once; its selected exponent is N_p.
The sample coefficient at a given integer is mu(rad(n)) if every nonzero
prime exponent equals the sampled N_p, and zero otherwise. This is not a
model in which the actual Mobius signs have been declared independent.

For Re(s)>1, the corresponding infinite Euler expectation is also valid:
sum_p E p^(-Re(s)N_p)<=sum_p p^(-Re(s))<infinity, and the unsigned subset
expansion is bounded by product_p(1+p^(-Re(s))). It gives Q_B(s).
One must NOT interchange expectation with reciprocal Euler products:
  E[product_p(1-p^(-N_p s))^(-1)]
is generally different from Q_B(s)^(-1). The latter uses a local renewal
of fresh independent exponent draws; the former reuses the same sampled
exponent in all its multiples.

For subsets S,T put I_S(x)=1_{product_(p in S)p^(N_p)<=x}. Then exactly
  E Z_x^2=sum_(S,T)(-1)^(|S|+|T|)E[I_S(x)I_T(x)],
  Var Z_x=sum_(S,T)(-1)^(|S|+|T|)Cov(I_S(x),I_T(x)),
  |A_B(x)-g_B(x)|^2
       =E|Z_x-g_B(x)|^2-Var Z_x.                 (5.2)
All sums are finite. The joint threshold probability for S,T is correlated
through every prime shared by the subsets. Events are coordinatewise
decreasing in the independent N_p. Their covariance is nonnegative: the
usual one-coordinate monotone covariance inequality, followed by induction
over the finite product coordinates, proves this directly. But its sign
in (5.2) is positive or negative according to |S|+|T|. Same-parity and
opposite-parity pairs must both remain. Removing the latter removes the
cancellation under investigation. Jensen gives an upper bound by the full
expected sample square; it does not prove that square is subpower.

A conditional one-prime decomposition also exposes the missing estimate.
With the other draws fixed, let Z_x^(p) omit p. Then
  Z_x=Z_x^(p)-Z_(x/p^(N_p))^(p).
Controlling its conditional variance requires the whole signed prefix
Z_y^(p) over the random quotient values. Independence of N_p has therefore
not supplied an independent bound on the signed complement.

6. Proved stochastic and prime-power components; the retained mean bulk
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
For x/2<p<=x the only possible subset containing p is its singleton with
N_p=1: a second prime factor or N_p>=2 makes the product exceed x.
Thus
  Z_x=Z_x,low - sum_(x/2<p<=x)1_{N_p=1},
where the two terms use disjoint sets of independent draws. In particular
  Var Z_x=Var Z_x,low + V_high(x),
  V_high(x)=sum_(x/2<p<=x)kappa_p(1-kappa_p).
The elementary product inequality gives
  1-kappa_p <= sum_i p^(-b_i).
For the proposed 0<b_i<1,
  V_high(x)<=sum_i[1+x^(1-b_i)/(1-b_i)].           (6.1)
Consequently integral_1^infinity V_high(x)x^(-2)dx is finite, bounded by
sum_i[1+1/(b_i(1-b_i))]. This is a genuine uniform, subcritical fluctuation
component theorem. It does NOT bound the full Var Z_x, the full expected
square, or the deterministic mean. The mean of this component is still
minus sum_(x/2<p<=x)kappa_p, with kappa_p tending to one.

More generally, group the finite expectation by subset cardinality:
  A_B(x)=1-U_1(x)+U_2(x)-U_3(x)+...,
  U_j(x)=sum_(|S|=j)Pr(product_(p in S)p^(N_p)<=x)>=0.              (6.2)
The sum terminates for every x. Since kappa_p tends to one,
  sum_(p<=x)kappa_p <= U_1(x) <= pi(x),
and the first quantity is at least pi(x)/2 minus a fixed finite head.
The earlier elementary central-binomial argument supplies pi(x)>=c x/log x
eventually. Hence U_1(x)>=c_B x/log x eventually. Every real pole center
in this pilot has exponent strictly below one, so it is o(x/log x).
Thus the COMPLETE alternating complement sum_(j>=2)(-1)^j U_j must cancel
the macroscopic negative singleton mean before a square-root-scale moment
can hold. Low variance of the random singleton contribution does not
produce that deterministic cancellation.

There is an additional complete peel, with no exponent cutoff error:
  U_1(x)=sum_(p<=x)kappa_p + D_B,pp(x),
  0<=D_B,pp(x)<=pi(sqrt x).                        (6.3)
Here D_B,pp includes ALL exponents e>=2 of the single-prime branch and
has zero prefix below four. Using the previously proved Chebyshev upper
bound pi(y)<=C_pi y/log y for y>=2,
  ||D_B,pp||_crit^2
    := integral_1^infinity D_B,pp(x)^2/x^2 dx
    <=4 C_pi^2/log 4,
  E_Dpp(X)<=6 C_pi^2/(log X)^2, X>=4.             (6.4)
After division by beta the costs divide by beta and beta^2 respectively.
This peel does not remove nonsquarefree MULTI-prime branches; those may
still have bulk size and must remain. Its cross with the retained remainder
also remains, or can be paid by a triangle inequality in both directions.

Every center term with r=1-t<1/2 has finite critical norm
  ||C_t x^r||_crit^2=C_t^2/(1-2r).
A term with r=1/2 has bounded integer dyadic energy and can be paid in a
cofinal moment comparison, but has infinite critical integral. A term
with r>1/2 must remain in the complete compensated remainder. These
analytic distinctions do not permit changing the frozen numerical output.

7. Preconditioning and why local probability mass is not global recovery
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Equation (4.1) makes this a fixed positive preconditioning of the original
prefix. On the continuous finite or global norm
  ||f||_sigma^2=integral_1^T |f(x)|^2 x^(-2sigma-1)dx,
with f=0 below one, a dilation f(x/d) has norm at most d^(-sigma)||f||.
Absolute coefficient bounds for K_B and its Dirichlet inverse therefore
give the valid conditional operator bound
  ||K_B||, ||K_B^(-1)||
      <=product_(t>0)zeta(sigma+t)^(|e_t|),
provided sigma+min(t:e_t!=0,t>0)>1.               (7.1)
It is a uniform bounded critical preconditioning if the smallest surviving
shift is greater than 1/2. The pole centers are then strictly subcritical
padding. At equality the epsilon cost diverges; below it (7.1) is not a
finite critical estimate.

Every admitted multi-shift word with total shift below one has a smallest
shift below 1/2. That smallest subset shift comes from singleton positions;
no pair can cancel it. Its exponent is positive, and admission forces it
to be one. The corresponding pole of K_B is at 1-min b_i>1/2 with positive
nonzero residue. Thus all such multi-words require a genuine supercritical
compensation, rather than being covered by the easy bounded critical
preconditioning theorem. Indeed f(x)=1 for x>=1 has finite critical norm;
K_B f is the prefix of v_B, whose Mellin transform has this pole. A finite
critical norm for that output would force holomorphy to the right of 1/2,
a contradiction. This proves unboundedness of the unrestricted critical
operator, not merely failure of an absolute bound. Affine centering of
the one actual source does not repair the linear operator on arbitrary
inputs.

Separately let rho_B=a_B^(-1), the NONNEGATIVE global renewal inverse.
Do not confuse it with the survival kernel v_B. Then
  sum_(d<=x)rho_B(d) A_B(x/d)=1.                   (7.2)
Its Dirichlet series is
  Q_B(s)^(-1)=zeta(s) product_(t>0)zeta(s+t)^(-e_t).
The shifted product's coefficients k have sum |k(n)|/n<infinity. Hence,
by dominated convergence in sum k(d)floor(x/d)/x,
  sum_(n<=x)rho_B(n)=c_B x+o_B(x),
  c_B=product_(t>0)zeta(1+t)^(-e_t)>0.            (7.3)
Thus sum_n rho_B(n)n^(-sigma) diverges for every sigma<=1. Local mass one
for the prime-exponent distributions has NOT become a critical mass-one
global renewal operator. A particularly concrete witness is
  f_0(x)=1 for 1<=x<2, zero otherwise.
It has ||f_0||_crit^2=1/2, while
  sum_d rho_B(d)f_0(x/d)
     =sum_(x/2<d<=x)rho_B(d)=c_B x/2+o_B(x),       (7.4)
whose critical norm is infinite. This rules out the proposed natural
positive-kernel norm contraction. It does not forbid a signed argument
which retains cancellations in the inverse or in (4.2).

8. Simple poles, complete centers, and the ordinary-zeta bridge
--------------------------------------------------------------
The pilot total shift below one and every merged positive exponent equal
to one make each positive stencil location t a simple real pole at
r_t=1-t in (0,1). All other zeta arguments there are positive real and
different from one, hence nonzero. The exact unnormalized residue is
  R_t^Q=product_(u!=t)zeta(1-t+u)^(e_u),
  C_t=R_t^Q/(1-t),
  F_B(x)=[A_B(x)-1-sum_t C_t x^(1-t)]/beta.       (8.1)
Terms with negative exponent cause a zero at their real pole location,
not an extra real pole. Pole order restrictions concern the MERGED stencil.
The displayed center is complete; it includes its value at x=1 and every
power, even a power analytically peelable from an all-scale comparison.

On Re(s)>1 the exact Mellin identity is
  s integral_1^infinity F_B(x)x^(-s-1)dx
    =(Q_B(s)-1)/beta
        -sum_t (C_t/beta)*s/(s-(1-t)).            (8.2)
Every fraction, including its constant part, remains. A cofinal family
of full integer dyadic moments E_F(X)<=C_q X^(1/q), for EVERY dyadic X
and unbounded q, gives finite weighted L2 for every sigma>1/2. Choose
1/q<2sigma-1 and sum the geometric dyadic bound. Between n and n+1 only
the smooth center changes; its derivative is bounded by
sum_t |R_t^Q|n^(-t)/beta. The finite head and this cell cost are integrable
in all these norms. Cauchy-Schwarz then makes (8.2) holomorphic on Re(s)>1/2.

At a nonreal zero rho of ordinary zeta with Re(rho)>1/2, e_0=-1 supplies
a pole of Q_B. Holomorphy requires at least one farther-right zero at
rho+t for a surviving positive exponent. If none existed, all shifted
positive factors would be nonzero, while any zeros in negative-exponent
factors would only increase the pole order. Multiplicities may be spread
among several shifted factors; no simple-zero or single-factor cancellation
assumption is needed. Iterating such a right shift, with fixed positive
minimum shift, reaches Re>1 after finitely many steps, contradicting the
Euler product. The usual symmetry then gives RH. This is a conditional
qualitative theorem; the complete cofinal premise has not been proved.

Quantitative recovery is not the constant beta or the local probability
mass. Away from the real pole of zeta, the exact modulus identity is
  |1/zeta(s)|=|Q_B(s)|
       product_(t>0,e_t>0)|1/zeta(s+t)|^(e_t)
       product_(t>0,e_t<0)|zeta(s+t)|^(-e_t).       (8.3)
It generates a shifted recovery tree, retaining all branches and powers
until the real part exceeds one. Mellin bounds for Q_B retain the full
norm of F_B and every fraction in (8.2). The depth is bounded by the
required rightward distance divided by min positive shift, but the branching
and powers can make the constants large. Near real zeta poles one must
clear them with (s-1)zeta(s), not divide by small sampled numerator balls.
No bounded critical inverse, all-height growth bound, or finite-prefix
inverse follows from the qualitative zero-chain argument alone.

9. Same-word control poles
-------------------------
For a base Dirichlet series F, applying the SAME append grammar gives
  F_B(s)=product_S F(s+t_S)^((-1)^|S|).            (9.1)
This is the reciprocal exponent convention to (1.1). For the one-source
control F=zeta, its simple pole at one occurs with exponent +1. For the
partial-prime-13 squarefree sign source,
  F(s)=zeta(s)/zeta(2s)
          *product_(p<=13)(1-p^(-s))/(1+p^(-s)),
its residue at one is also finite and positive. In both cases all shifted
F(1+t), t>0, are finite, positive, and nonzero. Consequently EVERY fixed
word retains a simple positive-residue pole at s=1. Its order is one,
not the word depth. The old product-of-increments control had a different
pole order; reusing that argument here would be incorrect.

Actual-zeta centers at 1-t<1 cannot remove this pole at one. Hence neither
control can satisfy all cofinal full-moment premises, irrespective of
finite gates. This is an analytic falsifier, not a numerical score. The
mass-one positivity proof of section 2 belongs to the actual 1-z local
source and does not extend to the control's initial 1/(1-z) or 1+z factors.
Control failure detects an all-scale mean/pole mismatch; it is not by
itself evidence that the actual composite cancellation has been explained.

10. Fresh positive-renewal pass on previous candidate 10
------------------------------------------------------
Keep its exact definitions u=1/3, v=2/3, w=1/(uv)=9/2,
  a_b=mu*(n^(-b)), h_b=(a_b-delta)/b,
  p=h_u*h_v, S(x)=sum_(n<=x)p(n),
  S=D+T+C, R_red=T+C-m_u x^(2/3).
Here D is the COMPLETE same-prime overlap; its critical-norm peel, the
smaller m_v x^(1/3) peel, and all hyperbola complements remain as proved
in SHIFTED_QUOTIENT_CANDIDATE_10_REVIEW_001.txt. This is not a new word
Q_(u,v), nor a new normalizer or changed candidate.

The Dirichlet inverse of each a_b is the positive multiplicative sequence
  r_b(n)=product_(p|n)(1-p^(-b)), r_b(1)=1,
  sum_n r_b(n)n^(-s)=zeta(s)/zeta(s+b).
It obeys 0<=r_b(n)<=1. Let rho=r_u*r_v. Direct convolution gives
  rho*p=w*(r_u-delta)*(r_v-delta)=:b_pos>=0.       (10.1)
The positivity is coefficientwise and includes the unit-zero conventions.
With B_pos(x)=sum_(n<=x)b_pos(n), the COMPLETE reduced renewal equation is
  sum_(d<=x)rho(d)R_red(x/d)
     =B_pos(x)-sum_(d<=x)rho(d)D(x/d)
          -m_u x^(2/3)sum_(d<=x)rho(d)d^(-2/3).   (10.2)
There is no missing prime overlap, residue, or quotient domain in this
identity. Its right side is a DIFFERENCE of complete positive branches;
the source sign or positivity of its individual branches does not make
the right side small.

The relevant mass can be proved without PNT or an RH-strength premise.
Since r_b=1*(mu(n)n^(-b)), the exact floor identity and the convergent
series at 1+b give, for 0<b<1,
  R_b(x):=sum_(n<=x)r_b(n)
     =c_b x+O_b(x^(1-b)), c_b=1/zeta(1+b)>0.      (10.3)
The error follows by bounding the floor loss by sum_(d<=x)d^(-b) and
