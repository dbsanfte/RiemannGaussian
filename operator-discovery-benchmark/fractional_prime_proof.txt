1. THE OPERATOR, ITS DOMAIN, AND A FORMAL INVERSE

Let D denote ordinary Dirichlet convolution, f(1)=1, and 0<alpha<1.
Set D_alpha f(1)=0 and D_alpha f(n)=(log n)^alpha f(n) for n>=2.
All powers here are positive-real powers of log n. Define

 L_alpha(f)=f_D^(-1) D D_alpha f,
 R_alpha(f)(1)=0, R_alpha(f)(n)=L_alpha(f)(n)/(log n)^alpha,
 B_alpha(f)=W R_alpha(f), W=sum_(k>=1)mu(k)U_k/k.             (1)

U_k is perfect-power placement, with zero coefficient at 1. The sums
are coefficient-local. V=sum U_k/k satisfies VW=WV=I, since the weight
at U_j is (1/j)sum_(d|j)mu(d). This holds for every formal sequence with
coefficient 1 equal to zero, not only for prime-supported sequences.

The equality f D L_alpha(f)=D_alpha f gives, at n>=2,

 f(n)=R_alpha(f)(n)+sum_(d|n,1<d<n)
        f(n/d)(log d)^alpha R_alpha(f)(d)/(log n)^alpha.       (2)

Thus first apply V to a proposed B output and then use (2) in increasing
n to recover a unique normalized f. This proves a universal triangular
bijection between normalized formal units and sequences with value 0
at 1. It does not assert a Dirichlet-product homomorphism.

At alpha=1, the derivation D_1 obeys Leibniz's rule and
R_1(f)=LOG_D f, hence B_1(f)=W LOG_D f is the established prime decoder.
For alpha!=1 the ordinary normalized-unit lift is
S_alpha(f)=EXP_D(R_alpha(f))=EXP_D(V B_alpha(f)). For zeta^beta input,
the R or B coefficient at squarefree pq is beta^2 times that for zeta.
Ordinary output root beta would instead multiply its logarithm by beta.
At alpha=beta=1/2 and n=6 these differ. B itself has coefficient 0 at
1 and is not a normalized unit to which ordinary unit roots apply.

2. WHAT ZETA SUPPLIES WITHOUT AN INPUT PRIME ORACLE

For the zeta coefficient sequence u(n)=1,

 Lambda_alpha(n)=sum_(d|n)mu(d)(log(n/d))^alpha,
 R_alpha(u)(n)=Lambda_alpha(n)/(log n)^alpha.                 (3)

The production program computes Lambda recursively by inverting the
divisor sum of (log n)^alpha. It does not accept prime indicators or
prime-weight data as inputs. Independent verification factors n and
computes (3) by squarefree subset differences. These are different
algorithms. The inverse computation still contains arithmetic work;
it is not a fast-primality claim or an estimate of prime distribution.

Write n=product p^e_p, m=omega(n), g=gcd(e_p), t_p=log p/log n.
All m steps t_p are positive and sum t_p<=1. Put

 b_k=(-1)^(k+1)binom(alpha,k)>0, sum_(k>=1)b_k=1, b_1=alpha,
 T_k(t)=sum_(S subset primes) (-1)^(m-|S|)(sum_(p in S)t_p)^k.

The multinomial expansion gives T_k=0 for k<m and T_k>0 for k>=m:
only monomials involving every one of the m variables survive.
In particular T_k(t)<= (sum t_p)^k. The binomial expansion, including
its absolutely convergent endpoint at sum t_p=1, gives

 R_alpha(u)(n)=(-1)^(m-1)sum_(k>=m)b_k T_k(t).

Removing perfect-power lifts as in (1) and using homogeneity yields

 B_alpha(u)(n)=(-1)^(m-1)sum_(k>=m)b_k T_k(t) J_(k-1)(g),   (4)
 J_(k-1)(g)=sum_(d|g)mu(d)d^(k-1)
           =product_(ell|g, ell prime)(1-ell^(k-1)).

The interchange is legitimate: the d sum is finite and each binomial
expansion is absolutely convergent. If g=1 then J=1. If g>1, J_0=0,
and all k>1 terms have sign (-1)^omega(g). Consequently, for EVERY
integer n>=2 and 0<alpha<1,

 sign B_alpha(u)(n)=(-1)^(omega(n)+omega(g)-1).               (5)

At least one term in (4) is strictly nonzero. This also explains why
using Omega(g) in place of omega(g) is a false short-training rule.

3. A SHARP UNIFORM PRIME/COMPOSITE SEPARATION

For positive x, x^alpha=c_alpha integral_0^infty
(1-exp(-xt))t^(-1-alpha)dt, c_alpha=alpha/Gamma(1-alpha)>0.
Applying the finite differences in (3) gives

 |Lambda_alpha(n)|=c_alpha integral_0^infty
 exp(-log(n/rad(n))*t) product_(p|n)(1-exp(-log(p)*t))
 t^(-1-alpha)dt.                                             (6)

If m>=2, discard the exponential and all but two factors. Formula (6)
then gives, for any two distinct divisors p,q,

 |R_alpha(n)| <= [(log p)^alpha+(log q)^alpha
                         -(log p+log q)^alpha]/(log n)^alpha
                 <= delta_alpha, delta_alpha=2^(1-alpha)-1.

The final inequality follows from concavity, and log n>=log p+log q.
Also |J_(k-1)(g)|<=g^(k-1). Combining this with (4) and
T_k(g*t)=g^k T_k(t), for m>=2 we obtain

 |B_alpha(n)| <= |R_alpha(n^(1/g))|/g <=delta_alpha/g.        (7)

For n=p^e with e>1, the k=1 term vanishes because J_0(e)=0, while
e*t_p=1. Therefore

 |B_alpha(p^e)| <= (1/e)sum_(k>=2)b_k=(1-alpha)/e.            (8)

Since 2^x-1>=x/2 for 0<=x<=1 (its derivative is at least log 2>1/2),
(8) is at most delta_alpha. At a prime p, (3) is (log p)^alpha and
the W sum has only its k=1 term, so B_alpha(p)=1 exactly. Hence

 B_alpha(p)=1 for every prime p,
 |B_alpha(n)|<=delta_alpha<1 for every composite n.           (9)

The constant is the supremum: for squarefree pq,
B_alpha(pq)=1-[(log p)^alpha+(log q)^alpha]/(log p+log q)^alpha.
Taking consecutive primes tending to infinity makes log p/log q->1,
so its magnitude tends to delta_alpha. This uses the already known
prime asymptotic p_j~j log j, not a new distribution theorem; see
https://dlmf.nist.gov/27.12.E1. Even Bertrand's classical bound suffices.
The test near 10^12 illustrates sharpness but does not prove it.

Equivalently, ||B_alpha(u)-1_primes||_(coefficient infinity norm)
<=delta_alpha, so the prime indicator is recovered uniformly as
alpha->1 from below. This is a statement about coefficients, not a
uniform critical-strip convergence statement for scalar Dirichlet series.

5. A NATIVE ZETA BRIDGE AND ITS RIGOROUS INFINITE TAIL

For Re(s)>1, termwise integration and absolute convergence give

 N_alpha(s)=c_alpha integral_0^infty
             [zeta(s)-zeta(s+t)]t^(-1-alpha)dt
           =sum_(n>=2)(log n)^alpha n^-s,
 L_alpha(u)(s)=N_alpha(s)/zeta(s)=sum_n Lambda_alpha(n)n^-s.

For alpha=1/2, t=u^2 gives the independently evaluable formulas

 N_half(s)=1/sqrt(pi) integral_0^infty
             [zeta(s)-zeta(s+u^2)]/u^2 du
          =-2/sqrt(pi) integral_0^infty zeta'(s+u^2)du.        (10)

The second equality is integration by parts. The endpoint terms vanish.
Away from the horizontal preimage of zeta's pole at 1, these integrals
continue holomorphically, by compact Taylor bounds at u=0 and
Euler-series estimates at infinity. The tested zero disks have large
nonzero imaginary part, so the pole is excluded throughout their paths.

For the first implementation, integrate the Taylor coefficients a_k
of zeta(s+z) through k=K at u<=d. If |z|<=R has |zeta(s+z)|<=M and
q=d^2/R<1, the neglected integral is bounded by
M/d * q^(K+1)/[(2K+1)(1-q)]. Native Arb quadrature handles d<=u<=U.
For u>=U use (zeta(s)-1)/U, with error at most

 2^(-x)(1+2/(x-1))/(2 log(2) U^3), x=Re(s)+U^2>1.

For the independent implementation of the derivative integral,
|zeta'(x+iy)|<=D(x)=2^(-x)[log2+2log2/(x-1)+2/(x-1)^2].
Its full omitted N_half tail is at most D(x)/(sqrt(pi)log2 U).
The completed independent audit uses this derivative integral at 448 bits,
U=9, changed quadrature edges, and an independent coefficient cutoff 6144.
The producer uses the subtraction quotient at 384 bits and cutoff 4096.

Equations (6)-(9) imply |Lambda_half(n)|<=sqrt(log n). For N>=3,
the native coefficient comparisons may therefore use the conservative
integer-log tail N^(1-sigma)[log N/(sigma-1)+1/(sigma-1)^2].

They are not numerical evaluations of the complete normalized B_half.

6. WHY THE DECODER HAS AN INVERSE-SQUARE-ROOT ZERO OBSTRUCTION

On Re(s)>1, normalization of coefficients is a fractional integral:

 R_half(s)=1/sqrt(pi) integral_0^infty
            t^(-1/2) L_half(s+t)dt,
 B_half(s)=sum_(k>=1)mu(k)R_half(k*s)/k.                     (11)

Let rho be a simple ordinary zeta zero, with a zero-free rightward
horizontal corridor apart from rho, and assume N_half(rho)!=0.
Then L_half has residue C=N_half(rho)/zeta'(rho)!=0. Put w=s-rho.
Split the t integral at a small fixed h. Subtract C/(w+t) in the
local part. Its analytic remainder and the t>=h part are holomorphic
near w=0 on the continued branch. The identity
integral_0^infty t^(-1/2)/(w+t)dt=pi*w^(-1/2) therefore yields

 R_half(s)=sqrt(pi)*C*(s-rho)^(-1/2)+H(s), H holomorphic.

For the two certified old zeros with Re(rho)=1/2, the k=2 part of
(11) is holomorphic on a checked horizontal corridor from 2*rho.
All k>=3 terms converge normally near rho in their initial domains,
by the bounded R coefficients and exponential decay in k. Consequently,
the completed numerator and corridor certificates prove that B_half has
the same leading inverse-square-root branch at these two old zeros.

This is branch retention, not preservation of the ordinary zeta zero
set by a normalized unit multiplier. EXP(R_half) has a branched
essential obstruction at these points. A general nonvanishing theorem
N_half(rho)!=0 for EVERY zeta zero is unproved. Even such a theorem
would require a separate global analytic-domain/estimate argument to
produce an RH consequence. Nothing excludes off-line zeta zeros here.

8. PRIOR ART AND WHAT IS ACTUALLY VERIFIED

The fractional zeta derivative itself is established prior art. Guariglia
(2019), equation (1.1), explicitly gives the positive-log Dirichlet series
with phase e^(i*pi*alpha) in his derivative convention and attributes the
construction to his 2015 work:
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13662-019-2202-5
The primary published PDF is archived in literature/fractional/guariglia_2019.pdf.
Our positive-log convention omits that phase by definition.
The integer-order generalized von Mangoldt coefficient mu*log^k is
also established; Pratt et al. (2020), section 2, specifies k in N:
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40687-019-0199-8
Guariglia (2024), Fractional calculus, Riemann zeta function and Euler
products, explicitly studies logarithmic fractional derivatives:
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405896324009169
Its full formulas still require comparison; abstract relevance alone
does not settle identity of operators. Fractional calculus, Dirichlet
inversion and W are not newly discovered primitives.
