1. Exact source factors, weights, and scope

Let U>=1 be fixed, A(n)=mu(n)1_(n<=U), c=sum_(n<=U)mu(n)/n, T=c delta-A,
E=delta+One*T. The Dirichlet polynomials obey T(s)=c-A(s), T(1)=0 and |c|<=2.
The complete E function is E(s)=1+zeta(s)T(s), holomorphic across s=1.
Fix nonnegative integers p,m, u=1/3, theta=2/3, and put
 F_U(s)=zeta(s+u) A(s)^p E(s)^m,
 B_j=A^p*T^j, r_j=p+j, 0<=j<=m.
Thus F_U(s)=zeta(s+u)sum_j binom(m,j)zeta(s)^j B_j(s).
This finite expansion is used only for moment upper bounds AFTER proving
the pole at 1 removable in the complete E factor. It does not license
discarding principal parts of the separate terms or any signed source cross.

For r_j>=1, B_j has support d<=R_j=U^r_j and coefficients bounded by
 |B_j(d)|<=3^j d_(r_j)(d).
Indeed |A|<=One restricted <=U and |T(1)|<=3, |T(d)|<=1 for d>1.
Replacing |T| by 3 One on [1,U] and convolving proves the displayed bound.
For r_j=0, B_j=delta and R_j=1; treat this constant polynomial separately.
The exact signed coefficients B_j are kept in the low-height Gram below;
their divisor envelope supplies optional proved fallbacks and the high-height
provider premise, rather than defining or replacing that joined low-height leaf.

The centered real staircase is
 C_U(x)=sum_(n<=x)(K*A^p*E^m)(n)-(M_U/theta)x^theta, x>0,
 K(n)=n^(-u), the coefficient prefix is zero for x<1, and
 M_U=A(theta)^p E(theta)^m.
For m=2 the ordinary coefficient moment equals M_U by the previous proof.
For m>=3 this memo does NOT claim convergence of that coefficient moment.
The residue M_U remains a valid ANALYTIC residue: the only crossed real pole
of F_U on a suitable line is theta. Its full J_theta compensation, including
the unit J_theta(1)=1, and its opposite drift in the source join must remain.

2. The additional high-height import and its actual premise

The new provider is the unconditional twisted critical fourth-moment bound
 integral_0^Y |zeta(1/2+it)|^4 |P(1/2+it)|^2 dt
       <= C_(lambda,epsilon,coefficient-class) Y^(1+epsilon),
where P(s)=sum_(d<=R)alpha_d d^(-s), alpha_d<<_nu d^nu for every nu>0
with uniform coefficient-class constants, and
 R<=Y^(1/4-lambda), fixed 0<lambda<1/4.                 (2.1)
The constant is uniform over complex coefficient phases in that class.
Do not assert this rule at heights failing its length premise.

Read primary research source: Bettin, Bui, Li and Radziwill,
 A quadratic divisor problem and moments of the Riemann zeta-function,
 https://arxiv.org/html/1609.02539v1
Introduction equation (1) states the needed upper bound; Theorem 1.1 gives
the stronger asymptotic in this length range. The paper credits Watt's theorem:
 N. Watt, Kloosterman sums and a mean value for Dirichlet polynomials,
 Journal of Number Theory 53 (1995), 179-210,
 https://doi.org/10.1006/jnth.1995.1086
The original publisher's abstract was read, but its formula is not exposed as
text there; the precise imported version (2.1) is the one directly read in
the Bettin et al. paper. No larger polynomial-length range is imported.
This is an additional mathematical trust primitive, not a consequence of the
previous unweighted fourth-moment provider alone.

For 0<sigma<1/2, form the normalized critical polynomial for B_j:
 P_j(s)=R_j^(-(1/2-sigma))
           sum_(d<=R_j)B_j(d)d^(1/2-sigma)d^(-s).
At s=1/2+it this equals R_j^(-(1/2-sigma))B_j(sigma+it).
Its coefficients are bounded by 3^j d_(r_j)(d), uniformly U, because
(d/R_j)^(1/2-sigma)<=1. For each fixed r and nu, d_r(d)<=C_(r,nu)d^nu;
this elementary divisor bound supplies the required coefficient class.
Multiplying any coefficient by d^(iv) does not change that class.
Consequently (2.1) gives
 integral_Y^(2Y)|zeta(1/2+it)|^4 |B_j(sigma+it)|^2 dt
       <= C R_j^(1-2sigma)Y^(1+epsilon)                (2.2)
if, for example, R_j<=(Y/2)^(1/4-lambda). Constants depend on fixed
sigma,j,p,lambda,epsilon, not U,Y or the coefficient phases.
The normalization exponent is NOT 2(1-sigma). It is important that the
polynomial remains inside the average.

3. Exact unweighted polynomial mean and a safe all-height fallback

For B(sigma+it)=sum_(d<=R)b_d d^(-sigma-it), the finite interval Gram is exact:
 integral_a^(a+Y)|B(sigma+it)|^2 dt
 =sum_(d,e<=R)b_d conj(b_e)(de)^(-sigma)
                integral_a^(a+Y)exp(it log(e/d))dt.    (3.1)
The diagonal is Y. For d!=e the entry is the full geometric integral, with
its phase; it is bounded by min(Y,2/|log(e/d)|).
An elementary estimate, requiring no imported Hilbert inequality, is
 integral_a^(a+Y)|B|^2
 <=[Y+4R(1+log R)]sum_(d<=R)|b_d|^2 d^(-2sigma).      (3.2)
To prove it, use |log(e/d)|>=(e-d)/R for e>d, sum by h=e-d, and apply
sum_d |v_d v_(d+h)|<=sum_d |v_d|^2. This keeps a safe factor4.

For the coefficient class in section 1 and 0<sigma<1/2,
 sum |B_j(d)|^2 d^(-2sigma)
 <=[3^(2j)/(1-2sigma)]R_j^(1-2sigma)(1+log R_j)^(r_j^2-1). (3.3)
Use d_r(d)^2<=d_(r^2)(d): pairs of r-bin valuations can be injected into
nonnegative r-by-r valuation matrices via a fixed matrix with those margins.
The usual elementary summatory bound for d_(r^2), followed by Abel summation,
proves (3.3). A slightly larger fixed logarithmic degree is also safe.
When Y>=R, the diagonal cost is essentially R^(1-2sigma)Y times logs.
When Y<R, the R-term is present: its exponent is R^(2-2sigma). Thus an
all-height Hilbert/Gram fallback does NOT eliminate the old low-height power.
There is no unlicensed assertion that R<=Y^(1/4-lambda) at every height.

4. Off-critical weighted fourth moment: a valid phase-uniform transfer

Direct three-lines applied to zeta(z)^2Q(z) is NOT sufficient for the desired
gain. Matching Q(a+it)=B(sigma+it) at a=1-sigma generally gives the wrong
critical coefficient amplification. Instead transfer zeta alone and retain
the finite polynomial, with phase rotations, inside each boundary average.

Let H(z)=zeta(z)^2-(z-1)^(-2)-2 gamma_E(z-1)^(-1). It is holomorphic on
1/2<=Re z<=2 and has polynomial growth at vertical infinity. In a strip of
width l=3/2 put x=a-1/2, 0<x<l. Its two Poisson kernels are
 K_0(x,v)=sin(pi x/l)/[2l(cosh(pi v/l)-cos(pi x/l))],
 K_1(x,v)=sin(pi x/l)/[2l(cosh(pi v/l)+cos(pi x/l))].
They are positive, their combined integral is 1, and they decay exponentially
in |v| for fixed x. The strip Poisson formula holds for real and imaginary
parts of H. It follows by exhaustion of the strip; polynomial growth kills
the harmonic measure of the remote horizontal edges. Jensen gives
 |H(a+it)|^2<=integral K_0(x,v)|H(1/2+i(t+v))|^2dv
               +integral K_1(x,v)|H(2+i(t+v))|^2dv.
At Re z=2, H is bounded. Restoring the explicit principal part only on
Y<=t<=2Y, Y>=2, and bounding its critical-boundary part, gives
 |zeta(a+it)|^4
 <= C_a[1+integral K_0(x,v)|zeta(1/2+i(t+v))|^4dv].    (4.1)
This proof also covers a=1 on the interval t>=2; no singular t=0 is integrated
when restoring the principal part on that line.

Multiply (4.1) by |B_j(sigma+it)|^2 and integrate t in [Y,2Y].
For |v|<=Y/2 set w=t+v, so w lies in [Y/2,5Y/2]. The polynomial becomes
 sum B_j(d)d^(iv)d^(-sigma-iw). Its normalized coefficient bounds are EXACTLY
those of section 2, independent of v. Apply (2.1) on a covering interval of
height comparable to Y and use R_j<=(Y/2)^(1/4-lambda). Integrating the
positive kernel costs at most its total mass.
For |v|>Y/2 use exponential kernel decay, the ordinary convexity bound for
zeta on the critical line, and
 |B_j(sigma+it)|<=C R_j^(1-sigma)(1+log R_j)^(r_j-1).
The remote contribution is at most a fixed constant times
 Y R_j^(2-2sigma)(1+log R_j)^C exp(-c_a Y).
Since R_j<=Y^(1/4-lambda), it is absorbed in
C R_j^(1-2sigma)Y^(1+epsilon), uniformly U,Y. The constant and restored-pole
terms are paid by (3.2)-(3.3), using the same length premise to absorb fixed
logs into Y^epsilon. Thus for each fixed a in (1/2,2),
 integral_Y^(2Y)|zeta(a+it)|^4|B_j(sigma+it)|^2dt
       <=C R_j^(1-2sigma)Y^(1+epsilon).                (4.2)
This is a proved transfer from (2.1), not an unproved weighted analogue of
the scalar three-lines rule. Complex phases and remote heights were included.
Weighted second moment follows by Cauchy from (4.2) and (3.2), with the same
R_j^(1-2sigma)Y^(1+epsilon) cost. Negative heights follow by conjugation and
the same phase-uniform coefficient class.

5. Complete high-tail compiler exponents, including j=0 and j=1

For fixed 0<sigma<1/2, u=1/3, lambda in (0,1/4), and any fixed total
epsilon>0, the following bounds hold at EVERY height
 Y>=2 U^(r_j/(1/4-lambda)):
 integral_Y^(2Y) |zeta(s+u)zeta(s)^j B_j(s)/s|^2 dt
       <=C U^(r_j(1-2sigma))Y^(d_j+epsilon),           (5.1)
where s=sigma+it and
 d_0=-sigma-u,
 d_1=1-u-3sigma,
 d_j=j-u-(j+3)sigma, j>=2.                            (5.2)
For j=0 use only (3.2), with the constant-polynomial branch separate.
For j=1 reflect by the functional equation and use the weighted second
moment from section 4. For j>=2 reflect all 2j powers, put four inside (4.2)
at a=1-sigma, and pay the remaining 2j-4 by convexity on that same line.
The shift zeta(s+u)^2 is paid by convexity; |s|^(-2)<=Y^(-2).
The arbitrarily small provider/convexity/divisor slacks can be allocated to
sum below the displayed fixed total epsilon. All constants are uniform U,Y,
and depend only on fixed p,m,sigma,u,lambda,epsilon and the imported provider.

Use a COMMON H=2U^tau, with tau>=max_j r_j/(1/4-lambda), or its dyadic ceiling
between H and 2H. Require EVERY d_j+epsilon<0. Summing all dyadic heights
starting at H, then using Cauchy ONLY for this high-tail upper estimate, gives
 I_high=(1/(2pi))integral_(|t|>=H)|F_U(s)/s|^2dt
 <=sum_j C_j U^[r_j(1-2sigma)+tau(d_j+epsilon)].        (5.3)
Factors (m+1)binom(m,j)^2, the negative-height cost, and
[1-2^(d_j+epsilon)]^(-1) are fixed constants. This is the exact common-cutoff
U exponent for each branch. With its own H_j=2U^(r_j/(1/4-lambda)), branch j
has exponent r_j[(1-2sigma)+(d_j+epsilon)/(1/4-lambda)].
Individual branch thresholds cannot replace the common threshold in the
definition of the complete joined low auxiliary without explicit extra bands.

In particular for p=2,m=3, all d_j are negative whenever 4/9<sigma<1/2.
Discarding the favorable negative H powers in (5.3) still gives the safe bound
 I_high<=C U^[(p+m)(1-2sigma)].                         (5.4)
At U<=N^alpha, N=4X-1, the high auxiliary energy exponent is at most
 (2sigma-1)+alpha(p+m)(1-2sigma)
       =(1-2sigma)[alpha(p+m)-1].                     (5.5)
It is strictly negative for alpha(p+m)<1. Thus the HIGH auxiliary of E^3 is
paid at alpha=1/24; no nominated sigma is needed, and a detector can enumerate
the entire rational feasible region. For m>=4 this fourth-plus-convexity
library has no belowhalf convergent high line: that is a provider limitation,
not an arithmetic impossibility theorem. Additional joint moment providers
must carry their own exact premises.

6. The compulsory full low Gram and coercive recovery

Define on the SAME contour, with the SAME U and common H,
 I_low=(1/(2pi))integral_(|t|<H)|F_U(s)/s|^2dt.
It is a finite Hermitian joined quadratic form. For real source weights,
 I_low=sum_(j,k)binom(m,j)binom(m,k)
          sum_(d,e) B_j(d)B_k(e)(de)^(-sigma) G_jk(log(d/e)),
 G_jk(w)=(1/(2pi))integral_(|t|<H)
   |zeta(s+u)|^2 zeta(s)^j conj(zeta(s)^k) exp(-itw)/|s|^2 dt. (6.1)
EVERY j,k cross, d=e diagonal, shared prime, repeated valuation, and unit is
present. Hermitian symmetry makes the whole value real and nonnegative; the
separate j,k terms are not nonnegative. A Gaussian height mask gives the
analogous exact rapidly convergent Gram; it must retain its full high remainder.
The independent CORE_WEIGHTED_MOMENT_SHARPNESS_REVIEW_021.txt supplies a
sign-sensitive Gaussian Gram and harmonic-null witness grammar in more detail.

Increasing tau makes the high-tail cost smaller but expands THIS low Gram.
There is no proof which optimizes tau and forgets that change. The elementary
global fallback remains
 I_low<=C U^[2(p+m)(1-sigma)]                          (6.2)
on a contour whose full global norm exists. It is not a signed-source gain.
For actual mu a new uniform bound I_low<=C U^b(1+log U)^k would pay the complete
component cofinally only after testing
 2sigma-1+alpha*b<=0,
with equality meaning a fixed logarithmic cost. A strict inequality pays a
bounded component. The detector can derive this leaf by backward exponent
search; it is an OPEN arithmetic input, not theorem (2.1) used out of range.

Here is exact recovery, including the integer/head issue. By the reviewed
Gaussian Perron argument, if the full line norm is finite then C_U is the
inverse Mellin transform of F_U(s)/s, with the theta pole removed as written
in section 1. Split that transform at |t|=H into real functions C_low,C_high.
They sum to C_U on ALL x>0 and are orthogonal in the global Mellin norm.
They need not be orthogonal on an individual block. No blockwise cross is
erased. Global norm is exactly I_low+I_high.

For n>=1 let q_low(n)=integral_n^(n+1)C_low(x)dx and likewise q_high, with
q_low(0)=q_high(0)=0. Let C_n=P(n)-(M_U/theta)n^theta. Then
 C_n=q_low(n)+q_high(n)+e_n,
 e_n=(M_U/theta)[integral_n^(n+1)x^theta dx-n^theta],
 |e_n|<=|M_U|n^(theta-1).                             (6.3)
This averages rather than samples the Mellin auxiliaries; arbitrary L2
functions may not be sampled at the integer points without a further rule.
The actual integer C_n, unit, and compensator remain exact in (6.3).
Cell averaging maps each global Mellin norm to weighted discrete l2 with a
fixed constant (at most4 for sigma<1/2). The previous full-history Hardy/Abel
rule then transfers q_low and q_high through every polynomial-log channel
and orthogonal cell mean. It retains every earlier prefix, not a cell restart.

The error e_n can be paid more sharply than putting its entire unit/head into
the same global norm. For p_channel(t)=sum c_l t^l, eta>=0, discrete Abel gives
 |channel(e)_n|<=2^ceil(eta)|M_U|
   [sum|c_l|+2(1+1/(1-theta))sum l|c_l|], n>=1.
Use sum_(k>=1)k^(theta-2)<=1+1/(1-theta). Hence its block and projected energy
is <=C |M_U|^2/X. The analytic residue bound is
 |M_U|<=C U^[(p+m)(1-theta)],
so this error has exponent -1+2alpha(p+m)(1-theta). For p2,m3,theta2/3,
alpha1/24 it is -31/36 and is paid. The continuous baseline floor error itself
has an even smaller local cost; this conservative channel bound includes all
history and the unit. No ordinary coefficient moment was assumed for m3.

Thus, with explicit fixed channel constants, the full projected component
storage is bounded by
 C X^(2sigma-1)(I_low+I_high)+C U^[2(p+m)(1-theta)]/X.  (6.4)
This is coercive two-regime recovery, not a statement that the high part alone
controls the source. All opposite analytic-residue drifts, other source modes,
previous paid pieces, and exact transport error -e remain in the full join.

7. Source-specific constraints and the genuinely arithmetic next leaf

The low-height power in (6.2) cannot be universally improved from only
|a_d|<=1,a_1=1,c=A(1),|c|<=2 and T(1)=0. The independent review021 constructs
an all-scale paired-dilation tail with harmonic mass zero, c=1, for which on
a fixed small-height interval A(sigma+it) has size U^(1-sigma). The complete
F_U/s then has squared mass comparable to U^[2(p+m)(1-sigma)]. This concerns
generic signed weights, not actual mu or a prescribed distant physical block.
No prime-parity folklore is substituted for that scoped obstruction.

Actual mu has extra exact arithmetic constraints which such generic witnesses
lack. For EVERY n<=U, A*One=delta and E(n)=c. Therefore
 (A^p*E^m)(n)=c^m d_(m-p)(n), n<=U, m>=p,             (7.1)
where d_0=delta and d_l=One^l for l>=1. For m<p the right side is instead
c^m mu^(p-m)(n), a defined inverse-divisor coefficient, not d with an undeclared
negative index. In particular the p2,m3 head equals c^3 One. This is a
source-specific SUPPORT-GAP/HEAD identity, not a new prime distribution theorem.
It can be compiled directly from mu*One=delta and verified with no fitted
parameter. Subtracting this head to form another auxiliary requires restoring
its complete finite coefficients and its analytic residue contribution.
It does not by itself estimate the remaining low-height Gram.

A concrete executable grammar can therefore keep B_j signed, generate the
full Hermitian form (6.1) or its Gaussian version, impose (7.1) and exact
finite inverse constraints, search rational contours/length margins/height
cutoffs, and derive the precise missing exponent b from (6.4). It may generate
paired finite-polynomial differences or cofactor Gram identities from those
constraints, but they count as bounds only after an all-U signed inequality is
proved. Coefficientwise absolute values, diagonal-only replacement, suppressing
binomial j,k crosses, or importing (2.1) below its length threshold are not
such proofs. A finite actual-mu Gram calibration is evidence for a proposed
leaf, not its uniform proof. No calibration was performed in this review.
