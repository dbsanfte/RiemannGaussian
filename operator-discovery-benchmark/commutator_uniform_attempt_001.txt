Quotient commutator: compensation identities and a local-law obstruction

Scope and outcome

This proof attempt concerns frozen pilot002 mutation64. It is analytical:
no numerical/source experiments, score inspection, tests, hashes or edits to
frozen source, data, contracts or soundness files were performed. The only new
artifact is this memo. The earlier paired identity is restated so this record
is self-contained. The definition of partial_prime_2 was read from the existing
contract, without reading its numerical responses.

The attempt supplies two results rather than a global closure:

1. A generic prefix-shift compensation identity replaces the original weighted
   mixed term by a discrete square increment and a simpler masked source term.
   Its complete normalized effect is uniformly bounded. Consequently such a
   prefix-shift mutation preserves the cofinal drift problem up to an additive
   constant; it does not prove that problem.
2. A genuine all-scale obstruction applies to reasoning using source increments,
   squarefree masks, coprime multiplication, the even-index law, and any finite
   set of correct prime values. Sources satisfying all these laws can have
   positive linear prefix growth. No nonnegative companion potential can give
   them the required cofinal drift. The obstruction does not apply to a proof
   exploiting the full actual-Mobius prime/divisor sign law globally.

The actual candidate and target

Let M(n)=sum_{1<=j<=n} mu(j), M(0)=0, chi(n)=mu(n)^2, and

  E(X)=X^-2 sum_{t=X}^{2X-1} M(t)^2.

Mutation64 has one weight1 state

  U(t)=mu(t)1_{floor(t/2)>=1}M(floor(t/4))
       -mu(max(1,floor(t/2)))M(floor(t/2)),
  V(X)=X^-2 sum_{t=X}^{2X-1} U(t)^2, W(X)=E(X)+V(X).

At t=1 both prefixes vanish and no mu(0) is requested. Thus W(1)=1. At t>=2
put n=floor(t/2), A_n=M(floor(n/2)), B_n=M(n). The state is U=a(t)A_n-a(n)B_n
with a=mu. For dyadic X=2Y, fibers t=2n,2n+1 are complete and Y<=n<=2Y-1.
Actual mu obeys

  mu(2n)=-1_{n odd}mu(n), mu(n)mu(2n+1)=mu(n(2n+1)).

Hence the exact paired square is

  U(2n)^2+U(2n+1)^2
    =D_n+2[1_{n odd}chi(n)-mu(n(2n+1))]A_n B_n,
  D_n=[1_{n odd}chi(n)+chi(2n+1)]A_n^2+2chi(n)B_n^2.       (1)

V(2Y) is (4Y^2)^-1 times the complete sum of (1). The child at 2X uses the
fresh n-range X..2X-1 and normalization (2X)^-2. Its parent at X uses
X/2..X-1 and normalization X^-2. Source factors inside the mixed term are
retained; neither it nor the diagonal is an unmasked lag identity.

First companion attempt: polarization cancels the correlation at a cost

Write T=a(t)A_n and R=a(n)B_n. Add the companion U_+=T+R to U_-=T-R. For
nonnegative fixed weights w_-,w_+,

  w_-U_-^2+w_+U_+^2
    =(w_-+w_+)(T^2+R^2)+2(w_+-w_-)TR.                 (2)

Except on a separately proved degenerate domain, generic cancellation of TR
requires w_+=w_-. Equal weights remove the quadratic-polynomial correlation
from (1), but leave the positive diagonals 2w(T^2+R^2). After normalization,
these are still source-gated macroscopic lag energies. They do not supply a
negative coefficient of the complete target E or an independent bound on it.

This is the general quadratic-bank rule: if states are c_j dot T, adding
nonnegative squared states gives Gram matrix Q=sum_j w_j c_j c_j^T, which is
positive semidefinite. Off-diagonal cancellation can diagonalize the bank;
it cannot make a remaining diagonal negative. Adding the target square adds
another positive diagonal. This is a pointwise cancellation statement, not
a claim that all signed temporal drifts of positive potentials are positive.
Temporal compensation would require an additional valid transport estimate.

For actual mu and w=1 the two-state potential is at most
2H_4(X)+2H_2(X)=E(X/4)/2+E(X/2) for dyadic X>=4, where
H_d(X)=X^-2 sum M(floor(t/d))^2. Unknown energies remain on the right. An
equal-weight companion has therefore removed the desired signed mechanism
while preserving the original target cost, rather than proving its drift.

Second companion attempt: a useful exact prefix-shift compensation

This identity holds for EVERY ternary source a(n) in {-1,0,1}, not only mu.
Let S(n)=sum_{j<=n} a(j), S(0)=0, n>=1, let A_n be any real quantity, and set

  U(t)=a(t)A_n-a(n)S(n),
  U'(t)=a(t)A_n-a(n)S(n-1),  n=floor(t/r).

With chi_a(n)=a(n)^2 and F_n=S(n)^2, one has U'=U+chi_a(n), and exactly

  U(t)^2-U'(t)^2
    =F_n-F_{n-1}-2a(t)chi_a(n)A_n.                  (3)

Proof: expand U^2-(U+chi_a)^2, use a(n)^3=a(n), chi_a(n)^2=chi_a(n), and
2a(n)S(n)=F_n-F_{n-1}+chi_a(n). No cancellation hypothesis is used.

For a complete r-fiber t=rn,...,rn+r-1 put

  H_r(n)=sum_{j=0}^{r-1} a(rn+j)
        =S(rn+r-1)-S(rn-1).

Summing (3) first over fibers and then over n=a,...,b gives

  sum_t [U(t)^2-U'(t)^2]
    =r(F_b-F_{a-1})-2sum_{n=a}^b chi_a(n)A_n H_r(n).  (4)

This is a concrete parameterized compensation identity. It replaces the
source-weighted product a(n)S(n)A_n H_r(n) in the original paired expansion
by endpoint squares and a source-masked correlation without S(n).

For mutation64 take r=2,A_n=M(floor(n/2)), and in its shifted companion replace
only the second prefix M(n) by M(n-1). At n=0 define both states to be0 with
the same domain guard; in the AST the shifted prefix endpoint may be
max(0,n-1), so no negative endpoint or mu(0) is evaluated. Formula (4) becomes

  X^2(V(X)-V'(X))
    =2[M(X-1)^2-M(X/2-1)^2]
       -2sum_{n=X/2}^{X-1} chi(n)M(floor(n/2))
                                 [mu(2n)+mu(2n+1)]          (5)

for dyadic X>=2. At X=1 both potentials are0. The endpoints in (5) are not
silently discarded. The residual is a genuinely simpler masked correlation,
but the entire expression is already uniformly bounded using |a|<=1.

Indeed |U|<=floor(n/2)+n<=3n/2, |U'-U|<=1, and n<=t/2. Consequently

  |U(t)^2-U'(t)^2|<=3t/2+1,
  |V(X)-V'(X)|<=9/4+1/(4X)<=5/2                    (6)

for every positive integer X; t=1 contributes zero and satisfies the bound.
The displayed constant follows by summing t=X,...,2X-1 exactly. This is a
uniform bounded difference, not a bound on either V separately. In particular

  |DeltaW(X)-DeltaW'(X)|<=5.

Thus a cofinal-q estimate for either potential implies the estimate for the
other with Cq enlarged by5, since ceil(X^(1/q))>=1. W'(1)=W(1)=1. This is
useful equivalence information for structural discovery but supplies no
proof that either estimate holds. Adding U'^2 as a positive companion does
not subtract it: the telescoping credit in (3) is a DIFFERENCE of squares.

Generic proof/detector hook for bounded prefix shifts

For any source |a|<=1, a state satisfying |U(t)|<=C t and a mutation D(t)
with |D(t)|<=d obeys

  |[ (U+D)^2 ]_X-[U^2]_X|<=3Cd+d^2.                (7)

This follows by summing 2|U D|+D^2 with the original normalization. Therefore
fixed finite shifts of a prefix endpoint inside a linearly growing state
give bounded changes in potential whenever the prefactors are uniformly
bounded. This statement does not extend automatically to nonlinear states
whose growth is quadratic or to shifts that grow with X.

A detector can parameterize r>=2, a map g(n) with 0<=g(n)<=kappa n, and states
 a(t)S(g(floor(t/r))) +/- a(floor(t/r))S(floor(t/r)),
using an n>=1 guard/max at n=0. It can synthesize:
 - the plus companion and exact Gram/off-diagonal cancellation (2);
 - the one-step prefix-shift companion and exact certificates (3),(4);
 - fixed bounded endpoint shifts, certified by (7);
 - fiber-source expansions H_r(n), retaining clipped boundary fibers if r
   does not divide X. Formula (4) itself is used only on complete fibers.

These are parameterized grammar/proof hooks, not a hardcoded64 identity or
evidence that a larger source footprint improves the signed estimate.

A stronger obstruction than the constant-source comparison

The earlier constant-one diagnostic fails mu's even-index and squarefree
laws. A countermodel satisfying those laws is the already defined diagnostic
partial_prime_2, extended here to all positive integers as

  f_2(n)=mu(n)^2*(-1 if n is even else1).

This definition uses the squarefree predicate, not a new computed source
table. It is multiplicative on coprime inputs, is zero precisely off the
squarefree support, satisfies f_2(n)^2=chi(n), f_2(1)=1, f_2(2)=-1, and

  f_2(2n)=-1_{n odd}f_2(n).

Let Q_odd(N) count odd squarefree integers <=N. Then exactly

  S_2(N)=sum_{n<=N} f_2(n)=Q_odd(N)-Q_odd(floor(N/2)),   (8)

the count of odd squarefree integers in (N/2,N]. This is positive linear in
N by elementary counting, without using any Mertens estimate. There are at
least N/4-1 odd integers in that interval. Odd nonsquarefree integers are
divisible by p^2 for an odd prime p<=sqrt(N). For each p their count is at
most N/(4p^2)+1. Since

  sum_{p odd} p^-2 <=sum_{k=3}^infinity k^-2
                    <=1/9+integral_3^infinity u^-2 du=4/9,

the union bound gives S_2(N)>=5N/36-sqrt(N)-1. In particular S_2(N)>=cN for
some fixed c>0 and all sufficiently large N. This is a proved all-scale
local-law obstruction. The run's finite partial_prime_2 numerical controls
were diagnostics, not this proof; no such responses are used here.

It follows that the pairing laws in (1), source increments, squarefree masks
and coprime multiplication alone cannot prove cofinal drift. They hold for
f_2, whose complete target energy is at least c^2 X on all sufficiently large
dyadic blocks. No added nonnegative potential can hide this target growth.

Finite-P extension: even arbitrarily long finite agreement is insufficient

Let P be ANY finite set of primes containing2, D=product_{p in P} p. Define
the source f_P by f_P(p)=-1 for p in P, f_P(p)=+1 for primes outside P, by
coprime multiplicativity on squarefree integers, and f_P(n)=0 on nonsquarefree
integers. Thus f_P^2=chi and it obeys the same even-index law as actual mu.
It agrees with mu whenever all prime factors lie in P. In particular, if P
contains every prime <=B, it agrees on EVERY integer <=B, while no actual
source computation at B is needed for this symbolic statement.

Here is a self-contained mean calculation. Let

  Q_D(T)=#{m<=T: m squarefree, gcd(m,D)=1},
  h_D=product_{p|D}(1-1/p)>0,
  Z_D=product_{p not dividing D}(1-1/p^2)>0.

The infinite product converges to a positive number because sum_p p^-2 is
finite and every factor is positive. The squarefree indicator expansion
chi(m)=sum_{k^2|m} mu(k), followed by the finite coprimality count, gives

  Q_D(T)=sum_{k<=sqrt(T),gcd(k,D)=1} mu(k)
             #{l<=T/k^2:gcd(l,D)=1}
        =h_D T sum_{gcd(k,D)=1} mu(k)/k^2+O_D(sqrt(T))
        =h_D Z_D T+O_D(sqrt(T)).                         (9)

The error is elementary: each coprimality count is h_D times its endpoint
plus O_D(1), there are at most sqrt(T) terms, and the absolutely convergent
tail contributes at most T sum_{k>sqrt(T)} k^-2=O(sqrt(T)). The Euler product
in (9) follows from absolute convergence, not cancellation of partial sums
of mu. No RH, subpower energy or prime-distribution estimate is used.

Every squarefree integer is uniquely d*m with d|D,gcd(m,D)=1, and f_P(d*m)
=mu(d). Therefore

  S_P(N)=sum_{d|D} mu(d)Q_D(N/d)
         =c_P N+O_D(sqrt(N)),
  c_P=h_D^2 Z_D>0.                                      (10)

This explicit positive constant is generic in P; no value is numerically
evaluated. It yields, by symbolic summation on X<=t<2X,

  E_P(X)=(7/3)c_P^2 X+O_D(sqrt(X)).                       (11)

Crucially f_P is NOT actual mu on all primes. The full law mu(p)=-1 for EVERY
prime, or equivalently the full divisor inverse identity, is not among the
countermodel's hypotheses. Indeed

  sum_{d|n} f_P(d)=product_{p|n}(1+f_P(p))

is nonzero when n>1 has all prime factors outside P. The no-go excludes
closures derived only from the listed local laws and restricted-P prime
values; it does not refute a global actual-mu argument using the missing law.

No-go theorem for ANY nonnegative companions in this local-law class

Fix any finite P, any finite W_P(1), and ANY potential V_P(X)>=0 finite at
each dyadic scale, not necessarily mutation64 or a polynomial potential.
If the cofinal-q drift claim held for W_P=E_P+V_P, telescoping at even one
fixed q>1 would give W_P(X)=O_q(X^(1/q))+O(W_P(1)). This contradicts (11) and
W_P>=E_P. Thus neither a companion bank nor an induction credit valid over
this entire local-law class can close the actual target's cofinal drift.

More precisely,

  limsup_{X dyadic -> infinity} [W_P(2X)-W_P(X)]/X
      >=(7/3)c_P^2.                                    (12)

For if that limsup were smaller, choose delta<(7/3)c_P^2 above it. Summing
DeltaW_P(X)<=delta X for all sufficiently large dyadic X would yield
W_P(X)<=delta X+O(1), contradicting (11). Thus positive linear innovations
must occur infinitely often in every such nonnegative extension. No bound
on V_P from above is required. This is an all-scale conclusion, unlike a
stored-constant finite failure, and its scope remains the countermodel class.

An actual-mu global companion that cancels completely: what it loses

The missing global divisor law does provide a useful generic proof hook.
For a source a satisfying sum_{d|m}a(d)=1_{m=1}, with prefix S,

  sum_{j=1}^N S(floor(N/j))
    =sum_{m=1}^N sum_{d|m} a(d)=1,  N>=1.              (13)

For actual mu this is exact. It is represented by the existing nested-sum
grammar: sum j=1..t of sum i=1..floor(t/j) of x(i); no zero source request
arises. The complete quotient bank is the state1, with potential1/X and
signed drift -1/(2X). If one removes its j=1 term, the remaining bank is
1-M(t). Adding it to M(t) gives the constant state, or subtracting1 gives an
identically zero state. Those cancellations erase the target information
from V; the target E is still required in W=E+V.

Thus using the constant state as the sole weight1 potential gives W=E+1/X,
DeltaW=DeltaE-1/(2X), and W(1)=2. Its cofinal claim is equivalent to the
original target claim up to a constant shift, not a proof. Adding that state
to64 instead gives W64+1/X, a bounded-padding equivalent of64 with initial2.
Squaring the
remainder 1-M(t) retains the unknown macroscopic energy instead. Replacing
prefixes in64 by such complete remainder banks is an exact rewrite, but
introduces all complementary quotient terms and retains their mixed costs.
Using only a partial quotient bank would invalidate (13). The full inverse
identity and its truncation complements are appropriate detector certificates;
they cannot be promoted to a subpower estimate by dropping the original E.

Induction-credit audit and remaining mathematical boundary

The unconditional comparison

  E(X)<=W64(X)<=E(X)+E(X/2)+E(X/4)/2, dyadic X>=4,

cannot close an induction on E(2X): the unknown current energy E(2X) remains
on the child's right side. A legitimate credit must be bounded and absorbed
with explicit constants, or supplied by an independent signed theorem.
Equations (3)-(7) certify bounded changes of potential but no such new credit;
(2) removes the signed correlation at a positive-diagonal cost; (13) cancels
a complete bank while returning only bounded padding. The countermodels
prove that a local-law-only credit with the desired conclusion is impossible.

The actual-mu global problem therefore remains open in this attempt. A proof
could still use the full prime/divisor sign law to estimate or compensate
the weighted mu(n(2n+1)) term together with all diagonals and the fresh-child
target. None of the above establishes that estimate. One fixed program, W(1),
fresh child X->2X, and constants Cq independent of every dyadic X for every
cofinally large integer q remain required. The structural compensation,
bounded-prefix-shift equivalence and finite-P no-go are the concrete outputs.
