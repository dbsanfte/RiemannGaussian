Reversible circuit proof attempt: selected IDs11,12,28,29

Scope and result

These IDs are from reversible-circuit-results/pilot-001/proof_queue.json,
not from either older potential study. Only their program/certificate/obligation
payloads and the reversible circuit contract/review were read. No discovery,
training or final numerical responses, fitted constants, source tables, tests,
hashes or frozen-file edits were used. This new memo is the sole owned artifact.

The attempt does not close a cofinal transformed moment estimate. It proves
several concrete reductions: a quadratic-character Euler identity for11/12,
exact initial phase aliases for28, a complete phase-flip-bank identity for28/29,
and an opposite-prime-phase compression with critical-weight bounded inverse.
An interposed sign prevents that compression through an explicit divisor
defect. These give generic grammar/proof hooks. A shifted-zeta pole argument
shows why an Euler factor applied to M cannot simply cancel the original
Möbius singularities. A finite-prime countermodel makes the source-information
boundary precise. No identity below is promoted to an unproved estimate.

Programs, full target, and recovery

Let M(n)=sum_{j<=n} mu(j), M(0)=0. For a fixed unit prime source chi define

  a_alpha,chi(p^k)=chi(p)^k (alpha)_k/k!, a(1)=1,
  (T_alpha,chi f)(n)=sum_{d|n} a_alpha,chi(d)f(n/d).

Here (alpha)_k=alpha(alpha+1)...(alpha+k-1). All positive divisors occur; the
input is M itself, not mu followed by prefix summation. Set chi_5(p)=-1 for
p mod5 in{1,4} and +1 otherwise. The four selected outputs are

  rho11=T_-1/3,chi5 M,
  rho12=T_-1/6,chi5 M,
  rho28=T_-1/6,chi5 (S_6 M),
    S_6(n)=-1 iff mu(floor(n^(1/6)))<0, otherwise +1,
  rho29=T_1/6,eta (P M),
    P(n)=(-1)^floor(n^(2/3)),
    eta(p)=-1 iff mu(floor(sqrt(p)))<0, otherwise +1.

Every prime coefficient and phase is fixed using the original actual source;
it does not change with intermediate circuit values, X or q. Define the
complete moments D_i(X)=X^-2 sum_{X<=n<2X} rho_i(n)^2. All mixed terms belong
to these squares. At n=1, rho11=rho12=rho28=1 and rho29=-1, so all initial
moments equal1. The stated gate requires one fixed circuit and cofinally many
integers q with finite constants independent of every dyadic X>=32; the full
finite head remains in inverse recovery. A finite set of q does not suffice.

All four certificates have recovery_power1: for sigma=1/2+epsilon,
 ||M||_{sigma,T}<=(1+1/epsilon)||rho_i||_{sigma,T}.
The norm is associated with sum_{n<=T}|f(n)|^2/n^(2sigma+1). Squared recovery
uses (1+1/epsilon)^2. Source phases are unit isometries and the inverse uses
the same fixed prime source and phases, in reverse order. These facts prove
a conditional moment-to-original-target implication, not the moment premise.

1. Single kernels11/12: exact Euler reduction and prime-power effects

Let lambda be the real quadratic Dirichlet character modulo5: lambda=+1 on
1,4, -1 on2,3 and0 on multiples of5. Thus chi5(p)=-lambda(p) for p!=5,
whereas chi5(5)=+1. For Re(s)>1 the absolutely convergent kernel series is

  A_alpha(s)=sum_n a_alpha,chi5(n)n^-s
    =zeta(2s)^alpha L(s,lambda)^(-alpha)(1+5^-s)^alpha.   (1)

Proof: for p!=5 use
 (1+lambda(p)p^-s)=(1-p^-2s)/(1-lambda(p)p^-s),
and retain the exceptional p=5 factor (1-5^-s)^(-alpha). The ratio of the
missing square Euler factor and that local factor is (1+5^-s)^alpha. Powers
in (1) are defined by the convergent Euler logarithms; no choice of branch
across an unknown L-function zero is assumed.

For alpha=-beta, beta=1/3 or1/6, this is
 A_-beta=zeta(2s)^(-beta)L(s,lambda)^beta(1+5^-s)^(-beta).
The p=5 correction cannot be discarded. This character identity is about
the kernel. It does not turn the transform of M into a transform of mu.

For every prime and k>=1,

  a_-beta,chi(p^k)=-[beta/k] chi(p)^k
                    product_{j=1}^{k-1}(1-beta/j).       (2)

In particular a(p)=-beta chi(p), a(p^2)=-beta(1-beta)/2,
a(p^3)=-beta(1-beta)(2-beta)chi(p)/6. Squares have a negative coefficient
independent of the prime phase; odd powers retain it. The magnitude bound
|a(p^k)|<=beta/k follows directly from the displayed product. On primes,
residues1,4 give coefficient+beta, residues2,3 and the exceptional prime5
give -beta. This is not a universal negative damping of the prefix.

The exact output at a prime and at its square is

  rho_beta(p)=M(p)-beta chi(p),
  rho_beta(p^2)=M(p^2)-beta chi(p)M(p)-beta(1-beta)/2.   (3)

Thus prime-power terms already retain a fresh full M prefix and lower-prefix
correlations. Moreover T_-1/3,chi5=(T_-1/6,chi5)^2 by Euler convolution.
IDs11 and12 are distinct orders in the same semigroup, not distinct unknown
prime sources. No ordering of their signed moments follows from that identity.

2. Why an Euler reduction cannot directly cancel the Mertens obstruction

In Re(s)>2, absolute convergence gives

  F_M(s)=sum_n M(n)n^-s
        =sum_k mu(k)sum_{n>=k}n^-s,
  sum_n rho_beta(n)n^-s=A_-beta(s)F_M(s).             (4)

The tail of n^-s has the trapezoidal/Euler summation expansion
 sum_{n>=k}n^-s=k^(1-s)/(s-1)+(1/2)k^-s+R_s(k),
where R_s(k)=O_s(k^(-Re(s)-1)), locally uniformly for Re(s)>1. One can
obtain this error by bounding each unit-interval trapezoidal error using
the second derivative of t^-s and summing. Consequently

  F_M(s)=1/[(s-1)zeta(s-1)]+1/[2zeta(s)]+G(s),       (5)

initially Re(s)>2, with G holomorphic on Re(s)>1 by absolute convergence
of sum mu(k)R_s(k). Formula (5) gives a meromorphic continuation there.
If zeta has a zero z with Re(z)>1/2, the first term has a genuine pole at
s=1+z. The other two terms are holomorphic there, so cannot cancel its pole.

Every fixed unit-source kernel A_alpha is holomorphic and NONZERO on Re(s)>1
by its absolutely convergent Euler product. Thus the pole in (5) survives
both kernels11 and12. In particular the tempting zeta(2s)^(-beta) factor
does not cancel a pole at1+z: it and all other kernel factors are nonzero
there. Confusing (4) with A(s)/zeta(s) loses the prefix and shifts the analytic
problem by one whole unit.

If cofinal moments for rho held, weighted L2 and Cauchy-Schwarz would make
its ordinary Dirichlet series holomorphic on Re(s)>3/2: choose sigma>1/2
with Re(s)>sigma+1. The surviving pole at1+z would be impossible. This is
a concrete obstruction to a naive Euler-factor cancellation proof, not an
assumption that an off-line zero exists and not a proof of RH. Phases28/29
change F_M to the series of S(n)M(n); the explicit formula (5) is not asserted
for those modulated input series. Their inverse-recovery obstruction remains.

3. Modulation28: initial aliases and the complete phase-flip split

The source phase S_6 is exactly+1 for1<=n<2^6 because its root index is1.
It is -1 for2^6<=n<4^6: the root indices2 and3 both have negative Mobius
value. At4^6 the root index4 is nonsquarefree, so the source_sign becomes+1.
These are exact phase-domain facts, not a source evaluation or a response
comparison. On1<=n<64 every divisor input lies in the first band; hence

  rho28(n)=rho12(n), n<64.                            (6)

For64<=n<128, only the diagonal input can be in the negative band, giving
 rho28(n)=rho12(n)-2M(n). If B(n)=sum_{d|n,d>1}a_-1/6,chi5(d)M(n/d), then

  rho28(n)=-M(n)+B(n), rho12(n)=M(n)+B(n),
  rho28(n)^2-rho12(n)^2=-4M(n)B(n).                  (7)

This is a full signed cross-term reduction at the first phase regime. It
does not assert a nonzero numerical difference: M or B may vanish. Discarding
the proper-divisor channel would falsely conclude that the phase has no
moment effect. Later intervals require all admitted phase-flip divisors.

Here is the global identity, valid for any real unit phase S, any divisor
kernel a, and any real input f. Define

  B_same(n)=sum_{d|n,S(n/d)=S(n)}a(d)f(n/d),
  B_flip(n)=sum_{d|n,S(n/d)!=S(n)}a(d)f(n/d).

Then T f=B_same+B_flip, S(n)T(S f)(n)=B_same-B_flip, and exactly

  [T(S f)(n)]^2-[T f(n)]^2=-4B_same(n)B_flip(n).     (8)

The diagonal belongs to B_same, so original target information is retained.
This isolates the complete signed modulation mechanism, with no independently
dropped terms. The comparison of block moments is the normalized sum of (8)
on the entire fresh block; it is not a comparison of individual components.
For complex f, replace the product by its real part with conjugation.

For28, S_6 is not completely multiplicative: S_6(2)=+1 but S_6(2^6)=-1.
It cannot be absorbed into coefficients by the completely-multiplicative
phase conjugation law. A slowly changing phase only defines long bands in
which (8) may vanish on some inputs; it does not uniformly reduce B_flip.
Proper divisors n/d cross earlier bands even when n lies deep inside one.

4. Modulation29: two phase scales, not a standard character

Write gamma=1/6 and eta(p)=source_sign(mu(floor(sqrt(p)))). Prime phase
eta is constant on each prime interval r^2<=p<(r+1)^2, with its value set
by the original mu(r). It is not the universal prime-domain value mu(p)=-1.
A zero mu(r) gives+1 by the exact source_sign convention, not a zero mask.

The positive-order prime-power kernel is

  a_gamma,eta(p^k)=[gamma/k]eta(p)^k
                    product_{j=1}^{k-1}(1+gamma/j),    (9)

and its Euler product on Re(s)>1 can be written

  A_29(s)=zeta(s)^gamma
     product_{p:eta(p)=-1}[(1-p^-s)/(1+p^-s)]^gamma.   (10)

The complete prime set is essential. No residue character or classical
Dirichlet L-function reduction for that set is proved. In particular an
asymptotic distribution or independent-sign law for these source-defined
prime intervals cannot be assumed.

The output phase P(n)=(-1)^floor(n^(2/3)) has exact band endpoints
h_k=min{n>=1:n^2>=k^3}; its kth band is h_k<=n<h_{k+1}. It is -1 on n=1,2
and +1 at n=3; the kernel prime phases on all inputs<=2 are+1. Thus29 has
an initial norm alias there with the unmodulated positive-order constant
prime kernel. Beyond that interval the global identity (8) applies with
T=T_gamma,eta and S=P, preserving both phase scales and their mixed terms.

At a prime and its square the exact output is

  rho29(p)=P(p)M(p)-gamma eta(p),
  rho29(p^2)=P(p^2)M(p^2)+gamma eta(p)P(p)M(p)
                                      -gamma(gamma+1)/2.  (11)

The whole-source prefix remains on the diagonal. Nonmultiplicativity is
already exact at prime powers: P(2)=-1 while P(2^3)=+1. On every cube m^3,
P(m^3)=(-1)^m. Thus for odd m the perfect-cube divisor subchannel d=e^3
has no phase flip (both m and m/e are odd); its complements must still be
kept. This is a local algebraic channel identity, not an all-scale moment
estimate for a thin cube subsequence.

5. Opposite-prime-phase companion: a square-supported compression

For ANY unit prime source chi and ANY admissible alpha,

  T_alpha,chi T_alpha,-chi=K_alpha,
  (K_alpha f)(n)=sum_{e^2|n}a_alpha,1(e)f(n/e^2).       (12)

Proof: the prime-power generating product is
 (1-chi(p)x)^(-alpha)(1+chi(p)x)^(-alpha)=(1-x^2)^(-alpha).
All nonsquare coefficients vanish. Every remaining coefficient is independent
of chi. This is an exact compression/alias certificate: the pair ERASES its
prime-phase information, not a new source-specific prime control mechanism.
The inverse is K_-alpha, valid on every finite initial interval with diagonal1.

If r=ceil(|alpha|), divisor-supported square dilation gives

  ||K_alpha||,||K_alpha^-1||<=zeta(2sigma+1)^r
     =zeta(2+2epsilon)^r for sigma=1/2+epsilon.        (13)

This bound remains finite at the critical weight sigma=1/2; zeta(2)<=2 by
the elementary integral bound. Squared energy recovery uses the square of
(13). This is stronger uniform recovery than the generic two-convolution
bound, but it still preserves M's unknown energy class. It does not prove
that a compressed output has subpower moments.

In particular negative residue kernels can be factored as
 T_-beta,chi5=K_-beta T_beta,-chi5. The source -chi5 equals lambda on primes
other than5 and equals-1 at5. Thus the negative-order single observers are
critically bounded square-filter modifications of a positive fractional
quadratic-character kernel with its exceptional local5 factor. Keeping this
factorization does not justify dropping either channel or its inverse cost.

6. Interposing a sign: exact defect that keeps prime interactions

The collapse in (12) is prevented in general by an intervening sign. For a
fixed unit phase S define U=T_alpha,chi S T_alpha,-chi f. Then exactly

  U=S K_alpha f+D,
  D=[T_alpha,chi,S]T_alpha,-chi f,
  [T_alpha,chi,S]g(n)
      =sum_{d|n}a_alpha,chi(d)[S(n/d)-S(n)]g(n/d).     (14)

Equivalently the coefficient multiplying f(n/h) in D is

  sum_{a|h}a_alpha,chi(a)a_alpha,-chi(h/a)
                              [S(n/a)-S(n)].        (15)

For a prime divisor h=p this is alpha chi(p)[S(n/p)-S(n)]. Therefore
nonsquare prime interactions survive precisely on phase-flip domains, with
the prime phase still present. At n=p the defect is that coefficient times
f(1); for actual M the diagonal remains S(p)M(p). This is an exact structural
mechanism and not a lower bound on its numerical effect.

The complete observed square is
 |K_alpha f|^2+2Re(S K_alpha f conjugate(D))+|D|^2.
The new defect square AND its mixed term must be estimated. Keeping one full
interleaved circuit preserves a proved inverse: two convolutions give generic
norm recovery (1+1/epsilon)^(2r), squared recovery (1+1/epsilon)^(4r).
The improved critical square-filter bound alone does not cover the defect.

If S is completely multiplicative, (14) can instead fold into Euler kernels:
 U=S T_alpha,chi*S T_alpha,-chi f. At primes with S(p)=+1 the local product
is square-supported; at primes with S(p)=-1 it is (1+chi(p)x)^(-2alpha).
Thus even that case may retain a prime subset, but is an Euler compression
rather than a genuinely output-dependent phase interaction. Phases28/29 are
not completely multiplicative, as the explicit prime-power checks show.

These identities suggest a generic detector/generator hook: certify opposite
prime-source pairs as square compression when adjacent, and when separated
by a unit phase record (15), phase-flip domains and the full defect cross term.
They do not license hardcoding candidate64 from the older study, a new ranking,
or assuming that a larger source footprint has analytic advantage.

7. Source-specific information needed; a precise broad-class obstruction

The inverse theorem itself excludes a source-independent moment proof. For
the source one, M_one(n)=n, and every fixed admissible circuit binds its phases
to that same source. If its output had cofinal subpower moments, inverse
recovery would give H_sigma(n)<infinity for every sigma>1/2. But
 H_sigma(n)=sum_n n^(1-2sigma) diverges for1/2<sigma<=1. No circuit or added
nonnegative channel can evade that retained target. This is not a counterexample
to actual mu; it identifies the necessity of using its global sign information.

The obstruction can respect all squarefree, coprime and even-index laws and
any finite actual-source calibration. For finite primes P containing2 set
 f_P(p)=-1 on P,+1 outside P, extend multiplicatively on squarefree integers
and set it0 otherwise. Then f_P^2 is the actual squarefree indicator,
f_P(2n)=-1_{n odd}f_P(n), and f_P agrees with mu on every P-smooth integer.
Taking P to include all primes<=H gives exact agreement on every source query
needed by any of these circuits through H; all phase and output values there
then agree as well. This is a symbolic statement, not a new finite evaluation.

Nevertheless the prefix has positive mean. If D=product_P p, elementary
squarefree counting gives
 Q_D(T)=#{m<=T:m squarefree,gcd(m,D)=1}
       =h_D Z_D T+O_D(sqrt(T)),
 h_D=product_P(1-1/p), Z_D=product_{p notinP}(1-1/p^2)>0.
To see this, expand the squarefree indicator as sum_{k^2|m}mu(k); the
coprimality count is h_D T/k^2+O_D(1), and the square-divisor sum and its tail
have error O_D(sqrt(T)). Then

  sum_{n<=N}f_P(n)=sum_{d|D}mu(d)Q_D(N/d)
                  =h_D^2 Z_D N+O_D(sqrt(N)).         (16)

All constants are positive and are not numerically evaluated. Thus its target
energy grows linearly. For each fixed circuit with f_P-bound phases, inverse
recovery forbids cofinal output moments. The source_sign laws, prime-power
identities, finite aliases and coefficient bounds alone therefore cannot
close the actual-mu claim. The full law mu(p)=-1 for EVERY prime/divisor
inverse identity is absent from this countermodel; a proof exploiting that
global law is not ruled out. The existing partial_prime_13 control belongs
to this finite-P class. Its finite numerical behavior is not used as proof.

8. Final proof boundary

No unknown Mertens energy, RH, sign independence, prime interval distribution
or finite fitted constant was assumed as an estimate. The selected circuits'
prime-power formulas retain their fresh full-prefix channel. Their Euler
compression and phase-flip identities are concrete reductions, and the
interposed opposite-phase family has a precise defect suitable for structural
generation. None controls its full signed square on every fresh dyadic block.

One fixed actual-source circuit, its finite head/initial1, complete moments,
and the same-source inverse recovery remain required for the cofinal gate.
The next genuine analytic step would be a source-specific bound for the
phase-conditioned divisor products in (8) or (15), with both channels and
every complementary divisor retained. If proved, the existing inverse gives
the original energy and zeta implication. In this attempt only the stated
identities and broad-class obstructions are proved; all moment/global/RH
flags remain false.
