CANDIDATE 10: COMPLETE DEGREE-TWO SHIFTED QUOTIENT

Scope and outcome
-----------------
This is a separate analytical review of the frozen selected candidate
  P(s)=H_u(s)H_v(s), u=1/3, v=2/3,
  H_b(s)=(zeta(s+b)/zeta(s)-1)/b.
It does not change the frozen study or earlier memos. No new arithmetic
calculation, numerical experiment, test, parameter search, or rescore was
performed. The finite admission/results reported by the parent are not used
as premises for any theorem here. The original source remains actual mu.

Two complete components can be removed rigorously: the entire same-prime
prime-power overlap, and the smaller residue term x^(1/3). Both have finite
critical weighted L2 norm. A complete weighted-divisor hyperbola expansion
also isolates a uniformly bounded kernel remainder; restricting its outer
sum to d<=x^(1/3) makes that remainder strictly subcritical, with explicit
cost. None of these removals discards a signed complement.

The remaining candidate-specific obligation is a mixed-prime/composite
correlation estimate, displayed in sections 5 and 8. The positive
prime-power-only branch already has bulk size at least c*x/log x, larger
than the x^(2/3) main term. Consequently the composite branch must cancel
that bulk before any RH-scale claim is possible. This compulsory correlation
is not proved by attenuating prime locations, removing a diagonal, or passing
finite gates. No full cofinal moment theorem or RH is proved here.

1. Exact source, coefficients, and both residue constants
--------------------------------------------------------
Let delta be the Dirichlet-convolution unit and k_b(n)=n^(-b). Define
  a_b=mu*k_b, h_b=(a_b-delta)/b, p=h_u*h_v,
  S(x)=sum_(n<=x)p(n), beta=1/(uv)=9/2.
All endpoints are inclusive, x is real >=1, and prefixes vanish below one.
Every h_b(1)=0. Thus p(1)=0, p(prime)=0, and each factor index in S(x)
is at most x/2. These prime-location zeros and factor lag are automatic for
ANY product of two zero-constant Dirichlet series. The matched comparator
(mu-delta)^{*2} has the same properties; they are not new mu correlations.

Set kappa_u=2/3, kappa_v=1/3, Z_u=zeta(2/3), Z_v=zeta(1/3), W=zeta(4/3).
The exact residues and main coefficients are
  R_u=beta*[W/Z_u^2-1/Z_u],
  R_v=beta*[Z_u/Z_v^2-1/Z_v],
  m_u=R_u/kappa_u, m_v=R_v/kappa_v.
The actual centered output is
  J(x)=S(x)-m_u*x^(2/3)-m_v*x^(1/3).                 (1.1)
No additional constant is subtracted because p(1)=0. Z_u and Z_v are
negative and nonzero; W is positive. These are exact special-function
constants, not fitted rational or numerical coefficients.
For Re(s)>1,
  s*integral_1^infinity J(x)x^(-s-1)dx
    =P(s)-m_u*s/(s-2/3)-m_v*s/(s-1/3).              (1.2)
Keep the entire compensating fractions, including their constant parts.

For every prime p and e>=1,
  h_u(p^e)=-3*(1-p^(-1/3))*p^(-(e-1)/3),
  h_v(p^e)=-(3/2)*(1-p^(-2/3))*p^(-2(e-1)/3).
For distinct primes p,q,
  p(pq)=beta*[(1-p^(-1/3))(1-q^(-2/3))
              +(1-q^(-1/3))(1-p^(-2/3))]>0.        (1.3)
As both primes increase this tends to 9, whereas the corresponding
(mu-delta)^{*2} coefficient is 2. Thus the candidate is NOT a uniform
coefficient attenuation of the matched comparator. For fixed b its h_b
coefficients include all composites, with signs (-1)^omega(n), not just
prime powers. Their signed interactions are indispensable.

There is an exact new-prime transfer law. Write b(n)=(a_u*a_v)(n) and
r_u(p)=1-p^(-u), r_v(p)=1-p^(-v). If m>1 and p does not divide m,
  p(mp)=-(r_u(p)+r_v(p))*p(m)
                         -beta*[r_v(p)*a_u(m)+r_u(p)*a_v(m)].
Indeed a_u,a_v,b are individually multiplicative, with local prime
values -r_u,-r_v,-(r_u+r_v); their three-dimensional transfer is diagonal,
while p=beta*(b-a_u-a_v) on integers greater than one. This retains the
linear source complements instead of pretending p is multiplicative.
For a squarefree n with j=omega(n)>=1 it gives the especially clean law
  p(n)=beta*(-1)^j*[product_(p|n)(r_u(p)+r_v(p))
                    -product_(p|n)r_u(p)-product_(p|n)r_v(p)].
The bracket is zero at j=1 and positive at j>=2: it is the sum of all
mixed two-color assignments of the distinct prime factors. Thus squarefree
semiprimes contribute positively, triples negatively, and so forth. This
is an exact actual-mu sign relation, not a uniform cancellation estimate.
It must not be extended to nonsquarefree coefficients, which have their
different repeated-prime local factors. When these j primes all increase,
the bracket tends to 2^j-2, and the matched comparator has coefficient
(-1)^j*(2^j-2). Again the large-prime coefficients are scaled up by beta,
not uniformly attenuated. Summing this weighted alternating prime-factor
law to square-root precision is part of the unresolved source estimate.

2. Exact same-prime overlap and a finite critical-norm peel
----------------------------------------------------------
Restrict h_u and h_v to prime powers, and within their product retain terms
whose two factors involve the SAME prime. Denote that coefficient sequence
d and its inclusive prefix D(x). For q=p^(-1/3) and e>=2,
  d(p^e)=beta*(1-q^2)*q^(e-2)*(1-q^(e-1))>=0,       (2.1)
zero otherwise. This follows by summing all exponent splits 1<=l<=e-1:
  beta*(1-q)(1-q^2)*sum_l q^(l-1+2(e-l-1)).
The COMPLETE mass at any one prime is exactly
  sum_(e>=2)d(p^e)
    =beta*(1-q^2)*[1/(1-q)-q/(1-q^2)]=beta.        (2.2)
Therefore for every real x>=1,
  0<=D(x)<=beta*pi(sqrt x).                         (2.3)
There is no exponent cutoff error in this estimate. This improves the
logarithmic tuple-count estimate by using the full fixed-shift geometric
mass, including every repeated-prime power.

The previously proved elementary Chebyshev upper bound supplies a finite
C_pi with pi(y)<=C_pi*y/log y for y>=2. For example, if the supplied
source lemma is psi(y)<=C_psi*y, split primes at sqrt y to obtain
  pi(y)<=sqrt y+2*C_psi*y/log y
        <=(2*C_psi+2)*y/log y, y>=2.
Consequently, D(x)=0 for x<4 and for x>=4,
  D(x)<=2*beta*C_pi*sqrt x/log x.
In the critical weighted norm ||f||_crit^2=integral_1^infinity |f(t)|^2/t^2 dt,
  ||D||_crit^2<=4*beta^2*C_pi^2/log 4<infinity.      (2.4)
Its complete integer block energy satisfies, for dyadic X>=4,
  X^(-2)sum_(X<=n<2X)D(n)^2
     <=6*beta^2*C_pi^2/(log X)^2.                 (2.5)
Use sum_(X<=n<2X)n<3X^2/2 and log n>=log X. The finite head is explicit.
This is an actual-prime component theorem using only a proved elementary
prime upper bound, not RH or PNT. It does not bound the mixed-prime branch.

3. Literal prime-power and composite decomposition
---------------------------------------------------
Write h_b=l_b+c_b^*, where l_b is its literal prime-power restriction
(the coefficients in section 1), and c_b^* is supported on integers with
at least two DISTINCT prime factors. The star distinguishes this sequence
from the single-quotient smooth coefficient c_b used in earlier memos.
Then, EXACTLY,
  p=l_u*l_v + l_u*(c_v^*) + (c_u^*)*l_v + (c_u^*)*(c_v^*).
Let T(x) be the mixed-prime portion of the l_u*l_v prefix, and C(x) the
prefix of the other three complete branches. All prime-power exponents,
all factor orders, all composite overlaps, and all signs are included.
Then
  S(x)=D(x)+T(x)+C(x),
  J(x)=R(x)+Q(x),
  R(x)=T(x)+C(x)-m_u*x^(2/3),
  Q(x)=D(x)-m_v*x^(1/3).                           (3.1)
The smaller pole term alone has
  ||m_v*x^(1/3)||_crit^2=3*m_v^2.
Thus
  ||Q||_crit<=2*beta*C_pi/sqrt(log 4)+sqrt(3)*|m_v|,                  (3.2)
and its whole integer energy is O(1/log^2 X)+O(X^(-1/3)), with the
explicit component constants above. In particular Q can be peeled without
an RH-scale assumption. The exact square always remains
  E_J=E_R+E_Q+2*X^(-2)sum_(X<=n<2X)R(n)Q(n).        (3.3)
No sign is asserted for this cross term. Triangle inequalities in BOTH
directions show that cofinal subpower complete-moment bounds for J and R
are equivalent, with enlarged constants and the full finite head included.
The peel changes the proof obligation, not the frozen program or its scores.

4. A mandatory large mixed correlation, not just a diagonal issue
----------------------------------------------------------------
All l_u*l_v coefficients are nonnegative. Fix the first factor's prime
at 2 and let the second factor be a varying prime p>=3. This contributes
to T, with weight
  beta*(1-2^(-1/3))*(1-p^(-2/3))
     >=gamma_0:=beta*(1-2^(-1/3))*(1-2^(-2/3))>0.
Hence
  T(x)>=gamma_0*[pi(x/2)-1] for x>=4.              (4.1)
This is only ONE orientation and one prime-power choice. Adding the other
positive terms cannot weaken it.

For completeness an elementary prime-count LOWER bound is available
without PNT. For N=floor(x/2), the central binomial coefficient satisfies
  psi(x)>=log binom(2N,N)>=2N log 2-log(2N+1).
The first inequality follows since every prime valuation of the binomial
is at most floor(log(2N)/log p). Also the supplied psi upper bound yields
  0<=psi(x)-theta(x)
     <=C_psi*[sqrt x+(log x/log 2)*x^(1/3)].
Thus for all sufficiently large x,
  theta(x)>=(log 2/2)*x,
  pi(x)>=theta(x)/log x>=c_0*x/log x,
with a fixed positive c_0 and finite threshold. This is a symbolic all-scale
argument after that threshold, not a new source-table experiment.
It follows that T(x)>=c*x/log x eventually. Both main terms in (1.1) have
smaller order than x/log x. The same-prime D has still smaller order.

Therefore a small FULL centered output requires C to cancel a macroscopic
positive mixed-prime branch. For example if a cofinal moment holds with
some exponent 1/q<1, then in the normalized block L2 norm
  ||T||_X>=c*sqrt X/log X,
  ||J||_X<=sqrt(C_q)*X^(1/(2q)),
while ||D-m_u*x^(2/3)-m_v*x^(1/3)||_X=O(X^(1/6)+1).
Hence
  ||C+T||_X/||T||_X ->0,
  ||C||_X/||T||_X ->1,
  <C,T>_X/(||C||_X*||T||_X) -> -1.                (4.2)
The statement is conditional on that full moment. It quantifies the exact
bulk anticorrelation which the candidate would have to exploit; it is NOT
a proof that the source attains it. The required RH-scale remainder is
stronger still than this bulk cancellation. Mean-mismatched controls can
fail badly while proving nothing about (4.2) for actual mu.

5. First precise next inequality
--------------------------------
After the rigorous peel, the remaining arithmetic theorem can be stated
without the one-prime diagonal or the subcritical pole term:
  For an unbounded/cofinal set of q>0, find finite K_q such that
  X^(-2)sum_(n=X)^(2X-1)
       |T(n)+C(n)-m_u*n^(2/3)|^2 <= K_q*X^(1/q)     (5.1)
for EVERY dyadic integer X>=1. This is a complete signed-square inequality,
including all three composite branches C and the exact larger main term.
It is equivalent to the original cofinal J moment by section 3. It remains
unproved. Dropping C leaves a positive branch with a supercritical moment;
dropping its cross term with T destroys precisely the necessary information.

6. Exact shift cocycle and its complete defect
---------------------------------------------
Let a_1=mu*n^(-1), D_1(s)=zeta(s+1)/zeta(s). It is an endpoint arithmetic
sequence, not the singular c_b formula at b=1. The exact cocycle is
  D_u(s)D_v(s+u)=D_1(s).
Accordingly
  D_u(s)D_v(s)=D_1(s)+D_u(s)[D_v(s)-D_v(s+u)],
  P(s)=beta*[D_1(s)-D_u(s)-D_v(s)+1
                    +D_u(s)(D_v(s)-D_v(s+u))].    (6.1)
On coefficients define w(n)=(1-n^(-u))*a_v(n), w(1)=0. Then
  S(x)=beta*[A_1(x)-A_u(x)-A_v(x)+1
                  +sum_(d*m<=x)a_u(d)w(m)].       (6.2)
There is also the reversed orientation, using
  w'(n)=(1-n^(-v))*a_u(n),
  a_u*a_v=a_1+a_v*w'.
These are exact divisor/factor-placement identities, with their full signed
defects. Neither unshifted product telescopes. The difference factors carry
actual source coefficients, not a nonnegative norm contraction.

At a prime, the cancellation in (6.1) is an algebraic identity between
1-q,1-q^2,1-q^3 and (1-q)(1-q^2). It adds no independent prime-sign fact.
Also A_1 is a bounded critical-norm preconditioning of M (absolute kernel
cost zeta(3/2)), not a proven subcritical function. Moving the defect into
an A_1 expression cannot close the theorem by declaring A_1 small.
D_1 has a pole at s=0 and a logarithmic Perron term; there is no finite
c_1=1/((1-1)zeta(0)) to use. Its low-exponent terms do not alter the critical
threshold, but they must be retained if that channel is explicitly centered.

7. Weighted divisor kernel: a complete uniformly bounded remainder
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Put m_2=mu*mu and
  k(n)=(k_u*k_v)(n)=n^(-2/3)*sigma_(1/3)(n),
  K(y)=sum_(n<=y)k(n).
Then a_u*a_v=m_2*k, so this single kernel exposes the entire degree-two
smoothing, without forgetting source or rectangle terms.
For alpha>0, alpha!=1, define
  S_alpha(y)=sum_(n<=y)n^(-alpha),
  e_alpha(y)=S_alpha(y)-y^(1-alpha)/(1-alpha)-zeta(alpha).
Euler summation gives EXACTLY
  e_alpha(y)=-{y}y^(-alpha)
                +alpha*integral_y^infinity {t}t^(-alpha-1)dt,
so |e_alpha(y)|<=y^(-alpha), including noninteger y>=1.
For alpha=4/3 this says
  S_(4/3)(y)=zeta(4/3)-3*y^(-1/3)+e_(4/3)(y).

At Y=sqrt y the complete hyperbola identity is
  K(y)=sum_(m<=Y)m^(-u)S_v(y/m)
       +sum_(n<=Y)n^(-v)S_u(y/n)-S_u(Y)S_v(Y).
Expand BOTH short-side S functions, retaining the rectangle. One obtains
  K(y)=C_u*y^(2/3)+C_v*y^(1/3)+C_0+Delta_K(y),      (7.1)
  C_u=(3/2)*zeta(4/3), C_v=3*zeta(2/3),
  C_0=zeta(1/3)*zeta(2/3).
A useful exact error formula is
  Delta_K(y)=(3/2)Y^(2/3)e_v(Y)
             +(3/2)Y^(4/3)e_(4/3)(Y)
             -3Y^(1/3)e_u(Y)-e_u(Y)e_v(Y)+T_1+T_2,
  T_1=sum_(m<=Y)m^(-u)e_v(y/m),
  T_2=sum_(n<=Y)n^(-v)e_u(y/n).
Here |T_1|<=1 and |T_2|<=3/2; the other bounds are respectively
3/2,3/2,3,1. Therefore
  |Delta_K(y)|<=19/2 for EVERY real y>=1.           (7.2)
This uses u+v=1 essentially. The bound includes all floor/end effects.
It is much sharper than an unstructured absolute divisor-error bound.
It is nevertheless a kernel estimate, not the full mu*mu convolution:
convolving it over every d<=x absolutely would cost O(x log x).

8. Complete outer hyperbola identity and a second subcritical peel
------------------------------------------------------------------
Choose U=x^(1/3), V=x^(2/3), so UV=x. Define the inclusive prefixes
  M(t)=sum_(n<=t)mu(n), M_2(t)=sum_(n<=t)m_2(n),
  T_K(x)=sum_(m<=V)k(m)M_2(x/m),
  T_b(x)=sum_(m<=V)m^(-b)M(x/m), b=u,v,
and the exact finite mismatches
  B_r(U)=sum_(d<=U)m_2(d)d^(-r)-1/zeta(r)^2,
  L_r(U)=sum_(d<=U)mu(d)d^(-r)-1/zeta(r), r=2/3,1/3.
These are differences against analytic values, NOT assumed convergent tails.
In particular the r=1/3 terms cannot be treated as ordinary convergent
series even by merely invoking a conjectural square-root M bound.

For B(x)=sum_(n<=x)(a_u*a_v)(n), exact hyperbola gives
  B(x)=sum_(d<=U)m_2(d)K(x/d)+T_K(x)-M_2(U)K(V).
Expanding ONLY the short-side K with (7.1) yields
  B(x)-C_u*x^(2/3)/Z_u^2-C_v*x^(1/3)/Z_v^2
    =C_u*x^(2/3)B_(2/3)(U)+C_v*x^(1/3)B_(1/3)(U)
       +C_0*M_2(U)+T_K(x)-M_2(U)K(V)+epsilon_K(x),
  epsilon_K(x)=sum_(d<=U)m_2(d)Delta_K(x/d),
  |epsilon_K(x)|<= (19/2)*x^(1/3)*(1+log x/3).      (8.1)
The last inequality uses |m_2(d)|<=d_2(d) and
sum_(d<=U)d_2(d)<=U(1+log U). The intersection term is not optional.
The entire short-side remainder has the explicit critical norm bound
  ||epsilon_K||_crit^2<=15*(19/2)^2,               (8.2)
because integral_1^infinity x^(-4/3)(1+log x/3)^2 dx=15.

For EACH original single A_b, the same U,V hyperbola identity gives
  A_b(x)-x^(1-b)/[(1-b)zeta(1-b)]
    =x^(1-b)L_(1-b)(U)/(1-b)+zeta(b)M(U)
       +T_b(x)-M(U)S_b(V)+epsilon_b(x),
  epsilon_b(x)=sum_(d<=U)mu(d)e_b(x/d).
The complete short errors obey
  |epsilon_u(x)|<=x^(1/9),
  |epsilon_v(x)|<=x^(-1/9),
  ||epsilon_u||_crit<=3/sqrt 7,
  ||epsilon_v||_crit<=3/sqrt 11.                   (8.3)
These follow from x^(-b)sum_(d<=U)d^b<=x^(-b)U^(1+b).
Again every quotient prefix and rectangle cost is retained.

Since S=beta*(B-A_u-A_v+1), define the following COMPLETE core:
  F(x)= C_u*x^(2/3)B_(2/3)(U)+C_v*x^(1/3)B_(1/3)(U)
       -(3/2)*x^(2/3)L_(2/3)(U)-3*x^(1/3)L_(1/3)(U)
       +C_0*M_2(U)-[zeta(u)+zeta(v)]M(U)
       +T_K(x)-T_u(x)-T_v(x)
       -M_2(U)K(V)+M(U)[S_u(V)+S_v(V)]+1.
Then the exact centered relation is
  J(x)=beta*F(x)+beta*[epsilon_K(x)-epsilon_u(x)-epsilon_v(x)].       (8.4)
The complete removed error has critical norm at most
  beta*[(19/2)*sqrt 15+3/sqrt 7+3/sqrt 11].          (8.5)
It also has decaying/subpower integer block energy by its explicit
pointwise bounds. Formula (8.4) is a second equivalent cofinal-moment
reduction. It is not a claim that any of the remaining B_r,L_r,M_2,T terms
are small in isolation. Each may require compensation by the others.

If desired combine BOTH rigorous peels into one next target:
  F_red(x)=beta*F(x)-D(x)+m_v*x^(1/3),
  J(x)=F_red(x)+D(x)-m_v*x^(1/3)
                      +beta*(epsilon_K-epsilon_u-epsilon_v)(x).    (8.6)
The last three complete components have known finite critical norm and
explicit cofinal-admissible integer energies. Their norm cost is bounded
by the sum of (3.2) and (8.5). Thus proving, for cofinal q,
  X^(-2)sum_(X<=n<2X)|F_red(n)|^2<=K_q*X^(1/q)      (8.7)
would prove the original full moment. Every unknown source term in F_red
is now explicit. No shortened sum has silently reset an error budget.

9. Why the decompositions do not close the signed theorem
----------------------------------------------------------
The sequence m_2 is the coefficient of 1/zeta(s)^2. Replacing M_2 or its
weighted mismatches by a conjectural square-root estimate reintroduces an
RH-strength source premise. The A_1 cocycle channel similarly retains M.
The bounded Delta_K estimate can only be peeled on the short side; the
long-side M_2 quotient sum and its rectangle remain. Their complete signed
interaction with the two M channels is precisely what absolute values miss.

After peeling the one-prime diagonal, the prime-power-only mixed part
still has the lower bound (4.1). The signs in the composite branches are
not uniformly negative, and overlap of prime factors changes their signs.
No monotonicity or positive-kernel estimate established here controls
T+C-m_u*x^(2/3) at square-root scale. The smaller residue term is harmless
in the cofinal theorem but is part of the actual frozen output; removing
it from the program or from reported scores would be a different study.

The exact infinite signed square of either R or F_red, not a diagonal
energy, is the next inequality. A finite reduction in energy relative to
(mu-delta)^{*2} is compatible with different constants, lower-order tails,
and the source/control mean mismatch. It proves none of (5.1), (8.7), or
the bulk correlation limit (4.2). The reported finite energy is evidence
for examining this correlation, not a uniform norm proof.

10. Recovery retained, including all multiplicities and costs
--------------------------------------------------------------
Suppose the complete cofinal J moment, equivalently either reduced target,
has been proved for every dyadic X. The within-cell error is only the smooth
normalizer difference, at most |R_u|n^(-1/3)+|R_v|n^(-2/3). The complete
weighted L2/Mellin argument in SHIFTED_QUOTIENT_ANALYTIC_REVIEW_001.txt then
makes (1.2) holomorphic in Re(s)>1/2.

At an ordinary nonreal zero rho of multiplicity m, if neither rho+1/3
nor rho+2/3 is a zero, both H factors have order-m poles, with nonzero
leading coefficients. Their product has order-2m, uncanceled by real main
terms. Thus holomorphy forces at least one farther-right ordinary zero.
More precisely if nu_i is the order at rho of zeta(s+b_i)-zeta(s), the
product's holomorphy requires nu_u+nu_v>=2m. At least one nu_i>=m then
forces that right-shifted zero to have multiplicity >=m. This handles
cross-factor cancellations, higher multiplicities, and coincident shifted
zeros. Repeating steps of at least 1/3 reaches Re>1 in finitely many steps,
contradicting the Euler product. Hence the cofinal theorem would imply RH.
No such theorem has been obtained in this memo.

Quantitative inversion is not an unqualified square root of P. For
z=1/zeta(s), r_u=zeta(s+u), r_v=zeta(s+v), the exact equation is
  uv*P(s)=(r_u*z-1)(r_v*z-1).
For |Im(s)|>=1 the real main poles are absent, and one valid bound is
  |1/zeta(s)|<=2*max(|1/zeta(s+u)|,|1/zeta(s+v)|)
                                     *[1+sqrt(uv*|P(s)|)].
The full P bound includes its two residue fractions and the Mellin norm
of the complete J. Recurse both possible right shifts until Re>=R>1,
where the terminal bound is zeta(R). A direct tree has depth at most
ceil(3*(R-Re(s))) and at most 1+2+...+2^depth nodes. Removal of the real
singularities uses F(s)=(s-1)zeta(s), with the exact cleared product as in
the earlier memo; it cannot be implemented by dividing by sampled small
numerators. These conditional constants and complete paths are retained.
This is a valid finite analytic bridge, not a bounded finite-prefix inverse
or an automatically recovered all-height M moment.
