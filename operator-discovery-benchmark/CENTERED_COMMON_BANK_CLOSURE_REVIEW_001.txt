CENTERED COMMON BANK: EXACT COMPENSATION, SMALLER SCALE, AND THE OPEN JOINT CREDIT

Scope and outcome
-----------------
This new analytical memo extends the conditional smaller-scale rule (9.5)
in PRIME_TRANSFER_MARTINGALE_REVIEW_001.txt. Inputs are that frozen memo,
PRIME_BANK_HANDOFF.txt, the exact ordered-prime obligation, the covariance
review and the prime-slide protocol. No numerical responses, experiments,
tests, source tables, hashes, frozen edits or subagents were used.

Two concrete conclusions are obtained.
  1. The entire reduced target has an exact centered three-state recurrence,
     with explicit smooth, floor, unit-strip, repeated-prime and pole terms.
     Its shared-prime centered square is controlled at a strictly smaller
     horizon by a coupled cumulative moment, without a separate companion
     assumption. This is a conditional proof rule, not a closed estimate.
  2. The isolated deterministic compensation P_theta cannot itself be paid
     by any cofinal critical-energy bound. A self-contained Mellin/Euler
     argument exhibits an unavoidable logarithmic branch at s=1, even if
     its harmonic constant vanishes to a finite order. The weighted and
     main components are analytic there. The actual smooth channel can
     cancel the branch; this memo does NOT exclude that joint cancellation.

The missing estimate is still a joint actual-source covariance. The new
obstruction directs it toward the compensated smooth/common kernel rather
than toward a separately small pole bank. No uniform target theorem or RH
claim is made. All global/RH flags remain false.

1. Fixed states, centers, and exact unit conventions
--------------------------------------------------
Keep u=1/3, v=2/3, beta=9/2, kappa_u=2/3, kappa_v=1/3. Let
  a_b=mu*(n^(-b)), B=a_u*a_v,
  p=beta*(B-a_u-a_v+delta), a=p-d,
where d is the COMPLETE same-prime prime-power part of p. In particular
p(1)=p(q)=0, a(1)=a(q^e)=0, a_b(1)=B(1)=1. No p multiplicativity is
assumed. The already proved d and smaller-pole peels remain full components.

Use inclusive prefixes at real t, zero below one:
  S(t)=sum_(k<=t)p(k), A_b(t)=sum_(k<=t)a_b(k), D(t)=sum_(k<=t)d(k),
  J(t)=S(t)-m_u*t^kappa_u-m_v*t^kappa_v,
  M_u(t)=A_u(t)-c_u*t^kappa_u,
  M_v(t)=A_v(t)-c_v*t^kappa_v,                    (1.1)
where for t>=1
  c_u=1/[kappa_u*zeta(kappa_u)], c_v=1/[kappa_v*zeta(kappa_v)].
These centers are evaluated at their displayed arguments, not rounded
arguments by default. The exact old residues are
  m_u=beta/kappa_u*[zeta(4/3)/zeta(2/3)^2-1/zeta(2/3)],
  m_v=beta/kappa_v*[zeta(2/3)/zeta(1/3)^2-1/zeta(1/3)].
They are not fitted constants.

For k>=2, z_u=p+beta*a_v, z_v=p+beta*a_u, z_0=z_u+z_v; all three z
sequences are zero at k=1. Their prefixes Z_b(t) therefore satisfy, t>=1,
  Z_u=S+beta*A_v-beta, Z_v=S+beta*A_u-beta,
  Z_0=2S+beta*(A_u+A_v)-2beta.                   (1.2)
Their EXACT pole/unit profiles are
  C_u(t)=m_u*t^kappa_u+(m_v+beta*c_v)*t^kappa_v-beta,
  C_v(t)=(m_u+beta*c_u)*t^kappa_u+m_v*t^kappa_v-beta,
  C_0(t)=C_u(t)+C_v(t),
  W_u=Z_u-C_u=J+beta*M_v,
  W_v=Z_v-C_v=J+beta*M_u,
  W_0=Z_0-C_0=2J+beta*(M_u+M_v).                (1.3)
In particular C_b(1)+W_b(1)=0, not C_b(1)=W_b(1)=0 separately.

2. Exact centered decomposition of K-L0+Lu-main
---------------------------------------------
Fix 3/4<=theta<1, integer n>=1, Y=n^theta, and
  Q_theta(n)={q prime:Y<q<=n/2}, m_q=floor(n/q).
There is no q with a cofactor one in this set. Define
  K(n)=sum_(k<=n,Pplus(k)<=Y)a(k),
  L_b(n)=sum_(q in Q_theta(n))q^(-b)*Z_b(m_q), b=0,u,v.
The complete reduced target is
  R_new(n)=K(n)-L_0(n)+L_u(n)-m_u*n^kappa_u.      (2.1)
The original reduced coefficient target satisfies R=R_new+L_v; its relation
to J is J=R+D-m_v*n^kappa_v. Those complete peels have proved costs.

Put
  P_theta(n)=m_u*n^kappa_u
             +sum_(q in Q_theta(n))[C_0(m_q)-q^(-u)*C_u(m_q)],
  H_R(n)=K(n)-P_theta(n),
  F(t)=(J(t),M_u(t),M_v(t))^T,
  b_q=(q^(-u)-2, -beta, beta*(q^(-u)-1)).
Then the exact decomposition is
  R_new(n)=H_R(n)+sum_(q in Q_theta(n))b_q*F(m_q).                 (2.2)
Equivalently its centered bank is -sum_q[W_0(m_q)-q^(-u)*W_u(m_q)].
Every floor, unit constant, smaller pole and smooth coefficient is present.
No smallness is asserted for H_R or for P_theta individually.

Replacing m_q^kappa by (n/q)^kappa is not a harmless unweighted-bank edit.
Its exact correction is
  E_(b,kappa)(n)=sum_(q in Q_theta(n))q^(-b)
                      *[floor(n/q)^kappa-(n/q)^kappa].          (2.3)
For 0<kappa<1 and n/q>=2 the absolute bracket is at most
kappa*floor(n/q)^(kappa-1), hence at most kappa. But the elementary
whole-bank bound is then kappa*sum_q q^(-b), of power n^(1-b), not
automatically critical padding for b=0 or u. Formula (2.3), with its
actual signs, must stay in the deterministic compensation if real powers
are preferred. All results below instead keep integer m_q and avoid this edit.

Even a constant change C_b -> C_b+c shifts the bank by
c*sum_(q in Q_theta(n))q^(-b). Its cofactor energy is bounded, while its
unweighted bank is not. A small cofactor-center change is not necessarily
a small prime-bank change. In particular C_b(1) may not be deleted from
(1.3) merely because the cofactor domain in (2.2) starts at two.

3. An exact coupled recurrence with a common smaller cofactor domain
-------------------------------------------------------------------
For j=u,v let K_j(n)=sum_(k<=n,Pplus(k)<=Y)a_j(k), including its unit.
For p put S_sm(n)=sum_(k<=n,Pplus(k)<=Y)p(k)=K(n)+D(n). The equality
holds because every nonzero d coefficient is at q^e with e>=2 and
q<=sqrt(n)<=Y. No repeated-prime coefficient is put into a large-prime bank.

The one-cofactor prime strip is
  U_theta(n)={q prime:max(Y,n/2)<q<=n}.
For q>Y the large prime occurs exactly once, and is coprime to its entire
cofactor. Local au/av multipliers are t_u(q)=q^(-u)-1 and t_v(q)=q^(-v)-1;
the B multiplier is t_B(q)=q^(-u)+q^(-v)-2.
Define the exact smooth/compensator vector H=(H_J,H_u,H_v)^T by
  H_J=S_sm-m_u*n^kappa_u-m_v*n^kappa_v
              +sum_(q in Q_theta(n))[-C_0(m_q)+q^(-u)*C_u(m_q)
                                                       +q^(-v)*C_v(m_q)],
  H_u=K_u-c_u*n^kappa_u
              +sum_(q in Q_theta(n))t_u(q)*c_u*m_q^kappa_u
              +sum_(q in U_theta(n))t_u(q),
  H_v=K_v-c_v*n^kappa_v
              +sum_(q in Q_theta(n))t_v(q)*c_v*m_q^kappa_v
              +sum_(q in U_theta(n))t_v(q).                    (3.1)
The last two strips use a_u(1)=a_v(1)=1 exactly. The p prime strip is
zero, because p(q)=0. These facts are distinct from the centered values
M_b(1), which need not be one or zero.

With
  A_q = [ t_B(q)   beta*t_v(q)  beta*t_u(q) ]
        [   0         t_u(q)         0      ]
        [   0           0          t_v(q)   ],
the full centered identity is
  F(n)=H(n)+sum_(q in Q_theta(n))A_q*F(m_q).     (3.2)
This follows directly from the largest-prime partition and (1.3), not
from an independence assumption. All cofactor arguments are <=n^(1-theta).
All repeated primes, high-smooth coefficients and same-scale arithmetic
remain in H. Its source-containing K,K_u,K_v are the unfinished part of
the construction; calling H a paid forcing would be an unproved claim.

For dyadic transport one can subtract lambda times (3.2) at n from
(3.2) at 2n or 2n+1. The difference retains H(child)-lambda*H(parent),
both complete prime sets and all cofactor cells. Child cofactor arguments
are <=(2n+1)^(1-theta). Thus any genuine parent-credit inequality may use
strictly smaller companions, but it still needs an estimate for the joint
fresh H/common-bank covariance. The local eigenvalue t_B tends to -2;
a fixed positive quadratic local contraction cannot provide that estimate.

4. The smaller-scale rule in a coupled energy, without separate premises
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Let Q be any fixed positive definite real 3x3 matrix and put
  E_F(X)=X^(-2)*sum_(X<=n<2X)F(n)^T*Q*F(n).
Use a simultaneous cumulative induction hypothesis
  sum_(1<=m<=T)F(m)^T*Q*F(m)<=A_epsilon*T^(2+epsilon),             (4.1)
at smaller T. This is ONE coupled hypothesis to be proved, not an external
bound for J, M_u and M_v separately. Its dyadic/cumulative formulations
are equivalent up to geometric-sum constants and a factor-two horizon.
Finite heads must be included if the dependency graph is implemented.

For (3.2) the centered shared-prime square D_F is
  X^(-2)*sum_(X<=n<2X)sum_(q in Q_theta(n))
                        F(m_q)^T*A_q^T*Q*A_q*F(m_q).
Since |t_B|<=2, |t_u|,|t_v|<=1, ||A_q||_Frobenius^2<=6+2beta^2,
put C_Q=(lambda_max(Q)/lambda_min(Q))*(6+2beta^2). Then (4.1) implies
  D_F<=C_Q*2^(2+epsilon)*(1+1/epsilon)
                                    *A_epsilon*X^((1-theta)*epsilon). (4.2)
The proof is the cofactor-cell rule: for fixed q, at most q integers n
have a given m_q, so the sum is <=q times (4.1) at T=2X/q. All q>X^theta.
Use sum_(j>Y)j^(-1-epsilon)<=(1+1/epsilon)*Y^(-epsilon).
The relaxed horizon is <=2X^(1-theta), below X when X>2^(1/theta);
the actual cofactor support is smaller. If complete dyadic intervals are
used instead of cumulative claims, enlarge this fixed head for their
factor-two endpoint. No same-scale auxiliary bound is being smuggled in.

For (2.2), ||b_q||^2<=4+2beta^2 gives the corresponding shared-prime
scalar bound with that constant, or its Q-dual norm counterpart.

The EXACT energy remainder of (3.2) is
  E_F(X)=D_F(X)+N_F(X),
  N_F=X^(-2)*sum_n[H(n)^T*Q*H(n)
             +2*sum_q H(n)^T*Q*A_q*F(m_q)
             +sum_(q!=r)F(m_q)^T*A_q^T*Q*A_r*F(m_r)].           (4.3)
All prime sets here are Q_theta(n). This retains distinct-prime, smooth,
pole and unit cross terms. A desired recurrence would have to establish
an actual signed bound for N_F, possibly with parent credit and proved
padding, inside the SAME coupled induction. Equation (4.2) is a genuine
smaller-scale component, but (4.3) alone is not a closure: assuming a
cofinal bound for N_F wholesale would simply rename the missing estimate.
The narrower theorem below shows why one of its tempting independent
payments, that of P_theta, is impossible.

5. A source-independent obstruction for isolated deterministic pole banks
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
The following theorem uses primes but no mu estimate, PNT, RH, numerical
constant or conditional companion moment.

Let C[m], m integer>=0, be a nonzero deterministic profile with
C[0]=C[1]=0 and |C[m]|<=A*m^kappa for m>=2, 0<kappa<1. Set
  f_C(1)=0, f_C(2)=C[2], f_C(k)=C[k]-C[k-1] for k>=3,
  F_C(s)=sum_(k>=2)f_C(k)*k^(-s).
Summation by parts gives the exact expression
  F_C(s)=sum_(m>=2)C[m]*[m^(-s)-(m+1)^(-s)],                    (5.1)
holomorphic on Re(s)>kappa. Its series is locally normally convergent
there because the bracket is O_K(m^(-Re(s)-1)). Equivalently
F_C(s)=s*integral_1^infinity C[floor(t)]*t^(-s-1)dt.
For the actual profiles, f_C(k)=O(k^(kappa-1)) after the finite head,
so their ordinary Dirichlet series is absolutely convergent on the same
half-plane. F_C is NOT identically zero: at its first nonzero increment,
the Dirichlet-series leading term as real s tends to infinity is nonzero.

Define the full staircase prime bank and its truncated version at integers n:
  B_full(n)=sum_(q prime)C[floor(n/q)],
  B_theta(n)=sum_(n^theta<q<=n/2,q prime)C[floor(n/q)], 0<theta<1.
Every profile value with floor<2 is zero. Their staircase extensions at
real t are the values at n=floor(t). On Re(s)>1, absolute integration
and t=q*u give the EXACT Mellin identity
  s*integral_1^infinity B_full(floor(t))*t^(-s-1)dt
       =F_C(s)*P_prime(s), P_prime(s)=sum_(q prime)q^(-s).        (5.2)
Here floor(floor(t)/q)=floor(t/q), so no floor error or unit term is
lost in the change of variable. Each coefficient really has threshold qk.

The removed small-prime bank is exactly
  B_full(n)-B_theta(n)=sum_(q<=n^theta,q prime)C[floor(n/q)].
Uniformly for every n>=1, including empty ranges,
  |B_full(n)-B_theta(n)|
    <=A*(1+1/(1-kappa))*n^lambda,
  lambda=kappa+theta*(1-kappa)<1.               (5.3)
Use floor(n/q)<=n/q and sum_(j<=n^theta)j^(-kappa)
 <=1+n^(theta*(1-kappa))/(1-kappa). Thus the removed bank's Mellin
transform is holomorphic on Re(s)>lambda, in particular near s=1.

For completeness, the prime-zeta logarithm is local and elementary.
On Re(s)>1, the absolutely convergent Euler product gives
  log zeta(s)=P_prime(s)+sum_(e>=2)P_prime(e*s)/e.                (5.4)
The last sum is holomorphic in a complex disk about one: choose its real
part >3/4; then e>=2 prime series are absolutely convergent, and their
uniform exponential tails in e give normal convergence. Since zeta has
a simple pole at one and (s-1)zeta(s) is analytic and nonzero in a small
disk, choose a slit disk and a logarithm to obtain
  P_prime(s)=-log(s-1)+A_0(s),                  (5.5)
where A_0 is analytic near one. No global zero-free region is needed.

F_C is analytic near one and not identically zero. It has a finite order
j>=0 zero there, possibly j>0, so F_C(s)=(s-1)^j*g(s), g(1)!=0. The term
  -(s-1)^j*g(s)*log(s-1)
does not extend holomorphically through one; for example its j-th
derivative has a nonzero logarithmic singularity. Subtracting the analytic
Mellin correction (5.3) cannot remove this branch. Therefore B_theta has
a nonholomorphic Mellin germ at one.

Finally a staircase function with a cofinal dyadic energy bound has a
holomorphic Mellin transform on Re(s)>1/2. Indeed E_B(X)<=C_epsilon*X^epsilon
implies sum_(X<=n<2X)|B(n)|<=sqrt(C_epsilon)*X^(3/2+epsilon/2).
The absolute integral over that block is <=a compact-set constant times
X^(1/2+epsilon/2-Re(s)). Choose epsilon<2Re(s)-1 and sum dyadically;
normal convergence also gives holomorphy. This contradicts (5.5).

Conclusion: a nonzero profile under these hypotheses cannot have a
cofinal critical-energy bound as an isolated moving-cutoff unweighted
prime bank. More strongly it cannot have E_B(X)=O(X^epsilon) at every
dyadic X for ANY fixed epsilon<1. A finite harmonic zero, or any finite
number of harmonic logarithmic-moment zeros at one, does not fix this.
Vanishing to every order would force F_C identically zero near one and
hence throughout its connected half-plane, contrary to its nonzero profile.
This theorem excludes paying the isolated deterministic bank. It says
nothing against a joint cancellation with the actual smooth or centered
source banks.

6. Application to the actual compensation P_theta
-------------------------------------------------
For the theorem use C[m]=C_0(m) for m>=2, and C[0]=C[1]=0. This explicit
finite-head convention matches Q_theta(n); it does not replace the original
C_0(1) in (1.3). Put kappa=2/3. Its leading coefficient is
  gamma_0=2m_u+beta*c_u
    =beta/kappa_u*[2*zeta(4/3)/zeta(2/3)^2-1/zeta(2/3)]>0.       (6.1)
Here zeta(2/3)<0 and zeta(4/3)>0. Thus C[m] is eventually nonzero,
has the required O(m^kappa) growth, and its F_C is analytic near one and
not identically zero. Whether F_C(1) happens to vanish is irrelevant;
the theorem permits a finite-order zero of ANY order.

The other two components of P_theta are analytic near one. For the
weighted profile use C[m]=C_u(m), m>=2, with the same zero head. Its
full bank has Mellin transform F_Cu(s)*P_prime(s+u), initially where
both series converge; both factors are analytic in a disk about one.
The removed weighted bank obeys, for every n>=1,
  |sum_(q<=n^theta)q^(-u)*C_u[floor(n/q)]|
       <=A_u*n^(2/3)*(1+theta*log n),           (6.2)
since kappa+u=1 and integer harmonic counting suffices. Its Mellin
transform is analytic on Re(s)>2/3. Thus the truncated weighted bank
is also analytic near one.

The integer main term m_u*n^(2/3) has a staircase Mellin transform
  m_u*sum_(n>=1)n^(2/3)*[n^(-s)-(n+1)^(-s)],
normally convergent for Re(s)>2/3. A real-power main term instead has
m_u*s/(s-2/3); the exact staircase difference is retained by the above
formula. Either representation is analytic near one, without equating
their transforms.

Consequently P_theta from (2.2) has the SAME nonremovable logarithmic
branch supplied by its C_0 unweighted bank. It cannot independently have
cofinal critical energy. The theorem is uniform in integer n for each
fixed theta<1. Constants and analytic neighborhoods may depend on theta;
there is no assertion at theta=1, where the bank is empty.

In contrast, K-P_theta may cancel this branch. The source K is not
analytic or small by itself under the supplied facts. Calling P_theta
unpaid is an instruction to retain it in the JOINT kernel, not a claimed
lower bound for R_new or a counterexample to RH.

7. Exact narrower pole/smooth covariance obligations
--------------------------------------------------
Let <f,g>_X=X^(-2)*sum_(X<=n<2X)f(n)g(n). Then exactly
  E_HR=E_K+E_Ptheta-2<K,P_theta>_X,              (7.1)
  E_Rnew=E_HR+E_centered_bank
                         +2<H_R,centered_bank>_X.             (7.2)
The unweighted pole entry of <K,P_theta> has the complete finite sum
  X^(-2)*sum_(h>=2,q prime)
        a(h)*sum_(n in I(h,q))C_0(floor(n/q)),
  I(h,q)={n integer:X<=n<2X, h<=n,
                      Pplus(h)<=n^theta<q, 2q<=n}.            (7.3)
It retains all smooth entries, bank exits and cofactor cells. If the
low smooth prefix is peeled, impose its exact h threshold and retain
the paid complement in the full square. Positive support gives
q>Pplus(h), so q does not divide h. It does not require any additional
coprimality between h and the cofactor floor(n/q).
The corresponding weighted/main entries use q^(-u)*C_u and n^kappa_u
on their exact domains, with the signs from P_theta.

To cancel E_Ptheta one cannot simply assume <K,P_theta> is its positive
norm. The actual desired credit involves BOTH E_K and the centered-bank
cross in (7.2), as well as distinct-prime covariance. A bound for (7.3)
alone would be a substantive narrower signed input, but its sufficient
sign and constant must follow from actual arithmetic rather than a fitted
coefficient. Likewise one may prove a joint pole/smooth cancellation
identity first; (5.2)-(6.2) show why an isolated norm payment cannot replace it.

The new proof hooks are therefore specific: (3.2) gives a finite cofactor
state alphabet and exact smaller-argument transfers; (4.2) pays its shared-prime
centered variance after the coupled induction step; (5.1)-(6.2) identify
an unavoidable isolated deterministic branch; (7.3) is the exact source
kernel that must participate in its cancellation. These hooks do not
supply the still missing distinct-prime/fresh smooth signed estimate.

Final status
------------
Proved: the complete centered target (2.2), the simultaneous three-state
transfer (3.2) including prime strips and units, the conditional strictly
smaller-scale shared-prime rule (4.2), and the source-independent isolated
deterministic-bank obstruction (5.1)-(6.2). The latter rules out separately
paying P_theta, with all staircases, finite-order harmonic zeros and removed
prime ranges accounted for. It does not rule out cancellation with K.
Open: a joint covariance estimate that closes this coupled induction,
the original all-scale cofinal moment and RH. No numerical/test work was
invoked and all older studies, sources, tests, receipts and memos are unchanged.
