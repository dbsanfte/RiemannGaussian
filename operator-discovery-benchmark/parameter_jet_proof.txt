PARAMETER JETS AND ACTUAL PRIME-POWER INFORMATION

Scope: universal operator identities with a guided recovery calibration. The
identities do not supply a new bound for primes or an RH proof. The finite
coefficient and numerical checks are independent implementations, not a
formal proof of all analytic arguments below.

1. General exact Dirichlet parameter jets

For any rational arithmetic sequence f with f(1)=1, write delta for the
Dirichlet unit and H=f-delta. At index n, H^{*k}(n)=0 for 2^k>n. Consequently
every formal power, logarithm and exponential is a finite calculation at that
index. This proves the identities in the locally nilpotent commutative
Dirichlet algebra, without multiplicativity or a prime oracle:

 f^{*(a+h)} = f^{*a} * exp_D(h log_D f),
 [h^j]f^{*(a+h)} = f^{*a}*(log_D f)^{*j}/j!.

The grammar returns Taylor coefficients; multiplying by j! returns ordinary
derivatives. The identities hold for every rational centre a and every
nonnegative integer j. Finite prefixes are exact restrictions of these
infinite arithmetic identities. They do not assert convergence of a
Dirichlet series for a generic input.

2. General logarithmic-kernel jets at exponent zero

For real u>0, real kappa and a>0, define
 K_(a,kappa)(u) = integral_0^u exp(kappa*v)*v^(a-1)dv / Gamma(a).
Expanding the exponential and integrating its absolutely convergent power
series gives
 K_(a,kappa)(u) = u^a/Gamma(1+a)
   * [1 + sum_(k>=1) a/(a+k)*(kappa*u)^k/k!].

For each fixed u>0 the right side is analytic in a near zero, and its
constant coefficient is one. It supplies the one-sided a=0 extension.
Uniform convergence on compact a disks of radius less than one justifies
coefficient extraction. With L=log(u)+EulerGamma,

 log[u^a/Gamma(1+a)] = L*a
    + sum_(j>=2) (-1)^(j+1)*zeta(j)*a^j/j,
 [a^j]sum_(k>=1) a/(a+k)*(kappa*u)^k/k!
    = (-1)^(j-1)*sum_(k>=1) (kappa*u)^k/(k^j*k!), j>=1.

The Gamma series follows by expanding the convergent Euler product; a
primary reference for the equivalent Gamma/psi series is DLMF 5.7.3-4:
 https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.7
For kappa=1, the first coefficient is
 L + sum_(k>=1)u^k/(k*k!) = Ei(u),
with the standard real positive-u exponential integral (DLMF 6.6.1):
 https://dlmf.nist.gov/6.6.E1

For a truncation at k=T and z=kappa*u, if |z|/(T+2)<1, the absolute tail of
the j-th scalar series is at most
 |z|^(T+1)/[(T+1)!*(T+1)^j] / [1-|z|/(T+2)].
Each subsequent term has smaller ratio because both factorial and k^j
increase. Adding this symmetric error ball certifies each kernel jet value.
This bound is at the declared u, not uniform in u or x.

Endpoint: K_(a,kappa)(0)=0 for every a>0. Its u>0 extension at a=0 is one.
Do not evaluate the above parameter derivatives at u=0 or commute the limits
a->0 and u->0. A summation over integers n<=x therefore becomes n<x when
this one-sided parameter jet is taken. The arithmetic limit below requires
x>1; it does not apply at x=1.

3. A matched derivative produces an actual signed prime error

The producer obtains mu by divisor inversion of the constant-one sequence,
without factorisation. Let nu_a=mu^{*a} and, for a>0 and real x>1,

 G_a(x) = sum_(n<=x)nu_a(n) K_(a,1)(log(x/n)).

At every fixed x this is a finite sum. At a=0+ its limit equals one.
The first coefficient of nu_a is log_D mu = -log_D 1. At a prime p the
formal local series of 1 is (1-z)^-1, whose logarithm is sum z^k/k. The
logarithm of the Euler product is the sum of these local logarithms, so

 (log_D mu)(n) = -1/k if n=p^k, and 0 otherwise, including n=1.

This is an all-index arithmetic identity. The evaluator's independent sieve
checks the actual prime-power support, rather than defining the producer's
coefficients by that support. Combining the finite coefficient derivative
and the fixed-u kernel derivative proves for every real x>1:

 E(x) := lim_(a->0+) [G_a(x)-1]/a
       = Ei(log x) - Pi_<(x),
 Pi_<(x) = sum_(p prime, k>=1, p^k<x)1/k.

Ei(log x) here is li(x) with the standard principal-value normalisation.
At an integer prime power, the strict count is essential; it differs from
the usual right-continuous count by the corresponding 1/k. Higher jets
contain signed products and convolutions of the same arithmetic information.
For instance, writing d=log_D mu and J_j(u)=[a^j]K_(a,1)(u),

 [a^2]G_a(x) = J_2(log x) + sum_(1<n<x)d(n)Ei(log(x/n))
                         + (1/2)sum_(1<n<x)(d*d)(n).

4. Root-count inversion supplies ordinary prime counts

For every real x>1, only finitely many k have 2^k<x, and

 pi_<(x) = sum_(k>=1) mu(k)/k * Pi_<(x^(1/k)).

Expanding Pi and grouping terms with exponent m=k*l gives coefficient
(1/m)sum_(k|m)mu(k), which is one for m=1 and zero for m>1. This proves
the identity for every x, including prime-power endpoints. The implemented
decoder tests n^k<x with exact rational inequalities to avoid numerical
root-boundary errors. This is a recovered representation of actual primes,
not a prime-distribution estimate.

5. Ordinary-zeta bridge and the missing signed control

For Re(s)>1, absolute convergence and the exponential-integral series give

 Mellin(E)(s) = -[log(s-1)+log zeta(s)]/s
             = -log[(s-1)zeta(s)]/s.

To check the first term, put u=log x: the integral of log u+EulerGamma
against exp(-s*u) is -log(s)/s, and integrating the positive-u series gives
sum_(k>=1)1/(k*s^(k+1)) = -log(1-1/s)/s. The prime-power contribution is
log zeta(s)/s by its absolutely convergent Euler logarithm. The logarithm
branch is the canonical one on Re(s)>1, real on the real axis.

Suppose a uniform bound |E(x)|<=C*x^(1/2)*(1+log x)^L for every real x>=2
were proved, with finite C and nonnegative integer L. The integrable
log-log singularity on 1<x<2 is confined to a bounded interval; it contributes
an entire Mellin function. The uniform bound makes the rest holomorphic on
Re(s)>1/2. Exponentiating -s*Mellin(E) then gives a holomorphic nonvanishing
function agreeing with (s-1)zeta(s) on Re(s)>1. The identity theorem extends
that equality to Re(s)>1/2. An ordinary zero of any positive multiplicity
there would contradict nonvanishing. The standard zeta functional equation
then gives RH. No uniform premise in this paragraph has been proved.

A sufficient parameter-family target is, for some a0>0, finite C and fixed L,
 |G_a(x)-1| <= C*a*x^(1/2)*(1+log x)^L
 for EVERY real x>=2 and EVERY 0<a<=a0.
Dividing by a and taking the already proved fixed-x limit gives the preceding
prime-error bound. Restricting x>=2 is necessary: Ei(log x)->-infinity as
x->1+, so a bounded target all the way down to one would be false.

Bounds on finitely many exponents or scales do not prove this coupled
premise. Constants O(1), rather than O(a), lose the derivative control.
The matched-path Mellin root is ((s-1)zeta(s))^-a/s, whose inversion costs
1/a; a smaller a alone therefore establishes no improved arithmetic bound.

The grammar also permits independent slopes q,r. The first jet of
nu_(q*a) with K_(r*a,1) has Mellin transform
 -[q*log zeta(s)+r*log(s-1)]/s.
If q!=r, the residual (q-r)*log(s-1) is singular at one. Thus a uniform
O(x^theta*(1+log x)^L) first-jet claim with theta<1 is impossible for that
unmatched path. This is an analytic obstruction, not a finite-growth test.

Acceptance: the proof backend may register these universal identities and
conditional bridges. It must not register an all-scale signed upper bound,
historical novelty, or RH on the basis of the numerical calibration.
