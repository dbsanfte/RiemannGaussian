053 INDEPENDENT PRIME-SCALE FILTER BRIDGE AUDIT

This is an exact conditional implication, not an estimate for the arithmetic
sum. Frozen051/052 proofs and files are unchanged; no scientific source arrays,
zero computations, old tests or candidate scoring are used in this audit.

1. Fixed arithmetic object and full center.
For one fixed rational 0<alpha<1, take the051 forward b=0,a=1,sign=-1 kernel
 K_alpha(n)=-sum_{d|n}mu(d)*(log d)^alpha, n>=2, K_alpha(1)=0.
Set A_alpha(x)=sum_{2<=n<=x}K_alpha(n), for every real x>=1. Frozen051 gives
0<K_alpha(n)<= (log n)^alpha. Frozen052 supplies the exact full positive center
m_alpha(x)=m_alpha(1), M_alpha(x)=m_alpha(1)(x-1), with
 m_alpha(1)=I_alpha^-1 integral_0^infinity
           [1/zeta(1+t)]t^-alpha-1dt,
 I_alpha=integral_0^infinity(1-exp(-u))u^-alpha-1du.
No truncated arithmetic moment, fitted scalar, leading-only asymptotic, or
ordinary-series convergence at a zero is substituted for this center.

2. The finite filter.
Fix integer q>=2 and one nonzero finite real rational polynomial
P(z)=sum_{j=0}^d c_j z^j. Define T_P(x)=sum_j c_j A_alpha(q^j x).
The exact identity P(q)=0 removes the growing linear center, leaving
 sum_j c_j M_alpha(q^j x)=-m_alpha(1)P(1).
P(1)=0 is optional: otherwise this fixed constant is harmless for a square-root
bound. Coefficients, degree and q are fixed, not fitted as functions of x.
The proposed arithmetic premise is a uniform bound for every epsilon>0 at all
integer scales (or all real scales if explicitly requested). This premise is
OPEN. Neither P(q)=0 nor pointwise positivity proves it.

3. Integer scales imply real scales.
For real x>=2 let N=floor x. In each term, the integers between q^j N and q^j x
number at most q^j. Their kernel values are at most
[log(q^d(N+1))]^alpha. Consequently
 |T_P(x)-T_P(N)| <= (sum_j |c_j|q^j)*[log(q^d(N+1))]^alpha.
For a fixed finite filter this is O((1+log x)^alpha), hence absorbed into every
x^(1/2+epsilon) bound. Any finite initial interval is priced as a compact head.
No interpolation from finite observations is used. q being an integer and the
known all-integer kernel bound are essential for this particular step.

4. Complete Mellin identity, including finite heads.
Let F_alpha(s)=sum_{n>=2}K_alpha(n)n^-s. Initially Re(s)>1 only,
 F_alpha(s)=I_alpha^-1 integral_0^infinity
           [zeta(s)/zeta(s+t)-1]t^-alpha-1dt.
Stieltjes integration and change of variable give exactly
 s integral_1^infinity T_P(x)x^-s-1dx
 =P(q^s)F_alpha(s)
  -s sum_j c_j q^(js) integral_1^(q^j) A_alpha(u)u^-s-1du.
Every finite-head term on the right is entire in s. The j=0 head is zero.
The left becomes holomorphic on Re(s)>1/2 under the all-epsilon real-scale
bound, by locally uniform integral domination. Hence the same premise implies
P(q^s)F_alpha(s) holomorphic there. Alternatively, the full center's transform
is m_alpha(1)/(s-1); multiplying by P(q^s) removes its simple pole at s=1
because P(q)=0. No critical-strip reciprocal Dirichlet series is asserted.

5. The annulus guard was sufficient but is unnecessary.
The first proposed guard excluded roots of P in sqrt(q)<|z|<q. That condition
would ensure P(q^rho) nonzero at any off-half-plane zero rho and directly
preserve052's divergence. It is a valid sufficient condition, but it excludes
filters unnecessarily. The following finite-order argument admits EVERY
nonzero finite P, regardless of roots in that annulus.

Assume an off-line ordinary zero exists. As in052, reflection gives one to the
right of1/2. Choose a rightmost zero rho at its fixed nonzero imaginary height.
This does not assume a globally rightmost zero. Frozen052 proves along the
zero-free horizontal ray, for delta>0 tending to0,
 F_alpha(rho+delta)=C_m delta^-alpha+O(1), C_m<0,
for every multiplicity m>=1 of zeta at rho. It retains all local and far-tail
terms; its uniform local remainder is O(t), not an unprocessed O(delta+t).

Let the analytic multiplier P(q^s) have zero order k>=0 at rho. Its order is
finite because P is a nonzero finite polynomial and q^s has nonzero derivative
(log q)q^s. Thus P(q^(rho+delta))=h delta^k+O(delta^(k+1)), h!=0. Multiplication
of the exact local estimate yields
 P(q^(rho+delta))F_alpha(rho+delta)
 =h C_m delta^(k-alpha)+O(delta^k).
Indeed the next multiplier term contributes O(delta^(k+1-alpha)), which is
O(delta^k) because 0<alpha<1, and the bounded remainder contributes O(delta^k).
If k=0 this diverges and cannot be holomorphic. If k>=1, k-alpha lies strictly
between k-1 and k. Were this product holomorphic at rho, its Taylor coefficients
of degrees0 through k-1 would all vanish: any first nonzero such term would
contradict its O(delta^(k-alpha)) behavior on positive delta. The holomorphic
product would then be O(delta^k), contradicting the nonzero h C_m term.
Hence a polynomial zero cannot erase this fractional singularity. This proof
does not need differentiation of the O(1) remainder or a global branch analysis.
The strict range 0<alpha<1 is essential; integer powers are not covered.

6. Conclusion and admission scope.
A uniform all-epsilon square-root bound for a fixed nonzero finite filter with
P(q)=0 would exclude every ordinary zeta zero to the right of1/2; reflection
therefore implies RH. The filter need not pass an annulus test. The finite
polynomial identity and this conditional implication may be admitted. The
arithmetic estimate remains OPEN, and no convergence theorem, converse, root
energy bound, numerical proof, or actual RH result follows from the certificate
alone. Every finite-head and full-center term is retained. Proof scope is an
independently reviewed typed analytic argument, not proof-assistant formalization.
