GLOBAL CARRY ROOT-LIMIT OPERATOR AND A ZERO-SENSITIVE ZETA CRITERION

These are written mathematical proofs with exact implementation checks,
not theorem-prover certificates. The operator is a candidate to this
detector; its historical originality has not been established. A reformulation
equivalent to RH is not a proof of RH or evidence that RH becomes easier.

1. CROSS-PRIME PRODUCT AND ITS DOMAIN

Omega(n)=sum_p v_p(n) is completely additive. Define
  (f G g)(n)=sum_{ij=n, Omega(i)&Omega(j)=0}f(i)g(j),
where & is binary AND. This follows the detector's recovered carry grammar
with potential V(n)=v_2(Omega(n)!). Its defect
  V(ij)-V(i)-V(j)=v_2(binomial(Omega(i)+Omega(j),Omega(i)))
is nonnegative, and equals zero precisely when the total degrees add
without binary carries. The potential defects telescope on triples.
At parameter zero, a product of weights is 1 precisely when its total
defect is zero, and 0 otherwise. This proves associativity. Commutativity
and delta_1 identity are immediate. The convolution is coefficient-local.

This product couples primes: delta_2 G delta_3=0, while ordinary and
primewise infinitary convolution both give delta_6. It need not preserve
multiplicativity. These facts are tested rather than silently assuming an
Euler product still exists.

2. NONLINEAR TRANSFER AND ROOT LIMIT

On rational arithmetic functions f(1)=1 define
  S(f)=EXP_G(LOG_*(f)),
with inverse EXP_*(LOG_G(f)). Coefficient-local formal series are justified
by n>=2^k for k nonunit factors, exactly as in root_transfer_proof.txt.
The transfer law S(f*g)=S(f) G S(g) and both inverse laws follow.
The root-order limit proof in that file uses only these algebraic laws and
therefore gives
  S(f)=lim_{q->infinity} q-fold G product of P_(1/q)^*(f)
coefficientwise, for EVERY normalized input, without an analytic assumption.
Its group laws do not imply that it is a linear algebra isomorphism.

For u(n)=1 all root coefficients are positive. The q-fold G root product
removes some nonnegative terms from the ordinary q-fold root product u,
so each coefficient lies in [0,1]. The limit coefficients s(n)=S(u)(n)
also lie in [0,1], giving absolute Dirichlet convergence for Re(s)>1.
At n=6 the only allowed root-product terms have one nonunit factor 6:
its coefficient is q*b_(1/q)(6)=q/q^2=1/q. The split 2,3 is forbidden
because the two total degrees both equal 1. Thus s(6)=0 even though
s(2)=s(3)=1. This is a provable cross-prime effect.

3. INDEPENDENT COEFFICIENT FORMULA AND RECOVERED MASK

LOG_*(u) is supported on prime powers, with coefficient 1/e at p^e.
In its G-exponential, the total degrees of participating prime powers
must have pairwise disjoint binary bits. For each prime, the factor degrees
partition the bits of v_p(n). Across distinct primes those exponent bit
sets must also be disjoint. Conversely these two conditions give every
allowed exponential term. Labels on the ordered factors contribute the
factorial canceled by the exponential coefficient, as in the independent
partition proof for the primewise transfer.

Consequently, with h_e from root_transfer_proof.txt section 3,
  s(n)=chi(n)*product_{p^e||n} h_e(0),
  chi(n)=1[the binary bit sets of the exponents v_p(n) are pairwise disjoint].
Equivalently
  chi(n)=1[sum_p s_2(v_p(n))=s_2(Omega(n))].
The equivalence follows by counting carries: binary digit sum never increases
under addition, and its total decrease is zero precisely when there are no
carries. The detector searches the two sides as anonymous digital features.
It checks direct generic LOG/EXP against the independent partition/mask
formula through n=4096. This finite check supports, but does not replace,
the universal coefficient argument above.

4. GRADED ANALYTIC REPRESENTATION

Let P(z)=sum_p p^(-z) for Re(z)>1. Its definition and convergence here use
the prime Dirichlet series. The familiar identity
  log(zeta(s))=sum_{k>=1}P(k*s)/k, Re(s)>1,
follows by the absolutely convergent Euler logarithm, with its normalized
branch. Grade arithmetic coefficients by Omega(n). At each grade k, the
Dirichlet sum of LOG_*(u) is P(k*s)/k.

On graded power series define x^i G x^j=x^(i+j) when i&j=0, and zero
otherwise. Put
  K(s,x)=sum_{k>=2}P(k*s)*x^k/k,
  K_even(s,x)=sum_{even k>=2}P(k*s)*x^k/k,
  K_odd(s,x)=sum_{odd k>=3}P(k*s)*x^k/k.
Their coefficient l1 norms converge locally uniformly on Re(s)>1/2,
since the first possible term is degree 2. The graded G product has
l1 norm <= the product of l1 norms, so its exponential is an entire
map of this Banach algebra. Evaluating a convergent graded series at x=1
is a bounded linear map, though it is NOT a G-algebra homomorphism.

Define the holomorphic functions, on Re(s)>1/2,
  A(s)=eval_1 EXP_G(K_even(s,x)),
  D(s)=eval_1[EXP_G(K_even(s,x)) G K_odd(s,x)],
  Q(s)=sum_{k>=2}P(k*s)/k,
  B(s)=A(s)*(1-Q(s))+D(s).
The odd-grade ideal squares to zero, and multiplication by x annihilates
it. Multiplication by x maps every even grade to the next odd grade and
preserves evaluation at 1. Thus the elementary finite/absolutely convergent
exponential identity gives, initially for Re(s)>1,
  F(s)=sum_n s(n)n^(-s)=A(s)*(1+P(s))+D(s)
      =A(s)*log(zeta(s))+B(s).
A is not identically zero: A(s)>=1 on real s>1/2, and A(s)->1 as s->+infinity.
This is a genuine analytic bridge to the half-plane relevant to RH. It
does not claim F itself has a single-valued extension through zeta zeros.

5. POLE CANCELLATION AND RH EQUIVALENCE

Let H={Re(s)>1/2}. The function Z(s)=(s-1)*zeta(s) is holomorphic on H,
with value 1 at s=1. On Re(s)>1 choose the normalized logarithms and put
  Phi(s)=F(s)+A(s)*log(s-1)=A(s)*log(Z(s))+B(s).

If RH holds, zeta has no zeros in H. The simply connected domain H then
admits a holomorphic logarithm of Z, agreeing with the initial branch.
The displayed formula proves Phi has a single-valued holomorphic extension
to all H.

Conversely suppose zeta has a zero rho in H of order m. A small loop around
rho changes log Z by 2*pi*i*m, hence changes Phi by 2*pi*i*m*A(s) at its
base point. Since A is holomorphic and not identically zero, it cannot
vanish on a whole punctured neighborhood. This monodromy cannot disappear,
even if A(rho)=0. Therefore Phi cannot extend single-valuedly to all H.
By zeta's functional equation and the known location of nontrivial zeros
in 0<Re(s)<1, absence of zeros in H is equivalent to RH. Hence
  RH <=> Phi extends holomorphically and single-valuedly to H.

This criterion is a reformulation. Proving its extension remains a global
problem equivalent to excluding off-line zeta zeros. The numerical detector
does not decide this criterion from finite samples.

6. VERIFICATION AND NOVELTY

The formal laws, bit-mask identity and independent coefficient route have
exact checks on unrelated inputs and larger, separate cutoffs. The affine
analytic representation can be checked at actual zeta values using graded
series and explicit tails. A controlled additional zero must produce a
nonzero monodromy, unlike the earlier symmetry-only tests.

The zero-parameter kernel is a degeneration of a classical potential-weight
convolution framework. Its specific cross-prime root-limit and analytic
representation require further publication-history review. The operator
is not counted as historically new merely because its formula or this RH
criterion was constructed in the present search.

7. CERTIFIED GRADED TAILS AND ZERO-SENSITIVITY CONTROL

For tested sigma>=3/4, P(z) at Re(z)>1 is evaluated independently from
actual Arb zeta values using
  P(z)=sum_{m>=1}mu(m)*log(zeta(m*z))/m.
This inversion follows by the absolutely convergent Euler logarithm:
the coefficient of P(k*z) is sum_{m|k}mu(m)/k, which is 1 for k=1 and
0 otherwise. With a=M+1, the tail beyond m=M is bounded by
  [(1+2/(a*Re(z)-1))*2^(-a*Re(z))]
   /[a*(1-2^(-a*Re(z)))*(1-2^(-Re(z)))].
To obtain it, use |log zeta(m*z)|<=sum_{n>=2}n^(-m*Re(z))
/(1-2^(-m*Re(z))) and the integral bound on that integer sum. The tested
domain satisfies log(zeta(Re(z)))<pi, so the principal Arb logarithm equals
the analytically normalized branch. Every finite prime-zeta coefficient
gets a complex error box from the proved tail before further arithmetic.

For the graded exponential tail choose r=2^(sigma/2)>1. Since k>=2,
  sum_k |K_k(s)|*r^k <= C,
  C=r^2/[2*(1-r*2^(-sigma))]
         *[2^(-2*sigma)+2^(1-2*sigma)/(2*sigma-1)].
The weighted l1 convolution norm is submultiplicative. Consequently the
exponential's weighted norm is <=exp(C). Its output grades beyond D have
unweighted norm <=exp(C)*r^(-D-1). This bounds BOTH the even (A) and odd
(D-function) output tails. The Q tail is bounded by
  (1+2/((D+1)*sigma-1))*2^(-(D+1)*sigma)
  /[(D+1)*(1-2^(-sigma))].
The numerical affine representation error is therefore at most
  tau_exp*(2+|log(zeta(s))|+C)+|A_head|*tau_Q.
Add N^(1-sigma)/(sigma-1) for the independent arithmetic Dirichlet sum.
global_analytic_probe.py uses these bounds with complex Arb balls. Perturbed
coefficients must exceed the same bound and are checked as negative controls.

The zero-sensitivity control takes a disk of radius 1/200 centered at
3/4+i. An Arb enclosure of the actual zeta on an enclosing rectangle proves
that this whole disk contains no zeta zero. It also excludes the pole.
Thus Z(s)=(s-1)zeta(s) has winding number zero there. The controlled model
(s-(3/4+i))*Z(s) has exactly one simple zero and winding number one. The
computed graded A at the loop basepoint, with its tail, excludes zero.
The logarithmic criterion therefore has monodromy zero for the actual
local Z, and 2*pi*i*A(basepoint)!=0 for the extra-zero model. Sampled phases
confirm the two windings, but the stated zero count comes from the ball
enclosure and the explicitly added linear factor, not a finite phase mesh.

This last check challenges the derived criterion with its zeta-specific
A,B kept fixed. It does NOT assert that substituting any analytic function
for zeta gives the arithmetic transfer of some different input sequence.
