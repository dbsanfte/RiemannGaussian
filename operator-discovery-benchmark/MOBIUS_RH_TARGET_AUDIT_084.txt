1. Exact constructive dilation inversion, including floors
Put M(x)=sum_(n<=x)mu(n), M(x)=0 for0<=x<1, and extend the sources to real x:
 G(x)=M(x/8)-M(x/10)-M(x/4)+M(x/5),
 F(x)=G(x)+cG(x/3), c=3547/8192.
These agree with their integer floor implementations, and G(x)=G(floor x),
F(x)=F(floor x), because all kernel divisors are positive integers.
For D_a h(x)=h(ax), exact algebra gives
 G(4x)=-(I-D_(1/2))(I-D_(4/5))M(x).
Consequently
 M(x)=-sum_(j,k>=0) G(4x*2^(-j)*(4/5)^k).
For each fixed x the nonzero summands form a finite set: G(t)=0 for t<4.
The identity follows by telescoping rectangular partial sums; the boundary
M arguments tend below1. This uses actual source identity, not an assumed
Mertens bound. For sigma>0, |G(t)|<=C t^sigma for all positive integer t
therefore implies, on all real x>=1,
 |M(x)|<= C*4^sigma*x^sigma /
              ((1-2^(-sigma))*(1-(4/5)^sigma)).
Integer bounds extend to real arguments by floor monotonicity of t^sigma.
Also G(x)=sum_(l>=0)(-c)^l F(x/3^l), again locally finite. Thus an F bound
transfers with the additional factor(1-c*3^(-sigma))^(-1), finite because
0<c<1. Conversely M bounds give both source bounds by finitely many dilations.
Hence each fixed source and M have equivalent power-growth bounds for every
sigma>0, with explicit constants. Neither inversion samples new arithmetic.

2. Independent Mellin check and RH implication
Initially Re(s)>1, integration of the step functions gives
 integral_0^infinity G(x)x^(-s-1)dx
   =[(2^(-s)-1)(4^(-s)-5^(-s))]/[s*zeta(s)].
For F multiply by1+c*3^(-s). Both factors of the first numerator can vanish
only on Re(s)=0, since equality of positive-base exponential moduli forces
that real part. The extra factor is nonzero for Re(s)>=0 because
|c*3^(-s)|<=c<1. A source O(x^sigma) bound makes its Mellin integral analytic
in Re(s)>sigma, by locally uniform absolute convergence, so the identity
excludes zeta zeros there. There is no cancellation of any positive-real-part
zeta zero by these multipliers. The point s=1 is harmless:1/zeta has a zero.
Every sigma=1/2+epsilon therefore excludes all zeros to the right of the
critical line; the zeta functional equation reflects any nontrivial zero to
1-conjugate(rho), giving RH. The constructive inversion above provides an
alternative reduction to the classical Mertens-growth equivalence.

3. Exponent distinction and a suitable recurrence proof family
With E>=source^2, a proved inequality
 E(u)<=r E(floor(u/2))+C_delta*u^(1+delta), r=3/2,
for every positive integer u, plus a complete finite base, yields
 E(u)=O_delta(u^(1+delta)), source=O_delta(u^(1/2+delta/2)).
Constants and the energy/control program may depend on delta. q=1 gives
O(sqrt u), which is stronger than the RH-equivalent epsilon family. This
audit does not assert O(sqrt u) impossible, nor that RH alone proves it.
One fixed delta>0 gives only a zero-free half-plane Re(s)>(1+delta)/2;
it does not establish RH. A finite list of positive deltas also falls short.

An implementable target family is delta_j=1/j for every integer j>=1.
Alternatively delta_j=2^(-j) works. Given epsilon>0 choose j with
1/(2j)<=epsilon. No uniformity of C_j across j is needed. A finite structural
acceptance must validate a parametrized construction and its proof FOR ALL j,
not only several generated members. A bounded pilot is invention evidence.

For exact recurrence certification choose rational A>=0 and C>=0 proving
base E(u)<=A*u^q, and establish A*(1-r/2^q)>=C. For rational q=a/b>0 and
rational r=p/t, strict contraction is exactly p^b<t^b*2^a (positive integers).
A rational lower enclosure l<=2^q obtained by l^b<=2^a gives the sufficient
rational inequality A*(1-r/l)>=C. For q>=1 and r=3/2, choose l=2 uniformly:
A=4C, enlarged for the finite base, works for ALL delta>=0. Odd u causes no
loss:floor(u/2)^q<=u^q/2^q. Base proofs can use a signed-data-free trivial
source/energy bound if available; a finite exhaustive base is also legitimate
when independently bound and not mistaken for the uniform drift leaf.
