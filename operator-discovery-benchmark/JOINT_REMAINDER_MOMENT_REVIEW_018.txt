JOINT REMAINDER MOMENT REVIEW 018
Complete signed short inverse weights, Mellin moments, and a paid E^2 component

Status: a new unconditional component estimate is proved below, using an
explicitly identified classical unconditional zeta moment theorem. The whole
candidate10/core/RH bound is NOT proved. This is an analytical proof-rule input,
not a source experiment, a fitted constant, or a numerical validation result.
No old file was changed and no arithmetic table, experiment, test, or hash was
computed for this review.

1. Definitions and the useful finding

All convolutions below are Dirichlet convolutions. Let U>=1 be an integer,
 A(n)=mu(n) 1_(n<=U), c=sum_(d<=U) mu(d)/d,
 T=c delta-A, E=delta+One*T.
Write A(s)=sum_(d<=U)mu(d)d^(-s), T(s)=c-A(s), and
 E(s)=1+zeta(s)(c-A(s)).
The exact relation T(1)=0 is retained BEFORE any absolute bound. Thus E(s)
extends holomorphically across s=1. Its value there need not be zero.

For u=1/3, theta=1-u=2/3, K(n)=n^(-u), put
 h=A*A*E*E, M=sum_(d>=1)h(d)d^(-theta),
 P(x)=sum_(n<=x)(K*h)(n), P(x)=0 for 0<x<1,
 C(x)=P(x)-(M/theta)x^theta.
The sum defining M is an ORDINARY conditional coefficient sum, not an
unjustified analytic-continuation convention; its convergence is checked in
section 3. In particular M=A(theta)^2 E(theta)^2.

There is a fixed finite constant C_* independent of U and X such that
 for EVERY integer U>=1 and EVERY integer X>=1,
 X^(-2) sum_(X<=n<2X) |P(n)-(M/theta)n^theta|^2
       <= C_* U^5 X^(-1/4).                              (1.1)
The left side is the complete centered prefix, including its unit and ALL
earlier coefficients. It is not a prefix restarted at X.
Equivalently its coefficient centering is (M/theta)J_theta with
J_theta(n)=n^theta-(n-1)^theta, INCLUDING J_theta(1)=1.

Consequently, with ONE N=4X-1 cutoff U=floor(N^(1/24)) for both parent and
child, this component has energy at X bounded by
 C_* 4^(5/24) X^(-1/24).                                (1.2)
At the child 2X, use exactly the same U and apply (1.1) at 2X. There is no
change of coefficients, no discarded pole compensation, and no current-scale
cycle. Multiplying by a fixed source coefficient such as beta costs its
square. This replaces the currently unpaid E^2 COMPONENT by a paid component;
it does not pay the remaining higher residual powers or their full join.

The mechanism is different from demanding a too-strong pointwise Delta_2:
the joined factor T(s)^2 cancels the pole at s=1, and a global L2 contour pays
the complete centered prefix after averaging. The cancellation is exact and
arithmetic-bound through c=A(1). The mean-value import is unconditional.

2. External theorem provenance and exactly what is imported

The sole substantive external mean-value input used here is
 I_4(Y)=integral_0^Y |zeta(1/2+it)|^4 dt
       <= B_4 Y(1+log Y)^4, EVERY Y>=2,                  (2.1)
for some fixed finite B_4. This is the classical unconditional fourth-moment
theorem of Ingham; its asymptotic implies this upper bound after enlarging one
fixed finite-head constant. Original primary publication:
 A. E. Ingham, Mean-Value Theorems in the Theory of the Riemann Zeta-Function,
 Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society s2-27 (1928), 273-300,
 https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/s2-27.1.273
The publisher exposes bibliographic metadata, not its full proof in this
session. A directly read primary research source corroborates the precise
unconditional fourth-moment input and gives a stronger theorem: Bettin, Bui,
Li and Radziwill, A quadratic divisor problem and moments of the Riemann
zeta-function, Theorem 1.1 with constant Dirichlet polynomials and zero shifts,
 https://arxiv.org/html/1609.02539v1
Its Introduction also states the classical k=2 result explicitly. No twisted
polynomial theorem, short-polynomial-length premise, RH, Lindelof, or conjecture
about higher moments is imported here. A detector may archive (2.1) as a
provider with unspecified but fixed B_4; this memo does NOT supply a numeric
B_4 or certify a fitted finite recurrence constant.

The functional equation is used in its standard exact form
 zeta(s)=2(2pi)^(s-1)sin(pi s/2)Gamma(1-s)zeta(1-s),
with Stirling bounds on fixed vertical strips. Official formula source:
 https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.4.E2
Convexity bounds below follow from this equation, the absolutely convergent
series to the right of 1, and the three-lines principle; no subconvexity theorem
is required. The Mellin/Perron/Hardy arguments needed here are provided below.

For a fixed a>1/2, (2.1) implies
 integral_Y^(2Y) |zeta(a+it)|^4 dt
       <= B_a Y(1+log Y)^4, Y>=2.                       (2.2)
Here is a way to avoid silently importing an off-line moment theorem. Remove
the full principal part at 1 from zeta(s)^2:
 H(s)=zeta(s)^2-(s-1)^(-2)-2 gamma_E (s-1)^(-1).
H is holomorphic on the strip 1/2<=Re s<=2. For each Y>=2 multiply H by
exp((s-i*3Y/2)^2/Y^2). The resulting analytic function has integrable vertical
L2 boundary norms and decays at vertical infinity. On Re s=1/2 its squared
norm is O(Y(1+log Y)^4): use (2.1), dyadic subdivision of the Gaussian tails,
and the bounded/subquadratically decaying removed principal terms. On Re s=2
the squared norm is O(Y). The L2 three-lines theorem gives the same upper
bound on every fixed intermediate line. The Gaussian has an absolute lower
bound on Y<=t<=2Y, and restoring the principal terms proves (2.2).
For completeness, the L2 three-lines theorem follows by applying the ordinary
three-lines theorem to integrals against compactly supported L2 dual functions,
or by the strip Poisson formula and Holder; Gaussian decay justifies all
boundary limits. Thus the constants in (2.2) depend only on a and B_4, not U.

For fixed 0<a<1 and every fixed epsilon>0, elementary convexity gives
 |zeta(a+it)| <= Z_(a,epsilon) |t|^((1-a)/2+epsilon/2),
 |t|>=2.                                                (2.3)
Indeed interpolate the lines -epsilon and 1+epsilon after removing the pole;
the functional equation bounds the former by O(t^(1/2+epsilon)) and the latter
is bounded. The interpolated exponent is (1-a+epsilon)/2. Removing/restoring
the pole changes only fixed constants. All constants also cover 2<=|t|<=a
fixed finite height. Compact portions of any specified non-pole line are
bounded by analyticity, not by source numerical evaluation.

3. Full conditional moment and pole audit

The earlier elementary hyperbola rule proves
 |sum_(n<=t)(E*E)(n)| <=30 U(1+log U) sqrt(t), t>=1.
Convolving the finite A*A preserves a sqrt(t) bound, with an explicit finite
multiplier sum |(A*A)(d)|d^(-1/2). Therefore Abel summation proves convergence
of sum h(d)d^(-theta) since theta=2/3>1/2. The tail is retained and tends to
zero. For E alone its bounded-prefix rule gives ordinary convergence at every
positive real theta. Its Dirichlet series equals 1-zeta(s)(A(s)-c) initially
Re s>1, and Abel continuation identifies the conditional value at theta.
Finite convolution and the same tail argument then give
 M=A(theta)^2 E(theta)^2.

The absolutely convergent Dirichlet function of K*h on Re s>1 is
 F_U(s)=zeta(s+u) A(s)^2 [1-zeta(s)(A(s)-c)]^2.           (3.1)
There is a simple pole at theta with residue M (possibly residue zero for a
degenerate cutoff). There is NO pole at 1: A(1)-c=0 removes the zeta pole in
each E factor. On the strip sigma<=Re s<=2 for any fixed sigma>0, these are
the only possible poles of F_U; its growth is polynomial in |Im s| for each
fixed U. There are no reciprocal zeta factors and no zero-free premise.

One must NOT split (3.1) into its zeta-power terms and forget their individual
principal parts at 1. Those parts cancel only in the full factor. Likewise
there is no removal of finite d>n in the complete T*T weighted Delta_2 formula.
Equation (3.1) is the exact Dirichlet function of that complete coefficient
array, including those compensating terms.

4. Uniform vertical L2 estimate

Choose a fixed sigma with 1/3<sigma<1/2. The finite weights satisfy
 |A(sigma+it)| <= U^(1-sigma)/(1-sigma),
 |A(sigma+it)-c| <= [(1-sigma)^(-1)+2]U^(1-sigma),        (4.1)
using the actual bound |c|<=2. Write d_sigma=(1-sigma)^(-1)+2.
The inequality |1-z|^4<=8(1+|z|^4) gives
 |F_U(s)|^2 <=8(1-sigma)^(-4)d_sigma^4 U^(8(1-sigma))
               |zeta(s+u)|^2(1+|zeta(s)|^4).           (4.2)
The U exponent has been rounded up on the term without zeta(s)^4, which is
valid for U>=1. No signed coefficient was discarded before the pole removal.

For t in [Y,2Y], Y>=2, the functional equation and (2.2) give
 integral_Y^(2Y) |zeta(sigma+it)|^4 dt
       <= C_sigma Y^(3-4sigma)(1+log Y)^4.             (4.3)
Use (2.3) with a=sigma+u and divide by |s|^2. The corresponding dyadic bound is
 integral_Y^(2Y) |F_U(s)/s|^2 dt
 <= C_(sigma,epsilon) U^(8(1-sigma))
    [Y^(-sigma-u+epsilon)
     +Y^(2-u-5sigma+epsilon)(1+log Y)^4].              (4.4)
Both exponents are negative if epsilon>0 is fixed sufficiently small and
sigma>(2-u)/5=1/3. Sum every dyadic height, including its negative counterpart;
the compact |t|<=2 portion satisfies the same U envelope by (4.1) and bounded
zeta values on the chosen non-pole line. Thus
 (1/(2pi)) integral_R |F_U(sigma+it)/(sigma+it)|^2 dt
       <= C_sigma U^(8(1-sigma)),                     (4.5)
uniformly EVERY U>=1. The constant is fixed once sigma and epsilon are fixed.
An explicit symbolic constant can retain the finite compact-line bound,
B_(1-sigma), convexity and Stirling constants, and the convergent series
 sum_(j>=1)2^(j*(2-u-5sigma+epsilon))(1+j log2)^4.
Nothing here fits those constants to source data.

At sigma=3/8 take, for example, epsilon=1/48. The second exponent in (4.4)
is -3/16 and the first is also negative. Then 8(1-sigma)=5, proving the
U^5 vertical norm. More generally the detector may enumerate rational sigma
and check the strict convergence inequalities rather than hardcode 3/8.

5. Gaussian contour proof: line L2 alone is not a pole-crossing license

Here are the necessary boundary arguments. Start at Re s=b=2, where the
Dirichlet series and Perron integral are absolutely controlled. For e>0 use
the multiplier exp(e s^2) and define
 P_e(x)=(1/(2pi i))integral_(b) F_U(s)exp(e s^2)x^s ds/s.
This is Gaussian smoothing in log x of the complete staircase P(x). The
original coefficients have polynomial/divisor growth for fixed U, so the
Gaussian convolution converges, and P_e(x)->P(x) at every non-integer x>0.

For fixed e>0 shift the contour to Re s=sigma. The fixed-U polynomial growth
on the intervening strip, multiplied by exp(-e t^2), sends both horizontal
edges to zero. The only crossed pole is at theta. The point s=1 is removable;
s=0 lies strictly to the left and is not crossed. It follows exactly that
 P_e(x)-(M/theta)exp(e theta^2)x^theta
   =(1/(2pi i))integral_(sigma)F_U(s)exp(e s^2)x^s ds/s.
Mellin Plancherel on the right gives its global squared norm with measure
x^(-2sigma-1)dx. Dominated convergence in (4.5) gives convergence of the
right-hand functions in that L2 space as e downarrow0. Pointwise convergence
of the left side off the integer set identifies the limit as the COMPLETE
C(x) defined in section 1. Therefore
 integral_0^infinity |C(x)|^2 x^(-2sigma-1) dx
   =(1/(2pi))integral_R |F_U(sigma+it)/(sigma+it)|^2 dt
   <= C_sigma U^(8(1-sigma)).                         (5.1)

The artificial extension of the centering to 0<x<1 is explicit: C(x) equals
-(M/theta)x^theta there. Its norm is finite because sigma<theta. We have not
altered any original coefficient below 1, and (5.1) includes the cost of this
entire lower tail. No conditionally convergent contour is shifted without the
Gaussian regulator. Uniformity in U comes from (4.5), not an assertion that
the horizontal fixed-U bounds are uniform before their limit.

6. Integer cells, finite head, and full channel history

Let C_n=P(n)-(M/theta)n^theta, C_0=0. For x in [n,n+1), n>=1,
 C_n=C(x)+(M/theta)(x^theta-n^theta).
The mean value theorem gives
 |x^theta-n^theta|<=theta n^(theta-1)
                         <=2 theta x^(theta-1).
For 0<sigma<1/2, comparing integer weights with the cell integrals and using
|a+b|^2<=2|a|^2+2|b|^2 yields the safe global bound
 sum_(n>=1)|C_n|^2 n^(-2sigma-1)
   <=8 integral_1^infinity |C(x)|^2 x^(-2sigma-1)dx
       +32|M|^2/(2+2sigma-2theta).                    (6.1)
The denominator is positive in the present range. It includes n=1; no finite
head or unit is discarded. For a generic abovehalf line 1/2<=sigma<theta<1,
replace the displayed constants 8 and 32 by 16 and 64: the same proof uses
2^(2sigma+1)<8 instead of <4. This supplies the quantitative integer transfer
on that branch as well. Moreover
 |M|<= [(1-theta)^(-2) (1+|zeta(theta)|d_theta)^2]
                   U^(4(1-theta)),
so |M|^2 has U exponent 8(1-theta)<=8(1-sigma). Combining (5.1) and (6.1)
proves a global weighted l2 bound with the same U exponent. Restrict it to
X<=n<2X and use n^(2sigma+1)<=(2X)^(2sigma+1). This proves (1.1), and the
general energy exponent 2sigma-1. In particular no endpoint floor error can
undo the negative energy exponent.

The current channel APIs use complete coefficients and logarithmic prefixes,
not local restarts. Here is a transfer rule that respects this. For a fixed
rational polynomial p(t)=sum_j c_j t^j and eta>=0, let
 V_p(n)=log(2n)^(-eta)sum_(k<=n)(C_k-C_(k-1))
                                    p(log k/log(2n)).
Define P_0=sum|c_j| and P_1=sum j|c_j|. Discrete Abel summation gives exactly
 sum_(k<=n)(C_k-C_(k-1))p(log k/L_n)
 =C_n p(log n/L_n)
   -sum_(k<n)C_k[p(log(k+1)/L_n)-p(log k/L_n)].        (6.2)
This retains k=1 and every earlier prefix k<X. Since all polynomial arguments
are in [0,1], the bracket is bounded by P_1/(k L_n).

On l2 with weights n^(-2sigma-1), the positive Hardy operator
 H(C)_n=sum_(k<n)|C_k|/k has norm <=1+1/sigma. To check this directly, after
isometry to ordinary l2 its matrix is n^(-sigma-1/2)k^(sigma-1/2), k<n.
Schur's test with h_k=k^(-1/2) has row bound <=1+1/sigma and column bound
<=1/sigma; the claimed larger constant is safe. Thus (6.2), L_n>=log2>1/2,
and eta>=0 imply
 ||V_p||_(sigma,discrete)
 <=2^ceil(eta) [P_0+2(1+1/sigma)P_1] ||C||_(sigma,discrete). (6.3)
The coefficient is rational for rational sigma and polynomials. There is NO
extra log-degree cost. This is a global Hardy rule, not the old pointwise
derivative rule applied only to the block.

For finitely many channels, sum their squared constants in (6.3). Orthogonal
cell means decrease their block l2 energy, so the same paid component estimate
transfers to the existing complete projected means for ANY cell partition.
One must apply channels to the centered coefficient component including its
J_theta compensator. The full source decomposition must retain the opposite
compensator, all other components, and transport -e exactly as before. This
theorem pays a component; it does not justify dropping any full-source cross.

7. A generic moment-provider/compiler rule and its actual frontier

The algebra applies more generally to F(s)=zeta(s+u)A(s)^p E(s)^m with integer
p>=0, m>=2 and 0<u<1. For 0<sigma<min(1/2,1-u), absolute short-weight mass
cost is U^(2(p+m)(1-sigma)) in the squared vertical norm.
The fourth-moment provider plus functional equation and convexity for the
EXTRA 2m-4 powers on the line 1-sigma gives
 integral_Y^(2Y)|zeta(sigma+it)|^(2m)dt
 <=C Y^(1+m-(m+2)sigma+(m-2)epsilon)(1+log Y)^4.
Consequently the dyadic Mellin exponent is
 d_m=m-u-(m+3)sigma+(m-1)epsilon.                      (7.1)
Admit only if d_m<0; thus sigma>(m-u)/(m+3) with a strict margin. The term
without zeta(s)^m is also tested separately. Choose epsilon below the margin.
For sigma>=1/2 but sigma<1-u, apply fourth moment directly at sigma and
convexity to the extra powers; the corresponding exponent is
 d_m=(m-2)-u-(m-1)sigma+(m-1)epsilon.                  (7.2)
This second branch must not be replaced by extrapolating (7.1) above 1/2.

After a valid contour/residue audit, the centered component energy exponent
with U<=N^alpha and N<=4X is
 e=2sigma-1+2alpha(p+m)(1-sigma).                     (7.3)
The detector can search rational sigma, applicable moment-provider branch,
epsilon margin, finite-factor costs, and e. It must distinguish:
 (a) pole removal proof, including multiplicities;
 (b) an imported UNCONDITIONAL even-moment provider;
 (c) convergence of the original conditional coefficient moment, if requested;
 (d) complete analytic residue compensation;
 (e) negative/zero/positive recovered energy exponent;
 (f) full channel transfer and exact source join.
These are composable rule premises, not a nominated numerical candidate.

For the actual p=2, alpha=1/24, u=1/3:
 m=2 requires sigma>1/3 and is paid when sigma<2/5. The interval is nonempty.
 m=3 requires sigma>4/9 in the belowhalf branch; then (7.3) is already positive
       (its infimum at 4/9 is 13/108). No paid certificate follows.
 m>=4 has no belowhalf admitted line. Its abovehalf line, if any, has positive
       energy exponent. For m>=5 the abovehalf threshold from (7.2) is at
       least theta=2/3, outside the present pole-crossing domain.
Thus all generated m=2..23 receive an honest frontier; classical fourth moment
does NOT pay the entire residual polynomial. Failure of this provider is a
language failure, not arithmetic disproof.

For m>=3, evaluating E(theta)^m A(theta)^p analytically supplies a residue, but
does not by itself prove convergence of sum h(d)d^(-theta). The ordinary
moment is proved above for m=2. A compiler requiring an ordinary moment for
other powers must provide that separate premise; it may otherwise label the
output an analytic-residue-centered estimate, not rename it a coefficient
moment. No extra moment premise is needed to declare those branches unpaid.

Finite B=muv restricted d<=V and its residual S=delta-b_v*B can also be included
on a line sigma+v>1: |B(s)|<=zeta(sigma+v) and
|S(s)|<=1+zeta(sigma+v)^2, uniformly V. Their fixed powers then cost constants,
not V powers. Their complete residues and any pole-removal identities still
must be checked. This does not rescue the unpaid common E^m modes.

8. Exact rational-frequency and gcd rules: useful but not enough alone

There is a second executable signed grammar which needs no zeta theorem.
For finite T supported d<=U with sum T(d)/d=0,
 E(n)=delta(n)+sum_(d|n)T(d),
 P_E(n)=1-sum_(d<=U)T(d){n/d}.
The non-unit periodic coefficient has exact Fourier coefficients at every
reduced nonzero frequency a/b, b<=U, (a,b)=1:
 C_b=(1/b)sum_(j<=U/b)T(bj)/j.
This keeps the signed weights together and has zero constant frequency.
Complete-period averaging gives the coefficient quadratic form
 mean |E(n)-delta(n)|^2
       =sum_(d,e<=U)T(d)conj(T(e))*gcd(d,e)/(de).
For the centered fractional-part prefix profile, the exact discrete covariance
is (gcd(d,e)^2-1)/(12de); hence its variance is the corresponding signed T
quadratic form. The full prefix mean is 1-(1/2)sum T(d) and must be retained.
All identities hold on a complete multiple of lcm(1,...,U); they are obtained
by enumerating common residue classes analytically, not an actual-source run.

For any finite interval a<=n<a+L the Fourier Gram entries are exactly
 sum_(n=a)^(a+L-1)exp(2pi i (nu-eta)n), including its phase and diagonal L.
This is a detector-generable exact finite-window signed quadratic form.
Replacing it by an asymptotic period average without its boundary error is
unsound when the period is enormous. Likewise the additive Fourier modes of E
do NOT propagate as independent modes through Dirichlet convolution: divisors
introduce cofactor-dependent rational frequencies and joined boundary terms.
The gcd/Gram rules expose possible auxiliaries, but do not themselves prove
the K*A^2*E^2 moment bound. The Mellin rule above is the concrete propagation
which preserves that exact joined multiplicative structure.
