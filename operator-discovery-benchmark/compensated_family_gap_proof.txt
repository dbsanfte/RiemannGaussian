AUTOMATIC ALL-INTEGER GAP CERTIFICATES FOR A SIGNED OPERATOR FAMILY
7 October 2026. This generalizes the selected-instance proof. It supplies a
proof method, not an expected output for the candidate search.

Input: rational 0<u<v<1, weights w_u=-a(v)/(a(u)-a(v)),
w_v=a(u)/(a(u)-a(v)), a(t)=1/t-t, and the proposed positive gap gamma.
For this run gamma is the previously inferred sharp two-distinct-prime gap.
It is an unproved global proposal until every case below is closed.

The operator is B=W(sum w_i R_(v_i)), on ordinary zeta's constant-one input.
The common atomic sign and uniform bounds, derived in Sections 1 and 2 of
compensated_global_gap_proof.txt, hold for EVERY 0<v_i<1:

 |B_(v_i)(n)| <= U_(v_i)(m)/g, m=omega(n)>=2,
 U_v(m)=m!/[(1-v)*prod_(j=1..m)(m*v/(1-v)+j)],
 |B_v(p^g)| <= (1-v)/g, g>=2.

U_v(m) decreases in m. Because the weights have opposite signs and the atomic
coefficients have a common sign, define

 A_m=max_i |w_i| U_(v_i)(m), m>=2,
 A_1=max_i |w_i|(1-v_i).

Then |B(n)|<=A_m/g. These are upper bounds over every prime size, every
exponent pattern and every gcd, proved by the positive Jordan series and
concavity of log(exp(x)-1). They are not estimated from finite data.

The fixed algorithm chooses the first m0>=3 with A_m0<gamma. Monotonicity
closes all m>=m0. For each 2<=m<m0 it chooses
G_m=floor(A_m/gamma)+1, closing every g>=G_m. It also chooses
G_1=floor(A_1/gamma)+1, closing the one-prime tail. Since the proposed
gamma is no larger than the weighted atomic bound at a balanced two-prime
product, G_1>=2 and G_2>=2 for these inputs.

The remaining one-prime cases g=2..G_1-1 have exact rational coefficients

 sum_(k|g)mu(k)/k * [1-h(1-k/g)],
 h(t)=sum_i w_i*t/[v_i+(1-v_i)t].

A rational failure gives an actual counterexample at n=2^g, independently
of prime-size questions. Otherwise these cases are fully closed.

For each remaining m>=2 and 1<=g<G_m, normalize the logarithm shares as
q_i=g*log(p_i)/log(n); sum_i q_i<=1. The coefficient is exactly

 B_g(q)=sum_(k|g)mu(k)/k * sum_(S subset {1..m})
             (-1)^|S| h(1-(k/g)*sum_(i in S)q_i).

For m=2,g=1 squarefree inputs, the independent rational pair identity
proves the sharp proposed gamma. If an exponent repeats, after relabeling
2*q_1+q_2<=1, so the algorithm uses q_1=x_1/2,q_2=x_2.
All other remaining regions use the broader simplex sum q_i<=1.

Every rational inequality +/-B_g<=gamma is homogenized in q_i and a slack
variable. Each denominator factor v_i*T+(1-v_i)*(T-S) is strictly positive
on T>0 and 0<=S<=T. If an exact homogeneous polynomial times a nonnegative
power of T has only nonnegative coefficients, its inequality is universal
on the entire closed simplex. The certificate stores the original integer
coefficient vector and the multiplying power. The independent checker
reconstructs every rational expression and coefficient vector separately.

Resource limits can leave regions unproved. A candidate with ANY such region
remains a numerical lead. Failure of this sufficient polynomial method is not
a counterexample. A global theorem is recorded ONLY when every residual region,
every finite prime-power case and every unbounded tail has been certified.

The construction partitions ALL composite integers, rather than testing a
bounded list of omega/g values. The common sign proof covers arbitrary
factor counts by the same positive multinomial expansion. The equality
sum_(k|g)mu(k)k^(j-1)=prod_(p|g)(1-p^(j-1)) is the finite distributive
identity for the squarefree subsets of the prime support, for arbitrary g.

The proposed gap is sharp whenever the global bound passes: the two-distinct-
prime formula approaches it along primes with logarithm ratio tending to one.
Bertrand's postulate supplies such a sequence. This classical dependency
concerns sharpness only; no prime-distribution theorem enters the upper bound.

The separate all-order zero-signature test and analytic representation remain
necessary for claims about ordinary zeta. A successful global coefficient gap
does not imply half-plane continuation, a signed cancellation bound or RH.
Historical priority of a compound is not decided by a successful certificate.
