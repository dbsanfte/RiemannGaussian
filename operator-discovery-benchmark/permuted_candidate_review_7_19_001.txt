1. Fixed programs, input and recovery

Let M(n)=sum_{j<=n}mu(j), M(0)=0. For a unit prime source chi write
 a_alpha,chi(p^l)=chi(p)^l(alpha)_l/l!, a(1)=1,
 (T_alpha,chi f)(n)=sum_{d|n}a_alpha,chi(d)f(n/d).
All divisors occur. The input is M, not mu followed by prefixing.

For n=m+r, m=2^k,0<=r<m, put pi(n)=m+rev_k(r), where rev reverses exactly
k low bits including leading zeros. pi(1)=1 and pi is a block involution.
Let U f(n)=f(pi(n)). The selected prime source is

  ell(p)=floor(sqrt(p)),
  chi(p)=-1 iff mu(p-ell(p))<0, otherwise+1.

Every coefficient is bound to this SAME original actual source, including
in the inverse. Put A=T_1/2,chi=I+L_a and B=T_-1/2,-chi=I+L_b. Then

  rho7=A U M,
  rho19=B U A M.

Neither raw word has a normalizer rewrite. Both certificates have one
permutation, no shear, recovery_power1. The admitted unsquared inverse bound
is3(1+1/epsilon), sigma=1/2+epsilon,0<epsilon<=1/2; squared recovery is
9(1+1/epsilon)^2. Their sharper separate fractional orders are1/2 and1,
respectively, but the certificate's ceiling gives the same displayed bound.

Initial values are rho7(1)=rho19(1)=M(1)=1. The finite head1..31 belongs to
each complete output. One fixed circuit must have, for cofinally many integers

This genuine all-scale premise, with the same-source inverse and full head,

Finite passes, including baseline passes, do not establish that premise.

2. Actual-prime phase: exact shifted product, with zero case retained

For prime p let ell=floor(sqrt(p)), r=p-ell. One has1<=ell<p, r>=1 and
gcd(p,r)=gcd(p,ell)=1. Since mu(p)=-1,

  mu(pr)=-mu(r), mu(pr)^2=mu(r)^2.

For a ternary value x, source_sign(x)=1+x-x^2. Consequently

  chi(p)=1+mu(r)-mu(r)^2
        =1-mu(r)^2-mu(p(p-floor(sqrt(p)))).            (1)

The squarefree gate and the odd signed term are both required. In particular
chi=+1 when r is nonsquarefree; replacing source_sign by mu(r) would create
a zero-valued mask and change the reversible kernel.

The root band ell^2<=p<(ell+1)^2 places r in
[ell^2-ell,ell^2+ell], with gcd(r,ell)=1. On an odd-prime band with ell odd,
r is even: if4 divides r then chi=+1; otherwise r=2v with v odd and
 chi(p)=1-mu(v)-mu(v)^2.
For ell even, r is odd and no such even-factor reduction applies. These are
actual arithmetic restrictions, not a distribution or independence theorem.

Identity (1) uses mu at a product beyond the saved finite source horizon.
It is an all-positive-integer analytical identity, not a license to substitute
that query into the frozen finite evaluator without its full source domain.
The existing evaluator correctly uses the original below-p index r.

Unlike a prime-domain query mu(p), this phase is not identically-1. Its global
role concerns Mobius along a prime and variable square-root shift, together
with the squarefree complement. No bound for the resulting weighted divisor
correlations follows just from the coprime product law.

3. Bit reversal: exact binary transport and complements

For n=m+r in a dyadic block,
 pi(2n)=m+pi(n), pi(2n+1)=2m+pi(n). Thus bit reversal exchanges low binary
digits with the location in child half-blocks. For k>=1,
 pi(n) is even exactly when0<=r<m/2, and an odd original n maps to the
upper half of its block. In particular an odd prime p in[m,2m) has
pi(p)>=3m/2, though pi(p) need not be prime or squarefree.

Let D_d f(n)=1_{d|n}f(n/d). For0<=j<=k, exact bit arithmetic gives

  (U D_(2^j) U f)(m+r)
     =1_{r<m/2^j} f(m/2^j+r).                        (2)

For j>k it is zero on this block. Proof: pi(n) is divisible by2^j exactly
when the highest j bits of r are0. Dividing removes the corresponding low
zero bits in rev_k(r); reversing within the resulting lower block gives
m/2^j+r. The complementary part r>=m/2^j is zero because that divisor path
is absent, not because a Mobius sum was discarded. At j=0 (2) is identity.

This is a generic conjugation certificate: sparse2-adic divisibility becomes
a contiguous same-offset embedding from a lower block. It does not identify
mu at the two translated indices. For actual M their difference is the full
signed interval sum between m/2^j+r and m+r, with length m-m/2^j. A trivial
bound for that interval is macroscopic and is not subpower padding.

Since chi(2)=source_sign(mu(1))=+1, the2-adic factor of A is
 sum_{j>=0}h_j D_(2^j), h_j=binom(2j,j)/4^j>0.
After conjugation it is the positive sum of the initial-segment copies (2).
Odd-divisor factors and their complements remain; this2-adic decomposition
does not erase them. B has2-adic coefficients b_0=1 and, for j>=1,
 b_j=(-1)^(j+1)h_j/(2j-1).
Thus19 alternates the lower-segment copies in its outer conjugated factor,
while its inner A still uses the original divisibility paths. They cannot
be merged through U as though both acted on the same divisor sets.

4. Exact prime and prime-power effects in the selected outputs

The positive kernel has
 a(p^l)=h_l chi(p)^l,
whereas b(p^l)=(-chi(p))^l(-1/2)_l/l!. In particular
 a(p)=b(p)=chi(p)/2, a(p^2)=3/8, b(p^2)=-1/8,
 a(p^3)=5chi(p)/16, b(p^3)=chi(p)/16.

At a prime p,

  rho7(p)=M(pi(p))+chi(p)/2,
  rho19(p)=rho7(p)+(L_a M)(pi(p)).                    (3)

The extra term is the COMPLETE proper-divisor sum at the bit-reversed
coordinate: sum_{e|pi(p),e>1}a(e)M(pi(p)/e). If pi(p) is composite this
includes every complementary divisor. Primality of p says nothing about
primality of pi(p), so replacing it with a single prime contribution is false.
The source identity (1) only controls the bounded outer prime contribution
in (3); it does not control the fresh M(pi(p)) or this lower-prefix channel.

On a prime-power output,7 has the exact formula
 rho7(p^k)=sum_{l=0}^k h_l chi(p)^l M(pi(p^(k-l))).
For19 the inner divisor set is that of pi(p^(k-l)), not generally another
power of p. On powers of TWO, however, pi fixes every coordinate2^j, and
the entire intervening permutation disappears from this chain. Hence

  rho7(2^k)=sum_{l=0}^k h_l M(2^(k-l)),
  rho19(2^k)=sum_{l=0}^k h_floor(l/2) M(2^(k-l)).      (4)

For the second formula the local generating product is
 (1-x)^(-1/2)(1+x)^(1/2)=(1-x)^(-1)(1-x^2)^(1/2);
the coefficient at degree l is h_floor(l/2), by the elementary partial
binomial-sum identity. All coefficients in (4) are positive. Thus there is
no source-sign damping on this dyadic-prime chain. Actual M values may of
course have different signs; no sign assumption is made. The chain is thin
and neither proves nor refutes the full dyadic moment gate.

5. Full same-block/lower-block decomposition, explicitly for7 and19

For a block B_m=[m,2m), define q(n)=M(pi(n)). Deleting all convolutions from
either raw program leaves precisely U, without normalizing or changing its
moment coordinates. Therefore its diagonal energy is EXACTLY E_M(m).

The complete lower-coordinate corrections are

  G7=L_a U M,
  G19=U L_a M+L_b U M+L_b U L_a M.                   (5)

Every proper divisor input is below the current dyadic block, and U preserves
that lower block. Thus both G values on B_m are linear combinations of
prefix coordinates M(u),u<m. Their coefficients can still query fixed fresh
source values through chi; this is not a claim of dependence only on past
SOURCE information. Explicitly,

 G7(n)=sum_{d|n,d>1}a(d)M(pi(n/d)),
 G19(n)=sum_{e|pi(n),e>1}a(e)M(pi(n)/e)
      +sum_{d|n,d>1}b(d)M(pi(n/d))
      +sum_{d|n,d>1}b(d)sum_{e|pi(n/d),e>1}
                                  a(e)M(pi(n/d)/e). (6)

No path denominator is multiplied across a changed permutation index.
Let L_i=m^-2 sum G_i(n)^2 and C_i=2m^-2 sum q(n)G_i(n). Then

  D_i(m)=E_M(m)+L_i(m)+C_i(m).                       (7)

Bit reversal is involutive, so changing coordinates n=pi(v) gives an
equivalent original-coordinate correction H_i(v)=G_i(pi(v)) and
 D_i=m^-2 sum_{v in B_m}[M(v)+H_i(v)]^2.
In particular7's complete fresh/lower correlation is

 C7=2m^-2 sum_{v in B_m,1<=u<m, pi(u)|pi(v),pi(u)<pi(v)}
                       a(pi(v)/pi(u))M(v)M(u).       (8)

This is an exact rearranged-divisibility constraint, with an actual signed
kernel and every proper divisor retained. It is a potential proof target,
not evidence that bit reversal respects multiplicative arithmetic of mu.

For either selected candidate the precise required signed inequality is

  2sum_{B_m} M(pi(n))G_i(n)
   <=-sum_{B_m}M(pi(n))^2-sum_{B_m}G_i(n)^2
                         +m^2 Cq ceil(m^(1/q)),      (9)

uniformly over dyadic m>=32 for one fixed program and cofinally many q.
A vanishing cross term, small G or a large absolute-path envelope cancellation
ratio does not pay the retained fresh energy in (9). Finite C signs or ratios
may diagnose (7) but do not establish or refute the existence of unknown
global Cq. No supplied finite result is promoted here.

6. Candidate19: ordinary Euler folding and the full commutator defect

For any unit prime source chi, the generic exact identity is

 T_alpha,chi T_beta,-chi=T_alpha-beta,chi Q_beta,
 (Q_beta f)(n)=sum_{e^2|n}a_beta,1(e)f(n/e^2).        (10)

It follows from (1-chi x)^(-alpha)(1+chi x)^(-beta)
 =(1-chi x)^(-(alpha-beta))(1-x^2)^(-beta) primewise.
Without U the selected parameters yield C=B A=T_1,chi Q_-1/2. Its local
coefficients are c(p^l)=chi(p)^l h_floor(l/2). Square support belongs to
Q, not the full C: C retains every squarefree prime-source contribution.
Unlike the same-order opposite-source product, (10) does NOT erase chi here.

With the actual U retained,

  rho19=U C M+Delta,
  Delta=[B,U]A M,
  [B,U]y(n)=sum_{d|n}b(d)y(pi(n/d))
             -sum_{e|pi(n)}b(e)y(pi(n)/e).           (11)

The divisor1 contributions cancel, so Delta is strictly lower-block. The
two proper-divisor domains can have overlaps and unmatched complements;
both displayed sums are part of the identity. At a prime p, for y=A M,
 Delta(p)=chi(p)/2-sum_{e|pi(p),e>1}b(e)y(pi(p)/e).
This exactly cancels the corresponding proper B sum in U C M, returning
the prime formula (3), while leaving its entire inner A complement.

Terminally applying U preserves full dyadic energy, so the complete square
can also be written
 D19=m^-2 sum_{B_m}[C M+U Delta]^2.
It contains the full C square,2(C M)(U Delta), and Delta^2. Deleting Delta
returns the folded observer, not the selected one; adding an opposite defect
as a separate nonnegative square leaves squared-complement costs. Neither
operation supplies an independent subpower estimate for M.

The square factor Q has norm and inverse <=zeta(2+2epsilon)^1/2, finite even
at the critical weight. The remaining T_1,chi still costs zeta(1+epsilon),
and the noncommutator-free19 requires its full proved stage inverse. This
better understood square factor does not make the signed defect negligible.

7. Concrete obstructions to plausible closures

First, a phase prime identity cannot cancel an output's fresh M coordinate
just by modifying the bounded contribution in (3). Prime-to-bit-reversed
coordinate transport is not multiplication by p; the complete interval
 M(pi(n))-M(n)=signed sum of mu between n and pi(n)
must be kept. Bit reversal has macroscopic displacement: for r=1,
 rev_k(r)=m/2, so |pi(m+1)-(m+1)|=m/2-1. The universal bounded-increment
padding estimate is then only O(m), not subpower. This statement is a bound
on available trivial reasoning, not an assertion of actual interval growth.

Second, for any fixed coefficient source, an unrestricted input supported
only in B_m has no lower-coordinate correction in (5). Its output on B_m is
just U times that input for either circuit, with exactly the original energy.
Thus source-free operator norms or lower-coordinate bounds alone cannot
prove the desired moment. An actual-source argument must exploit the
correlations between the complete fresh prefix and (6).

Third, the source-one control makes7 particularly explicit: chi=+1 at every
prime, every a(d)>0, and M_one(n)=n. Consequently rho7_one(n)>=pi(n)>0,
and D7_one(m)>=E_one(m), which grows linearly. For19, whether or not individual
coefficients cancel, its fixed same-source inverse forbids cofinal moments
on that coherent input: H_sigma(n)=sum n^(1-2sigma) diverges for1/2<sigma<=1.
These are symbolic all-scale conclusions, not numerical control evaluations.

The same broad-class obstruction can agree with actual mu through any finite
horizon. For finite primes P containing2 set f_P(p)=-1 on P,+1 outside P,
extend multiplicatively on squarefree integers and set nonsquarefree values0.
It matches mu whenever all prime factors lie in P. If P includes every prime
<=H, all source queries and outputs of the selected words through closed H
match actual mu, with their own fixed source phases consistently bound.
But elementary squarefree counting gives

  sum_{n<=N}f_P(n)=c_P N+O_P(sqrt(N)),
  c_P=[product_{p in P}(1-1/p)]^2
                         product_{p notinP}(1-1/p^2)>0.

To derive this, count squarefree integers coprime to D=product_P p using
sum_{k^2|n}mu(k), then sum their counts with mu(d),d|D. The absolute square
divisor tail and finite coprimality errors are O_P(sqrt(N)); no Mertens
estimate is used. Every fixed reversible circuit on this source must fail
cofinal moments by inverse recovery of its linearly growing prefix.

This proves a limitation of local-law/finite-calibration reasoning. It does
not refute actual-mu estimates: f_P lacks mu(p)=-1 for EVERY prime, so an
argument using that global law, such as (1), is outside the countermodel's
full hypotheses. What remains missing is a uniform signed estimate using
that source-specific information strongly enough to close (8),(9) or(11).

8. Concrete generic structural/proof hooks and final status

The proved identities can be parameterized rather than hardcoded to IDs:
 - bit-reversal conjugation of2-adic dilation is exactly (2), exposing lower

   specify the complete signed proof obligation in its actual coordinates.

These are analytic reductions and obstruction certificates, not a new score,
a ranking, or a theorem of advantageous source footprint. No cofinal bound
