MIXED-CUTOFF CONVOLUTION AND ALL-SCALE PRICING REVIEW 069
=======================================================

Scope and disposition
---------------------
This is a source-only review. It reads MOBIUS_FLOOR_CONTROL_HANDOFF.txt,
MOBIUS_SIGNED_LAG_HANDOFF.txt, the complementary-convolution review066,
and the source constructors mobius_sparse_prefix_066.py,
mobius_signed_lag_067.py and mobius_floor_control_068.py. It writes only
this review. The newly landed mobius_convolution_scale_069.py was also
read as source, without importing or executing it. No scientific result
array, saved target value, numerical study, LP, hash, old scientific
runner or old test was evaluated.

The proposed grammar is mathematically valid for ordinary mu. Its exact
mixed-cutoff identity represents a radius-R row using signed arithmetic
queries only through U=max_i u_i whenever product_i(u_i+1)>R. It gives
a genuine radius reduction when U<R and also raises the polynomial
degree. This information reduction is not an estimate for
the resulting signed polynomial. It does not prove RH or the raw head.

Two positive pricing backends are supplied below, with exact finite
certification procedures and all their premises exposed. A new explicit
actual-source calculation proves that plain absolute double-Abel pricing
and unsigned weighted Cauchy/Gram pricing have linear cost on an admitted
joined row, even when a square-root Mertens premise is provisionally
available. Extracting the unit and paying its center separately also has
a linear barrier. These conclusions concern these proof prices, not the
signed row or RH. Coupled signed contractions remain possible and unpaid.

1. The same source, including joins and direct coefficients
-----------------------------------------------------------
For the registered p<q and N>=2, let D=floor(4N/p), r in[p/q,1], and

 W_b(d)=floor(4N/(bd))-3floor(2N/(bd))+2floor(N/(bd)),
 f_y(d)=1[d<=y]*(W_p(d)-r W_q(d)),
 g(d)=-1[d>y]*(W_p(d)-r W_q(d)).

All these functions are on positive integers, and g vanishes for d>D.
As in the admitted source, |g|<=2. Coalesce the signed step dictionary

 a_T=g(T)-g(T+1), 1<=T<=D,
 g(d)=sum_T a_T 1[d<=T].

The symbol a_T here denotes a source step coefficient; the arithmetic
cutoff functions below are denoted A_i to avoid identifying them.
Coalesce the signed six-floor coefficients f_Z from both channels, with
Z=floor(vN/b), (v,c)=(4,1),(2,-3),(1,2). Then f_y is the full six-floor
combination plus g. Applying the actual ordinary-mu inverse and taking
the adjacent row difference gives precisely

 c_j=d_j+L_j(mu),  d_j=f_j,
 L_j(mu)=sum_(n>=1) mu(n) kappa_j(n),
 kappa_j(n)=g(jn)-g((j+1)n),  |kappa_j(n)|<=4.           (1)

For a full floor atom, the inverse is a step and its row difference is
the atom at its horizon; this is why d_j=f_j rather than an additional
unpriced convolution. Uniformly sum_j|d_j|<=12. The source bound can be
smaller after joining, but 12 is a safe paid value for both endpoints.

For every T add a_T at A=floor(T/j) and -a_T at
A=floor(T/(j+1)); delete A=0 and coalesce equal A. Denote the resulting
signed coefficients h_(j,A). Then

 kappa_j(n)=sum_A h_(j,A) 1[n<=A].                      (2)

The integer row radius R_j may be D//j or the smaller maximum A after
coalescing. An empty row has L_j=0. No absolute value is taken before
these joins. Rational r is handled with its exact common denominator.

The new grammar acts on (1), not the old core_inverse fill. It must keep
the same ordinary mu across channels, rows, factors and moments. The
square-root raw head has H=floor(sqrt N) and
1<=j<ceil(H/2). Endpoint convexity of its absolute norm is available;
it does not license endpoint convexity of an indefinite correlation.

2. Exact all-scale mixed-cutoff identity
---------------------------------------
Let delta(1)=1, delta(n)=0 for n>1, and One(n)=1 for every n>=1.
Dirichlet convolution is

 (v*w)(n)=sum_(ab=n) v(a)w(b).

It is associative and commutative. The complete ordinary-mu identity is
mu*One=delta. One^0 means delta, not the constant sequence One.
For k>=1 and integer cuts u_i>=0, put

 A_i(n)=mu(n)1[n<=u_i],
 B_i(n)=mu(n)-A_i(n)=mu(n)1[n>=u_i+1].

Write A_S=*_(i in S) A_i. Expansion gives

 B_1*...*B_k*One^(k-1)
   =mu+sum_(nonempty S subset {1,...,k})
           (-1)^|S| A_S*One^(|S|-1).                  (3)

Proof: a subset S in the expanded product has k-|S| copies of mu.
For S empty, mu^k*One^(k-1)=mu. For |S|>=1, cancel each of the
k-|S| copies of mu with a copy of One. There remain |S|-1 copies
of One. This also covers S equal to the full set, with no mu left.
All operations are finite on each coefficient; no convergence or
analytic continuation is used.

Every nonzero coefficient of B_1*...*B_k starts at
P=product_i(u_i+1). Multiplication by One^(k-1), whose arguments are
positive integers, cannot reduce support. Therefore, if

                    P>R,                              (4)

then for EVERY integer 1<=n<=R,

 mu(n)=sum_(nonempty S)(-1)^(|S|+1)
                       (A_S*One^(|S|-1))(n).           (5)

The strict inequality in (4) is essential. At P=R the product of first
long arguments is in the queried interval; it need not have zero mu.
For example, k=2,u_1=u_2=1,R=4 leaves the coefficient B_1*B_2(4)=1.
One may replace (4) by a separately proved stronger support gate using
actual first nonzero long arguments. Such a replacement must identify
and pay its additional arithmetic queries. The integer support gate
(4) requires no signs and works even when some first arguments are
squareful. It is sufficient, not necessary for accidental vanishing.

No mu(0) is introduced. If a cut is zero, its A_i is zero, so the
corresponding terms vanish, and (3)-(5) remain true. For k=1, (4) says
u_1>=R, and the rule is simply the original short sequence; it gives no
support reduction. Empty row radii require no cutoff identity.

The coefficients in (3) are not arbitrary guesses: generic algebraic
coefficient synthesis can discover these signs, but the independent
certificate must prove the coefficient identity before the support
gate is used. Rational linear combinations of valid identities remain
valid with all residual supports and coefficients included. A least
squares relation at a finite collection of mu values is not this proof.

3. What the reduced-radius polynomial actually is
-------------------------------------------------
Let kappa have support in [1,R] and define d_r=One^r. In particular
d_0=delta, d_1=One; for r>=1, d_r(m) counts ordered factorizations of m
into r positive integers. For a nonempty S of size s, let

 K_s(t)=sum_(m<=R/t) d_(s-1)(m) kappa(tm).

For s=1 this is kappa(t), because d_0 has only its unit value. Then (5)
gives the exact functional identity

 L(mu)=sum_(nonempty S)(-1)^(s+1)
       sum_(a_i<=u_i, i in S) [product_(i in S)mu(a_i)]
                              K_s(product_(i in S)a_i). (6)

Only mu values at indices <=U=max_i u_i are queried. The source kernels
are evaluated at products through R. These geometry queries are not
additional mu queries. If all cuts equal u, (6) can be grouped by size,
with coefficients (-1)^(s+1) binomial(k,s); their absolute coefficient
mass is 2^k-1. Grouping does not remove degree or cancellation obligations.

Repeated factors are allowed and necessary. In particular,
mu(a)mu(b) equals mu(ab) only when gcd(a,b)=1. A shared prime can give
mu(a)mu(b)!=0 while mu(ab)=0. Deleting noncoprime pairs or imposing
squarefreeness of the product changes (6). Squareful individual factors
have mu(a)=0 and may be deleted; equal indices satisfy
mu(a)^2=1[a squarefree]. More generally, repeated appearances of an
index reduce by mu(a)^(2h)=1[a squarefree] and
mu(a)^(2h+1)=mu(a). These are exact reductions of the polynomial, not
independent sign replacements. All surviving signs are shared.

The ordinary-mu unit mu(1)=1 must be retained. Its extraction creates
lower-degree terms and deterministic constants. Those constants need
not be small; pricing them separately can destroy the main cancellation.
Sections 5 and 9 give the exact bookkeeping and an actual-source barrier.

For k=2, set u=u_1,v=u_2 and K=K_2. Then

 L(mu)=sum_(a<=u)mu(a)kappa(a)+sum_(b<=v)mu(b)kappa(b)
                      -sum_(a<=u,b<=v)mu(a)mu(b)K(ab). (7)

For the joined row (2),

             K_j(t)=sum_A h_(j,A) floor(A/t).           (8)

Equation (8) includes all floor endpoints, both source channels and the
j,j+1 difference before any pricing. It is also equal to
sum_m[g(jtm)-g((j+1)tm)]. A bilinear backend should bind its kernel to
both representations, rather than optimize the channels independently.

4. Rational splits, arithmetic radius and work
----------------------------------------------
Any nonnegative rational exponents and positive rational scale factors
may propose cuts u_i=floor(lambda_i R^theta_i). The proof gate is still
the exact integer product (4), not a rounded sum of exponents.
For theta=p/q and lambda=a/b in reduced form, the integer threshold
u<=lambda R^theta is decided by

                    b^q u^q<=a^q R^p.

Thus integer roots suffice; floats are unnecessary. Integer bit budgets,
cut counts, polynomial degree, coefficient height and work caps are
implementation restrictions, not mathematical restrictions on rational
denominators. A fixed whitelist cannot represent a cofinal exponent
family unless the selected family is separately shown sufficient.

As a sufficient design rule, if sum_i theta_i>=1 and
product_i lambda_i>=1, then product_i(floor(lambda_i R^theta_i)+1)>R
for every R>=1. At equality of the exponent sum, factors below one can
fail eventually; the exact gate is authoritative. For k=2, the pair
floor(R^theta),floor(R^(1-theta)) always passes the gate, for any
rational theta in[0,1]. Its query exponent is max(theta,1-theta), whose
minimum is 1/2. A tiny split theta does not make BOTH queries tiny.

For fixed k, any cuts passing (4) satisfy (U+1)^k>R. The minimum possible
integer U is floor(R^(1/k)), attained by equal cuts. This is a real
query-radius improvement. Ordinary mu through that smaller radius can
be computed exactly by a sieve or reconstructed from prime factors.
For k=2 the familiar short-factor information is enough to reconstruct
the entire finite table through R; this does not supply a cancellation
estimate for its sums. Full prime or multiplicative information is
arithmetic determination, not a growth bound.

A conservative streamed evaluation of a size-s term in (6) enumerates
s short factors and s-1 unrestricted One factors, hence at most

       R*(1+log R)^(2s-2)

ordered positive tuples. To see this, the number of r-factor tuples of
product <=R is at most R*(sum_(n<=R)1/n)^(r-1), by summing the final
factor as a floor. The restrictions on short factors only reduce this
count. One may therefore charge O(2^k R(1+log R)^(2k-2)) arithmetic
operations per row, times declared exact coefficient/bit costs and source
evaluation costs. This is deliberately a safe bound, not an optimized
all-scale algorithm. Streamed space is O(U+k) plus source storage.

For k=2, if E is the number of coalesced endpoints, (8) computes each
kernel value in O(E) exact floor/rational operations. There are at most
min(uv,R*(1+log R)) active pairs. A direct grid costs O(Euv) and can
stream with O(v) row storage. Balanced cuts have uv<=R, so this is not
an intrinsic quadratic-in-R job. Coalescing equal products or quotient
events can improve cost, but must be checked independently. For many
rows, charge the sum of their actual radii and endpoint counts; do not
equate the maximum short query radius with total work or output size.

If mu values are used only by a final evaluator, the synthesizer can
construct (3)-(8) from source geometry alone. Prefix premises below are
symbolic inputs and must be marked conditional until certified. Computing
the smaller signed prefixes to evaluate a finite polynomial does not
certify their all-scale Mertens premise.

5. Unit, diagonal and deterministic center for the bilinear rule
----------------------------------------------------------------
For u,v>=1, split the bilinear term B in (7) as

 B=K(1)+sum_(2<=a<=u)mu(a)K(a)+sum_(2<=b<=v)mu(b)K(b)
       +D+O,
 D=sum_(2<=a<=min(u,v))1[a squarefree] K(a^2),
 O=sum_(2<=a<=u,2<=b<=v,a!=b)mu(a)mu(b)K(ab).

Consequently the complete raw coefficient is

 c=d+2kappa(1)-K(1)-D
     +sum_(2<=a<=u)mu(a)[kappa(a)-K(a)]
     +sum_(2<=b<=v)mu(b)[kappa(b)-K(b)]-O.              (9)

The unit corner is counted once in B, not twice; each linear sum has its
own copy of kappa(1). The diagonal limit is min(u,v), not max(u,v).
No coprimality simplification has been assumed. If u=v the two linear
sums combine with coefficient 2, and off-diagonal pairs may be paired
as 2 times a<b. If cuts differ, a pair present on only one side is not
silently doubled. If either cut is zero, use (7) directly with its empty
sum; formula (9) presumes both contain the unit.

All deterministic values in (9) can be computed without mu signs, using
an unsigned squarefree mask. Exact computation does not establish a small
asymptotic bound for d+2kappa(1)-K(1)-D. It is generally unsafe to pay
that center and the signed pieces by a separate triangle inequality.

The same warning applies when replacing the diagonal with a cheaper
upper bound. In a signed expansion D is not a nonnegative energy: K(a^2)
can have either sign. By contrast the diagonal D_I in the067 correlation
identity is genuinely nonnegative; it is a different object.

6. Exact prefix summation by parts as a conditional positive backend
--------------------------------------------------------------------
For a finite sequence w on 1..u, extend w(u+1)=0. Then

 sum_(a<=u)mu(a)w(a)=sum_(s<=u)M(s)[w(s)-w(s+1)],       (10)
 M(s)=sum_(a<=s)mu(a), M(0)=0.

For the bilinear term define F(a,b)=K(ab) inside
1<=a<=u,1<=b<=v, and F=0 whenever a=u+1 or b=v+1. K itself is zero
after its true support. The rectangle padding is additional and essential:
K((u+1)b) may be nonzero and is nevertheless outside the short sum.
Set

 Delta F(s,t)=F(s,t)-F(s+1,t)-F(s,t+1)+F(s+1,t+1).

Applying (10) twice proves exactly

           B=sum_(s<=u,t<=v)M(s)M(t) Delta F(s,t).      (11)

This is valid at unequal cuts and with all four boundary cases. Products
larger than R have zero source kernel, not an omitted free sign. Endpoint
floor conventions are floor(A/t), consistent with the inclusive source
indicator. In particular one must use the zero-padded F, rather than
differentiate the unrestricted K(ab) at the top/right boundary.

Suppose independently certified nonnegative numbers p_s bound |M(s)|,
for all s<=U=max(u,v). Then

 |B|<=V(p;F):=sum_(s<=u,t<=v)p_s p_t |Delta F(s,t)|.    (12)

A transparent RH-strength-shaped premise is

 |M(s)|<=C s^sigma, 1<=s<=U, sigma=1/2+eta, eta>0.     (13)

Under this premise (12) becomes C^2 V_sigma(F), where
V_sigma(F)=sum s^sigma t^sigma|Delta F(s,t)|. The two short linear
terms in (7) have prices C times their weighted first differences.
Thus a safe price for c is |d|+C V_sigma(w_u)+C V_sigma(w_v)
+C^2 V_sigma(F), with w_u,w_v the zero-padded short kappa sequences.
This proves a finite implication. Neither C nor (13) is supplied by
ordinary-mu multiplicativity or by the support gate.

One may use p_s=min(s,C s^sigma), because |M(s)|<=s is unconditional.
One may also insert independently certified exact small prefix bounds.
Using mu(1)=1 gives the exact first prefix. If the unit has been removed,
the prefix of mu on 2..s is M(s)-1; it is NOT M(s). A safe price is
min(s-1,C s^sigma+1). Off-diagonal removal replaces F with its
diagonal-deleted kernel and changes all mixed differences; it cannot
reuse (12) for the unchanged F while dropping the diagonal contribution.

For degree s in (6), repeated Abel summation gives a product of s
prefixes multiplying the s-fold mixed difference of the zero-padded
kernel. The premise (13) then produces C^s times its positive weighted
variation. This is an exact generalization, but its coefficients and
constants can grow with s and the mixed-cutoff geometry.

Exact finite implementation need not evaluate irrational powers. For
sigma=p/q>0 and an integer grid G, choose rational upper weights

 w_s=ceil_root_q(s^p G^q)/G,  w_s>=s^sigma,
 0<=w_s-s^sigma<1/G.

An integer q-th-root comparison certifies this. The price using w_s w_t
is rational when C and source coefficients are rational. Relative to
the ideal price, its rounding excess is at most

 C^2 sum_(s,t)[(s^sigma+t^sigma)/G+1/G^2]
                                           |Delta F(s,t)|. (14)

This excess must be paid in an all-scale statement. A fixed numerical
grid supplies exact finite bounds, but does not automatically preserve
an unproved ideal asymptotic exponent. Use integer/bit/work caps before
building the grid; (11)-(14) give a valid zero-optimizer backend.
Float LPs are neither required nor an oracle for its premises.

7. Weighted Cauchy and Gram alternatives
----------------------------------------
Let F_(a,b)=K(ab) on the short rectangle; delete squareful individual
indices if desired. Choose positive rational weights alpha_a,beta_b,
and define known unsigned masses

 A_alpha=sum_(a<=u)alpha_a 1[a squarefree],
 B_beta=sum_(b<=v)beta_b 1[b squarefree],
 H=sum_(a<=u,b<=v)|F_(a,b)|^2/(alpha_a beta_b).

Weighted Cauchy proves

                   |B|<=sqrt(A_alpha B_beta H).        (15)

This requires no Mertens premise. An exact rational square-root upper
enclosure turns it into a finite positive price. Cauchy uses the same
ordinary mu in the actual expression; relaxing it for the inequality
does not imply the source identity for arbitrary independent signs.

A sharper sufficient Gram certificate is

 F diag(beta)^(-1) F^T <= lambda diag(alpha), lambda>=0 (16)

in positive-semidefinite order. It yields
|B|<=sqrt(lambda A_alpha B_beta). With rational data, an exact PSD
certificate or checked rational LDL decomposition with the necessary
zero-pivot consistency proves (16); floating eigenvalues do not.
The matrix-size, sparsity and bit costs must be charged. It is often
possible to stream the Frobenius sum (15), whereas a dense Gram costs
O(u^2 v) arithmetic operations and O(u^2) storage before exact checking.

When cuts overlap, an alternative is to pad to U, symmetrize the joined
quadratic kernel and keep one common vector x_a=mu(a). A diagonal
weight domination of both signs of that symmetric form yields a price
based on sum w_a mu(a)^2. A tighter certificate may retain the linear
terms and the known unit in an augmented quadratic form. Such a coupled
certificate is legitimate only with its exact PSD and boundary proof.
The unsigned diagonal mass is a paid quantity; a signed energy such as
sum_a |sum_b mu(b)F_(a,b)|^2/alpha_a is an additional arithmetic leaf
unless independently bounded. It must not be called paid merely because
it is nonnegative or evaluated exactly.

8. Universal paid pieces and the limit of the triangle price
-------------------------------------------------------------
The source offers the elementary radius and amplitude bounds

 |sum_(a<=u_i)mu(a)kappa_j(a)|<=4 min(u_i,R_j).

For equal cuts floor(R_j^(1/k)), the degree-one contribution to the
square-root head is at most a constant depending on k times

 D^(1/k) H^(1-1/k)=O_k(N^(1/2+1/(2k)))  (k>1).         (17)

Indeed sum_(j<=H)j^(-1/k)<=1+H^(1-1/k)/(1-1/k).
This is a genuine unconditional partial payment. A cofinal family
k->infinity makes its loss arbitrarily small for any prescribed epsilon,
with k fixed after epsilon is chosen. For k=2 its cost is N^(3/4), so
balanced bilinear support reduction alone does not pay the full head.
All degree>=2 terms, units created by their expansion, and their signed
cross cancellation still require control. Constants 2^k and C^k are
allowed to depend on epsilon for fixed k, but cannot be silently uniform
in k or R when the search chooses growing degree.

Let M1=sum_T|a_T|T, for which the admitted source has the uniform
M1=O(N log(2N)) bound. For t>=1, the source windows imply

 sum_(j in head)|K_j(t)|
 <=sum_T|a_T| sum_j[floor(T/(jt))-floor(T/((j+1)t))]
 <=M1/t.                                               (18)

The positive differences telescope; extending the head only increases
the majorant. Therefore an absolute bilinear factor price gives at most
M1*(sum_(a<=u)1/a)*(sum_(b<=v)1/b), or N times logarithms rather than
square root. Using actual unequal row cuts only reduces this upper bound.
It does not turn it into the desired exponent. For higher degree,
|K_s(t)|<=4*#ordered(s-1)-factor tuples of product<=R/t; positive
factor-count prices are again of order R with logarithmic factors.

The already paid067 energy diagonal and fixed near-lag envelopes remain
valid for the same source after (6). For a dyadic row block I=[L,2L-1]
clipped to the head, they give

 D_I<=4M1/L, |R_I(h)|<=4M1/L,
 E_I<= (4+8B)M1/L+(2 sum_(h>B)R_I(h))_+.

At B=0 the paid diagonal head transfer is
O(sqrt N log(2N)^(3/2)). The far positive part is the explicit unpaid
leaf. Nothing in the new convolution identity changes that moment or
removes the leaf. It is useful to retain this genuinely paid component
as a comparator to any new positive price.

9. An actual joined-source barrier for the plain new backends
------------------------------------------------------------
Use the admitted family p=3,q=5,r=1,N=15k,y=16k,D=20k, with integer
k>=1, at row j=1. Directly from the joined bounded W source,

 kappa(n)=1[8k<n<=10k]-1[16k<n<=20k], d_1=0.           (19)

For example g itself equals -1 on (16k,20k] and zero elsewhere: on
(16k,20k], W_3=1 and W_5=0; outside the tail g is zero. Its adjacent
row difference gives (19), with inclusive right endpoints. Thus

 K(t)=floor(10k/t)-floor(8k/t)-floor(20k/t)+floor(16k/t),
 K(1)=-2k, K(2)=-k, K(4)=-ceil(k/2), kappa(1)=0.       (20)

These are floor calculations from the source, not evaluations of mu.

(a) Plain double Abel. If u,v>=2, the (1,1) mixed coefficient of the
zero-padded rectangle is its interior coefficient

 Delta F(1,1)=K(1)-2K(2)+K(4)=-ceil(k/2).

Therefore V_sigma(F)>=ceil(k/2) for EVERY sigma. Under premise (13),
C>=1 because M(1)=1, and the bound C^2 V_sigma(F) is linear in k.
Even substituting the exact prefix bound p_1=1 leaves this linear
contribution in (12). A square-root Mertens premise does not repair
the plain termwise-absolute double-Abel price. The problem is a large
signed geometric coefficient whose cancellation with other prefix
terms is discarded. Adding the two separate linear prices cannot lower
the bound. Any implementation claiming this plain backend has an
all-scale square-root cost on all required joined rows is false.

(b) Unsigned weighted Cauchy/Gram. In (15), squarefree unit mass gives
A_alpha>=alpha_1 and B_beta>=beta_1, while
H>=K(1)^2/(alpha_1 beta_1). Thus the price is >=|K(1)|=2k for EVERY
positive choice of weights. In (16), its first diagonal entry gives
lambda alpha_1>=K(1)^2/beta_1, so its resulting price is also >=2k.
Likewise an absolute operator domination of the symmetric form includes
its unit diagonal. These barriers apply to the stated unsigned prices;
they do not rule out a coupled augmented certificate using actual
arithmetic constraints and signed interactions.

(c) Paying an extracted center separately. Formula (9) has center
2k before extracting D. Let A=floor(10k/t)-floor(8k/t)>=0. Since
floor(2x)=2floor(x)+e with e in{0,1},

 K(t)=-A-e_(10k/t)+e_(8k/t)<=1.

Writing m=min(u,v), the extracted diagonal obeys D<=m-1, hence

           center=2k-D>=2k-m+1.                        (21)

For balanced cuts or any m=o(k), this positive center alone is linear.
When the right side is positive its absolute value is at least that
quantity. If m is large the displayed lower bound may be uninformative;
no stronger claim is made. Extracting no diagonal gives the exact center
2k regardless of cuts. Thus the customary separated-center triangle
price loses precisely the cancellation needed in this source family.

For balanced u=v=floor(sqrt(20k)), (4) holds and u,v>=2. For k>=1
these cuts are less than 8k, so BOTH short linear sums in (7) vanish.
The actual signed row is exactly minus the short bilinear polynomial.
Its raw sum is not bounded below by any of the prices above. A large
positive price is a limitation of the proof relaxation, not evidence
that the actual row has linear growth. The polynomial is therefore a
real support-reduced arithmetic object, but these plain backends do not
control it at RH strength.

Exact contraction of a growing low-prefix region, a subtraction that
keeps its resulting center coupled, or an arithmetic signed-energy
certificate could escape the displayed coefficient barriers. Such a
modification must state its new domain, work, precision, remaining price
and uniform bound. It cannot inherit an all-scale rate from (12) or (15).

9A. Stronger barrier after collecting the COMPLETE prefix polynomial
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The conclusion is not confined to pricing the subset terms separately.
Consider compiling all of (6) into the prefix variables M(s), collecting
equal sorted-index monomials, joining the direct atom and every subset
term, and substituting M(1)=1 before any pricing. This is a valid exact
compiler. Its full polynomial still has a scalar-envelope obstruction.

Choose the common fake prefixes

              m(1)=1, m(s)=0 for every s>=2,
              chi(1)=1, chi(2)=-1, chi(n)=0 for n>=3.

Here chi(s)=m(s)-m(s-1). They satisfy |m(s)|<=C s^sigma for every
C>=1,sigma>=0. They also satisfy the unit, |chi|<=1, squareful zeros,
and ordinary coprime multiplicativity. In fact
chi=delta_1-delta_2 is the multiplicative function supported on the
divisors of 2. It has chi(2)=-1, but chi(p)=0 for odd primes, and it
fails chi*One=delta at every odd integer >1. These fake assignments
are not ordinary mu; they are feasible for a relaxation retaining only
the listed scalar/prefix constraints. Shared prefix variables and
collection of all monomials do not exclude them.

For degree 2 and BOTH cuts >=2, the short cutoff sequences on this
assignment are identically chi. At the joined source (19),
L(chi)=kappa(1)-kappa(2)=0. Therefore the COMPLETE polynomial evaluates
to

 2L(chi)-L(chi*chi*One)
       =-[K(1)-2K(2)+K(4)]=ceil(k/2).                  (24)

Consequently ANY certified absolute range upper bound for the complete
polynomial based only on the scalar envelope must be at least ceil(k/2).
This includes an exact optimizer with all cancellations retained; it is
not a defect of triangle pricing, tensor ordering or coefficient rounding.
For sigma=1/2+eta, eta<1/2, (24) rules out a uniform O(k^sigma) price
for this relaxation. The source's actual ordinary-mu value is still
unconstrained by this lower bound on a relaxation's price.

There is also an exact higher-degree expression. Let d denote the
convolution degree, while k continues to denote the source scale in
N=15k. Suppose ALL d cuts are >=2. All their fake A_i equal chi, even
when the cutoffs are unequal. Define

 D_r(x)=sum_(n<=x)d_r(n), with D_0(x)=1[x>=1].

Since
chi^s=sum_(ell=0)^s(-1)^ell binomial(s,ell) delta_(2^ell),
the complete fake response is exactly

 P_d(k)=sum_(s=1)^d(-1)^(s+1)binomial(d,s)
         sum_(ell=0)^s(-1)^ell binomial(s,ell)
          [D_(s-1)(10k/2^ell)-D_(s-1)(8k/2^ell)
           -D_(s-1)(20k/2^ell)+D_(s-1)(16k/2^ell)].    (25)

This formula involves only divisor counts and source endpoints, and
requires no scientific mu evaluation. It holds regardless of which
zero-padded prefix tensors were used to compile the same exact polynomial.
For d=2 it is precisely (24).

For each FIXED d>=3,

 P_d(k)=(-1)^d*2^(1-d)/(d-2)! * k*(log k)^(d-2)
                      +O_d(k*(log(2k))^(d-3)).          (26)

Here and below logarithms are natural. A short elementary proof is
available without an analytic number-theory premise. For r>=2,

 D_r(x)=x*(log x)^(r-1)/(r-1)!
                         +O_r(x*(log(2x))^(r-2)),

whereas D_1(x)=floor x. The r=2 formula follows from
sum_(n<=x)floor(x/n)=x sum_(n<=x)1/n+O(x). For the induction step,
sum D_(r-1)(x/n) and compare the leading harmonic-log sum with
the integral of (log(x/t))^(r-2)/t; the accumulated error is
O_r(x(log(2x))^(r-2)). For a fixed s>=2, inserting this into the bracket
in (25) gives leading factor -2k/(s-2)! times

 sum_(ell=0)^s (-1)^ell binomial(s,ell)/2^ell=2^(-s)

and power (log k)^(s-2). Only s=d contributes to the highest logarithmic
power. Multiplying its coefficient (-1)^(d+1) proves (26). For s=2,
floor errors are O_s(1), which also gives the exact degree-2 refinement.
Thus every fixed degree 2..8, and indeed every fixed d>=2, has a
linear-or-larger response on this feasible fake assignment once all cuts
contain 2. In balanced support reduction that condition holds at all
sufficiently large scales. The signs alternate with d, but an absolute
range upper bound must pay either sign.

Because this assignment sets every variable other than M(1) to zero,
the deterministic constant of the FULLY collected polynomial after
M(1)=1 substitution is exactly P_d(k). Its signed cancellation occurs
at the actual higher prefixes, not within the collected constant. Hence
a price |C0|+sum_(s>=1)A_s(sigma)C^s is explicitly limited by (24)-(26),
even though the compiler correctly collected all subset cancellations.
Keeping the constant syntactically joint does not overcome the scalar
range obstruction. Additional feasible-set information is necessary.

Anchors and arithmetic constraints change the scope. For example an
exact anchor M(3)=-1 excludes this particular fake prefix. One should
not infer that one more anchor proves an all-scale law. For a fixed
finite prime set P, the truncated-prime function

             chi_P=*_(p in P)(delta_1-delta_p)

is again multiplicative, has unit 1 and squareful zeros, and obeys the
ordinary prime values at every p in P. Taking P to contain all primes
<=B makes it agree with ordinary mu through B. It has finite support,
so its prefixes obey |m_P(s)|<=C_P s^sigma with a finite constant C_P
independent of k. Once all cuts include its fixed support, the same
argument as (26) gives, for fixed d>=3,

 P_(d,P)(k)=(-1)^d*2/(d-2)!
           *[product_(p in P)(1-1/p)]^d
           *k*(log k)^(d-2)+O_(d,P)(k*(log(2k))^(d-3)).

For d=2 it gives a nonzero linear leading term with O_P(1) error.
More explicitly, write Q_P=product_(p in P)p and
alpha_P=product_(p in P)(1-1/p). Once both cuts are >=Q_P and
8k>=Q_P, the degree-2 fake response obeys

             |P_(2,P)(k)-2k alpha_P^2|<=4*4^|P|.       (27)

Indeed L(chi_P)=0, K(t)=-2k/t+e_t with |e_t|<=4, and
sum_n chi_P(n)/n=alpha_P. There are 2^|P| nonzero coefficients,
all of absolute value one, so the bilinear error is bounded by the
stated finite pair count. The constant C=2^|P| is always sufficient
for its prefix envelope when sigma>=0. This constant is fixed after
P is chosen and independent of k; it is not uniform in growing P.
The Euler product here is finite and strictly positive. This extended
adversary is feasible only when the proposed scalar-envelope constant
C also covers its finite prefix constant C_P; no claim is made for a
smaller prescribed C. It proves that fixed finitely many prime anchors
plus multiplicativity, as a general class of relaxations, still need
an additional cost argument. Growing anchors or the full complete-floor
arithmetic identities can exclude these functions. They then carry new
work/support dependencies and may eventually encode the target; their
mere correctness does not establish a uniform growth bound.

The genuinely stronger alternatives are coupled ordinary-mu identities,
a signed contraction using them, or a bound on an arithmetic energy that
excludes these fake prefixes with a provable cost. Any general multiplier
or annihilator polynomial grammar should record which extra constraints
are used, prove their finite identities and support, and charge the
resulting center/remainder. Identity synthesis and all-scale positive
price synthesis remain distinct mathematical steps.

10. What would make support reduction a closed induction
---------------------------------------------------------
Support reduction gives smaller argument indices; it is not a norm
contraction. A valid induction must specify all of the following.

* A family of propositions with its exact uniform parameters, including
  every required source prefix, both endpoint ratios, cutoff geometries
  and any auxiliary weighted kernels. If the price uses M(t), the
  smaller-scale Mertens proposition is a separate lemma family unless
  it is already proved as part of a simultaneous induction.
* A well-founded rank. For an R-induction every unresolved arithmetic
  query and invoked lemma must have rank <R; U<R is useful, but an
  encoded source center, a horizon-R Mertens query, or an unbounded
  weighted energy at current scale breaks the purported reduction.
* A finite base interval and independently certified constants.
* A uniform price inequality that preserves a chosen invariant, including
  units, diagonals, direct atoms, polynomial coefficients, support,
  rounding, coefficient height and all source/scale dependences.

For example suppose the desired normalized target at scale R, under
smaller-rank bounds with constant C, has certified price

             |T_R|/R^alpha<=A_R+sum_(s=1)^k B_(s,R)C^s,
             A_R>=0, B_(s,R)>=0.                       (22)

If sup_R A_R<=A and sup_R B_(s,R)<=B_s, a sufficient invariant is

                  C>=A+sum_s B_s C^s,                 (23)

with that SAME C covering every base case and parameter instance.
Then strong induction is valid if all dependencies are smaller-rank
instances of the stated family. Formula (23) is a concrete missing
lemma condition, not something implied by the exact identity.

For a linear price with B_1<1, one may choose
C>=A/(1-B_1), also covering the bases. If B_2>0 is present, taking C
larger can make matters worse. With only degrees one and two,

 B_2 C^2-(1-B_1)C+A<=0.

It requires 1-B_1>0 and a nonnegative discriminant
(1-B_1)^2-4B_2 A, and the admissible interval between the roots must
contain a base-case constant. Higher-degree prices likewise require
an invariant interval or a separate contraction argument. Merely
observing B_2 or a finite ratio below one at a few R does not establish
this uniform inequality. Coefficients in repeated-cut identities can
include binomial(k,s); prefixes contribute C^s, not a single C.

If the target is the raw head norm while the induction premises concern
M, an Mertens induction and the head-transfer estimate must both close.
Small-radius ordinary-mu information alone provides neither. Conversely,
an induction on source head norms must show that its derived kernels
K_s are members of the controlled smaller-scale source family or prove
an additional norm transfer. They are generally new weighted kernels,
not automatically the same N,y source at a smaller N.

The actual-source barriers in section 9 show that (22) with
alpha=1/2+epsilon and these plain prices has an unbounded normalized
coefficient or center for epsilon<1/2. It cannot supply (23) uniformly.
This identifies a specific obstacle to a closed induction, rather than
assuming RH to bound the very leaf the construction creates.

11. Recommended mathematical boundary for the next implementation
------------------------------------------------------------------
The mixed-cutoff grammar is a sound extension of the finite identity
language. Admit it as an exact ordinary-mu rule with support certificate,
coefficient replay, rational split support, source joins, query radius,
degree and complexity metadata. Its finite evaluations are source-bound
arithmetic, not independent uniform upper laws.

The supplied Abel and Gram formulas are valid finite conditional or
unconditional prices, respectively. Preserve their explicit barrier
family in the analytical scope; do not promote their finite successful
prices into a claimed RH-strength all-scale mechanism. In particular,
an extracted unit center must remain coupled if its signed cancellation
is used. A future signed completion must prove the coupling rather than
label a target-valued constant paid.

A substantive new discovery object would be a source-bound coupled
pricing or contraction rule, with a uniform coefficient law and a
well-founded lemma graph meeting (22)-(23), or a directly proved signed
energy/head bound. The useful paid pieces are the direct six-floor mass,
the067 diagonal/near envelope and, for growing fixed degree, the small
linear terms in (17). The higher-degree joined polynomial remains the
arithmetic object requiring a new theorem. The full raw head, far
correlations, filled-core transfer and RH remain OPEN.
