Independent review: arithmetic prefix averaging and target recovery

Initial mathematical verdict

The proposed contraction is valid, including a measurable t-dependent bank
size, complex-valued f, arbitrary admitted zero terms, and finite horizons.
The dilations must be DISTINCT INTEGERS >=2 (or separately satisfy d_j>=j+1).
N(t) must be a finite nonnegative integer wherever the operator is evaluated.
The averaging is over precisely the first N(t) entries of one fixed ordered
sequence. An arbitrary selected subset or a differently normalized weighted
bank is not covered by the stated certificate.

This certifies a recovery operator. It does NOT certify a uniform bound for
the residual, a numerical result, or RH. No numerical responses, source tables,
experiments, tests or hashes were used for this initial proof review.

Operator and truncated norms

Fix distinct integers 2<=d_1<d_2<..., either an infinite sequence or a finite
sequence of length L. In the finite case require N(t)<=L. Let N be measurable
and finite with values in the nonnegative integers. Source-free N is required
for the proposed candidate semantics. The inequality itself holds for any
such fixed N; source-free is not needed by Jensen.

For a measurable complex f set f(u)=0 for 0<u<1, and define

  Af(t)=0 if N(t)=0,
  Af(t)=N(t)^-1 sum_{j=1}^{N(t)} f(t/d_j) otherwise.

For sigma>=1/2 and finite T>=1, use
  ||f||_{sigma,T}^2=integral_1^T |f(t)|^2 t^(-2sigma-1) dt.

For f in this L2 space, Jensen/Cauchy-Schwarz gives pointwise

  |Af(t)|^2<=sum_{j>=1} 1_{N(t)>=j}/N(t) |f(t/d_j)|^2,

where the fraction is defined as0 when the indicator is false. Integrating
nonnegative terms and applying Tonelli, then t=d_j u in each term, gives

  ||Af||^2 <=sum_j d_j^(-2sigma)
       integral_1^{T/d_j} [1_{N(d_j u)>=j}/N(d_j u)]
                          |f(u)|^2 u^(-2sigma-1) du
    <=sum_j [1/(j d_j^(2sigma))] ||f||^2.              (1)

An integral whose upper endpoint is below1 is zero. The omitted portion
u<1 contributes exactly zero by the declared extension of f. On every
admitted term N(d_j u)>=j, so its reciprocal is at most1/j even though the
bank changes with t. No monotonicity of N, independence among terms, or
prime distribution theorem is used. Complex f is covered by the usual
modulus-square Jensen inequality. Changing values at integration endpoints
does not affect the conclusion.

Infinite sums and finite bank cutoffs

Each pointwise bank is finite. Infinite sequence interchange in (1) uses
Tonelli and not an unjustified absolutely convergent signed sum. Since d_j
are distinct integers >=2, d_j>=j+1. Thus for sigma>=1/2,

  sum_j 1/(j d_j^(2sigma))<=sum_j 1/(j d_j)
    <=sum_{j=1}^m 1/(j d_j)+sum_{j=m+1}^infinity 1/(j(j+1))
    =c_m, c_m=sum_{j=1}^m 1/(j d_j)+1/(m+1).         (2)

The baseline telescoping sum is1. In fact
  1-c_m=sum_{j=1}^m [1/(j(j+1))-1/(j d_j)].
Hence c_m<=1, with c_m<1 exactly when some observed d_j>j+1. For the prefix
2,3,5, c_3=1/2+1/6+1/15+1/4=59/60. Primality is unnecessary for this numeric
contraction witness. A program calling the sequence primes still needs its
own correct sequence/domain linkage; checking these three numbers alone
does not certify the semantics of an arbitrary candidate bank.

For a fixed finite bank of known maximum size L, one can instead use the
smaller coefficient sum_{j=1}^L 1/(j d_j), always <=1-1/(L+1)<1, even if
there is no gap. The conservative infinite-extension certificate (2) is
valid for finite banks too. If no gap is observed it simply does not certify
strict contraction for arbitrary growing banks. Distinct real dilations
>=2 alone do not imply d_j>=j+1, so that tail formula would be invalid.

N=0 is handled explicitly. N=+infinity is outside this definition. A bank
including entries with d_j>t is allowed: those terms are zero but still
count in its denominator. Omitting such terms while retaining or changing
the denominator requires checking that the resulting expression is still
the declared prefix average. Truncating the bank at a fixed maximum L is
valid if its N never exceeds L.

Inverse residual bound and finite-horizon endpoints

Let R=(I-A)f and c=c_m<1. Inequality (1) yields

  ||R||>=||f||-||Af||>=(1-sqrt(c))||f||,
  ||f||<=(1-sqrt(c))^-1 ||R||.                        (3)

All norms here are on the SAME finite horizon and weight. Af up to T uses
only f up to T/2, so it never demands unknown values beyond the horizon.
The inverse does not have a boundary remainder at T or an initial f below1.

Even an a-priori L2 hypothesis on f can be avoided if R is L2. For any K
with 2^K>T, the delay d_j>=2 implies A^K f(t)=0 for t<=T, after the declared
zero extension. For a fixed N, A is linear and

  f=sum_{k=0}^{K-1} A^k R on [1,T].

Each term is L2 by (1); its norm is at most c^(k/2)||R||. Summing the finite
geometric series proves (3). Almost-everywhere representatives cause no
problem: the dilations are countable, and each dilation preserves null sets.
This finite delay argument also verifies that a genuine residual norm bound
cannot coexist with a hidden non-L2 homogeneous solution on a finite horizon.

For an exact rational certificate there is no need to encode a square root.
Since 1-sqrt(c)=(1-c)/(1+sqrt(c))>=(1-c)/2,

  ||f||<=[2/(1-c)] ||R||,
  ||f||^2<=[4/(1-c)^2] ||R||^2.                     (4)

These constants are uniform in sigma>=1/2, T, N and the admissible source.
They are conservative upper bounds, not the exact optimal operator norm.

Bounded signed selectors and finite stage composition

The same proof covers
  A_beta f(t)=N(t)^-1 sum_{j<=N(t)} beta(t,d_j)f(t/d_j)
with measurable complex coefficients |beta(t,d_j)|<=1, and the same zero
case. Jensen bounds its square by the average of |beta*f|^2, which is at
most the average of |f|^2. A coefficient involving the fixed actual-mu
source is permissible if its modulus bound is proved; freeze that function
when treating A_beta as a linear operator on the input f. Multiplying
selectors known to lie in {-1,0,1} preserves this bound. An arbitrary sum,
unbounded scalar, or a division by a possibly zero selector does not.

For finitely many stages f_0=f, f_l=(I-A_l)f_{l-1}, put R=f_k. Every stage
preserves the extension zero below1. Applying (4) to each stage on the SAME
finite horizon and weight gives
  ||f||<=product_{l=1}^k [2/(1-c_l)] ||R||,
  ||f||^2<=product_{l=1}^k [4/(1-c_l)^2] ||R||^2.
An intermediate input need not be an ordinary Mertens prefix. Its continuous
step extension still works for integer dilations if every selector and bank
count is frozen on integer cells; f_l(t/d) is then evaluated in its own
argument cell, with no new root/global-context substitution. Source/domain
validation must prove those identities for the complete staged program.
The constants multiply and remain finite; no residual estimate is inferred.

Discrete Mertens energy: the required bridge

Take f(t)=M(floor(t)). To infer a continuous residual bound from integer
candidate values, define N(t)=N(floor(t)) (or freeze a block-context program
on each integer cell). With integer d_j,

  floor(t/d_j)=floor(floor(t)/d_j),  t>=1.

Thus R is constant on [n,n+1) and equals the candidate's complete integer
residual r(n)=M(n)-N(n)^-1 sum_{j<=N(n)} M(floor(n/d_j)), with the zero-bank
case r(n)=M(n). If N is genuinely arbitrary measurable between integer
samples, those samples alone give NO bound on its continuous residual norm.
The theorem remains true, but this particular finite-data bridge does not.

Write E_R(X)=X^-2 sum_{n=X}^{2X-1}|r(n)|^2. At sigma=1/2,

  E_M(X)/4<=integral_X^{2X}|M(floor(t))|^2 t^-2 dt,
  integral_Y^{2Y}|R(t)|^2 t^-2 dt<=E_R(Y).

Applying (4) on T=2X and partitioning [1,2X] into dyadic blocks gives

  E_M(X)<=4[4/(1-c)^2] sum_{Y dyadic,1<=Y<=X} E_R(Y).  (5)

Suppose one fixed residual program satisfies E_R(Y)<=K_epsilon Y^epsilon
for every dyadic Y and every epsilon>0. Then the geometric sum in (5) is at
most K_epsilon [2^epsilon/(2^epsilon-1)] X^epsilon. Therefore E_M has the
same cofinal subpower property, with finite uniform constants. A residual
potential W_R=E_R+nonnegative storage with its own cofinal signed drift would
imply this premise by telescoping, retaining W_R(1). No such residual drift
or uniform growth estimate is established by the recovery certificate.

For the actual residual r(1)=M(1)=1, because all d_j>=2. Its initial energy
is not lost through the below1 extension, despite [1,1] having zero continuous
measure. The block [1,2] contains exactly that initial integer value.

Zeta implication, conditional on the genuine cofinal residual premise

The same residual premise also implies integral_1^infinity |R(t)|^2
t^(-2sigma-1)dt<infinity for every sigma>1/2: choose epsilon<2sigma-1 and
sum its dyadic block bounds. Uniform recovery and monotone convergence then
give the identical statement for M(floor(t)). For any s with Re(s)>1/2,
choose 1/2<sigma<Re(s). Cauchy-Schwarz gives absolute convergence of

  F(s)=s integral_1^infinity M(floor(t)) t^(-s-1) dt,

because the remaining squared weight is t^(-2(Re(s)-sigma)-1). On compact
subsets of Re(s)>1/2 the same argument, including logarithmic derivatives,
gives holomorphy. For Re(s)>1, summation by parts identifies F(s)=1/zeta(s).
The meromorphic continuation of zeta and the identity theorem give
zeta(s)F(s)=1 throughout that half-plane, with the removable product at
the pole s=1. Therefore zeta has no zeros with real part>1/2. Its functional
equation then places all nontrivial zeros on the critical line.

The only external standard facts used in that last step are zeta's
meromorphic continuation/simple pole and reflection formula, checked against
NIST DLMF sections25.2 and25.4:
https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.2 and https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.4.

This is a conditional implication of an ALL-SCALE residual estimate. Finite
candidate values, a source-free bank definition and c<1 alone prove none of
that premise. The recovery operator is a new sufficient route to the original
target; it does not make signed source cancellation disappear.

Candidate-domain boundaries to preserve in implementation

- Link the bank to one fixed ordered integer sequence; an observed list with
  a gap certifies its arbitrary distinct-integer continuation, not an unrelated
  sequence or a subset chosen separately at each t.
- N must count exactly the admitted prefix and define the zero case. Variable
  source-free cutoffs, floor roots and fixed finite caps are allowed if they
  really produce that finite prefix. The proof requires no monotone cutoff.
- Keep f=0 below1; in an AST use empty prefix sums there, never a point source
  request at0. Keep the complete original residual, including its denominator.
- For integer residual energies use the stated step extension. Programs with
  block parameter X can be frozen on their own dyadic block and integer cells
  to define one measurable N on the whole positive axis; the source f itself
  must remain the same global M prefix. Fresh child blocks are fully included.
- The same fixed program and cofinal-q quantifier are required for a residual
  drift proof. No candidate choice depending on observed responses supplies
  a universal residual inequality.

The initial operator inequality and conditional target/zeta recovery pass
this independent proof review within these scopes. Checker API/schema and
finite resource limits are to be agreed before implementation. Global/RH
flags remain false unless the separate residual premise is genuinely proved.

Growing-prefix limitation and narrowed checker scope

Before any actual recovery-candidate run, root narrowed the implementation
to the generic numerical witness theorem, with no full candidate-AST linkage.
The checker API is verify_witness(divisors,meter), returning canonical rational
strings c, norm_bound=2/(1-c), squared_bound=4/(1-c)^2. It checks an ordered
integer witness of length1..64, entries2..4096 and strict c<1. Its theorem
still assumes a COMPLETE ordered prefix average. It does not prove that an
AST, predicate, cutoff, source selector or actual research candidate has that
form, and does not certify any residual energy estimate. This limitation is
intentional; no actual arithmetic study of that candidate family was run.

There is also a precise reason not to infer cancellation from a growing
prefix. If N(t)->infinity, consider inputs f supported in [B,infinity),
where N(t)>=L for all t>=B. In the change-variable proof every nonzero input
has u>=B, hence N(d_j u)>=L. For j<=L use1/N<=1/L; for j>L use1/N<=1/j.
At sigma>=1/2 this gives the tail operator bound

  ||Af||^2 <=epsilon_L^2 ||f||^2,
  epsilon_L^2 <=(H_{L+1}-1)/L+1/(L+1) ->0,

where H_k=sum_{j=1}^k 1/j. The same conclusion holds for bounded signed
selectors. Consequently the HIGH-INPUT tail residual I-A approaches the
identity in operator norm: for those inputs,
 (1-epsilon_L)||f||<=||(I-A)f||<=(1+epsilon_L)||f||.
This does not assert that arbitrary actual-Mobius output-tail values equal
M pointwise, nor remove possible contributions from the fixed initial input
segment to a later output tail. It does show that large prefix size alone
provides no uniformly improving cancellation mechanism in the critical norm.
Recovery remains a sound conditional theorem; it is not a reason to spend a
research run on an otherwise unsupported growing-prefix residual family.
