3. An unconditional new helper-decay observation, proved analytically

Define E_H(X)=X^-2 sum_(X<=n<2X)|H_X(n)|^2. The already admitted helper prices
are C1=324 for A1 and C2=2304 for A2, with prefix exponent1. Keeping the scale
powers, rather than replacing them by their worst uniform constant, proves

 E_H03(X)<=162*X^(-1/4)+1152*X^(-2/5), all integer X>=1.

For02 the first term similarly contributes at most162*X^(-2/7) after the
two-term square inequality. Its second term is at the paid power boundary,
but the actual prime-only support supplies a logarithmic gain. Here is an
elementary proof, requiring no PNT or short-interval prime theorem.

Let theta(t)=sum_(p<=t)log p. For integer N>=3 put m=ceil(N/2). Every prime
m<p<=N divides binom(2m,m); hence
 theta(N)-theta(m)<=log binom(2m,m)<=2m*log2.
Induction, with N=1,2 checked directly, gives theta(N)<=4N*log2:
 theta(N)<=6m*log2<=3(N+1)*log2<=4N*log2.
Therefore theta(t)<=4t*log2 for every real t>=1.
For p>y_X, log p>=log y_X>=(1/2)log(2X), so for every t<=2X,
 # {y_X<p<=t} <=8*(log2)*t/log(2X).
Empty intervals and t<1 are separately zero; no finite head is omitted.

Since |h1(p)|<=beta and h1 has unit0, for every X<=n<2X,
 |(1/2)X^(-1/2)A1(floor(n/3);y_X)|
 <=12*(log2)*X^(1/2)/log(2X).
Its energy is at most144*(log2)^2/log(2X)^2. Consequently

 E_H02(X)<=162*X^(-2/7)+288*(log2)^2/log(2X)^2,
 for every integer X>=1.

Both displayed bounds tend to zero. The same argument handles the other
selected helpers:01 has E_H<=324*(log2)^2/log(2X)^2;04 has
E_H<=81*X^(-2/257);05 has E_H<=324*X^(-2/257). These are source-bound
analytical consequences, not numerical slopes. They have not been added to

4. What vanishing helpers do and do not establish

For the normalized block inner product, the norm triangle inequality gives
 |sqrt(E_G(X))-sqrt(E_F(X))|<=sqrt(E_H(X)) ->0.
Thus a compensator with this decay cannot conceal a power-growing full
endpoint. More precisely, if E_F(X_j) tends to infinity along a sequence,
then E_G(X_j)/E_F(X_j) tends to1. This is conditional on such growth, not a
proof that the actual E_F grows. It also does not exclude an inequality for
increments of G: a correction small compared with endpoint energy can still
change its scale differences.

The exact relation is E_G=E_F+2<F,H>+E_H. Vanishing E_H does NOT imply
<F,H> tends to zero without an independent bound on F. Cauchy gives only
|<F,H>|<=sqrt(E_F*E_H). The generic coefficient estimate allows positive
power for E_F; combined with the above decays it leaves a positive power for
this covariance. Using the sought full-energy estimate to dispose of this
cross would be circular. Even a subpower bound alone does not imply a
logarithmically decaying cross for the boundary term in02.

Both02/03 and the priced baseline have positive fitted C. If any of their
uniform recurrences were proved, the present forcing provider would give the
same log^4 growth class. Replacing1/16 by1/64 improves a conditional constant,
not its power/logarithmic growth theorem. The17 finite checks establish no
uniform recurrence. Conversely, no failed norm-only countermodel disproves
the actual arithmetic recurrence.

They retain the unit and every d<=n, including floor arguments near one.
The035 basis change/recovery converts these into exact discovered-state

The stage037 generic countermodel has the correct envelope and retained units

proof attempt should impose only independently replayed exact constraints and
require a PSD/induction/endpoint certificate that pays the complete defect.
