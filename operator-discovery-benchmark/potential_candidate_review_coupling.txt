Common notation and the complete obligation
------------------------------------------
M(y)=sum_{1<=n<=y}mu(n), M(0)=0. Every selected program reviewed here has one
state with weight1. Write its state U_X(t), where X<=t<=2X-1, and retain
  E(X)=X^-2 sum_{t=X}^{2X-1}M(t)^2,
  V(X)=X^-2 sum_{t=X}^{2X-1}U_X(t)^2, W(X)=E(X)+V(X).
For every program, the full signed increment is exactly
  [sum_{t=2X}^{4X-1}(M(t)^2+U_{2X}(t)^2)
     -4 sum_{t=X}^{2X-1}(M(t)^2+U_X(t)^2)]/(4X^2).
The factor4 on the parent and the fresh child parameter2X are essential.
None of the masks, factor sums or prime banks replaces E_mu.

The open obligation is one fixed program and
  exists integer q0>=1; for every integer q>=q0 there exists finite C_q>=0,
  independent of every dyadic X>=1, such that
  W(2X)-W(X)<=C_q*ceil(X^(1/q)).
Constants may depend on q and the fixed program. They cannot silently depend
on the moving modulus, root cutoff, newly admitted prime set or X. A finite
set of q/scales does not discharge these quantifiers. An eventual uniform
estimate can absorb finitely many initial scales into an explicitly justified
C_q, but cannot omit them or W(1). Structural V>=0 establishes only E<=W.

ID16: moving residue-zero mask
-----------------------------
Set a(X)=max(2,floor(X^(1/6))). The AST is exactly
  U_X(t)=B_a(t)=sum_{i<=t,a|i}mu(i).
This is an arithmetic subsequence, not M(floor(t/a)) without qualifications.
If a is not squarefree, B_a is identicallyzero. If a is squarefree,
  B_a(t)=mu(a)*C_a(floor(t/a)),
  C_a(y)=sum_{k<=y,gcd(k,a)=1}mu(k).
Indeed mu(ak)=mu(a)mu(k) for coprime k, and iszero for k sharing a prime
factor with squarefree a. Consequently B_a^2=mu(a)^2*C_a^2. The sign of the
single factor mu(a) disappears; the signed correlations within C_a remain.
An argument that uses unrestricted M(t/a) must restore the coprime complement.

There is also an exact finite target-prefix expansion. If a is squarefree,
let S(a) be the positive integers whose prime factors all divide a, including1.
Then
  C_a(y)=sum_{d<=y,d in S(a)}M(floor(y/d)).
To see this, expand the right side coefficientwise: removing all powers of
primes dividing a cancels a coefficient whenever such a prime divides its
index, and otherwise leaves mu(index). Squaring this expression retains every
d/e interaction; replacing it by the sum of individual squares is incorrect.
For the especially long a=2 regime,
  C_2(y)=M(y)+C_2(floor(y/2)),
  B_2(t)=-sum_{j>=1}M(floor(t/2^j)),
with the last sum terminating at argumentzero. This is a finite identity,
not a bound on these correlated earlier prefixes.

The max2 matters: a(X)=2 for every1<=X<3^6=729, including the entire region
where floor(X^(1/6)) is1. For k>=3, a(X)=k on k^6<=X<(k+1)^6. A child uses
b=a(2X). Different moduli need not define nested multiple sets:2 and3 are
already a counterexample. Let K(t)=B_b(t)-B_a(t). The exact cutoff contribution
on the child block is
  (4X^2)^-1 sum_{t=2X}^{4X-1}[2 B_a(t)K(t)+K(t)^2],
in addition to the fixed-a normalized parent/child transport. K includes both
added and removed residue-zero channels. At squareful k the entire auxiliary
state vanishes, but a later squarefree child can restore it; those transitions
are part of an all-scale estimate, not removable dormant intervals.

The target complement R_a(t)=sum_{i<=t,a does not divide i}mu(i) satisfies
M=B_a+R_a. Thus M^2+B_a^2=R_a^2+2R_a B_a+2B_a^2; the signed complement cross
term is unavoidable in any such rewrite. The elementary bound |B_a(t)|<=t/a
gives V(X)<=4X/a(X)^2, hence only an O(X^(2/3)) large-scale upper bound. It does
not produce the cofinal signed increment estimate. The missing lemma concerns
the full target plus correlated restricted prefixes, uniformly over moving a.

At X1, a=2 and t=1 contribute no multiple: V(1)=0 and W(1)=1.

ID61: coprime output mask on a signed long-tail state
--------------------------------------------------
Set m(X)=max(2,floor(sqrt(X))) and
  D(t)=M(t)-M(floor(t/3))=sum_{floor(t/3)<i<=t}mu(i).
The state is exactly U_X(t)=G_m(t)*D(t), with G_m(t)=1_{gcd(t,m)=1}.
Only the output location t is masked; the source indices inside D are not
restricted to coprime or prime inputs. Since G_m is0 or1,
  U_X(t)^2=G_m(t)[M(t)^2-2M(t)M(floor(t/3))+M(floor(t/3))^2].
The negative cross term belongs to the stored square and must remain present.

G_m depends only on rad(m), not on exponents in m. For example m=2,4,8 give
the identical odd-t mask. This is an actual arithmetic mask identity, not an
empirical response alias. More generally the complete inclusion-exclusion is
  G_m(t)=sum_{d|m}mu(d)*1_{d|t}.
All divisors and signs are retained. m=2 for1<=X<9; for k>=3 it is k on
k^2<=X<(k+1)^2. Successive parent/child moduli can gain or lose prime factors
and are not nested coprime domains.

For b=m(2X), the change of the child mask contributes exactly
  (4X^2)^-1 sum_{t=2X}^{4X-1}(G_b(t)-G_m(t))*D(t)^2,
in addition to fixed-mask normalized transport. This term contains positive
gained locations and negative lost locations. Density heuristics involving
phi(m)/m do not bound the actual weighted distribution of D(t)^2. Rewriting
on the complement must restore (1-G_m)D^2; it cannot remove unmasked M^2 from E.

The elementary |D(t)|<=t-floor(t/3) gives only V=O(X). The inequality
D^2<=2M(t)^2+2M(floor(t/3))^2 can relate storage to target energies, but it
supplies no independent cofinal innovation bound on those same target terms.
The unresolved lemma must retain their signed cross correlation and uniformly
control all moving output masks, including their gained/lost residue classes.

At X1, m=2, gcd(1,2)=1 and D(1)=1: V(1)=1 and W(1)=2.

ID67: truncated Mobius factor bank minus a root prefix
----------------------------------------------------
Let R=floor(X^(1/6)), s(t)=floor(t^(1/6)), and
  D_d(t)=M(t)-M(floor(t/d)),
  B_R(t)=sum_{d<=R}mu(d)*D_d(t).
The exact state is U_X(t)=B_R(t)-M(s(t)). The d1 summand is identicallyzero:
mu(1)*(M(t)-M(t))=0. Every squareful d also vanishes. Neither observation
licenses dropping the remaining squarefree d/e cross terms.

The small-root regimes simplify substantially by algebra alone:
* For1<=X<64, R=1 and U=-M(s(t)). Because t<128, s(t) is1 below64 and2 from64,
  so M(s(t)) is respectively1 and0. Thus
    V(X)=1/X                  for1<=X<=32,
    V(X)=(64-X)/X^2           for33<=X<=63.
  In particular for dyadic X<=16, both parent and child lie in the first
  branch and Delta V=-1/(2X). This is initial padding, not long-tail coupling.
* For64<=X<729, R=2 and B_R=-D_2. Throughout this regime t<1458, s(t) is2
  below729 and3 from729. Since M(2)=0 and M(3)=-1,
    U=-D_2(t)+1_{t>=729},
    U^2=D_2(t)^2-2*1_{t>=729}*D_2(t)+1_{t>=729}.
  The boundary terms begin on a parent block at X365, not at X64. A child
  always recomputes R at2X; it can have R3 while the parent still has R2.
* For729<=X<4096, R=3. The only nonzero factor summands are d2,d3, and
  s(t) is3 or4 on the whole block. M(3)=M(4)=-1, so exactly
    U=-2M(t)+M(floor(t/2))+M(floor(t/3))+1.
  This complete coupled square has all cross terms and its additive1. It
  cannot be reduced to separate stored squares at the three prefix scales.
At R4 the new d4 coefficient iszero, but the t-dependent root prefix can
still change. For larger roots, every newly admitted squarefree factor and
every change of M(s(t)) remains part of the fixed program.

A useful general identity is
  U=M(R)*M(t)-sum_{d<=R}mu(d)M(floor(t/d))-M(s(t)).
The middle sum does NOT collapse to1: its full d<=t version is
  sum_{n<=t}(mu*mu)(n),
not sum mu(d)*floor(t/d). Any proposed full-convolution rewrite must retain
the complement sum_{R<d<=t}mu(d)M(floor(t/d)) with its proper sign. In
particular the inverse-divisor identity with an unweighted floor is a
different expression and cannot supply the missing estimate here.

Writing R'=floor((2X)^(1/6)), J(t)=sum_{R<d<=R'}mu(d)D_d(t), gives
U_{2X}(t)=U_X(t)+J(t) at a common t. Its complete child cutoff correction is
  (4X^2)^-1 sum_child[2 U_X(t)J(t)+J(t)^2].
Expansion of B_R^2 retains mu(d)mu(e)D_d D_e for every pair; subtraction of
M(s(t)) retains -2M(s(t))*B_R and the full root-prefix square. The simple
bound |M(s(t))|<=s(t) makes the root-only normalized storage O(X^(-2/3)),
whereas |B_R(t)|<=Rt yields only an O(X^(4/3)) upper bound for bank storage.
Initial padding and a small root term therefore cannot certify the coupled
long-tail part or its moving-factor boundary costs.

At X1 the bank iszero and U=-1: V(1)=1 and W(1)=2.

ID42: positive-coefficient coupled moving prime bank
--------------------------------------------------
Let P=floor(X^(1/3)) and F_P(t)=sum_{p<=P,p prime}M(floor(t/p)). The AST is
U_X(t)=F_P(t), with no subtracted M(t) term and no additional mu(p) factor.
Origin metadata saying coefficient0 does not create an absent term. Although
mu(p)=-1 would give a common overall sign and hence the same square for this
actual prime domain, there is no alternating sign between distinct prime
coefficients. The signed source information is inside the different M(t/p).

The complete square is
  F_P^2=sum_p M(floor(t/p))^2
        +2 sum_{p<q}M(floor(t/p))*M(floor(t/q)).
Every p/q cross term belongs to V. Diagonal-only bank storage is a different
candidate. There are no primes when X<8; for8<=X<27 the bank is just p2;
for27<=X<125 it contains p2,p3, including the composite cutoff4 interval.
New primes enter at X=p^3; composite cube thresholds do not add a prime.

Coefficientwise one may write F_P(t)=sum_{n<=t}a_P(n), with
  a_P(n)=sum_{p|n,p<=P}mu(n/p)
        =-mu(n)*omega_P(n)+sum_{p<=P,p^2|n}mu(n/p),
where omega_P counts distinct prime divisors up toP. The second term is
important: exactly-one-squared-prime inputs can contribute even though mu(n)=0.
Inputs with a cube or two squared primes contributezero there. Thus the bank
does not simply equal a prime-count weight times the original mu sequence.
Replacing the finite prime domain by all p<=t also requires its omitted
P<p<=t complement; no full-domain identity removes that tail for free.

Let P'=floor((2X)^(1/3)) and J(t)=sum_{P<p<=P',p prime}M(floor(t/p)). The
same-bank signed transport must be supplemented by exactly
  (4X^2)^-1 sum_child[2F_P(t)J(t)+J(t)^2].
J^2 retains every new-prime pair, and F_P*J every old/new pair. A larger cutoff
and positive external coefficients do not determine the sign of these actual
prefix correlations. The elementary |F_P(t)|<=t*sum_{p<=P}1/p gives at most
O(X*(1+log P)^2) storage. Even the elementary uniform O(t) bound for this
root-ratio new-prime strip yields only O(X) boundary-square storage. Small
prime denominators alone do not supply arbitrarily small power increments.

At X1 the prime bank is empty: V(1)=0 and W(1)=1. Its later activation must
be retained even though the initial stored potential iszero.

Consequences for a proof queue
-----------------------------
These ASTs admit exact simplifications and expose concrete missing lemmas:
restricted-prefix correlation and changing-modulus terms for16; masked
long-tail correlation and gained/lost coprime locations for61; truncated
Mobius-factor convolution and root/bank cross terms for67; complete old/new
prime-prefix correlations and prime-domain complements for42. None of the
elementary bounds above gives the required cofinal signed innovation bound.

The long dormant modulus/root regimes and actual arithmetic mask aliases
should be separated from genuinely coupled large-root formulas analytically.
They explain what the programs ARE, without evaluating or ranking their
responses. No finite constant is extended to another q, no per-regime constant
is treated as uniform in X, and no omitted component is silently restored by
an RH conclusion. The anchored costs are W1=1,2,2,1 for16,61,67,42 respectively.
The conditional telescoping and ordinary-zeta bridge in the existing soundness
memo remain conditional on the complete cofinal all-dyadic estimate. This
review proves only the displayed algebraic descriptions and elementary size
bounds, not that estimate or a new global-bound/RH claim.
