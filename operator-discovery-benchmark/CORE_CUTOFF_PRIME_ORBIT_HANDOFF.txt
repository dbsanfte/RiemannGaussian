Structural lessons and next search direction
Single-prime programs are universally singleton partitions in a strict dyadic
block. Distinct block members cannot divide each other: a larger integer
multiple would exceed 2B. Surviving multi-prime groups involve incomparable
prime products; equal-number prime exchanges preserve the Mobius sign.
Common cofactor alone therefore does not guarantee opposite arithmetic signs.
For id 7 every squarefree composite is in cofactor 1; primes in(B,2B] remain
singletons. Its absolute-group cost prevents prime/composite cancellation
between these groups. No global sign or asymptote for prime event work is
known here: that work depends on the complete previous Mobius prefix.

Review026 proves one useful uniform ancillary control: replace all old-window
masks inside a block by the common H_B. Exact polarization writes joined work
as the difference between the actual child energy and the zero-sign child
energy. The inherited high-tail and finite-head bounds then give, for ANY
partition and every partial cutoff,
