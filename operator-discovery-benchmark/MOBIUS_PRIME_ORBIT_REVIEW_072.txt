Scope
-----
This source-only review reads MOBIUS_MOMENT_SCALE_HANDOFF.txt,
MOBIUS_MOMENT_SCALE_REVIEW_071.txt, DETECTOR_INDEPENDENCE_HANDOFF.md,
and, at the root's request, SPARSE_HEAD_MECHANISM_REVIEW_067.txt.
It inspects no scientific072 result, old study/suite, hash, or other file;
it runs no experiment and writes only this file. No subagent is used.
The fixed-prime positive-price obstruction was already proved in067.
Its extension to arbitrary exchange transport below is the new scoped
statement, rather than a rediscovery of the fixed-prime statement.

The main results are an exact component-optimal transport lemma, an
exact no-gain theorem for single-prime matchings on the admitted source,
a concrete disjoint exchange gain, a growing-scope survivor estimate,
and a complete graph-energy transfer with a paid diagonal. None closes
the signed cross-component leaf, the joined J estimate, or RH.

1. Preserve the actual source and the complete remainder
-------------------------------------------------------
For the original joined source keep, literally,

 k_j(n)=g(jn)-g((j+1)n),
 z_j=sum_n mu(n) k_j(n), c_j=d_j+z_j,
 J_j=z_j+b_j H_mu(u_j)H_mu(v_j).                    (1)

Thus a complete z-head theorem pays the actual c head after adding
sum_j|d_j|, and it pays J after adding the full separable moment price.
Conversely a complete J theorem pays z after the same addition. This
is a triangle equivalence up to a paid term, not a new cancellation rule.
The071 price, including its C^2 term, auxiliary Mertens hypotheses and
all rank bases, must be retained if this conversion is used.

Ordinary mu is shared by every row, channel and orbit. The unit has
mu(1)=1; squareful integers have value zero. Kernels are joined before
every norm. There is no deletion of reciprocal rectangle terms with
ab>R: the passage to (1) uses the complete identity already proved071.
Direct atoms can be added to k_j(1), but keeping sum_j|d_j| as a separate
paid term is simpler and avoids a hidden direct-atom cross term.

For N=15k,y=16k,p=3,q=5,r=1,j=1, k a positive integer,

 w(n)=1_(8k,10k](n)-1_(16k,20k](n), d_1=0,b_1=-2k,
 z_1=M(10k)-M(8k)-M(20k)+M(16k),
 J_1=z_1-2k H_mu(u_1)H_mu(v_1).                   (2)

For fixed admissible sublinear cuts the short branches vanish eventually.
All results below apply to this complete z_1 and hence to its actual J
with the explicit additional separable cost in(2).

2. Exact finite prime block and shared-head robust prices
-------------------------------------------------------
Let S be a finite set of distinct primes and P=product_(p in S)p.
Extend every kernel by zero beyond its declared support. Every squarefree
n has a unique representation n=md, d|P, gcd(m,P)=1; mu(n)=mu(m)mu(d).
Squareful terms are zero. Therefore

 B_j(m)=sum_(d|P)mu(d)k_j(dm),
 z_j=sum_(gcd(m,P)=1)mu(m)B_j(m).                 (3)

This proves the requested identity with full support, including m=1,
outside primes, and divisors whose arguments lie outside support.
The squarefree-coprime absolute price

 U=sum_(j in head,m:gcd(m,P)=1)mu(m)^2|B_j(m)|     (4)

is a valid complete z-head price. Replacing mu(m)^2 by1 is also valid
and does not require an arithmetic mask in the producer.

For shared signs, (4) need not be the best robust vector price. For a
finite set A of unresolved squarefree anchors, vectors v_a=(B_j(a))_j,
and a known vector q (unit, direct atoms, and any separately certified
anchors), the exact sign-relaxation price is

 sup_(epsilon_a=+/-1)||q+sum_a epsilon_a v_a||_1
 =max_(eta_j=+/-1)[eta.q+sum_a|eta.v_a|].          (5)

Proof: ||x||_1=max_eta eta.x, and the two finite maxima commute.
One shared epsilon_a is used across the entire head. Allowing separate
epsilon_(j,a) instead gives the weaker rowwise triangle price. Formula
(5) is sharp for this sign relaxation; it is not a claim that actual
outside-prime Mobius values attain every relaxed sign assignment.
Known anchors must stay inside q, rather than being granted free signs.

For one row, the sharp relaxed price is |q|+sum_a|B(a)|. In particular
the known unit does not remove an unresolved absolute survivor. Knowledge
that all prime anchors have sign -1 may aggregate their contributions
into q; for(2) all top-band prime contributions have the same sign, so
this does not reduce the isolated top-band contribution's absolute cost.

3. General squarefree exchange graph and exact transport lemma
--------------------------------------------------------------
An admissible edge joins u=am and v=bm, where a,b are coprime squarefree
positive integers and gcd(m,ab)=1. The multiplier factorizations certify
s=mu(a)mu(b) in{+1,-1}, and the exact identity is

 mu(u)=s mu(v).                                  (6)

It remains true when m is squareful: both sides are zero. One may discard
such vertices using a separately checked squarefree mask, or retain them
with a weaker unweighted price. No actual mu(n) table is needed to check
the local relation; however multiplier factorization and squarefree
mask access are arithmetic inputs and must be charged/provenanced.
Cycle signs are consistent even on unmasked vertices: the distinct-prime
count omega(am)=omega(a)+omega(m) under the stated coprimality condition,
so edge signs equal the ratio of the formal labels(-1)^omega(n). This
is an algebraic justification, not permission to evaluate all global
vertex labels without charging the associated arithmetic access.

Take any finite graph of these edges on squarefree integers, allowing
zero-kernel intermediate vertices. On each connected component C choose
an anchor a_C. Edge signs are globally consistent: a product of edge
signs around any cycle is1, by(6) and nonzero mu on squarefree vertices.
Hence a checker can assign labels l_n in{+1,-1}, l_(a_C)=1, satisfying
mu(n)=l_n mu(a_C), without querying mu(a_C). Define

 V_(j,C)=sum_(n in C) l_n k_j(n).
 z_j=sum_C mu(a_C)V_(j,C).                       (7)

Unmatched vertices are singleton components. Arbitrary edge capacities
or disjoint matching constraints restrict the available graph further;
they do not change(7) for any genuinely established component relations.

Transport lemma. For a scalar kernel on this finite graph, allow arbitrary
real flows along the annihilator columns e_u-s e_v and price the remaining
coefficient vector by its l1 norm. The minimum residual price is exactly

                       sum_C |V_C|.              (8)

Proof: every annihilator has zero l-labelled sum in its component, so
sum_(n in C)|residual_n|>=|sum_(n in C)l_n residual_n|=|V_C|.
A spanning tree transfers all coefficients to its anchor, leaving the
single coefficient V_C there; induction from the leaves proves attainment.
The same proof works for each row. Capacities can prevent attainment but
cannot beat the lower bound. This proves a precise limit for all fractional
allocations and geometry-adaptive matchings, not only a particular greedy
edge selection. Known anchor signs may be retained and jointly priced
as in(5);(8) describes unsigned residual triangle, not every possible
signed arithmetic proof.

For a finite exchange dictionary let S contain EVERY prime occurring in
ANY multiplier. Each edge preserves the outside-S part

                rough_S(n)=n/gcd(n,P),            (9)

on squarefree vertices. Indeed only primes of S are exchanged. Thus each
graph component lies inside one full P divisor block. Aggregating a whole
P block can only reduce the sum of absolute component totals. Consequently

 unsigned exchange transport cost >= U_P(w),      (10)

where U_P is the full-block scalar price from(4). This remains true with
arbitrary graph paths, zero-kernel intermediates beyond the original
support, adaptive geometry-based edge selection, and real flow weights.
It fails if new relations connect distinct rough parts; that is precisely
the additional arithmetic mechanism such a stronger method must supply.

4. Single-prime matching cannot improve the concrete joined source
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Consider any disjoint collection of admissible edges n<->pn, p prime,
with the remaining vertices unmatched. An edge's exact contribution is
mu(n)[w(n)-w(pn)] when p does not divide n. Its triangle cost on a
squarefree pair is |w(n)-w(pn)|; an unmatched vertex costs |w(n)|.

For(2), supported vertices of equal coefficient have ratio at most10/8
within the lower band and20/16 within the upper band, both less than2.
Thus no prime edge connects supported vertices of equal coefficient.
A prime edge joining the two bands must have p=2 since20/8<3, and it
then joins coefficient+1 to coefficient-1. For every admissible pair,

              |w(n)-w(pn)|=|w(n)|+|w(pn)|.        (11)

Edges with one or two zero coefficients satisfy the same equality.
Squareful pairs contribute zero on both sides. Therefore EVERY disjoint
single-prime matching, with optional unmatched vertices, has exactly
the initial price sum_n mu(n)^2|w(n)|. Selection from floor geometry,
varying the prime from edge to edge, and optimizing capacities cannot
improve that cost. Prime2 reinforces the signed target rather than
canceling it. This theorem is more specific than067's fixed-P barrier.

It is scoped to disjoint pair pricing. Composing several primes into a
larger component permits rational exchange ratios and is covered by(8),
not by the exact equality(11).

5. Concrete disjoint exchange cancellation with a uniform count law
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The following two edge families are genuinely different from prime
involutions and can be selected using geometry alone:

 3m<->5m, 16k/5<m<=10k/3, gcd(m,15)=1;
 3m<->7m,  8k/3<m<=20k/7, gcd(m,21)=1.           (12)

In each family the first vertex has coefficient+1 and the second has
coefficient-1. Both multipliers have Mobius value-1, so(6) gives equal
vertex signs and the two contributions cancel exactly. Within a family
all endpoints are distinct. Across the two families, the lower endpoints
lie respectively in(48k/5,10k] and(8k,60k/7], which are disjoint, and the
upper endpoints lie in(16k,50k/3] and(56k/3,20k], also disjoint. Thus the
two families together are an admissible matching without any capacity
overlap. Squareful m give zero endpoints and are retained as zeros.

Let Q_P(A,B) count squarefree m in(A,B] coprime to P. The exact matched
price is

 sum_n mu(n)^2|w(n)|
 -2 Q_15(16k/5,10k/3)-2 Q_21(8k/3,20k/7).        (13)

This is a valid uniform source rule, not an arithmetic fit to finite mu
values. It produces a constant-factor saving, not a square-root theorem.

For completeness an elementary count law, useful also for growing scope,
is proved here. Write s=omega(P),

 delta_P=(6/pi^2) product_(p|P) p/(p+1).

For any real X>=1,

 |Q_P(0,X)-delta_P X| <= (2^s+2)sqrt(X).          (14)

To prove it, use mu(n)^2=sum_(d^2|n)mu(d) and the exact coprime count
sum_(e|P)mu(e)floor(Y/e). This latter differs from Y phi(P)/P by at most
2^s. Restrict d<=sqrt(X), gcd(d,P)=1. The errors sum to at most
2^s sqrt(X). Extending sum mu(d)/d^2 to infinity costs at most2sqrt(X)
(the bound sum_(d>floor sqrt(X))d^-2<=2/sqrt(X) suffices). Its absolutely
convergent Euler product equals delta_P/(phi(P)/P), establishing(14).
The identity sum n^-2=pi^2/6 only normalizes this positive product; the
proof also works with that series left unevaluated.

Subtracting two versions of(14) proves an interval error no larger than
(2^s+2)(sqrt(A)+sqrt(B)). Applying it to(13), the two savings have leading
coefficients respectively k/pi^2 and3k/(2pi^2). The baseline has leading
coefficient36k/pi^2. Thus(13) gives leading price67k/(2pi^2)+O(sqrt(k)),
compared with36k/pi^2+O(sqrt(k)). These constants are analytical outputs
of the declared exchange families, not observed scientific072 results.
This finite dictionary improves a price while retaining a linear rate.

6. Fixed and growing multiplier scope: survivor and rank costs
-------------------------------------------------------------
For every finite P, full-block B(m)=-1 when16k<m<=20k: the d=1 term is
-1, and every d>=2 has dm>20k and coefficient zero. Therefore(10) implies

 exchange triangle price >= Q_P(16k,20k)
 >=4k delta_P-(2^s+2)(sqrt(16k)+sqrt(20k)).        (15)

For fixed P this is linear. This recovers the067 obstruction as the
special full-block case and EXTENDS it to every fixed exchange dictionary,
including all geometry-adaptive transport plans over that dictionary.
The constants belong to the entire dictionary prime scope; they cannot
be presented as independent of a growing S.

Equation(15) is nevertheless a uniform growing-s estimate. Since
p/(p+1)>=2/3 for every prime,

 delta_P>=(6/pi^2)(2/3)^s.                        (16)

For example if s<=c log(k) with c<1/(2 log3), the ratio of the error in
(15) to its displayed main lower bound tends to zero, uniformly over
the choice of those s primes. The surviving price is then at least a
positive constant times k^(1-c log(3/2)). This exponent exceeds1/2.
The statement is unconditional and explicitly counts scope, rather than
reusing a fixed-P asymptotic at growing P. Larger scopes lie outside this
estimate's effective regime; failure of the estimate proves no gain.

There is a separate large-prime survivor, independent of block size.
If every dictionary multiplier is <=L<16k, then its prime factors are
<=L. Every prime n in(16k,20k] is an outside-S anchor and gives B(n)=-1.
Thus the absolute component price is at least

                       pi(20k)-pi(16k).           (17)

With the explicitly imported premise pi(20k)-pi(16k)>=c k/log(k),
as supplied by the prime number theorem for large k, this rules out any
O(k^(1/2+epsilon)) unsigned component price for epsilon<1/2 when L<16k.
This review does not prove or require that external prime-counting
premise for its unconditional claims(11),(13),(15),(16). Formula(17)
itself is exact without the premise. Treating prime signs as known merely
aggregates their positive contribution; cancellation against other rough
components would require a signed control beyond component triangle.

Small multiplier rank does NOT imply small anchor rank. The surviving
anchors in(15),(17) are at the current radius20k. Even an edge am<->bm
with a,b small can involve m of order k; its identity licenses the sign
relation, not a smaller-rank Mobius premise on that anchor. If multipliers
reach order k to absorb such primes, their factorization/primality scope
has itself reached the current rank. The same common-rank and base audit
as071 remains necessary.

A full P block has2^s formal divisor terms, although support pruning can
reduce active terms. This is a grammar/evaluation charge, not an automatic
lower bound on every possible implementation. The dictionary, all graph
vertices/edges used, exact factorization certificates, capacity checks,
support extensions, and shared-vector price algorithm must have explicit
costs. One cannot substitute s alone for maximum arithmetic query radius.

At the opposite extreme, prime division edges for every needed prime,
with all squarefree vertices through radius R present, connect every such
vertex to1. Its label is then mu(n), and(7) has a single known anchor with
V_j=z_j. Thus complete-scope transport reconstructs the exact target even
without receiving a mu table. This is the orbit analogue of070's complete
ideal correction, not an independent uniform growth theorem.

7. A different composable reduction: graph-aggregated signed energy
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Transport can be used without taking an unsigned component triangle.
Here is a complete conditional transfer whose diagonal is uniformly paid.
Use one finite valid exchange graph common to a dyadic row block I,
and retain the actual shared anchor signs t_C=mu(a_C). After discarding
only certified squareful components, define

 D_I^G=sum_C sum_(j in I)V_(j,C)^2,
 C_I^G=sum_(C!=C')t_C t_(C')sum_(j in I)V_(j,C)V_(j,C').

Expanding actual squares gives exactly

             sum_(j in I)z_j^2=D_I^G+C_I^G>=0.    (18)

All ordered cross pairs are included. If the graph is built without a
squarefree mask, use the exact diagonal with t_C^2 and bound it above by
the same unsigned D_I^G; squareful components and their cross terms have
actual coefficient zero. No independent copies of signs are introduced.

Let Q bound, for every row and component, the number of vertices in that
component on which k_j is nonzero. Zero-kernel intermediates do not count.
Cauchy-Schwarz within each component proves

 D_I^G<=Q sum_(j in I,n)squarefree(n) k_j(n)^2
       <=Q D_I^original<=4Q M1/L                 (19)

for I subset[L,2L-1], using067's actual-source diagonal theorem. The
squarefree mask in the middle line is1 or0, not an independent arithmetic
copy. For unmasked graphs the weaker all-n sum gives the same final
bound from067's proof. Q may be replaced by the exact row/component
occupancies inside the sum when that finer source norm is proved.
The displayed coefficient4 uses the admitted nonnegative r<=1 range.
For a different nonnegative bounded r, replace4 by2(1+r), as follows
directly from |k_j(n)|<=2(1+r) and quotient telescoping. Parameter
dependence of M1 and D is retained; p or q varying cannot silently be
absorbed into a universal N-only constant.

Partition the head into B dyadic row blocks, each starting at L and of
size at most L. Let Q_I be its declared occupancy cap. From(18),(19),
the COMPLETE original head obeys

 sum_head |c_j|
 <=sum_head|d_j|+2 sqrt(M1) sum_I sqrt(Q_I)
   +sum_I sqrt(|I| max(C_I^G,0)).                 (20)

Proof: rowwise Cauchy bounds a block's l1 by the square root of its size
times its actual energy; sqrt(x+y)<=sqrt(x)+sqrt(max(y,0)) for x>=0 and
x+y>=0 then separates its paid diagonal and its signed cross risk.
For a fixed registered source M1=O(N log(2N)), B=O(log(2N)). If all
Q_I are polylogarithmic, the paid part is O(sqrt(N) polylog(N)). If
Q_I<=N^delta, its explicit exponent is(1+delta)/2, with the usual cofinal
delta/epsilon quantifiers required for every-epsilon transfer. For a
whole P block Q<=2^s, giving explicit diagonal inflation2^(s/2).

Equation(20) is a meaningful new modular reduction: geometry-adaptive
local exact cancellation can alter cross-component kernels while the
entire diagonal has a proved cost. It avoids claiming that(13)'s linear
absolute residual is already small. The precise unclosed leaf is the
last sum in(20), with actual current-rank shared anchor signs. A useful
detector output must prove its uniform bound or a signed recurrence
controlling it. Evaluating its finite value, assuming independent signs,
or dropping it repeats the existing target rather than pays it.

This is closely related to067's signed off-diagonal route, but it applies
after detector-chosen arithmetic exchanges and tracks the resulting
diagonal inflation. It is not claimed as a new unconditional signed
estimate. If graph selection uses actual mu or cross-risk evaluation,
that data must be evaluator-only unless the adaptive protocol explicitly
licenses it; a finite selection gain then still needs a uniform theorem.

8. Admission conditions and disposition
---------------------------------------
A geometry-only exchange search can honestly discover a matching or
transport rule, verify its exact identities and capacities, and produce
a reusable paid constant-factor gain such as(13). It cannot claim a new
sublinear growth law merely from an optimal finite transport cost.
Any all-scale claim must specify the dictionary as a function of source
parameters, certify every edge's coprimality and factorization, retain
unmatched vertices and zero extensions, prove its source norm bound,
pay growing arithmetic rank and construction costs, and keep the same
joined kernel, shared signs, units and direct atoms.

The strongest viable result of this review is(20) with its explicit
cross-component leaf, together with the exact exchange/component lemmas.
For pure unsigned transport, fixed finite dictionaries are blocked on
the actual source, single-prime disjoint matchings cannot improve it at
all, and slowly growing prime scope still leaves the quantitative
survivor(15). Larger multiplier scope may compute the complete target;
that extreme must be distinguished from a new uniform inequality.
Bounding the actual J or complete c still requires signed control across
the remaining current-rank anchors, plus the explicitly paid071 moment
term if translating to J. No uniform Mertens invariant or RH theorem has
been proved here.
