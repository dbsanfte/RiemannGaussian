1. ESTABLISHED FRACTIONAL ZETA DERIVATIVE

Emanuel Guariglia, Riemann zeta fractional derivative--functional equation
and link with primes, Advances in Difference Equations 2019, article 261.
Primary article: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13662-019-2202-5
Archived published PDF: literature/fractional/guariglia_2019.pdf, obtained
from the German National Library at https://d-nb.info/1198402598/34.

Equation (1.1) is exp(i*pi*alpha)*sum_(n>=2)(log n)^alpha/n^s.
The paper attributes this derivative to Guariglia (2015). Our N_alpha
omits that phase by explicit convention. This establishes that N_alpha
is a known fractional zeta derivative, not a discovered primitive.
The forward Caputo formula (2.1) also supplies established context for
our integration-by-parts evaluation. The paper's Theorem 4.3 concerns
a convergence/divergence comparison; it is not an imported prime-count
identity, zero-free theorem, or RH proof in this detector.

2. ESTABLISHED INTEGER-ORDER ARITHMETIC LOGARITHMS

Pratt et al., More than five-twelfths of the zeros of zeta are on the
critical line, Research in the Mathematical Sciences (2020), section 2:
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40687-019-0199-8
The paper defines generalized von Mangoldt functions mu*log^k for
integer k. That is exactly our Lambda_alpha at integer order. Extending
the exponent to a real parameter cannot alone establish originality.
Ordinary Dirichlet inversion and perfect-power Mobius removal W also
have established prior art recorded in novelty_audit.txt.

3. CLOSEST LOGARITHMIC-FRACTIONAL SOURCE: COMPARISON INCOMPLETE

Guariglia, Fractional calculus, Riemann zeta function and Euler products,
IFAC-PapersOnLine 58(12) (2024), 215-219, doi:10.1016/j.ifacol.2024.08.192.
Primary abstract: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405896324009169
It explicitly discusses logarithmic fractional derivatives and an Euler
product generalization using a homomorphism. The full formulas were not
retrieved: the publisher returned 403 for the PDF, while its public
Elsevier endpoint supplied bibliographic metadata only. Access evidence
is preserved in literature/fractional/reference_access.json and
literature/fractional/elsevier_2024_xml.xml. No access restrictions were
bypassed, and the abstract is not treated as a full formula comparison.

There are two materially different candidate meanings of logarithmic
fractional derivative: D_alpha(log zeta) and (D_alpha zeta)/zeta.
For alpha=1 these agree (up to the derivative sign convention).
For alpha=1/2 they differ at squarefree pq: the former has zero
coefficient there, while the latter has
sqrt(log(pq))-sqrt(log p)-sqrt(log q), which is strictly negative.
The first interpretation, followed by normalization and W, gives the
classical prime indicator at every order; the second gives our mixed
composite coefficients. Which meaning the 2024 paper uses must be
checked against its actual definitions, not inferred from its title.
