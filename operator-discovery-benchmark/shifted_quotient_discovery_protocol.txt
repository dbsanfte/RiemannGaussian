SHIFTED QUOTIENT STRUCTURAL CANDIDATE STUDY -- 9 October 2026

Purpose: search complete signed arithmetic-factor interactions, not improve
the optimizer on previously selected reversible circuits. No historical
novelty claim. Twenty-five fixed programs, with no fitted coefficients.

Atom H_b(s)=(zeta(s+b)/zeta(s)-1)/b; distinct rational shifts in
{1/6,1/3,1/2,2/3,5/6}; all products of one, two or three atoms.
General parser allows rational denominators up to24. At finite positive b
these coefficients include composites, not only prime powers. The pointwise
zero-shift limit has coefficients (-1)^k Lambda^{*k}; no uniform interchange
in x is asserted. Every overlap and composite complement is retained.

Summatory outputs subtract every simple real-pole residue term, including
terms whose exponent is below one half. No regression fits these terms.
The normalizer always uses true zeta, including for negative controls.
The subtraction of delta_1 is exact. It is not a Perron constant fit.

Complete signed energy E(X)=X^-2 sum_{X<=n<2X} G(n)^2. Absolute envelope
retains cumulative absolute coefficient values plus every absolute main
term. Its cancellation ratio is diagnostic only and never selects a lead.

Comparator for degree k is (mu-delta_1)^{*k}, with its complete summatory
energy. This has the same Dirichlet pole order and the same factor-index
lag as the candidate; comparison to mu^{*k} would falsely reward removing
fresh coordinates. Neither comparison establishes a quantitative inverse.

times its same-degree comparator and pass every training-constant growth
test. Interval-indeterminate comparisons fail admission conservatively.

Within each degree select exactly one challenger by (number of failed
validation growth tests, maximum validation E/E_comparator, candidate id).
This ranking concerns the full energy, never internal cancellation alone.
Keep admission status separate: a challenger can fail the numerical-lead
gate and remains a diagnostic challenge, not a successful RH candidate.
Rank uses floating midpoints only to order finite interval results; every
inequality claim/admission decision uses rigorous interval comparisons.
