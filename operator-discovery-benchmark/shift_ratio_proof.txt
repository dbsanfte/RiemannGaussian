UNEQUAL-SHIFT ARITHMETIC LOGARITHMS AND ZETA ZERO RETENTION
7 October 2026. A candidate family; historical priority is unresolved.
No new prime distribution estimate or RH proof is claimed.

1. GENERAL COEFFICIENT DEFINITION AND INVERSE

For a normalized arithmetic function f(1)=1 let q be its Dirichlet
convolution inverse. For v>0 define R_v(f)(1)=0 and

 R_v(f)(n) = sum_(ab=n,b>1) q(a) f(b) log(b)/(log(b)+v log(a)). (1)
 B_v(f) = W R_v(f),  W=sum_(k>=1) mu(k) U_k/k.

U_k places coefficients at perfect powers, with input coefficient 1 zero.
V=sum U_k/k is the inverse of W by the divisor identity sum_(d|j)mu(d).
The a=1 term of (1) is f(n); every other a,b is less than n. Given B,
apply V, then solve (1) for f(n) in increasing n, updating q(n) by
Dirichlet inversion. This proves a coefficient-local triangular bijection
for arbitrary real or complex normalized formal units. It supplies no
prime indicators, prime factors or prime weights to the coefficient engine.

At v=1 the kernel is log(b)/log(n), so R_1=LOG_D and B_1 is the
classical Mobius perfect-power prime decoder. For v!=1, R_v is nonlinear
in Dirichlet products. At n=pq, p!=q, input zeta^beta multiplies its
zeta coefficient by beta^2, rather than beta. Ordinary root commutation
is therefore false. Formal invertibility does not assert a homomorphism.

2. UNIVERSAL SIGN, SHARP PRIME GAP AND LEAST-PRIME ENVELOPE

Put u(n)=1, c=1-v, 0<v<1, n=product p^e_p, m=omega(n),
g=gcd(e_p), t_p=log(p)/log(n), and

 T_j(t)=sum_(S subset primes)(-1)^(m-|S|)(sum_(p in S)t_p)^j,
 J_(j-1)(g)=sum_(d|g)mu(d)d^(j-1)
            =product_(ell|g,ell prime)(1-ell^(j-1)).

The rational kernel h(S)=(1-S)/(1-c*S), with S=log(a)/log(n), has
geometric weights b_j=v*c^(j-1)>0, sum b_j=1. Subset differences give

 R_v(u)(n)=(-1)^(m-1) sum_(j>=m)b_j T_j(t),
 B_v(u)(n)=(-1)^(m-1) sum_(j>=m)b_j T_j(t) J_(j-1)(g).     (2)

T_j is zero for j<m and positive for j>=m by its multinomial expansion.
All series converge absolutely, including sum t_p=1, since c<1.
For g>1 the j=1 term vanishes and every j>1 Jordan factor has sign
(-1)^omega(g). For g=1 it equals 1. Consequently

 sign B_v(u)(n)=(-1)^(omega(n)+omega(g)-1), for every n>=2.  (3)

Also |J_(j-1)(g)|<=g^(j-1), so

 |B_v(n)| <= |R_v(n^(1/g))|/g.                            (4)

The geometric expansion has the positive integral

 |R_v(n)|=v/c integral_0^infinity
   exp[-(1-c sum t_p)*z] product_(p|n)(1-exp(-c*t_p*z)) dz. (5)

Since sum t_p<=1, the exponential is at most exp(-v*z).
For m>=2 discard all but two factors. Their product is maximal, at
fixed sum <=1, when both steps are 1/2. Integration gives

 |R_v(n)| <= delta_v=(1-v)/(1+v),
 |B_v(n)| <= delta_v/g, if m>=2.                          (6)

For n=p^e, e>1, (2) and |J| bound give
|B_v(p^e)| <= (sum_(j>=2)b_j)/e = (1-v)/e <= delta_v.
For n=p, B_v(p)=1. Thus every prime has value 1 and every composite
has modulus <=delta_v<1, at every scale. The gap is sharp: for pq
with log p/log q tending to 1, (5) tends to its balanced two-step value.
Bertrand's theorem supplies such prime pairs; this is established input.
At v=1 the composite coefficients vanish, and (3) no longer asserts
strict signs. Uniform coefficient convergence to the prime indicator
as v tends to 1 does not imply uniform critical-strip convergence.

Dropping all but the least step in (5), then using (4), gives

 |B_v(n)| <= t/(v+(1-v)*g*t) <= t/(v+(1-v)*t) <= t/v,
 t=log P^-(n)/log n.                                      (7)

The exponent one is sharp: at n=2q with q prime tending to infinity,
the magnitude is (1/v-v)*t+O(t^2). For reference the pure-power value is
R_v(p^e)=v/(e-1+v) and B_v(p^e)=sum_(d|e)mu(d)*v/[e+(v-1)d].
Extrapolating (3) to v>1 fails, for example at v=3/2 and n=64.

3. ALL-SCALE SECOND MOMENTS, WITH A DECLARED SIEVE IMPORT

We import the published theorem Phi(x,y)<x/log(y), x>=y>=2,
where Phi includes 1 and integers whose least prime exceeds y.
For 1<y<2 the same inequality follows from Phi(x,y)<=x< x/log(y).
Primary source: Fan and Pomerance, Theorem A, author manuscript:
https://math.dartmouth.edu/~carlp/erat060523rev.pdf
The full external sieve proof has not been independently formalized here.

For integer X>=2, put V_n=min(1,log P^-(n)/log X) on X<=n<=2X.
The number with V_n>t is at most 2X/(t log X), 0<t<1.
Boundary equalities occur at finitely many t and do not change the
layer-cake integral. Let h(t)=t/[v+(1-v)t]. From (7),

 sum_(X<=n<=2X)|B_v(n)|^2
 <= integral_0^1 (h(t)^2)' * 2X/(t log X) dt
 = 2(1+v)/v * X/log X.                                   (8)

This includes v=1 by continuity or direct integration. For complete
Fourier rows A_a=sum sqrt(w_n)B_v(n)exp(2pi i a n/H), H>=X+1,
0<=w_n<=1, orthogonality gives sum_a |A_a|^2=H sum_n w_n|B_v(n)|^2.
Thus (8) multiplies by H. Residue injectivity is necessary; H<=X is
not certified. At v=1/2, M2<=6X/log X. This is a known-sieve
consequence, not an independent prime-distribution or cancellation bound.

4. FRACTIONAL LOGARITHMS ARE A BETA MIXTURE OF THIS FAMILY

If 0<alpha<1 and V has Beta(alpha,1-alpha) density, Euler's beta
integral gives E[1/(1+x V)]=(1+x)^(-alpha), x>=0.
Taking x=log(a)/log(b) yields

 E[log(b)/(log(b)+V log(a))]=(log(b)/log(ab))^alpha.

Therefore R_alpha(f)=E R_V(f) and B_alpha(f)=E B_V(f), coefficient
by coefficient, for every normalized formal unit. For zeta these also
hold as absolutely convergent Dirichlet series on Re(s)>1. At alpha=1/2,
V=sin(theta)^2 for uniform theta in [0,pi/2]. The deterministic kernel
is distinct from every single fractional kernel for 0<v<1: its geometric
weights have b_1=v, b_2=v(1-v), whereas the fractional weights have
b_1=alpha, b_2=alpha(1-alpha)/2. Equality of both is impossible.

A positive mixture supported on v>=epsilon>0 preserves prime value
one and the nonzero local zero signatures below. Jensen and (8) give
M2 <= 2(1+E[1/V])*X/log X if that inverse moment is finite.
Every finite positive mixture has the same triangular inverse using
its averaged kernel, since the a=1 term remains f(n). Its sharp composite
gap is E[(1-V)/(1+V)]; its zero-order coefficient is m*E[V^(-m)]>0.
The public grammar accepts arbitrary finite rational atoms and positive
rational weights with exact sum one. No particular beta law, zero order
or QRH estimate is supplied to choose the atoms. Three changed mixtures
are checked on arbitrary real and complex units and on actual integers.
The beta mixture has infinite E[1/V] for alpha<1; hence (8) cannot
be averaged into a finite fractional M2 constant by this argument.

The compound family also has an established-primitive expansion. Let
R_j(f)=(f_Dinv * [(log n)^j f(n)])/(log n)^j, with j a positive integer.
For v>1/2 and q=(1-v)/v<1, geometric expansion of t/(v+(1-v)t) gives

 R_v(f)=(1/v) sum_(j>=1)(-q)^(j-1) R_j(f).                 (4a)

This is coefficient-local absolute convergence: 0<=t=log b/log(ab)<=1.
For zeta a K-term remainder is <=2^omega(n)*q^K/(2v-1).
At v=1/2 even the prime coefficient's geometric series oscillates between
2 and 0. Equation (4a) cannot be extrapolated there as a convergent series,
although the rational kernel itself remains well defined. Thus this is
a resummation/deformation built from known integer-logarithm primitives,
not evidence for a historically new primitive. Exact historical priority
of the full compound construction and its estimates remains unresolved.

5. DIRECT NATIVE ZETA DEFINITION AND COMPLETE INFINITE TAILS

For Re(s)>1, absolute Euler series and termwise integration give

 R_v(s)=-integral_0^infinity zeta'(s+t)/zeta(s+v*t) dt,
 B_v(s)=sum_(k>=1)mu(k) R_v(k*s)/k.                        (9)

Equations (2) and (5) give |R_v(n)|<=1 for 0<v<=1. The native R
integral truncated at T has error at most
zeta(sigma+v*T)[zeta(sigma+T)-1], sigma=Re(s)>1, using
|1/zeta(s+v*t)|<=zeta(sigma+v*t) and the differentiated Euler series.
For B truncated after k=K, its remaining tail is at most

 2^(-(K+1)*sigma)/(K+1)/(1-2^(-sigma))
                         *[1+2/((K+1)*sigma-1)].           (10)

Here zeta(x)-1<=2^-x[1+2/(x-1)], and the geometric tail is dominated
at its first term. A coefficient prefix through N has omitted tail
<=N^(1-sigma)/(sigma-1), by |B_v(n)|<=1. The independent analytic
checker changes variables to u=v*t, changes cutoffs and meshes, and
uses factor/subset coefficients instead of the production recursion.

6. LOCAL ZERO SIGNATURE WITHOUT AN UNKNOWN FRACTIONAL NUMERATOR

Let F be meromorphic, normalized at right infinity, with a locally
uniformly convergent defining integral tail in the neighbourhood under
discussion. Let rho be an isolated zero of order m>=1 with a zero-free
rightward corridor. These tail assumptions hold for zeta by its Euler
series and for the rational calibration toys directly. Put
w=s-rho. In a small initial integration segment, F(s+v*t) has a pole
in its reciprocal at t=-w/v of order m. Subtract its partial fractions.
The only multivalued term after integration is A_v(w)log(w), where
A_v(w) is minus the residue of -F'(s+t)/F(s+v*t) at t=-w/v.
At w=0, F'(rho+t)/F(rho+v*t)=m/v^m * 1/t+O(1), hence

 A_v(0)=m/v^m !=0.                                       (11)

The higher partial-fraction coefficients vanish to the appropriate
orders in w: expanding F(rho+z)=z^m a(z), a(0)!=0, the coefficient
of (w+v*t)^(-j) is divisible by w^(j-1). Its integrated term at the
lower endpoint is therefore holomorphic. Thus the whole local form
is A_v(w)log(w)+holomorphic, not merely an unexplained leading fit.
For a simple zero the full analytic logarithmic coefficient is

 A_v(w)=F'(rho+(1-1/v)*w)/(v F'(rho)).                     (12)

This is structurally unconditional at a zero with the stated corridor;
it needs no separate nonvanishing of a fractional numerator. For
Re(rho)>1/2, all scaled k>=2 terms of B_v are regular near rho because
Re(k*rho)>1. They converge normally. Hence B_v retains (11).
The tested critical-line zeros additionally require zero-free k=2
corridors, which are certified from earlier independently checked disks.
No new zeta zero is located and the old disks are not a global zero audit.

7. A POLE-REGULARIZED RH EQUIVALENCE, NOT AN RH PROOF

Fix 0<v<1, G(s)=1/zeta(s), a(s)=v+(1-v)s. On Re(s)>1 define

 T_v(s)=B_v(s)+v G'(a(s)) log(s-1),                        (13)

using the real-positive branch to the right of 1. RH is equivalent to
single-valued holomorphic continuation of T_v to Re(s)>1/2.

Under RH, G is holomorphic in that half-plane and vanishes simply
at 1. The integral in (9) is holomorphic off the slit (1/2,1], since
the denominator has no zeros and its pole becomes a reciprocal zero.
The only numerator pole is the double pole of -zeta' at s+t=1.
Its t residue is v G'(a(s)). Its integrated logarithmic term is
-v G'(a(s))log(s-1), canceled by (13). The double-pole endpoint term
is G(a(s))/(s-1), which is holomorphic at 1 since G(a(1))=0.
Contour deformation across the slit gives the same residue cancellation
at every interior point. The scaled k>=2 sum is normally convergent
and holomorphic throughout Re(s)>1/2. This proves the forward direction.

Conversely suppose an off-line zero has real part >1/2. Choose a finite
height T above its absolute ordinate and unequal to every zero ordinate.
Among the finitely many zeros with |Im rho|<T choose one with maximal
real part sigma>1/2. A small rightward strip at its ordinate is zero-free.
Thus (11) applies to the branch obtained from Re(s)>1. At a(rho),
Re a(rho)>sigma and |Im a(rho)|<T, so G'(a(s)) is holomorphic near rho.
Also log(s-1) is holomorphic there. The k>=2 terms are holomorphic.
They cannot cancel the nonzero logarithmic coefficient m/v^m in (11).
This contradicts single-valued holomorphic continuation of T_v.
Zeros with real part <1/2 are paired to those >1/2 by the functional
equation, so the reverse direction implies RH. This argument uses a
finite-height extremal zero, not an assumed globally rightmost zero.

The same equivalence holds for EVERY finite positive mixture:

 T_nu(s)=B_nu(s)+log(s-1)*sum_i w_i*v_i*G'(v_i+(1-v_i)s).

Under RH this is a finite positive sum of the holomorphic T_vi.
For the reverse direction the same finite-height extremal zero works
simultaneously for all v_i. Every regularizer is holomorphic there,
while the logarithmic coefficient is m*sum_i w_i/v_i^m>0. No choice
of a particular atomic law or presumed zero multiplicity is needed.
This conclusion cannot be transferred to the beta mixture merely by
interchanging its local limit: E[V^(-m)] diverges for that law. The
coefficient beta identity on Re(s)>1 remains valid, but its local zero
asymptotics do not follow from a uniform finite-mixture logarithm limit.

Equivalence (13) gives a concrete zero-sensitive target to the detector.
The coefficient second moment (8) does not prove the required
holomorphic continuation. That unresolved step is the root difficulty.

8. PRIORITY AND VERIFICATION STATUS

Validity, empirical rediscovery and historical novelty are separate.
The primitive Dirichlet inverse, logarithmic weights, reciprocal zeta,
perfect-power Mobius inversion, beta integral and layer-cake/sieve tools
are established. The compound unequal-shift family and (13) require a
primary-source priority review; absence of search hits is not evidence
enough. The missing full 2024 fractional-calculus paper remains an
explicit comparison gap. Numerical checks do not verify the external
sieve proof, global RH, or historical priority.
