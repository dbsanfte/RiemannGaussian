Post-outcome solver remedies for the enlarged factor cone

The frozen critical CLARABEL failure and unsigned exact-reconstruction resource failure remain original outcomes. A floating objective about 4.0002 is neither an exact certificate nor proof of infeasibility. The supplied unsigned witness lies in the enlarged cone by zero extension: retain its 106 nonnegative square weights, 231 signed relation weights and both rational PSD Gram matrices, append 176 and 88 zero weights respectively. Old groups, lhs and horizons must match exactly. The independent polynomial/PSD checker can reuse the old exact certificates, since zero-weight additions change no polynomial. This checks supplied finite cone membership; it supplies no critical estimate and performs no new optimization. The separate embedding audit records this check and input hashes.

A useful generic fallback is a sparse LP on a declared subset of the SAME cone, not a relaxation whose answer is accepted as a certificate. Fix rational PSD Gram matrices (including zero, a prior certified pair, or independently certified rational proposals), move their exact polynomial contribution to the lhs, and solve for all structural nonnegative square weights, signed relation weights and nonnegative scalar slacks. A feasible exact reconstruction belongs to the full cone. LP failure says nothing about the full PSD cone. The unsigned calibration provides a known feasibility sanity check for this route, without supplying critical weights. A warm start may use a prior solution padded with zeros; this is disclosed supplied initialization, not discovery.

For a floating SDP proposal, use a predeclared support-thinning ladder on scalar coefficients: for example relative thresholds 10^-4,10^-6,10^-8,10^-10,0, with deterministic ties, together with explicit support and work caps. Relations have signed weights; nonnegative scalar columns must retain their sign constraint. Reconstruct exact coefficient equality on each retained support using rational elimination, and check EVERY original monomial, not merely the support equations. Record every rejected/missing attempt and threshold. A threshold cannot silently remove a column then certify the truncated target. Support selection is a heuristic; exhaustion is not infeasibility.

Gram sparsification is more delicate: entrywise rounding, deleting small off-diagonals, or an exact coefficient correction can destroy PSD. Every proposed pair must pass rational PSD verification and full original polynomial equality. One may fix already certified matrices and run the LP fallback, or parameterize nonnegative combinations of fixed rational rank-one directions, which is again a declared subset of the full PSD cone. Neither method proves full-cone infeasibility when it fails. Coefficient rescaling or monomial-row equilibration is permitted only with an explicit invertible rational map and exact conversion back to the original column system.

The reconstruction preflight should depend on the ACTUAL retained support after exact zero removal, rather than a worst-case dense column count when the witness is sparse. Polynomial expansion of zero-weight groups can be skipped while complete identity verification still checks the original lhs. This reduces implementation cost without changing the cone. Denominator/bit/work/time caps remain explicit; a cap failure remains missing_resource. Larger caps require a new sealed run, preserving this run's failure. No SDP rerun or new fitted score is included in this note.

The arithmetic gate remains separate from numerical remedy: zero formal scalars on the 352 new factor columns leave the old scalar budget module and mu cubic gamma condition unchanged. A newly admitted finite certificate still requires an all-scale nonconstant identity and a justified critical scalar-budget bound. The supplied unsigned witness has cubic cost; its exact embedding is a robustness check, not progress on that uniform signed bound.
