Coordinate objective and exact one-coordinate formula
Let A*x=b be the retained302-row,687-column system after positive exact
row/column infinity scaling, with x>=0 and r=A*x-b. For a fixed lambda>=0,
the new numerical objective is
  F_lambda(x)=1/2*||A*x-b||_2^2+lambda*sum_j x_j.
For nonzero column a_j and a proposed coordinate change delta, the varying
part is delta*(a_j^T*r+lambda)+1/2*delta^2*||a_j||_2^2,
subject to delta>=-x_j. Its exact minimizer is
  x_j_new=max(0,x_j-(a_j^T*r+lambda)/||a_j||_2^2),
  delta=x_j_new-x_j, r_new=r+delta*a_j.
Thus the prescribed update is the constrained coordinate minimizer in exact
real arithmetic. Keeping the original nonnegative coordinates avoids an
unrestricted elimination-variable interpretation. The fixed ascending
retained-column order determines the cyclic trajectory.

Positive row scaling preserves the equation system, and positive column
scaling is an invertible nonnegative change of coordinates. In particular,
strict support membership is unchanged under an exact positive scale.
The unit L1 cost in scaled coordinates is a new heuristic objective; it
need not preserve original representative L1 values or their minimizers.
That choice is sound because the final admission concerns feasibility and
a full exact identity, not a numerical minimum or an original objective.

Penalty schedule, residual refresh and phase termination
At x=0 the constrained gradient is -a_j^T*b+lambda. Therefore
lambda_ref=max(0,max_j a_j^T*b) is the smallest nonnegative penalty making
the zero vector satisfy the exact coordinate stationarity inequalities.
The frozen schedule uses lambda_ref*2^-e for odd e=1,3,...,31, then zero:
sixteen positive-exponent phases plus one final phase, seventeen in total.
lambda_ref may be zero; repeated zero penalties remain prescribed phases.
Every phase warm starts from the previous phase's complete vector.

A phase may stop after a full sweep changes no stored coordinate. This is
a floating trajectory stop condition, not a convergence, KKT or exact
optimality theorem. Roundoff can make an update unchanged while a real
coordinate optimum differs. Conversely, residual drift can change an update
or apparent score without changing the exact feasibility of any rational
support. Refresh the complete residual from A*x-b every16 completed sweeps
and at every phase end. If a phase ends on the16th-sweep boundary, the
periodic refresh and mandatory phase-end refresh may both be charged.
Only after the phase-end refresh and finite metrics are complete may a
checkpoint be appended. A partial interrupted phase is not a checkpoint.

Numerical safeguards
Use a stable Euclidean norm such as math.hypot for target, columns and
refreshed residual. Target norm must be positive; every column norm and
its squared denominator must be positive and finite. In the actual exact
infinity-scaled model, a nonzero column has an entry of absolute value1,
so its squared norm is at least1 and at most the row count. Generic tiny
helpers still need guards against zero or underflowed squared denominators.
Stable dot accumulation reduces avoidable cancellation but does not turn
binary64 scores into exact evidence.
The implemented refresh collects finite sparse products by row and uses
math.fsum over those products and -b_i. Its nnz+m temporary entries remain
in the numerical peak allowance. This improves cancellation handling without
certifying the rounded products or refreshed score.

Reject nonfinite input conversion, norms, penalty reference/penalties, dot
products, update arithmetic, stored coordinates, residual updates and
diagnostics. A clamp to zero must not conceal an infinite intermediate.
Exact nonzero coefficients converting to floating zero must be rejected.
Relative residual is a finite residual norm divided by a finite positive
target norm. Neither a small residual nor a finite objective supplies an
arithmetic error bound or interval enclosure. Resource/nonfinite interruption
must mark the numerical trajectory incomplete and forbid restoration from
its partial vector or an earlier checkpoint.

Frozen sparse work reservation
For dimensions m,n and exact nonzero count z, define
  setup=8z+32(m+n)+65536,
  sweep=8z+32n+2m+64,
  refresh=8z+16(m+n)+256.
Choose the largest common integer S in1..10000 satisfying
  setup+17*(S*sweep+(ceil(S/16)+1)*refresh)<=190000000.
An integer binary search can choose S solely from m,n,z and allocation,
before coefficient-dependent norms, penalties or scores. If no S fits,
return S=0 and perform no iteration. Seventeen complete phases with k_p
completed sweeps each use at most
  setup+sum_p k_p*sweep+sum_p(floor(k_p/16)+1)*refresh,
which the declared ceil(S/16)+1 reserve bounds, including early phase stops.
This deliberately conservative formula is a declared sparse work proxy;
it is not a measured Python/BLAS operation count or convergence guarantee.
The refresh allowance was increased from4z to8z before tests/freeze so
stable per-row math.fsum accumulation and its sparse temporary arrays are
explicitly paid by the frozen numerical reservation.

Charge setup before setup arithmetic, a whole sweep before visiting its
coordinates and a refresh before recomputation. Inner charge(0) calls only
check deadlines; they do not add work beyond the frozen blocks. Counters
must retain blocks already charged when a sweep or refresh is interrupted.
For every outcome the exact charged numerical total is
  setup_charged*setup+sweeps_charged*sweep+refreshes_charged*refresh.
Completed trajectories require17 finite checkpoints, per-phase sweep caps
and matching charged-block counts. Coordinate visits and residual-entry
updates receive consistent bounds; all support scans, finite checks,
checkpoint arrays and copies must fit the declared proxy allowances.
