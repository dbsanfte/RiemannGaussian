Signed rough-number leading term: derivation
Define A(x,y)=sum_(n<=x, Pminus(n)>y) mu(n), with1 included (Pminus(1)=infinity).
Define Dickman rho(s)=1 on0<=s<=1 and rho'(s)=-rho(s-1)/s for s>1.
For fixed U>1, the signed rough asymptotic is
 A(x,x^(1/U))=-rho(U-1)*x/log x+o(x/log x).
The proposed hypothesis has the correct sign and normalization.

Exact smallest-prime decomposition gives
 A(x,y)=1-sum_(y<p<=x) A(x/p,p).
This is EXACT for mu: the cofactor of the smallest prime has all its prime
factors strictly above p, so repeated p never enters. Split p>sqrt(x),
where the inner sum is1. The prime contribution is -pi(x)+pi(sqrt(x)).
For p=x^t, y<p<=sqrt(x), bounded-U induction and PNT/partial summation give
 inner A(x/p,p)~-(x/p)*rho(1/t-2)/((1-t)log x).
Consequently the leading coefficient h(U) satisfies
 h(U)=-1+integral_(1/U)^(1/2) rho(1/t-2)/(t*(1-t)) dt
     =-1+integral_1^(U-1) rho(s-1)/s ds
     =-rho(U-1).
For1<U<=2 the integral is empty and h=-1, consistent with primes only.

For completeness, the induction is on successive bounded intervals of U.
On a compact subinterval below t=1/2 the inner parameter is bounded away
from1 and the PNT induction is uniform. Boundary strips can be removed
and restored by domination: numbers with all prime factors>y, x<=y^B,
are O_B(x/log y)+1. To see this bound, split by their bounded number of
prime factors, choose all but the largest, bound that largest prime by
pi(x/m)<=C*x/(m*log y), and bound the remaining reciprocal prime sums
by O_B(1) using PNT/partial summation. The extra1 contributes O(sqrt x)
in the recursion. Thus dominated convergence applies to the prime integral.
One can also expand the finite squarefree prime-factor simplex directly;
there are finitely many factors because U is fixed. This avoids importing
a separate black-box theorem about signed rough numbers.

Dickman positivity and inexpensive envelope
The delay equation implies, for s>=1,
 s*rho(s)=integral_(s-1)^s rho(t)dt.
The two sides have equal derivative and agree at1. A first zero of rho
would contradict the positive integral over its preceding interval. Hence
rho>0 everywhere, and its differential equation gives strict decrease for
s>1. On[1,2], rho(s)=1-log s. In particular
 rho(3)=1-log3+integral_1^2 log(v)/(v+1)dv.
The integrand is increasing on[1,2], since its derivative has numerator
1+1/v-log v>0. A64-panel right-endpoint sum is therefore an upper bound.
