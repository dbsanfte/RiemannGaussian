1. Exact digit reduction, including the constant identity
=========================================================
For any fixed integer q>=2, P(z)=sum_{j=0}^m c_j z^j, P(q)=0, put

 digit_r(y)=floor(q^r y)-q floor(q^(r-1)y), r=1,...,m,
 b_r=sum_{j=r}^m c_j q^(j-r).

The elementary telescoping expansion of floor(q^j y) proves

 sum_j c_j floor(q^j y)=sum_{r=1}^m b_r digit_r(y).              (2)

Each digit is in {0,...,q-1}; all floor endpoints are exact. Also

 sum_r b_r=(P(q)-P(1))/(q-1)=-P(1)/(q-1).                      (3)

Thus P(1)=0 means the digit coefficients have zero sum; it does NOT mean
their mu-weighted arithmetic contributions cancel.

For the selected quadratic define

 W(y)=floor(4y)-3floor(2y)+2floor(y)
     =digit_2(y)-digit_1(y).

Writing r={y}, its exact values are
 0 for 0<=r<1/4; +1 for 1/4<=r<1/2;
 -1 for 1/2<=r<3/4; 0 for 3/4<=r<1.

Hence the entire selected expression is

 T2(N)=-sum_{d<=4N} mu(d)w(d)W(N/d).                            (4)

The comparison expression is

 T1(N)=-sum_{d<=2N} mu(d)w(d)digit_1(N/d).                      (5)

The unit contributes zero in these formulas; all other prime powers and
composites are retained. The bound |W|<=1 is paid, but applying it termwise
to (4) gives only O(N log(N)^alpha), not (S2).

2. Exact reciprocal-interval pairing: the missing arithmetic is visible
=======================================================================
For every integer k>=0 set

 I_k^+(N)=(N/(k+1/2), N/(k+1/4)],
 I_k^-(N)=(N/(k+3/4), N/(k+1/2)].

Let M_alpha(I)=sum_{d integer in I}mu(d)w(d). Formula (4) becomes

 T2(N)=sum_{k>=0} [M_alpha(I_k^-(N))-M_alpha(I_k^+(N))].         (6)

This sum has finite actual integer support. Adjacent intervals share the
exact boundary N/(k+1/2); one interval is open there and the other closed,
as displayed. Their lengths differ, so replacing them by equal-length or
normalized averages drops a complement term. Every gcd/prime-power sector
of mu is contained in (6); the identity is not a prime-only approximation.

There is a reusable fully paid small-denominator removal. For any integer
0<=D<=4N, the d<=D contribution to (4) has magnitude at most

 D*(log(4N))^alpha.                                             (7)

Choose any fixed allocation 0<c0<Ctarget and
D=floor(c0*sqrt(N)/(log(4N))^alpha). Then (7)<=c0 sqrt(N).
A sufficient remaining source lemma is

 |sum_k [M_alpha(I_k^- intersect (D,4N])
             -M_alpha(I_k^+ intersect (D,4N])]|
       <=(Ctarget-c0)*sqrt(N), for all integer N>=2.             (8)

This is a distinct finite reciprocal-interval correlation expression with
an explicit paid head. It is OPEN, and is equivalent to the difficult full
frontier up to the paid head; its mere generation is not a new scale gain.
Separate absolute estimates for its many intervals lose the useful pairing.
Bounds on a single unweighted Mertens prefix do not automatically give (8)
with its declared constant and exponent. An assumed RH-strength companion
bound must be exposed as a dependency, not used as an unconditional provider.

3. Actual mu(2m) pairing: one logarithmic increment is paid, not the target
========================================================================
For odd m, mu(2m)=-mu(m); for a denominator divisible by 4, mu is zero.
Splitting (4) gives the exact complete cofactor formula

 T2(N)=sum_{m odd, m<=4N} mu(m)
       [w(2m)W(N/(2m))-w(m)W(N/m)].                            (9)

The first term vanishes for m>2N, so the common 4N horizon adds no false
terms. Put Delta w(m)=w(2m)-w(m)>0. Then

 T2(N)=Phi(N)+R_delta(N),
 Phi(N)=sum_{m odd, m<=4N}mu(m)w(m)
                      [W(N/(2m))-W(N/m)],
 R_delta(N)=sum_{m odd, m<=2N}mu(m)Delta w(m)W(N/(2m)).          (10)

If digit_0(y)=floor(y)-2floor(y/2), the bracket in Phi is exactly
2 digit_1(y)-digit_0(y)-digit_2(y). It is NOT identically zero. Thus an
unsuppressed w(m) term survives actual prime-2 pairing. Dropping it and
bounding only Delta w would be a false recovery argument.

The unit m=1 must be kept in R_delta: w(1)=0 but w(2)>0. Its contribution
is w(2)W(N/2), and need not vanish at odd integer N.

Concavity proves for m>=2

 0<Delta w(m)<=alpha*log(2)*(log(m))^(alpha-1),
 Delta w(m)<=log(2)^alpha, including m=1.                       (11)

Splitting the sum at sqrt(N) yields the unconditional explicit envelope

 |R_delta(N)|
 <=(1+sqrt(N))*log(2)^alpha
       +2*alpha*log(2)*N*((log(N))/2)^(alpha-1), N>=2.           (12)

The count was enlarged deliberately, so no prime-count estimate is hidden.
This proves a logarithmic improvement over the naive N log(N)^alpha envelope,
but its second term is still larger than sqrt(N) asymptotically. It cannot
pay (S2). Further fixed-order log differences produce more logarithmic
savings, not a power saving by themselves. The signed joint Phi+R_delta is
the exact frontier; bounding these pieces independently can lose cancellation.

For perspective, without w the odd-divisor identity is unusually explicit:

 sum_{m odd, m<=x} mu(m)floor(x/m)
  =number of powers of 2 not exceeding x.

Indeed sum_{m|n, m odd}mu(m) is 1 exactly when n has no odd prime divisor,
and is zero otherwise. For real x>=1 the count is 1+floor(log_2 x).
It follows that sum_{m odd}mu(m)W(x/m)=-1 for x>=1, and the corresponding
unweighted difference in (9) is zero for x>=2. This explains a genuine
unweighted cancellation. It does NOT extend to w(m): the weighted residual
in (10) is precisely the information that the unweighted identity omits.

4. Output prime transport: a positive defect and a full joint frontier
======================================================================
Unlike the general 051 kernel with b>0, this b=0 K depends only on rad(n).
Thus for every prime p,

 p|m => K(pm)=K(m),
 p not dividing m, m>=2 => 0<K(pm)<=K(m).                       (13)

The latter follows from multiplying (1)'s integrand by 1-p^-t in [0,1].
The unit m=1 is a mandatory exception: K(1)=0 and K(p)=log(p)^alpha>0.
For odd m>=3 define the prime-2 insertion defect

 D2(m)=K(m)-K(2m)
      =I_alpha^-1 int_0^infinity
          2^-t product_{p|m}(1-p^-t)t^(-alpha-1) dt>0
      =-sum_{d|m}mu(d)*(log(2d))^alpha.                         (14)

This is a positive shifted-log source, with all valuations retained. It is
not a random or independent-prime estimate, and 0<D2(m)<=K(m). Again (14)'s
positivity excludes m=1; its formal difference there is -K(2).

For integer N>=2 put

 B(N)=sum_{N<m<=2N} K(m),
 J(N)=sum_{2N<n<=4N, n odd} K(n),
 D(N)=sum_{N<m<=2N, m odd} D2(m)>=0.

Partition the child block into its even and odd terms. Its even terms are
K(2m), N<m<=2N; when m is even they equal K(m), and when m is odd they
equal K(m)-D2(m). Therefore

 B(2N)=B(N)-D(N)+J(N),
 T2(N)=B(2N)-2B(N)=J(N)-B(N)-D(N).                             (15)

This is a source-specific joint construction. The fresh odd output block
must match the parent block PLUS the known positive prime-2 defect. Neither
pointwise monotonicity nor positivity determines the fresh odd block's mass.
The resulting squared expression retains all six terms:

 (J-B-D)^2=J^2+B^2+D^2-2JB-2JD+2BD.                           (16)

Dropping the positive BD or either negative cross is not an exact proof.
A detector can seek a genuine uniform prediction/transport lemma for these
bound source states. Merely renaming J-B-D as an innovation and assuming its
bound restates the target. The proof task is the actual fresh/parent/defect
correlation, with prime and composite sectors joined, rather than optimizing
an arbitrary constant predictor.

The same decomposition is generic for any FIXED actual prime p. Define

 B_p(N)=sum_{N<m<=pN} K(m),
 J_p(N)=sum_{pN<n<=p^2 N, p not dividing n} K(n),
 D_p(m)=K(m)-K(pm), for p not dividing m and m>=2,
 D_p(N)=sum_{N<m<=pN, p not dividing m} D_p(m).

Then every prime valuation and every residue not divisible by p is retained,
and the exact source-specific identities are

 D_p(m)=I_alpha^-1 int_0^infinity
            p^-t product_{r|m}(1-r^-t)*t^(-alpha-1) dt
       =-sum_{d|m}mu(d)*(log(pd))^alpha>0,
 B_p(pN)=B_p(N)-D_p(N)+J_p(N),
 A(p^2 N)-(p+1)A(pN)+pA(N)
             =J_p(N)-(p-1)B_p(N)-D_p(N).                       (15p)

Here r in the product ranges over distinct prime divisors of m. If p divides
m, its actual defect is ZERO by radical invariance; the displayed positive
integral must NOT be used without the p-free mask. If m=1, its actual defect
is -log(p)^alpha; N>=2 excludes that unit from the block sums but a prefix
version must restore it. These masks are part of the compiler contract.

The positive integral gives a useful unconditional pointwise certificate:

 0<D_p(m)<=K(m),
 D_p(m)<=min_{r|m}[(log(p)+log(r))^alpha-(log(p))^alpha],
 D_p(m)<=alpha*product_{j=1}^{k-1}(j-alpha)
                *product_{r|m}log(r)/(log(p))^(k-alpha),
 k=omega(m), p not dividing m, m>=2.                            (15b)

This follows by dropping factors or by the same k-fold derivative/Gamma
argument as 051, now with strictly positive base log(p). It is not an
estimate for the unresolved fresh residues. The full squared generic
frontier contains -2(p-1)J_p B_p, -2J_p D_p, and +2(p-1)B_p D_p along
with its three positive squares. A prime/residue auxiliary grammar should
preserve that signed joint expression before applying a majorant.

5. The two polynomial shapes are recoverably related at the SAME alpha
======================================================================
The exact polynomial factorization gives

 T2(N)=T1(2N)-T1(N).                                            (17)

Suppose hypothetically that |T2(N)|<=Ctarget sqrt(N) for ALL integer N>=2.
An integer halving argument pays recovery of T1, including odd-scale jumps.
For n>=4 write n=2m+r, m>=2, r in {0,1}. Then

 T1(n)=T1(m)+T2(m)+r*Delta_m,
 Delta_m=K(4m+1)+K(4m+2)-2K(2m+1),
 |Delta_m|<=2*(log(4m+2))^alpha.                               (18)

The last constant uses positivity: each positive or negative side has at
most 2(log(4m+2))^alpha, so the absolute difference is bounded by their maximum.
After halving to 2 or 3, there are at most floor(log_2 n)-1 jumps. The forcing
sum is bounded by sqrt(n)*sum_{l>=1}2^-l/2=sqrt(n)/(sqrt(2)-1).
The finite head obeys |T1(2)|,|T1(3)|<=5(log(6))^alpha without evaluating K.
Hence for every integer n>=2,

 |T1(n)|<=5(log(6))^alpha
       +Ctarget*sqrt(n)/(sqrt(2)-1)
       +2*(floor(log_2 n)-1)*(log(2n))^alpha.                  (19)

In particular the recovered leading constant is Ctarget/(sqrt(2)-1), with
an explicit O(log(n)^(alpha+1)) term. No geometric-subsequence interpolation
or omitted rounding/jump term is hidden. Conversely, a uniform |T1(N)|<=C1
sqrt(N) immediately yields |T2(N)|<=C1*(sqrt(2)+1)*sqrt(N).

Thus these shapes do not define different square-root growth classes for a
fixed alpha; (19) turns a uniform bound of either type into a square-root
bound of the other, with changed constants. The quadratic removes the
constant filtered mean of T1, which can improve early amplitudes without
proving a new all-scale exponent. This equivalence does not compare selected
alpha=1/5 with the comparison alpha=1/2. A proof can still be easier in one
representation, but that ease has to come from arithmetic identities such
as (9) or (15), not from the polynomial factorization alone.

6. Why the fractional-zero bridge does not itself refute the proposed C
======================================================================
At a critical-line zero rho, |2^rho|=sqrt(2). Neither root 1 nor root 2 of
the quadratic multiplier can meet it. The reverse triangle inequality gives

 |(2^rho-1)(2^rho-2)| >=(sqrt(2)-1)*(2-sqrt(2))>0,
 |2^rho-2|>=2-sqrt(2)>0.

Thus neither selected shape erases the fractional singularity. However the
053 proof only refutes holomorphy across an OFF-line zero. If a uniform
sqrt(x) bound held and rho were ON the critical line, its Mellin estimate
along rho+delta would permit O(1/delta), whereas the local singularity is
only O(delta^-alpha), alpha<1. That is compatible. There is no contradiction
for alpha=1/5 or alpha=1/2 and no deduction of a numerical lower limit on C
from this estimate. Promoting the qualitative RH bridge to a fixed-constant
refutation would require an additional rigorous argument not supplied here.

The selected assertions still imply RH if true, but may fail at a finite
integer because of their particular constants. Neither alternative has
been settled by this source-only review. Critical poles are a useful exact
scope guard; they are not a substitute for testing or proving the full
signed source inequality.
