MIXED ADDITIVE / DIVISOR INDEX SEARCH: PROOF AND SCOPE

Status: written universal proofs with independent exact and Arb witnesses.
This is not machine formalization. Historical originality of the specific
prime-binary application is unresolved. No estimate establishing RH is
proved, and this candidate is not on the verified-historically-new list.

1. Units, index laws and finite coefficient computations

All arithmetic arrays have indices n>=1, with index zero unused. D denotes
ordinary Dirichlet convolution, with unit delta_1. To compare it with
additive convolution, place f(n) at additive degree n-1. Thus

  (f A g)(n) = sum_(i+j=n+1) f(i)g(j).

Its unit is also delta_1 after this explicit reindexing; the raw additive
unit is delta_0. A factors of augmentation degree at least one cannot
contribute to coefficient n after n-1 factors. The D bound floor(log2 n)
is not an A bound. Indeed LOG_A(delta_1+delta_2)(16)=1/15, whereas using
the D termination bound gives zero. The index-offset grammar uniquely
recovers i+j-1 when the unit is fixed at delta_1.

Write S(n) for the set of occupied binary positions of n-1 and k(n)=|S(n)|.
Let C be carry-free shifted additive convolution:

  (f C g)(n) = sum_(T subset S(n)) f(1+v(T))g(1+v(S(n)\T)),
  v(T) = sum_(j in T) 2^j.

This is classical subset convolution on finite bit sets. It is associative,
commutative and unital: a q-fold product sums over ordered disjoint
decompositions of S(n). All decompositions involve indices at most n,
so every coefficient of a formal LOG/EXP is finite and prefix stable.
At coefficient n the augmentation ideal is nilpotent above k(n) factors.
Formal LOG_C and EXP_C are mutually inverse on the normalized unit group.
The grading k(n) is additive on allowed products, giving the Euler
recursion used by the production implementation. Anchored set partitions
give the independent implementation.

Define the invertible diagonal map

  (M f)(n)=f(n)/k(n)!.

Transport C by M to a product B:

  f B g = M((M^-1 f) C (M^-1 g)).

An allowed split of k bits into a and k-a bits has weight
a!(k-a)!/k!=1/binomial(k,a). B remains associative and has unit delta_1.
The general grammar M^post EXP_C M^pre LOG_D is consequently an exact
unit-group isomorphism from D to the product transported by M^post;
its inverse is EXP_D M^-pre LOG_C M^-post. These facts follow from formal
LOG/EXP and transport, rather than from agreement on finite samples.

2. Selected candidate and its exact meaning

The selected program is

  N_beta(f) = M EXP_C(beta LOG_D(f)),       f(1)=1.

N_1 is invertible with inverse EXP_D LOG_C M^-1. It satisfies
N_1(f D g)=N_1(f) B N_1(g), and
N_alpha(f) B N_beta(f)=N_(alpha+beta)(f). Thus N_(1/q) is a q-th root
of N_1 under B. Neither statement identifies it as a D root or as the
pointwise q-th root of its Dirichlet series.

For the zeta input u(n)=1, put g=LOG_D(u). On Re(s)>1 its Dirichlet
series is the normalized log(zeta(s)), and its exact coefficients are

  g(n)=1/r if n=p^r for a prime p and r>=1; otherwise g(n)=0.

No prime-power locations are supplied to the native-zeta decoder. Its
finite value-set prior, however, is this classical fact, so the decoder
is a calibrated recovery experiment and is not assumption-free learning.

For n>=2 the candidate has the universal coefficient law

  F_beta(n) = 1/k(n)! sum_(partitions pi of S(n))
                  product_(T in pi) beta g(1+v(T)),
  F_beta(1)=1.

Equivalently, only partitions whose block value plus one is a prime power
contribute, and that block contributes beta divided by its prime-power
exponent. Expansion of EXP_C proves the formula: for l nonempty blocks,
the l! possible orders cancel the exponential denominator l!.

This operation uses actual additive prime-power locations. For example,
F_1(7)=1 but F_1(11)=3/4, although both inputs are prime and both n-1
have two occupied bits. Swapping the prime labels 7 and 11 fixes u but
does not fix N_1(u), so the operation is not invariant under arbitrary
relabeling of the free prime monoid.

The ordinary-root negative control is exact. At n=6,
F_(1/2)(6)=1/8, whereas the D half-root of F_1 has coefficient zero.
This distinguishes the two products before any analytic inference.

3. A learned v2 recurrence, now proved for all indices and parameters

When n is even, bit zero lies in S(n). In an admissible partition, the
unique block containing bit zero has an even value plus one. The only
even prime powers are 2^r, so this block must have value 2^r-1: a prefix
of r occupied bits. It is contained in S(n) exactly when r<=v2(n).
Removing it leaves mask n-2^r, containing k(n)-r occupied bits. Hence

  F_beta(n) = sum_(r=1)^v2(n) (beta/r)
              ((k(n)-r)!/k(n)!) F_beta(n-2^r+1),   n even.

This is a polynomial identity in complex beta. The empirical grammar
scored 384 recurrences; 12 fitted the four training powers of two at
beta=1, and precisely this rule survived all fresh even indices through
1024 at beta=1/2,1/4,2/3. The partition argument supplies the universal
proof; the finite search alone does not.

When S(n) has one bit, its only partition has one block, so
F_beta(2^j+1)=beta g(2^j+1). The separately evaluated j=0,...,20 controls
test this exact sparsity law without pretending a finite dense array
reaches all those indices.

4. Finite ordinary-zeta roots and the carry-free root-order limit

Let d_alpha=u^(D alpha), the classical fractional divisor coefficients.
For real beta in [0,1] and positive integer q set

  L_(q,beta) = M (d_(beta/q) C ... C d_(beta/q)), with q factors.

At a nonunit prime power, d_alpha(p^e)=alpha(alpha+1)...(alpha+e-1)/e!
lies between zero and alpha when 0<=alpha<=1. Multiplicativity gives
0<=d_alpha(n)<=alpha for every n>1. A partition pi with l blocks
contributes (q)_l product_(T in pi) d_(beta/q)(1+v(T))/k(n)!, where
(q)_l=q(q-1)...(q-l+1). This contribution is at most beta^l/k(n)!.
For fixed n, q*d_(beta/q)(n) tends to beta*g(n), by formal expansion of
EXP_D((beta/q)g). Therefore L_(q,beta)(n) tends to F_beta(n).

The bound just proved is uniform in q and beta in [0,1]. Section 5 gives
a summable majorant on every Re(s)>=sigma>0. Dominated convergence
therefore upgrades the coefficient limit to local-uniform convergence
of Dirichlet series throughout Re(s)>0, not merely Re(s)>1. This claim
is restricted to the stated real parameter interval. The finite q
profiles are verification witnesses; the proof is the uniform majorant.

5. Universal domain theorem for the zeta input

Let B_k(lambda)=sum_(partitions of a k-element set) lambda^(number of blocks)
be the weighted Bell polynomial. Its entire generating function is

  sum_(k>=0) B_k(lambda) z^k/k! = exp(lambda(e^z-1)).

This follows by the classical labelled-set partition expansion. For
lambda>=0 and any R>0, positivity at z=R implies

  B_k(lambda)/k! <= C_R/R^k,
  C_R=exp(lambda(e^R-1)).

For |beta|<=lambda the partition formula gives
|F_beta(n)|<=B_(k(n))(lambda)/k(n)!. The same bound holds uniformly for
the finite root lifts in section 4 with lambda=beta or lambda=1. At
beta in [0,1], it also implies 0<=F_beta(n)<=B_k(1)/k!<=1: inject each
set partition into a permutation by turning each increasingly ordered
block into a cycle. This last bound alone would only give Re(s)>1;
the entire generating function gives the stronger result below.

In the mask row 2^J<=n-1<2^(J+1), exactly binomial(J,k-1) indices have
k occupied bits. Its total coefficient majorant is at most

  (C_R/R)(1+1/R)^J.

For Re(s)>=sigma>0, multiplication by n^-sigma bounds this row by
(C_R/R) Q^J, where Q=2^-sigma(1+1/R). Choose R>1/(2^sigma-1), so Q<1.
It follows that the series

  F_beta(s)=sum_(n>=1) F_beta(n)n^-s

converges absolutely and locally uniformly on Re(s)>0, uniformly for
beta in every compact complex set. Each coefficient is a polynomial
in beta. Thus F is jointly holomorphic there and entire in beta. The
tail after n=2^K is at most (C_R/R)Q^K/(1-Q). This proves the precise
domain used in the analytic and actual-zero certificates. No corresponding
growth claim for arbitrary input f, or for the raw unnormalized C transfer,
is inferred from it.

For the grammar variants with pre>=0 and post>=1, the extra factorial
denominators only decrease this partition majorant. This proves their
positive-half-plane certificates too; the other finite growth observations
remain observations without a claimed universal domain theorem.

The independent verifier refines the tail by summing eight exact Bell
row masses, then bounding the remaining rows at a different radius. Its
source calculation instead uses the simple geometric bound above.

6. Two calibration transfers that expose invalid zeta bridges

The unnormalized additive transfer U=EXP_A LOG_D sends u to an ordinary
generating function

  U(z)=exp(beta sum_(p prime,r>=1) z^(p^r-1)/r),  0<=z<1.

For any beta>0 it has no convergent Dirichlet half-plane. To prove this,
suppose its Dirichlet series converged at one s. Its terms would tend
to zero, bounding the positive coefficients by C*n^K for some K>=0.
Consequently log U(z)=O(log(1/(1-z))) as z tends to 1. At z=exp(-1/x),
the prime terms p<=x give log U(z)>=beta*pi(x)/e. This would force
pi(x)=O(log x). Partial summation would then make sum_p 1/p finite.
But that sum diverges: otherwise the finite Euler products would be
uniformly bounded, contradicting H_N<=product_(p<=N)(1-1/p)^-1 and
H_N tending to infinity. This uses no prime number theorem.

The reverse transfer V=EXP_D LOG_A has a very different domain. Since
the additive generating function of u is 1/(1-z), its A logarithm has
coefficient h(n)=1/(n-1), n>=2. Its Dirichlet series is

  V(s)=exp B(s),
  B(s)=sum_(n>=2) n^-s/(n-1)=sum_(k>=1)(zeta(s+k)-1),  Re(s)>0.

The last equality follows from 1/(n-1)=sum_(k>=1)n^-k. The shifted-zeta
series continues normally on compact subsets away from 0,-1,-2,...:
its sufficiently late terms are bounded by a convergent geometric series.
At each excluded point B has one simple pole with residue one, so V has
an isolated essential singularity. Everywhere else V is holomorphic and
zero-free. In particular its formal group inverse does not preserve
actual zeta zeros. Positive coefficients give sum_n V(n)/n=exp B(1)=e,
and the Rankin tail at sigma>1 is at most e*N^(1-sigma).

For an independent native reference, rearranging one shifted term gives

  B(s)=zeta(s+1)-1 + sum_(n>=2) n^-s/[n(n-1)].

Its correction tail after N is at most 2*N^(-sigma-1)/(sigma+1) for
sigma>0. This is distinct from the production reference's 48 shifted
native zeta values and geometric tail.

7. Native-zeta recovery and the two representations

For s_in=2048+i*t the source begins with Arb's native log(zeta(s_in)).
After already certified coefficients below n have been subtracted,
multiplication by n^s_in leaves g(n) plus an unknown tail of magnitude
at most

  (n/(n+1))^2048 * (1+(n+1)/2047).

This follows from 0<=g(m)<=1 and the decreasing-series integral bound.
Distances to all values 0,1/e, e<=floor(log2 n), uniquely isolate the
exact rational g(n), inductively. The normalized log branch is unambiguous
at this real part because zeta is extremely close to one. The source
recovers through 64 at 16384-bit precision; the independent decoder
uses real part 1024, 10240 bits, cutoff 128, recomputed exact prefixes,
reversed candidate order, and eight explicit tail terms before an integral.

Applying the selected transform to these native-origin coefficients
matches independent arithmetic coefficients through the common cutoff.
Six fresh complex output points compare their sums with proved infinite
Bell tails. This establishes a native-input coefficient bridge. It is
not a claimed closed scalar formula F_beta(s)=H(zeta(s)) at those points.
At the same six points V has a separate direct scalar shifted-zeta
comparison. The independent checks change arithmetic cutoffs, native
references and tail bounds; both implementations reject a perturbation
of the n=2 coefficient, with the independent perturbation smaller.

These are six comparison cases with two tested operations in each, not
twelve newly certified zeta zeros or twelve independent scalar formulas
for F. All coefficient uniqueness and complex comparisons use Arb balls.

8. The actual zeta root issue

The existing authoritative certificates isolate simple critical-line
zeta zeros near heights 14.1347251417 and 21.0220396388. We reuse those
certificates and run new controls for these operations on the full disks.
The geometric Bell ratio is below one with R=4 in the source and R=3
independently at the disks' minimum real parts. Section 5 therefore
certifies F_beta holomorphic across both disks for every beta. No finite
F sum there is called a converged numerical value. For V, two different
native formulas give a strictly positive modulus lower bound over each
disk, certifying loss of the original zeta zero directly.

At a simple zeta zero rho, an ordinary fractional power zeta(s)^beta
has monodromy exp(2*pi*i*beta). For beta not an integer, multiplication
by a nonzero holomorphic correction on that disk cannot remove it.
F_beta is single-valued holomorphic there and is not identically zero
(its series tends to one as real s tends to infinity). Thus it cannot
equal that fractional zeta power times such a correction across the disk.
This conclusion does not require knowing whether F_beta(rho) is zero.
The exact D/B counterexample in section 2 gives a separate algebraic
reason to reject equating the two kinds of root.

An unconditional critical-strip convergence theorem for these typed
roots consequently supplies no RH test by itself. We prove no zero-free
region for F and no new estimate about zeta zeros. A later construction
would need a rigorously controlled relationship to ordinary zeta roots,
not just an algebraic fractional-power identity in B.

9. Prior art and reproducible verification

Subset convolution is classical; Bjorklund, Husfeldt, Kaski and Koivisto,
Fourier meets Mobius: fast subset convolution (2006 preprint), explicitly
define the identical bit-set product. Bell partition generating functions
are classical, as are formal arithmetic LOG/EXP and diagonal transport.
The prime-binary application, its v2 recurrence and the positive-half-plane
domain theorem are verified here as compound arithmetic consequences.
The reviewed sources do not establish their precise publication history.
Failure to find a match does not certify a historically new operator.
