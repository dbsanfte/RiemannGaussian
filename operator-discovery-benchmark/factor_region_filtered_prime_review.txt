POST-FREEZE ANALYTICAL EXTENSION — FILTERED PRIME REGIONS
Read-only with respect to all frozen stage sources. This file supplies a proof

1. Imported theorem and scope.
The only imported distribution theorem is the classical Prime Number Theorem,
pi(y)~y/log y as y tends to infinity. Official source: NIST DLMF equation27.2.3,
https://dlmf.nist.gov/27.2.E3 ; theorem context and provenance:
https://dlmf.nist.gov/27.12 . This is an unconditional established theorem,
not RH, random-sign modelling, or newly discovered prime information. The
argument below provides an existential sufficiently-large threshold, not a
numerically explicit threshold from PNT alone.

2. Exact filtered singleton and endpoints.
Use the frozen source partition theta=phi=1/2, z=u=floor sqrtX, and component5:
prime-small FALSE, core-small TRUE, odd. For integerX>=16 this source class
consists EXACTLY of primes p>z throughout the complete horizon2X-1. A nonunit
core has a prime factor strictly greater than the leastprime p>z and therefore
cannot be <=u; odd exponent>=3 implies p^3>X^(3/2)>=4X. For target
mu^{*r}*lambda^{*l}, k=r+l>=1, its coefficient at every prime is -k.
For a fixed finite rational filter with positive integer divisors,
 S5(n)=-k sum_d w_d max(0,pi(floor(n/d))-pi(z)), X<=n<2X.
This equality retains the event endpoint d*p<=n and the source cutoff p>z.
For sufficiently largeX, floor(n/d)>=z for every retained divisor and every
n in the block. For example X>=4 dmax^2 suffices: n/d>=X/d>=2sqrtX, so
floor(n/d)>=floor sqrtX. Thereafter the max is unnecessary. The classification
always uses z(X), including the floor arguments; it does NOT use sqrt(n/d).

3. Uniform PNT and exact fixed c filter.
For fixed finite divisors, the PNT asymptotic is uniform for n in[X,2X): all
arguments floor(n/d) tend to infinity uniformly, and the defining error
sup_{y>=X/(2dmax)}|pi(y)log(y)/y-1| tends to zero. Also
log(n/d)/log n=1+O(1/logX), uniformly, and rounding changes the scale byO(1).
The subtraction pi(z)sum_d w_d is O(sqrtX), hence negligible compared with
X/logX. With P(1)=sum_d w_d/d,
 S5(n)=-k P(1) n/log n + o(X/logX), UNIFORMLY over the whole block.
For frozen c filter C(n)-(11/4)C(floor(n/6)), k=2 andP(1)=13/24.
Once floor(n/6)>=z,
 S5(n)=-2[pi(n)-pi(z)]+(11/2)[pi(floor(n/6))-pi(z)],
and consequently S5(n)~-(13/12)n/log n uniformly. No fitted direct or
innovation constant appears in this derivation.
For any fixed filter with P(1)!=0, a block Riemann sum gives

For this c filter the leading constant is1183/432. This positive asymptotic

4. Refutation quantifiers and boundary of the theorem.
If a frozen program includes a positive FIXED weightv on the square of this
singleton divided by X^2 ceil(X^eta), with fixed0<=eta<1, then its potential
is asymptotic to v*(7/3)k^2P(1)^2 X^(1-eta)/(logX)^2. The ceiling is1 foreta0
and asymptotic toX^eta foreta>0. All other potential terms must be nonnegative.
Thus W=E_G+V is eventually at least a positive constant times that expression.
The proposed all-cofinal dyadic innovation premise would telescope to
W(X)<=W(1)+2Cq(X^(1/q)-1)/(2^(1/q)-1) for every integerq>=q0. Choose suchq
with1/q<1-eta. Along dyadicX the lower bound contradicts this upper bound.
Therefore the ORIGINAL existential global POTENTIAL-INNOVATION claim is
refuted for these exact programs, even with unknownCq andunknownq0. This
is stronger than a finite fitted-constant failure. It does not refute the
ordinary direct critical filtered-energy claim, or RH: the full signed Gram
may cancel a large singleton diagonal against other components.
If P(1)=0, PNT alone only gives S5=o(X/logX); it does not give a nonzero
next term, a lower bound, or a critical energy estimate. A stronger controlled
prime asymptotic is required for that singular case. Moving filters, growing
numbers of divisors, eta>=1, negative potential weights, and signed sums of
unsquared components are outside this rejection proof.

6. Interpretation for discovery.
These arguments explain why uncentered large-prime singleton/contrast
potentials cannot be Lyapunov corrections of subpower size. They are useful
postselection rejection certificates with real arithmetic scope. They do
not replace the still-missing uniform signed cross-core correlation bound.
The theorem does not remove these productions from a frozen dictionary or
silently center, normalize, or retune an original candidate.
