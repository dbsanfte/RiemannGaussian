is a bounded first argument language, not an autonomous general theorem

Analytical results and precise limitation
-----------------------------------------
Sol6.1 reviews, checked by the root agent, supplied the following component
theorems. They are analytically established inputs to the detector, not
claims that finite response fitting invented or proved them automatically.

1. The whole within-child-pair oscillation has normalized energy
O(X^-1 log^3 X)+O(X^-5/3). The low-frequency parent/child covariance remains.

2. Shifting/averaging by H creates a complete correction with energy
O(H^2/X*(1+log X)^3), plus the explicit smooth cost O(H^2 X^-5/3).
The full constants, child boundaries, both channels, and nested stage are
proved in OBSTRUCTION_AUXILIARY_ANALYTIC_REVIEW_001.txt section5a.
Exponents at most1/2 give cofinal padding;2/3 requires a new offset-correlation
lemma. The detector correctly retains that unsupported regime as open.

3. These smoothing changes leave an EQUIVALENT joint energy. The proof graph
keeps the same unresolved equivalence class. Known small correction costs
do not imply a strict full-energy contraction. Indeed such a contraction
would already supply the missing target bound. A deterministic n^a model,
1/2<a<1, also shows why local increment controls alone cannot prove RH-scale
energy. This is a limitation of those inputs, not a counterexample to mu.

4. The prime-specific next reduction is stronger than introducing another
equivalent smoothing. A complete largest-prime decomposition gives the
three-channel transfer with smaller cofactors. For cutoff x^(5/6), one
ENTIRE branch weighted by p^(-2/3) satisfies
  |L_v(x)|<=9*beta*x^(4/9)*(1+log x/6), beta=9/2,
and its critical norm squared is at most(9*beta)^2*(153/2).
This branch can be paid and removed with both-direction triangle costs.
The unweighted and p^(-1/3) prime banks, their signed covariance with the
complete smooth-number sector, and every moving-cutoff boundary remain.
