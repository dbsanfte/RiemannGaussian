2. Angular work is rotation information, not full-core energy
-------------------------------------------------------------
Let s_i range over the four coefficient modes nu0,nu1,mu,nu2, and put
  A_i(x)=sum_(n<=x)s_i(n), B_i(x)=sum_(n<=x)s_i(n)log n,
  H_i(x)=int_1^x A_i(t)dt/t.
Exact finite Abel summation gives
  B_i(x)=A_i(x)log x-H_i(x).                             (2.1)
For the COMPLETE symmetric prefix-square kernel, the mixed angular statistic
  Angular_ij(X)=X^(-2)*sum_(h,k)s_i(h)s_j(k)log(h/k)W_X(h,k)
equals exactly
  X^(-2)*sum_(n=X)^(2X-1)[B_i(n)A_j(n)-A_i(n)B_j(n)]
 =X^(-2)*sum_n[A_i(n)H_j(n)-H_i(n)A_j(n)].               (2.2)
This is a skew correlation of the state and its Volterra memory.
The determinant version [s_i(h)s_j(k)-s_j(h)s_i(k)]log(h/k) is twice (2.2).

For any single core or scale-filtered core, the corresponding same-channel
angular statistic is IDENTICALLY ZERO on every symmetric kernel: f(h)f(k)
is symmetric and log(h/k) is antisymmetric. It cannot serve as a nonzero
energy detector. Mixed channels avoid this zero, but leave a radial mode.
If A_i(x)=v_i Q(x) for a fixed vector v, ALL mixed angular values vanish
while the full core projection is (c dot v)Q(x), c=(beta,-beta,-beta,-R_u).
There is no generic inequality controlling its norm by angular values alone.

This remains an obstruction even with a retained unit and coefficient
envelopes. Set every coefficient channel equal to n^(-1/3). They have unit
one and coefficients <=1, all angular quantities vanish, but c dot (1,1,1,1)
=-beta-R_u is nonzero and their projected dyadic energy grows on the order
of X^(1/3). This is a counterexample to source-independent recovery from
units/envelopes/angular information, NOT an actual Mobius source and NOT a
counterexample to a prospective theorem using its full Euler identities.
Those identities would have to supply an additional arithmetic lemma.

Boundary flux does not automatically supply it. In logarithmic coordinates,
H_i'=A_i, so the derivative of H_i H_j gives the SYMMETRIC A_i H_j+H_i A_j,
whereas (2.2) gives its antisymmetric part. These recover an A/H cross, not
A_i A_j. Differentiating again introduces actual coefficient/jump terms;
deleting them loses the whole prefix target. Endpoint values of H alone do
not bound its derivative energy. One can construct two separated triangular
prefix pulses, rescale the second so their integrals Q(x)dx/x cancel, and
leave both endpoint fluxes unchanged. Their coefficients can be bounded by
one while their critical energy grows with their support horizon. Placing
all channels on this same radial pulse leaves all angular values zero.
Thus an endpoint-only flux plus angular schema requires a new arithmetic
constraint or an explicit positive common-mode measurement.

There is also a quantitative inverse-frequency guard. Under y=log x and
the norm transform a_i(y)=exp(-sigma y)A_i(exp y), Volterra memory is
convolution by exp(-sigma y)1_(y>=0). Its skew part has Fourier multiplier
  -2i*tau/(sigma^2+tau^2).
It vanishes at tau=0 and decays at high |tau|. Recovering a whole norm from
that operator would require inverse losses of order 1/|tau| at low frequency
and |tau| at high frequency. A finite-domain version has head/tail flux as
well. No spectral gap, band limit or bounded inverse for actual source
states is assumed. Local division by log(h/k) has the same issue:
at h=k+1, 1/log(h/k) is of order k, not a logarithmic cost in the horizon.
The multiplier describes the continuous weighted-norm Volterra operator;
the literal dyadic statistic is the finite sum (2.2), whose common-mode
nullspace was established separately and without a Fourier approximation.

Some narrow complements ARE independently paid. For any fixed graph on
coefficient indices with maximum TOTAL degree D, its unordered pair
contribution to the full core kernel has absolute normalized cost at most
  2D*C_f^2*(1+log(2X-1))^3.                             (2.3)
Indeed 2|f(h)f(k)|<=f(h)^2+f(k)^2, W_X<=X, and
sum_(n<=N)d_2(n)^2<=sum_(n<=N)d_4(n)<=N(1+log N)^3.
Thus fixed-offset near-diagonal graphs are paid; a growing multiplicative
near-diagonal cone has unbounded degree and is NOT covered by (2.3).

The high-product angular probe differs from (2.2) only by a paid low-product
piece. At hk<=X, W_X=X and |log(h/k)|<=log(hk)<=log X, so for the unscaled
mode pair
  |Angular_full-Angular_(hk>X)|<=(1+log X)^4.             (2.4)
The actual source constants multiply this bound. Diagonal angular values
are zero. Consequently a high-product restriction does not secretly repair
the common-mode nullspace through its discarded head.

3. A coercive symmetric log-bank work, with exact finite head
-------------------------------------------------------------
For ANY actual real prefix Q, zero below one, define
  B_Q(x)=sum_(n<=x)q(n)log n,
  P_Q(x)=B_Q(x)/log x, x>1,
  H_Q(x)=int_1^x Q(t)dt/t,
where q is its exact coefficient sequence. Abel summation gives
  P_Q=Q-H_Q/log x.                                      (3.1)
Unlike angular work, the symmetric work Q*P_Q retains the radial mode.
Use x0=64, H0=H_Q(x0), and split H_Q=H0+H_tail on x>=x0.
For sigma>=1/2 put
  r=1/(sigma log x0)<1/2,
  c_head=x0^(-sigma)/(sqrt(2sigma)*log x0).
The inequality r<1/2 follows from log2>2/3, hence log64>4. No numerical
evaluation of the source is needed to choose this tail boundary.

The finite-horizon Hardy estimate is
  ||H_tail||_(sigma,x0..T)<=||Q||_(sigma,x0..T)/sigma.
Proof: integrate H_tail(x)^2*x^(-2sigma-1) by parts. The lower boundary
is zero and the upper boundary is nonpositive; Cauchy gives the displayed
estimate. Therefore ||H_tail/log x||<=r||Q||, with the SAME truncated
horizon. The remaining constant head satisfies ||H0/log x||<=|H0|c_head.

Define the SIGNED, fully joined bank work
  Work_Q,sigma(T)=int_(x0)^T Q(x)P_Q(x)x^(-2sigma-1)dx.
The exact lower bound is
  Work >= (1-r)||Q||_tail^2-|H0|c_head||Q||_tail.
Young's inequality then proves the unconditional recovery theorem
  ||Q||_tail^2 <= 2*Work/(1-r)
                         +H0^2*c_head^2/(1-r)^2.        (3.2)
This is a ONE-SIDED coercive signed-work bound up to a paid finite head.
Work may be negative, but only by the controlled head term. One must retain
that term and the complete signed cross in Q*P_Q. No bound for Work is
inferred from coercivity; that is the new candidate arithmetic lemma.

The reverse comparison follows from ||P_Q||<= (1+r)||Q||+|H0|c_head.
Thus finite/cofinal norm control of this symmetric work is an equivalent
full-norm presentation with constants, not an independent source estimate.
Its value is an honest recovery certificate and a source-specific lower-
argument bank representation, in place of nonexistent angular coercivity.

For Q=F, (1.2) gives the explicit source-free finite-head bounds
  |H0|<=C_f*x0*log x0,
  ||F||_(crit,1..x0)^2<=C_f^2*x0*(1+log x0)^2.           (3.3)
They include f(1)=-beta-R_u. For a scale-filtered Q, replace C_f by the
coefficient bound C_f(1+|a|), as in section 5.

To express the work through actual source identities, set A_i to the four
complete mode prefixes and c=(beta,-beta,-beta,-R_u). The exact log-divisor
multipliers, including all prime powers d, are
  lambda0(d)=d^(-u)-2,
  lambda1(d)=d^(-u)-1-d^(-v),
  lambda_mu(d)=-1,
  lambda2(d)=d^(-u)-d^(-v).
Then
  B_F(x)=sum_(d<=x,d>=2)Lambda(d)
                              *sum_i c_i lambda_i(d) A_i(x/d). (3.4)
All arithmetic arguments are <=x/2. Unit cofactors are part of each A_i,
and Lambda includes every power of each prime. Multiplying (3.4) by the
ENTIRE F(x)/log x, with the norm weight, is the complete Work in (3.2).
The second leg is current-scale, but there is no q-free forcing disguised
as a smaller argument. No raw mode or deterministic pole is paid separately.

4. Exact angular transfer classification for the CURRENT core
--------------------------------------------------------------
For a finite symmetric kernel K and a pair of modes i,j, their mixed
logarithmic statistic has the complete pullback
  sum_(h,k)s_i(h)s_j(k)log(h/k)K(h,k)
   =sum_(d>=2)Lambda(d)*sum_(H,L)s_i(H)s_j(L)
      *{[lambda_i(d)-lambda_j(d)]*[K(dH,L)+K(H,dL)]/2
       +[lambda_i(d)+lambda_j(d)]*[K(dH,L)-K(H,dL)]/2}.   (4.1)
This follows from the convolution log derivation, with BOTH orientation
supports retained. It requires no coprimality; common primes and repeated
valuations survive exactly.

With du=d^(-u),dv=d^(-v), the six pair differences are
  nu0,nu1 : dv-1;
  nu0,mu  : du-1;
  nu0,nu2 : dv-2;
  nu1,mu  : du-dv;
  nu1,nu2 : -1;
  mu,nu2  : -1-du+dv.                                 (4.2)
Only nu1/mu has zero constant term and the true d^(-1/3)-d^(-2/3)
attenuation. This is an exact generic compiler classification, not a
channel nominated from numerical responses. The common nu0 symmetric
mode remains unsuppressed and is not recovered by that angular channel.

For product-only kernels, the commutator in (4.1) vanishes. For W_X with
full product/diagonal restrictions it retains max/prefix, product birth,
and unequal diagonal exits. On the flat interior dH,dL<=X the W part is
equal in both placements; product masks also agree since the product is
dHL. The remaining frontier coefficient is lambda_i+lambda_j, which can
be unsuppressed. A boundary is not paid just because it has a name or lies
outside that flat interior. Diagonal angular zeros and the paid diagonal
target bound must not be confused with deletion of near-diagonal pairs.

Equations (4.1)-(4.2) are useful detector constraints: a program can search
source differences whose symmetric transported term has a proved prime
gain, then conjecture a JOINT bound for its exact boundary flux plus the
retained symmetric work. Claiming recovery from the angular term alone
violates section 2, even if every local valuation identity is exact.

5. Invertible scale filters and bounded energy-increment schemas
----------------------------------------------------------------
Fix an integer q>=2 and an exact real a. Define the FULL core filter
  G(x)=F(x)-aF(x/q),
with the actual integer-floor step extension, zero below one. Its coefficient
sequence is
  g(n)=f(n)-a*1_(q divides n)f(n/q), g(1)=f(1).
All arithmetic/center cross terms remain inside G^2. Write
  E_G(X)=X^(-2)*sum_(n=X)^(2X-1)G(n)^2.

A valid generated universal lemma is the ONE-SIDED increment bound
  E_G(2X)-E_G(X)<=B for every dyadic X>=1, B>=0.           (5.1)
With the exact head A=E_G(1), telescoping proves
  E_G(X)<=A+B log2 X.                                  (5.2)
Negative increments are allowed; an absolute increment bound is a stronger
unnecessary variant. Calibration can propose/freeze B and holdout can
reject it, but no finite pass proves (5.1) for all X.

Here is a complete discrete recovery theorem. Put r=|a|/sqrt(q). The finite
causal inverse is EXACTLY
  F(n)=sum_(j=0)^floor(log_q n) a^j G(floor(n/q^j)).
There is no unknown prehistory or missing unit. From (5.2), cumulative
summation over dyadic blocks gives
  S_G(T)=sum_(n<=T)G(n)^2
         <=(4/3)T^2*[A+B log2 T].
Each cofactor floor(n/q^j) has at most q^j preimages. Minkowski consequently
gives
  S_F(T)/T^2 <=(4/3)[A+B log2 T]
                              *(sum_(j=0)^floor(log_q T)r^j)^2.
Thus for every dyadic X>=1,
  E_F(X)<=(16/3)[A+B log2(2X)]
                      *(sum_(j=0)^floor(log_q(2X-1))r^j)^2.  (5.3)
This includes the finite head, all floors and q not a power of two.

If |a|<sqrt(q), (5.3) is at most
  (16/3)[A+B log2(2X)]/(1-|a|/sqrt(q))^2.                (5.4)
If |a|=sqrt(q), replace the INVERSE-COST factor
(1-|a|/sqrt(q))^(-2) by
  [1+floor(log_q(2X-1))]^2,                             (5.5)
giving cubic-log recovery. At sigma>1/2 the equality case has the bounded
norm constant 1/[1-q^(1/2-sigma)]; at the critical finite norm its inverse
cost is logarithmic. Equality must not be labeled bounded critical recovery.
For |a|>sqrt(q), the causal cost includes a power of X. A separate source-
specific exponent improvement is then required; smaller measured G energy
does not remove that cost.

The generated lemma (5.1) is meaningfully STRONGER than mere cofinal energy,
not an equivalent tautology with a fitted normalization. For a generic
bounded-coefficient source, choose F(n)=sqrt(n)(log n)^2 with a finite
adjustment of its head. Its coefficients are bounded at large n and its
energy has only powers of log X, hence is cofinal. At a=sqrt(q), the leading
filter is a nonzero constant times sqrt(n)log n, so E_G has a log^2 X term
and its dyadic increments are unbounded. Nonresonant a leaves an even
larger log power. This is a source-independent strictness example, not an
actual-source asymptotic. The all-scale increment conjecture could be
false for the actual core even when its RH-equivalent cofinal goal is true.

Nor can the threshold be bypassed by a mean-canceling fit. A generic prefix
F(n)=n^b, 1/2<b<1, with a=q^b, has bounded coefficients and only a small
floor residual G(n)=O(n^(b-1)), while E_F grows as X^(2b-1). This gives a
literal obstruction to universal no-power recovery beyond the threshold.
The unit/head spawns the growing homogeneous mode. At negative a a
log-periodic signed analogue has the same energy obstruction. These are
generic recovery guards, not proposed replacement arithmetic sources.

6. Filtered source banks and a signed work-increment schema
-----------------------------------------------------------
The filtered core retains an exact source-specific log bank. Its log prefix
is
  B_G(x)=B_F(x)-aB_F(x/q)-a log(q)F(x/q).                 (6.1)
The last term is essential: log(n) on q times a cofactor is log q plus
the cofactor log. Filtering every mode prefix in (3.4) therefore gives
  B_G(x)=sum_(d>=2)Lambda(d)*sum_i c_i lambda_i(d)
                           [A_i(x/d)-aA_i(x/(dq))]
                                         -a log(q)F(x/q). (6.2)
All modes and all prime powers are retained. The extra lower-prefix term,
unit and integer floors must be part of any generated work/flux program.

Apply section 3 to Q=G. The coefficient envelope is C_g=C_f(1+|a|), because
d_2(n/q)<=d_2(n) on q multiples. Thus its complete finite head is paid by
(3.3) with C_g. A second valid generated lemma is
  Work_G,crit(2T)-Work_G,crit(T)<=B_w
      for all dyadic T>=64, B_w>=0.                      (6.3)
Since Work(64)=0, this implies Work(T)<=B_w log2(T/64). At sigma=1/2 the
coercivity theorem gives for the tail
  ||G||_(crit,64..T)^2
     <=2B_w log2(T/64)/(1-r0)
          +H0^2/[64*(log64)^2*(1-r0)^2],
  r0=2/log64<1/2.                                      (6.4)
Add its explicit finite-head norm. Each dyadic block satisfies
  E_G(X)<=4||G||_(crit,X..2X)^2,
because the exact step integral weights are 1/[n(n+1)]>=1/(4X^2).
Consequently (6.3) implies a bound of the form A_w+B'_w log2 X, which feeds
directly into (5.3). This is a sufficient whole-target proof schema with
positive recovery and signed arithmetic work; no angular inverse is used.

The exact work can be evaluated as a finite sum with positive interval
weights int_n^(n+1) x^(-2sigma-1)/log x dx multiplying G(n)B_G(n). Replacing
those weights by endpoint samples is an approximation requiring its own
error bound, not the exact theorem. The source bank representation (6.2)
is what makes this a constructive operator-plus-argument conjecture rather
than a free positive metric on unrelated channels.

A bounded work-increment premise is again stronger than merely cofinal
core growth. The work is norm-equivalent after its finite head, but its
increments encode a specified logarithmic rate. The detector may propose
and finitely falsify this rate; its proof status must stay CONJECTURED
until a uniform source argument establishes it. No independent assumption
about an RH-equivalent mode or q-free companion is allowed to prove it.

7. Detector admission and falsification constraints
----------------------------------------------------
The next detector can enumerate exact q,a filters, lemma type (5.1) or
(6.3), the full source/flux AST, constants frozen after calibration, and
their recovery certificates. These are actual bound proposals. Fresh
validation can reject them on a larger scale or falsify a proposed signed
comparison, while every pass remains finite evidence. The universal claim
and its constants must be explicit, independent of the omitted scales.

Useful reusable proof checks are:
  * full four-mode core, unit, eta and original recovery (1.1) retained;
  * exact |a| versus sqrt(q) classification and actual inverse cost;
  * an increment lemma specifying all dyadic scales, head and one-sided
    or absolute direction, not merely a least-slope numerical ranking;
  * exact source bank (6.2), including the -a log(q) term;
  * angular-only programs flagged as noncoercive unless a proved positive
    symmetric/common-mode measurement or new arithmetic constraint remains;
  * local prime gains classified by (4.2), complete max/product/diagonal
    fluxes retained, and no near-diagonal division without its inverse cost;
  * paid bounded-degree graph/head/diagonal unions counted once, with growing
    degree or omitted first-valuation sectors explicitly unpaid;
  * exact numerical identities and inequality enclosures distinguished from
    a universal proof of the conjectured bound.

Angular and flux probes are useful DIAGNOSTICS for a structural argument.
For instance a candidate can propose that the complete nu1/mu angular
transfer and its boundary flux provide a negative credit in the symmetric
work (6.2). The detector must also specify the unsuppressed nu0/common
projection and an exact inequality recovering the whole signed G state.
Sections 2/4 show why merely reporting a suppressed angular term does not
provide that inequality. This guard is numerically falsifiable whenever
the proposed complete comparison fails, and mathematically rejects an
unjustified generic inverse even before any source evaluation.

Final proof boundary
--------------------
There is no free angular recovery. The new coercive symmetric log-work
identity provides a valid bounded recovery with a complete finite head;
the scale-filter energy/work increment schemas provide exact polylogarithmic
consequences with explicit critical costs. They are reusable detector proof
templates, capable of expressing a substantive all-scale growth conjecture
rather than just improving a numerical constant. Their actual signed
source increment bounds and any angular-to-symmetric arithmetic credit
remain open. No full-core bound or RH advance is claimed.
