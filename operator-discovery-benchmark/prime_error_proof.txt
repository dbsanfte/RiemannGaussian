SIGNED PRIME-ERROR DILATIONS WITHOUT A NONVANISHING-MULTIPLIER GATE

Scope: a universal conditional zero-faithfulness theorem and a bounded
construction/estimate search. The all-scale signed arithmetic premise is
unproved. The analytic proof below is mathematical prose, not a formal proof
assistant artifact. Finite audits check its arithmetic, local residue rules,
actual-zero calibration and quantitative constants independently.

1. Actual arithmetic first jet and a bounded-interval truncation

Let mu be the factor-free Dirichlet inverse of the constant-one sequence.
The negative first coefficient-power jet is lambda=-log_D(mu)=log_D(1).
As proved in parameter_jet_proof.txt, lambda(p^k)=1/k at actual prime powers,
and lambda(n)=0 otherwise. In particular 0<=lambda(n)<=1, lambda(1)=0.
It is produced by finite-at-each-index Dirichlet logarithms, without primes
or factorisation; the independent evaluator uses a prime sieve instead.

For rational beta define e_beta(x)=li(x)-beta*sum_(n<x)lambda(n) for x>1.
Its initial Mellin transform, Re(s)>1, is
  -[log(s-1)+beta*log zeta(s)]/s.
The branch is canonical on Re(s)>1 and real on the real axis there.

The function li(x)=Ei(log x) diverges logarithmically in log x as x->1+.
Define E_beta(x)=0 for 0<x<2 and E_beta(x)=e_beta(x) for x>=2. The strict
endpoint at two gives E_beta(2)=li(2), with the prime-two jump immediately
to its right. Define the ENTIRE function
  B_0(s)=integral_1^2 li(x)*x^(-s-1)dx.
It is entire because the log-log singularity is integrable and log x is
bounded on this compact interval, uniformly on compact s sets. Then
  Mellin(E_beta)(s) = -[log(s-1)+beta*log zeta(s)]/s - B_0(s).
Changing a bounded interval supplies no new arithmetic estimate.

2. General finite dilations, including multipliers with zeros

Let distinct delays d_j>=1 be positive rationals, and c_j rational. The
grammar normalizes d_0=1,c_0=1, so
  R(t)=sum_j c_j E_beta(t/d_j),
  D(s)=sum_j c_j*d_j^(-s).
D is entire. As real s->infinity, D(s)->1; hence it is not identically zero.
It may vanish at arbitrary isolated points, including inside Re(s)>1/2.
Absolute convergence on Re(s)>1 and a change of variables give
  H_R(s)=Mellin(R)(s)
    =-D(s)*[log(s-1)+beta*log zeta(s)]/s-D(s)*B_0(s).

The earlier 1/zeta-based bridge needs multiplier nonvanishing to prevent
erasing a zero pole. This logarithmic bridge does not. The following
argument applies to any non-identically-zero meromorphic multiplier on the
claimed domain, although this arithmetic pilot supplies entire finite sums.

Suppose a proved uniform signed estimate makes H_R holomorphic on the
connected half-plane Re(s)>eta, eta>=0. The quotient
  Q(s)=s*H_R(s)/D(s)+s*B_0(s)
is MEROMORPHIC there; zeros of D give at worst isolated poles. In Re(s)>1,
away from isolated D zeros, differentiate the initial identity:
  Q'(s)=-1/(s-1)-beta*zeta'(s)/zeta(s).
Both sides are meromorphic on the claimed half-plane, so the meromorphic
identity theorem extends this equality through it.

A derivative of any meromorphic function has residue ZERO at every point.
Indeed a local Laurent series sum_(k=-K)^infinity a_k z^k differentiates to
sum k*a_k*z^(k-1); its z^-1 coefficient comes only from k=0 and is zero.
At an ordinary zeta zero of multiplicity m>=1, zeta'/zeta has residue m.
Consequently the right-hand side has residue -beta*m at that zero. For
beta!=0 this contradicts zero residue, irrespective of the vanishing order
of D at that point. Multiplying a logarithmic singularity by a finite-order
zero does not erase its information.

At s=1, zeta has order -1, so the right side has residue beta-1. Therefore
if eta<1, a uniform signed bound is impossible unless beta=1. Even a zero
of D at one cannot repair an unmatched logarithmic residue. For beta=1,
the pole terms cancel, Q'=-F'/F where F(s)=(s-1)zeta(s). The apparent pole
at one is removable since F(1)=1. A PROVED uniform premise with eta<=1/2
excludes every ordinary zero right of the critical line, regardless of its
multiplicity and regardless of isolated D zeros. The standard zeta
functional equation then implies RH.

The independently re-certified known critical zero excludes eta<1/2 for
beta=1. This obstruction applies even when D vanishes at that zero. It does
not require RH or a lower bound for |D| on the critical half-plane.
Classical dependencies: meromorphic identity theorem, Laurent series and
residues (primary reference https://dlmf.nist.gov/1.10), the meromorphic
continuation and simple pole of ordinary zeta (https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.2),
and the ordinary-zeta functional equation for the RH reflection step.

3. Whole-unit arithmetic increments, not sampled real interpolation

For every real N<=t<N+1, integer N>=1, consider x=t/d, y=N/d, d>=1.
Their separation is <1/d<=1. On x,y>=2, li is increasing with derivative
1/log x<=1/log 2. The strict count of any sequence with coefficient
magnitudes <=1 changes by at most one because the interval has length
strictly less than one. Thus
  |E_beta(t/d)-E_beta(N/d)|<=|beta|+1/(d*log 2).
If both arguments are below two, the increment is zero. If activation at
two falls inside the interval, add |li(2)|. Integer d places activation at
the integer 2d, so it cannot fall strictly inside [N,N+1); this addition
is needed only for noninteger rational d.

Hence a proved, scale-independent increment cost is
 B=sum_j |c_j|*(|beta|+1/(d_j log 2)
                      +[d_j noninteger]*|li(2)|).
For theta>=0 and ell>=0, a uniform INTEGER bound
  |R(N)-A|<=C*N^theta*log(e+N)^ell for EVERY integer N>=1
implies for EVERY real t>=1
  |R(t)-A|<=C*t^theta*log(e+t)^ell+B.
No Lipschitz continuity across prime-power jumps is asserted. Truncating at
two avoids the earlier unbounded log-log activation singularity. This is
a proved global transfer rule, not a proof of its signed integer premise.

The real bound gives holomorphic Mellin transform on Re(s)>theta by
dominated convergence. A constant offset contributes A/s, meromorphic
only at zero, which is outside that open domain for theta>=0. Thus the
residue argument above applies with eta=theta. Any finite nonnegative
rational logarithmic power ell is allowed; it changes constants, not eta.

4. Quantitative cost and isolated multiplier zeros

Set p=ceil(ell). For t>=1, log(e+t)<=log(e+1)+log t and log(e+t)>=1, so
for sigma>theta,
 |Mellin(R-A)(s)| <= C*sum_(j=0)^p binom(p,j)*log(e+1)^(p-j)*j!
                                     /(sigma-theta)^(j+1) + B/sigma.
Call the right side H_sigma. For sigma>=0 the compact correction satisfies
 |B_0(s)| <= log(2)*(2+EulerGamma-log(log 2))=:J_0.
Proof: for 0<u<=log2<1,
 |Ei(u)|<=EulerGamma-log u+exp(u)-1<=1+EulerGamma-log u.
Integrate this against exp(-sigma*u)<=1 to obtain J_0.

For beta=1 and a declared s with D(s)!=0, the exact initial branch and its
conditional continuation give
 |log F(s)| <= (|s|*H_sigma+|A|)/|D(s)|+|s|*J_0,
 log|1/zeta(s)| <= log|s-1|+(|s|*H_sigma+|A|)/|D(s)|+|s|*J_0.
The exponential reconstruction cost is charged. It is not the linear
recovery cost of a direct inverse-zeta operator. At D zeros the qualitative
argument still works, but this displayed quantitative division bound is
unavailable. A separate removable-quotient certificate would be needed for
an all-height quantitative bound. The pilot reports a fixed evaluation
point and never labels its cost an all-height lower bound for |D|.

5. Positive-support and delayed-support controls

Replace lambda(n) by one for n>=2, or by the actual coefficients up to a
fixed cutoff and one thereafter; lambda(1)=0 in both controls. Their
Dirichlet series L(s) equals zeta(s)-1 plus a finite entire correction.
Their E_beta transform contains -log(s-1)/s-beta*L(s)/s-B_0(s).
The same meromorphic quotient would satisfy
  Q'(s)=-1/(s-1)-beta*L'(s).
The derivative L' has residue zero, so the right side has residue -1 at one.
This is impossible for eta<1, for EVERY normalized finite dilation filter.
In particular D(1)=0 can remove the control's linear pole term but cannot
remove the li logarithm. These controls remain globally false even if an
archived bound passes their finite points. The evaluator records both facts.

6. Search scope and acceptance

Frozen generation includes signed finite differences, unrestricted rational
polynomials, mixed rational delays, and least-squares coefficient inference.
Training-only offsets, power slopes and logarithmic powers generate bound
proposals. Candidate estimates are frozen before validation; selection is
sealed before evaluator-owned irregular final inputs. The worker cannot
read final data or make network, subprocess or literature calls.

The probe certifies complete point sums, exact logarithmic coefficients,
local Laurent/residue arithmetic, native zeta logarithms with proved tails,
and a guided actual-zero calibration using a multiplier that vanishes at
that zero. The native contour evaluator uses a local zeta Taylor polynomial
through degree eleven and certified remainders from its twelfth derivative
over a convex complex box. For M>=|zeta^(12)|/12!, the errors are at most
M*|s-centre|^12 for zeta and 12*M*|s-centre|^11 for its first derivative.
These are enclosures of the ACTUAL zeta function, not substitutions of a
polynomial for its zeros. Analytic quadrature receives these certified balls;
balls outside the derivative box are rejected and subdivided.
The failed direct-quadrature preflight is retained with its original sources
and parameters; it consumed no discovery-final inputs.
The probe does not certify complete window moments or prove a new
uniform arithmetic estimate. No historical novelty is claimed. The signed
integer premise, any all-height quantitative quotient bound, and RH remain
unproved; universal zero-faithfulness alone does not discharge them.
