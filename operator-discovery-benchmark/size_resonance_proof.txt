SIZE ROOT LIFTS: ARTIFICIAL SINGULARITIES AND THE INFINITE-ORDER LIMIT

Research record, 7 October 2026. This adds a negative analytic gate to
the previously proved size-carry algebra. The operators remain valid
formal operators and absolutely convergent zeta series on Re(s)>1.
The stronger RH-half-plane regularity proposed for individual lifts is
ruled out below. The paired identity is unaffected. No RH conclusion,
historical originality, or theorem-prover certification is claimed.

1. NOTATION AND A CIRCLE-MEASURE LEMMA

Fix b>=2, tau=2*pi/log(b), s_m=1+i*m*tau, m an integer. Put
theta(n)={log_b(n)} and psi(n)=ceil(log_b(n))-log_b(n), both in [0,1).
The q-fold floor no-carry product permits an ordered factorization
  n=n_1*...*n_q
exactly when sum theta(n_j)<1. For ceiling use sum psi(n_j)<1.
Indeed the total defect is floor of the corresponding sum of fractional
parts, by telescoping the height potential. These facts also hold for
unit factors. They follow from the exact product proved previously.

For a>0 let d_a be the nonnegative coefficients of the normalized zeta
power zeta(s)^a on Re(s)>1. For epsilon>0 define a positive measure on
the circle R/Z by
  mu_(epsilon,a)=epsilon^a*sum_n d_a(n)*n^(-1-epsilon)*delta_theta(n).
Its mass is epsilon^a*zeta(1+epsilon)^a, tending to 1. Its mth Fourier
coefficient is epsilon^a*zeta(1+epsilon+i*m*tau)^a. For each fixed m!=0
the zeta value stays bounded: zeta is holomorphic at s_m, its only pole
being 1. Since a is real, the modulus of its normalized power is |zeta|^a.
Thus all nonconstant Fourier coefficients tend to zero. Finite
trigonometric polynomials are dense among continuous circle functions;
positivity and convergence of the total mass therefore prove that mu
converges weakly to uniform Lebesgue measure. The same is true for psi,
whose circle phase is the negative of theta's, including exact b-powers.

Products of any fixed number of these positive measures converge to the
product of uniform measures. Functions with only limit-measure-null
discontinuities also have convergent integrals. The coordinate cut at
theta=0 and the boundary sum theta=1 are null for the product uniform
measure. Consequently the simplex indicator and its Fourier phase can
be used in this conclusion; no unproved equidistribution of primes or
zeta zeros is being assumed.

2. UNIVERSAL ABELIAN LAW FOR EVERY FINITE ROOT ORDER

Let L_(b,q,-)(u^{*alpha}) be the q-fold floor product of the ordinary
qth root d_(alpha/q), q>=1, alpha>0. For ceiling replace - by +.
Absolute convergence and nonnegative coefficients hold on Re(s)>1,
since all its coefficients are bounded by d_alpha.
Using a=alpha/q in the preceding product-measure lemma gives
  lim_(epsilon down to 0) epsilon^alpha
      L_(b,q,-)(u^{*alpha})(s_m+epsilon)
    =A_q(m)=1/(q-1)!*integral_0^1 t^(q-1)*exp(-2*pi*i*m*t) dt.
For ceiling the limit is the complex conjugate of A_q(m).
This is a limit along the real direction from the domain of absolute
convergence. It does not assert local meromorphic continuation.
The result is independent of b and alpha once this normalization is used.

At m=0, A_q(0)=1/q!. At m!=0 put w=1/(2*pi*i*m). Integration by parts
gives A_1(m)=0 and
  A_q(m)=-w/(q-1)!+w*A_(q-1)(m), q>=2,
hence
  A_q(m)=-sum_(r=1)^(q-1) w^r/(q-r)!.
In particular A_2(m)=-1/(2*pi*i*m).

For every q>=2 and positive integer m, Im A_q(m)>0. Pair the first and
second half of each sine period in integral t^(q-1)*sin(2*pi*m*t) dt.
Sine is positive on the first half; the value of the strictly increasing
weight t^(q-1) is larger at the matching point of the second half, where
sine has the opposite sign. Thus that sine integral is strictly negative,
and the imaginary part of exp(-2*pi*i*m*t)'s integral is strictly positive.
For negative m use conjugation. Therefore none of these A_q(m) vanishes.

Every finite q>=2 lift consequently has a non-removable boundary
singularity at EVERY s_m, m!=0, on Re(s)=1. Multiplication by a branch
of (s-1)^alpha that is analytic and nonzero near s_m cannot remove it.
Such points lie inside the proposed RH half-plane Re(s)>1/2, although
actual zeta is finite there and is known to be zero-free on Re(s)=1.
Thus the individual lifts cannot have the proposed single-valued
holomorphic extension after ordinary pole cancellation, independently
of RH. For alpha=1 the leading divergence has pole-like order one;
calling this an isolated meromorphic pole would require additional
continuation evidence and is deliberately avoided.

3. THE EXACT GRID SEARCH AND ITS RIGOROUS ERROR

The numerical geometry stage uses boxes of side 1/M in [0,1]^q.
It computes the coefficients of (1+z+...+z^(M-1))^q for total degree
0..M-1 using cumulative sums, without a preloaded factorial formula.
All boxes with sum of lower endpoints <1 give an upper volume bound;
all boxes whose sum of upper endpoints <1 give a lower bound. Their
integer counts, divided by M^q, enclose the actual simplex volume.
A 600-model grammar uses rational scales, powers of q and factorials
at shifted q arguments. It trains at q=2..5 and tests q=6,7,8,10,12,16
at a finer mesh. Passing syntactic aliases are not distinct operators.

For nonzero m, the phase is evaluated at each box center. Its variation
within a box is <=pi*|m|*q/M. Therefore the complex integral differs from
the computed upper-box sum by at most
  (pi*|m|*q/M)*upper_volume+(upper_volume-lower_volume).
This certifies the numerical simplex Fourier calculations. The universal
factorial and polynomial laws follow from section 2, not a finite mesh.

The independent checker obtains the counts from stars-and-bars binomial
coefficients and the Fourier integral from direct quadrature. It also
checks the polynomial recurrence exactly over rational coefficients and
tests the all-arity formula at fresh frequencies. These are independent
checks of implementation, not replacements for the written theorem.

4. A RAPID HURWITZ FORMULA FOR A GENUINE ZETA-INPUT ENCLOSURE

Let J_1(s)=sum theta(n)*n^-s, initially Re(s)>1. Since
floor(log_b(n))=sum_(k>=1)1[n>=b^k],
  J_1(s)=-zeta'(s)/log(b)-sum_(k>=1)zeta(s,b^k).
For a=b^k, Euler-Maclaurin gives
  zeta(s,a)=a^(1-s)/(s-1)+a^(-s)/2+s*a^(-s-1)/12+R(s,a),
with, for sigma=Re(s)>0,
  |R(s,a)|<=|s*(s+1)|/[12*(sigma+1)]*a^(-sigma-1).
The bound follows from |periodic B_2|<=1/6 in the integral remainder.
Summing the three displayed terms geometrically makes the residual sum
absolutely and locally uniformly convergent on Re(s)>0. Its truncation
after k=K has error at most
  |s*(s+1)|/[12*(sigma+1)]
       *b^(-(K+1)*(sigma+1))/(1-b^(-sigma-1)).
The independent zeta derivative is calculated by differentiating the
finite Euler-Maclaurin expression for sum_(n>=1)n^-s, with arithmetic
cutoff N=96 and order R=12. The undifferentiated remainder is bounded by
  |B_(2R)|/(2R)! * |(s)_(2R)|
         *N^(1-sigma-2R)/(sigma+2R-1).
The periodic Bernoulli Fourier series proves its supremum is <=|B_(2R)|.
On a circle of radius r=1/10 around the evaluation point, use the smaller
real part sigma-r and |s+j|+r for each factor of the rising factorial.
Cauchy's derivative estimate bounds the derivative remainder by this
whole-circle bound divided by r. This differentiates a rigorously
controlled finite arithmetic expression, without differentiating an
unqualified asymptotic error or invoking a native zeta derivative.

The geometric first term has residue -1/(2*pi*i*m) in J_1 at s_m, m!=0.
This supplies an explicit independent route to the q=2 Abelian law.

For the ordinary square root g=d_(1/2), define J_half by weighting its
coefficients with theta. The exact floor identity previously proved is
  L_(b,2,-)(u)=zeta-2*zeta^(1/2)*J_half+J_1.
On Re(s)>1, |J_half(s)|<=zeta(sigma)^(1/2). Thus actual Arb zeta and
the Hurwitz formula give a rigorous enclosure for the infinite lift,
without truncating a slowly convergent Dirichlet series. After scaling
by epsilon at s=s_m+epsilon, its center is epsilon*(zeta+J_1) and its
error is at most
  2*epsilon*|zeta(s)|^(1/2)*zeta(sigma)^(1/2)
       +epsilon*Hurwitz_tail.
The unknown-root term tends to zero as O(sqrt(epsilon)). It cannot cancel
the nonzero leading J_1 residue.

The ceiling identity is
  L_(b,2,+)(u)=2*zeta^(1/2)*(R_(b,1/2)+J_half)-R_(b,1)-J_1.
Its scaled center is -epsilon*(R_(b,1)+J_1); add
2*epsilon*|zeta|^(1/2)*|R_(b,1/2)| to the floor error bound.
The paired root has scaled modulus <=epsilon*|zeta|^(1/2)*|R_(b,1/2)|,
tending to zero. Its previous zero-free correction proof is unaffected.

The production probe uses K=28, the B_2 remainder, and native
hypergeometric corrections. At bases 2,6,12, harmonics 3,-5,7, and
epsilon=2^-20,2^-28,2^-36, it certifies both scaled lift values within
half the modulus of their predicted nonzero opposite residues. Separate
Arb neighbourhoods certify actual zeta is zero-free at these locations.
These are 27 artificial-singularity controls, distinct from the earlier
78 arithmetic-to-zeta comparison cases. The independent checker uses a
different cutoff, the weaker B_1 Euler summation remainder, its own
zeta derivative route, and a rational series for the correction.

5. THE INFINITE ROOT-ORDER LIMIT HAS A DIFFERENT OBSTRUCTION

For beta>0 let U_(b,-,beta)=EXP_Kminus(beta*LOG_*u); use Kplus for +.
Let g(n)=LOG_*u(n), equal to 1/j at p^j and zero otherwise, and
  L_epsilon=beta*log(zeta(1+epsilon)).
Define a probability measure on the angle circle by
  nu_epsilon=(1/log(zeta(1+epsilon)))
                 *sum_n g(n)*n^(-1-epsilon)*delta_theta(n).
For each fixed m!=0 its Fourier coefficient is
LOG(zeta(1+epsilon+i*m*tau))/log(zeta(1+epsilon)). The numerator has a
finite limit, with the logarithm normalized from Re(s)>1: the classical
zero-free line Re(s)=1 implies zeta is nonzero at s_m, allowing this
particular local logarithm to extend. The denominator tends to infinity.
The same positive-measure argument as section 1 gives weak convergence
of nu_epsilon to uniform measure, for theta and for psi.

The true infinite-order zeta-input series has the positive expansion
  U(1+epsilon)=sum_(k>=0) L_epsilon^k/k!
           *integral 1[sum angles<1] d(nu_epsilon^product k).
At each fixed k the probability tends to 1/k!, giving a term asymptotic
to L_epsilon^k/(k!)^2. For any fixed real N>=0, choose an integer k>N.
Positivity then proves
  U(1+epsilon)/(log(1/epsilon))^N -> infinity.

For any fixed lambda>0, 1[sum angles<1]<=exp(lambda-lambda*sum angles).
Summing the complete positive exponential gives
  U(1+epsilon)<=exp(lambda+L_epsilon*nu_epsilon(exp(-lambda*angle))).
The bounded angular function has only a null-limit-measure jump at the
circle cut, so its integral tends to (1-exp(-lambda))/lambda. That value
can be made arbitrarily small by choosing a fixed sufficiently large
lambda. Since L_epsilon~beta*log(1/epsilon), for every a>0 this implies
  epsilon^a*U(1+epsilon)->0.
Both conclusions hold for every fixed b>=2 and beta>0 and both orientations.

Thus U grows faster than every log power but slower than every positive
power of 1/epsilon. It cannot be meromorphic in any neighbourhood of 1:
a removable singularity is bounded and a finite-order pole has power
growth. More strongly no multiplication by (s-1)^a for any finite real
a makes U holomorphic at 1. For a<=0 this is already unbounded. For
a>0 a holomorphic result would have a finite integer zero order k and
give U~c*epsilon^(k-a); this is either positive-power divergence, a finite
nonzero limit, or convergence to zero, all contradicting the two bounds.
The result is not an assertion that 1 is an isolated essential singularity;
continuation to a punctured neighbourhood has not been established.

The finite-q residue 1/q! tends to zero with q, and epsilon*U also tends
to zero. Both normalized iterated limits are zero. This vanishing leading
residue does not establish holomorphic regularity of U; its proved growth
still rules it out. The detector records that stronger inference as false.

6. RIGOROUS LOWER BOUNDS FROM ACTUAL ZETA ANGULAR MOMENTS

The probe also computes actual normalized LOG(zeta) moments with certified
branch corridors from sigma=4 to sigma=1+epsilon. Every interval image is
Arb-certified to exclude zero. Its convex rectangle then has argument
width less than pi, so the principal logarithm of consecutive endpoint
ratios gives the correct continuous increment. At sigma=4, |zeta-1|<1
anchors the principal branch to the normalized Euler logarithm.

There is a finite numerical lower bound on U itself. Fix 0<r<=1/2,
c=r/2 and the Fejer kernel F_N. Outside [0,r] the circular distance
from c is >=r/2, so
  F_N(theta-c)<=C=1/[N*sin(pi*r/2)^2].
Since F_N<=N everywhere, (F_N(theta-c)-C)/N minorizes 1[theta in [0,r]].
Integrating against the positive unnormalized g measure gives the actual
mark mass lower bound
  A_r >= ((1-C)*log(zeta(sigma))
      +2*sum_(m=1)^(N-1)(1-m/N)
          *Re(exp(2*pi*i*m*c)*LOG(zeta(sigma+i*m*tau))))/N.
For ceiling replace LOG by its conjugate. If the computed lower bound
is positive, every exponential term using k atoms in this interval
survives whenever k*r<1, hence
  U(sigma)>=sum_(k=0)^K A_r^k/k!, K*r<1.
This bound uses the complete actual infinite zeta input. It is not a
finite-array extrapolation. The production probe uses r=1/4, N=32, K=3;
the independent checker uses a different Fejer degree, interval and K.

If the angular measure were exactly uniform, the corresponding complete
model would be sum L^k/(k!)^2=I_0(2*sqrt(L)), a classical Bessel series.
Weak convergence at each fixed k does not justify replacing a growing
number of terms by uniform probabilities. No Bessel asymptotic for the
actual U is asserted by this experiment.

7. SCOPE AND SOURCES

The new singularity law is a verified consequence and a negative analytic
gate, not a newly counted primitive. It does not invalidate the formal
size products or their previously proved paired identity. It rejects
their individual unmodified RH-half-plane holomorphy criterion. More
general continuations with additional singularity subtractions remain
unestablished. No conclusion about off-line zeros of zeta is inferred
from singularities created by the transform itself.
