Exact objective and projected trial
Let A*x=b be the same licensed302-row,687-column separator equations after
exact positive row/column infinity scaling. The proposed numerical objective
is unpenalized nonnegative least squares,
  f(x)=1/2*||A*x-b||_2^2, x>=0.
At an extrapolated point y, let r_y=A*y-b and g=A^T*r_y. Given L>0, the
trial z=P_+(y-g/L), where P_+ clamps each coordinate to zero, is the exact
minimizer over z>=0 of g^T*(z-y)+(L/2)*||z-y||_2^2. It is therefore a
valid constrained proximal-gradient trial in exact real arithmetic.
Extrapolated y need not be nonnegative; projected z and accepted x must be.
Clamping y itself would alter the declared acceleration recurrence.

Directional backtracking condition
For d=z-y, the quadratic identity is
  f(z)=f(y)+g^T*d+1/2*||A*d||_2^2.
Consequently the trial acceptance rule
  ||A*d||_2^2<=L*||d||_2^2
is exactly the usual quadratic majorization condition along this direction.
It is not a test that L bounds the curvature in every direction. The initial
L0=max_j||A_j||_2^2 is positive for the licensed nonzero columns and no
larger than a global spectral curvature bound, but it is not generally
that global bound. For example two identical unit columns have L0=1 while
their Gram matrix has largest eigenvalue2. No Gram matrix is needed to
apply the directional check.

In exact arithmetic a sufficiently large L, such as ||A||_F^2, majorizes
every direction, so doubling L can eventually accept. This observation
explains backtracking; it is not an admission premise, a numerical operation
count or an authorization to retry beyond the frozen32-trial cap. Keeping
L after accepted steps makes its sequence nondecreasing. Failed trials must
leave y, its base residual and gradient unchanged; only L/trial data change.
The same candidate z is used for d, A*d and its reported fresh residual.

Acceleration and restart
Start cold with x=y=0 and t=1. After accepting z, use
  t_new=(1+sqrt(1+4*t^2))/2,
  y_new=z+((t-1)/t_new)*(z-x), x_new=z.
The old x is required in the extrapolation; assigning x=z first would erase
momentum. Every64 accepted steps restart with y=z and t=1. The restart
index is the accepted-step count, not the number of backtracking trials.
No penalty continuation, Gram solve, new ray generation or inherited
unchanged-coordinate stopping rule belongs to this design.

The exact recurrence is a familiar accelerated projected-gradient method
with directional backtracking and periodic restarts. This review licenses
no convergence-rate, monotone-residual or finite optimality theorem for its
floating implementation. Accelerated iterates can have increasing residuals;
reported best eligible checkpoints need not be the final iterate. Restarting
changes the trajectory without changing the exact feasibility problem.

Numerical stability and finite checks
Compute A*y-b, A*d and A*z-b as fresh sparse row sums using finite products
and math.fsum, and gradients as finite sparse dot products. Use math.hypot
for Euclidean norms. Stable accumulation improves cancellation behavior,
but products, divisions and final scores remain rounded. Separate rounded
computations of A*y, A*d and A*z need not obey the exact quadratic identity.
Thus a passed floating direction test remains proposal machinery, not an
independently certified majorization or arithmetic inequality.

Require every input conversion, L0, L, gradient, candidate before clamping,
stored trial coordinate, d, sparse product, squared norm, L*||d||^2,
t recurrence, momentum term, extrapolated coordinate and diagnostic to be
finite. L and any division denominator must be strictly positive. Check the
candidate before max(0,candidate), so infinite arithmetic cannot hide as a
stored zero. An overflowing right side of the acceptance test cannot imply
automatic acceptance. Stable norms can be finite while their square is not;
square/product checks are separate. Tiny squares may underflow in binary64,
another reason the acceptance result is not exact evidence.

Exact positive scaling preserves nonnegative feasibility and strict support.
Every exact nonzero coefficient converting to zero or a nonfinite float
must be rejected by the existing model builder. The unpenalized objective
differs from032's scaled unit-L1 continuation and may produce denser
supports. No sparsity or improvement theorem follows from faster kernels.

Work accounting at accepted boundaries
Use fixed declared setup, gradient, trial, commit and final-diagnostic
blocks, charged before their work. With z the sparse nonzero count,
  setup=8z+32(m+n)+65536,
  gradient=8z+32(m+n)+512,
  trial=16z+64(m+n)+1024,
  commit=32(m+n)+512,
  final=12z+64(m+n)+2048.
Their exact numerical work sum is
  setup_charged*setup + gradient_blocks*gradient + trial_blocks*trial
  + commit_blocks*commit + final_charged*final.
Proxy constants must cover sparse products/sums, vector operations, support
and finite scans, diagnostics, checkpoint export and selected-vector copies,
as well as temporary sparse row-product buckets. Additional deadline/live
checks use charge(0) to preserve block reconciliation.

Before beginning each iteration require available work for
  gradient+32*trial+commit+the still-unspent final-diagnostic block.
This is a worst-trial guard for one iteration, not a charge for all32 trials
or a reservation for32 trials at every possible future step. Charge only
actual trial blocks and preserve their counters even on failure. Before any
setup, require minimum_attempt=setup+gradient+32*trial+commit+final.
If it does not fit, return unattempted incomplete with no numerical work.
The setup path leaves one guarded first iteration and the final allocation.
The final
block cannot be counted both as already spent and as reusable remaining work.

If the guard fails at an accepted boundary, normal bounded search may finish
and preserve its selected checkpoint, provided final diagnostics complete.
This is a budget stop, not convergence. The minimum-attempt guard and full
first commit mean every completed trajectory has at least one checkpoint.
Stop at at most10000 accepted steps. The frozen zero_reported_residual stop
follows immediately after a checkpoint whose floating residual norm is zero.
This is a diagnostic stop, not an exact feasibility test; no later checkpoint
is permitted after that zero. Failure during setup, a trial,
commit or final diagnostics, or exhaustion of32 trials without acceptance,
must instead leave the trajectory incomplete, clear selected and forbid
exact restoration from any earlier best checkpoint.

Completed trajectories have gradient_blocks=commit_blocks=accepted records,
one final block, and accepted<=trial_blocks<=32*accepted. Record each
accepted iteration's trial count to reconcile the total. Interrupted
trajectories can have work belonging to at most one additional current
iteration; already charged base/trial/commit work remains counted even if
no accepted record is appended. Counter ordering and checkpoint insertion
must make these prefixes independently checkable without numerical replay.
Each checkpoint's cumulative_operations is after its commit and before final
charging. After final charging, work_boundary requires
operations+gradient+32*trial+commit>max_operations. This is the same failed
boundary guard with final already charged, not a second final reservation.

Final diagnostic and strict selection
After normal boundary/max-step termination, compute a fresh final residual,
gradient and projected-gradient mapping at the final accepted x, using the
current positivefinite L. One conventional mapping is
  G_L(x)=L*(x-P_+(x-grad f(x)/L)).
Its norm, together with residual/support statistics, is only diagnostic.
Even a rounded zero mapping does not certify KKT conditions or optimality;
even an exact NNLS obstruction would concern only the selected generators,
not primal cone infeasibility or all PSD directions.

After each accepted trial has a fresh finite residual, record residual and
strict support size. Among records with1..443 positive coordinates select

positive entry, however tiny; no trimming, threshold or alternate support
trial is licensed. A complete trajectory without an eligible checkpoint is
NONCERTIFIED. A partial trajectory is UNKNOWN and cannot restore.
