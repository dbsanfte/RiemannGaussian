1. FORMAL ARITHMETIC DEFINITION AND CLOSED INVERSE

Use ordinary Dirichlet convolution D in characteristic zero, unit delta_1,
normalized f(1)=1 and augmented h=LOG_D(f). Let pi retain PRIME coefficients
only, discarding proper prime powers and other composites. For p<q prime,
d=q-p, r>=1 integer, define the symmetric bilinear kernel

  J_r(h,k) = sum_(p<q) (h(p)k(q)+h(q)k(p))/2
      * [delta_(p^2*d^r)+delta_(q^2*d^r)-2delta_(p*q*d^r)].

Only pi h and pi k are used. Put

  A_t(h)=h+t J_r(h,h),
  S_t(f)=EXP_D(A_t(LOG_D(f))).

All three indices exceed both inputs. The smallest image is p^2*d^r,
and p^2*d^r>=p^2*d>p+d=q for p>=2,d>=1. Consequently every coefficient
is a finite polynomial in strictly smaller input coefficients. Prefix
semantics are valid on arbitrary signed rational inputs, not just zeta.
For a prefix N, min(p,q)<=sqrt(N) and d^r<=N/min(p,q)^2; hence all
contributing primes are at most sqrt(N)+(N/4)^(1/r). Producer cutoffs use
an integer upper bound on this quantity, rather than sparse zero filling.

Every output index is composite, so pi J=0. Therefore

  pi A_t(h)=pi h,
  A_t(A_u(h))=A_(t+u)(h),
  S_t S_u=S_(t+u), S_t^-1=S_(-t).

This closed inverse is a quadratic shear in logarithmic coordinates.
There is no inverse-tree recursion. The elementary-shear construction is
known algebra; the arithmetic output-support proof makes it apply here.
S is not an ordinary unit-group homomorphism: J(h+k,h+k) has the generally
nonzero extra term 2J(h,k). It also fails universal input-root commutation.

For ordinary input and output roots, respectively,

  S_t(f^beta)=EXP_D(beta*h+t*beta^2*J(h,h)),
  S_t(f)^beta=EXP_D(beta*h+t*beta*J(h,h)),
  their D quotient=EXP_D(t*beta*(beta-1)*J(h,h)).

Thus the logarithmic input amplitude is quadratic, although the scalar
Laplacian cancellation is a second difference in Mellin values. These
are distinct degrees and must not be identified. All prime coefficients
of both LOG and f itself are fixed, since f(p)=LOG_D(f)(p).

The finite support grammar also retains the ODD-input projection: two odd
inputs have even difference, so all correction outputs are even. The same
projection-annihilation proof gives an additive-parameter group for that
different kernel. Its zeta domain and complete fibres are not inferred
from the selected prime-only operator.

2. COMPLETE PRIME FIBRES AND SIGN SEPARATION

For EVEN r, diagonal images p^2*d^r and q^2*d^r are perfect squares.
The off-diagonal image pq*d^r is not a square: p and q do not divide d,
so their valuations are exactly one and its squarefree part is pq.
Positive and negative coefficient supports are disjoint. For the zeta
input each unordered prime pair has weight one, so diagonal coefficients
are nonnegative and nonsquare coefficients are nonpositive.

An off-diagonal image uniquely determines its prime pair: factor N,
retain primes of odd valuation, require exactly two p<q, and test
N=pq(q-p)^r. This is a universal complete-fibre criterion, not a finite
injectivity inference. Diagonal images aggregate edges. For a square N,
each endpoint p must divide sqrt(N); require sqrt(N)/p=d^(r/2), and test
the other endpoint p-d or p+d for primality. These tests recover EVERY
diagonal fibre. No individual diagonal inverse or isometry is claimed.

The first correction coefficients are +1 at 4, -2 at 6 and +1 at 9,
from primes 2,3 with gap one, for every r. The independent checker uses
the complete square/squarefree-part rules, FLINT primality and reconstructed
factor products, rather than the producer's prime-pair enumeration. It
checks all 32764 encoded indices across r=2,4,6,8 through 8192.

3. ZETA BRIDGE AND TWO DIFFERENT CONVERGENCE DOMAINS

For u(n)=1, h=LOG_D(u) equals 1/e at p^e. Its PRIME projection is the
prime indicator. Thus the correction, initially on Re(s)>1, is

  H_r(s)=sum_(p<q) (q-p)^(-rs) (p^-s-q^-s)^2.

This grouped sum extends H holomorphically to

  Re(s)>2/(r+2), r>=3,

and converges absolutely AS PAIRED TERMS at its endpoint, locally uniformly
on compact subsets of the closed half-plane. The boundary is the optimal
right-half-plane threshold. In particular H_4 is holomorphic on Re(s)>1/3.

Proof above the threshold: set sigma=Re(s)>0 and bound |s| on a compact
set. In the balanced region q-p<=p, the fundamental theorem of calculus
gives

  |p^-s-q^-s|<=|s|(q-p)p^(-sigma-1).

If u=2-r*sigma>-1, summing integer gaps d<=p bounds a dyadic block by
O(|s|^2 X^(2-(r+2)*sigma)). If u=-1 there is a logarithm; if u<-1 the
gap sum is bounded and the block is O(|s|^2 X^(-2*sigma-1)). These bounds
are summable above 2/(r+2). In the unbalanced region q>2p, bound the
summand by 4p^(-2sigma)q^(-r*sigma). Summing p<=q/2 gives the same bulk
threshold when 2sigma<1, with a logarithm at 2sigma=1. For 2sigma>1,
the remaining requirement r*sigma>1 is weaker for r>2. This proves
normal absolute convergence of the PAIRED sum on the stated domain.

At sigma_0=2/(r+2), r>=3, we have 2sigma_0<1 and r*sigma_0<2. PNT gives
O(X/log X) primes in a proportional interval. For a balanced block the
derivative estimate and d<=O(X) bound each pair by O(|s|^2 X^-2).
The block has O(X^2/(log X)^2) pairs, so its total is O((log X)^-2),
summable over dyadic X. In the unbalanced region, partial summation gives

  sum_(p<=q/2)p^(-2sigma_0)=O(q^(1-2sigma_0)/log q),

leaving sum_q 1/(q log q), which converges by PNT. This proves endpoint
paired convergence uniformly for bounded |s|. No prime-pair conjecture
or sieve asymptotic is used for this endpoint.

Sharpness: separated proportional prime intervals have order X^2/log^2 X
pairs. For every fixed positive real sigma, their difference has size
at least c_sigma X^-sigma. Thus their paired contribution is at least
c X^(2-(r+2)*sigma)/log^2 X, proving divergence for 0<sigma<2/(r+2).
At the isolated parameter s=0 each paired term vanishes; that does not
provide normal convergence on an open half-plane below the proved edge.
For r=2, the fixed prime p=2 already gives divergence at sigma=1/2
by sum_q 1/q, so its endpoint does not share the r>=3 improvement.

For EVEN r>=4 the ORDINARY arithmetic coefficient series J(pi h,pi h)
instead has exact absolute abscissa 1/2, including endpoint convergence.
Indeed sign separation gives its absolute total exactly as

  B_r(sigma)=sum_(p<q) (q-p)^(-r*sigma)(p^-sigma+q^-sigma)^2.

The balanced region has the same bounds as the gap correlation and its
diagonal parts are comparable. Near-pair Cauchy/bin-count lower bounds
from PNT force divergence below sigma=1/2. Above it an integer majorant
suffices. At the endpoint the uniform unconditional prime-pair sieve bound
from gap_proof.txt section 4 gives summable balanced blocks O(log^-2 X).
Here r/2>=2, so its gap weights have a convergent singular-series sum.
The unbalanced part is bounded by sum_q q^(-r/2)log log q, which converges.

This is a genuine cancellation-domain difference: paired H_4 extends to
Re(s)>1/3, but its ORDINARY coefficient series is not absolutely convergent
below 1/2. Splitting the paired square into three infinite sums in the
smaller domain is invalid. The scalar identity is first proved where all
ordinary series converge and then continued using the grouped H.

4. SHARP WEIGHTED BILINEAR NORM AND ITS LIMIT

Let A_sigma be the absolute coefficient norm sum |h(n)|n^-sigma, sigma>=0.
The prime projection is a contraction. For an edge p<q with d=q-p, the
weighted output absolute mass divided by the two input weights is

  C(p,d)=d^(-r*sigma)[(q/p)^sigma+(p/q)^sigma+2].

For sigma>0 write x=1+d/p. This bracket is
4cosh^2((sigma/2)log x). Increasing p decreases it. At fixed p,

  d/dd log C = -r*sigma/d
               +sigma/(p+d)*tanh((sigma/2)log x)<0.

Thus the maximum is attained at p=2,d=1, which is an actual prime edge.
The half in the symmetric bilinear definition gives the EXACT norm

  ||J_r||_(A_sigma x A_sigma -> A_sigma)
       =[(3/2)^sigma+(2/3)^sigma+2]/2.

The value at sigma=0 follows directly. Inputs delta_2,delta_3 attain it;
their three images are distinct. This is a bilinear norm, not an asserted
sharp quadratic norm on h=h. It applies even without the square/nonsquare
separation, by triangle inequality; the extremizing single edge has no
within-edge cancellation.

For zeta's prime indicator, however, ||pi LOG_D u||_(A_sigma)=P(sigma)
diverges for sigma<=1. The generic bounded bilinear estimate therefore
does not transfer zeta to the critical strip. The paired cancellation proof
supplies separate arithmetic information. Neither norm is an RH estimate.

5. ORDINARY ROOTS, ACTUAL ZEROS, AND RH SCOPE

With t=1 selected, the proved scalar formulas initially for Re(s)>1 are

  S_1(u^beta)(s)=zeta(s)^beta exp(beta^2 H_r(s)),
  S_1(u)(s)^beta=zeta(s)^beta exp(beta H_r(s)).

For arbitrary t insert t in each amplitude. Both corrections are
holomorphic and zero-free on Re(s)>2/(r+2), r>=3. At r=4 this includes
whole neighborhoods of the two certified simple critical-line zeros.
Hence at any zero rho in that domain, multiplicity m is retained in the
ordinary fractional-root exponent m*beta. Around rho a noninteger m*beta
has the usual nontrivial ordinary-zeta root monodromy. For beta=1 the
transformed function is locally holomorphic with the same zero multiplicity.

The positive-beta full ordinary transformed series still has exact absolute
abscissa 1, despite signed coefficients. Absolute convergence above one
follows from EXP_D's absolute majorant. On the real axis the formula
diverges as s decreases to 1: zeta(s)^beta diverges while the exponential
has a finite nonzero limit. Absolute convergence at one would contradict
that limit by dominated convergence. This argument does not require
nonnegative actual coefficients; at t=beta=1 the coefficient at 6 is -1.

After canceling the known pole with F(s)=(s-1)zeta(s), all dyadic roots
F^(2^-j)exp(2^(-2j)H_r), j>=1, extending single-valuedly and holomorphically
to Re(s)>1/2 is equivalent to RH. Under RH a global LOG F exists there.
Conversely a zero of multiplicity m in that half-plane would require
2^j|m for every j, an impossibility. This remains an equivalent criterion,
not a continuation estimate excluding off-line zeros. One fixed root
order alone does not exclude multiplicities divisible by that order.

6. GRAPH POSITIVITY DOES NOT REPLACE HOLOMORPHY

For real s=sigma>0, H is the positive graph Dirichlet energy of the
values x_p=p^-sigma with edge weights |p-q|^-r*sigma. The weights 1,-2,1,
constant-vector null law and positive graph-Laplacian form are classical.
The 49-program completion grammar recovers these weights by constant and
first-difference annihilation; this is a known-operator calibration.

For complex s the holomorphic H uses (x_p-x_q)^2 and d^-rs, without
conjugation. The positive Hermitian expression instead uses

  E_sigma(t)=sum_(p<q)d^(-r*sigma)|p^(-sigma-it)-q^(-sigma-it)|^2.

It depends on both s and its conjugate and generally differs from H.
All six actual-zeta scalar groups reject replacing H by this expression.
Real-axis positivity cannot be carried over as complex holomorphic
positivity. The scalar exponential factor proves no positive spectral
operator whose eigenvalues are Riemann zeros.

At sigma<=1/2 the vector x_p is not in l2, although its paired graph energy
can be finite. This is a finite-energy configuration, not an assertion
that it is in the domain of a self-adjoint Hilbert-space operator. The
finite graph computations do not establish a critical-line spectral RH
model, a determinant identity, or its required trace/domain properties.
