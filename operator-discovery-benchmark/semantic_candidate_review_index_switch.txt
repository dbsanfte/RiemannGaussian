Common notation and the required endpoint accounting
----------------------------------------------------
M(n)=sum_{1<=i<=n}mu(i), M(0)=0. All eight states have weight1. For their
exact U_X(t), retain
 E(X)=X^-2 sum_{t=X}^{2X-1}M(t)^2,
 V(X)=X^-2 sum_{t=X}^{2X-1}U_X(t)^2, W=E+V.
Every increment is the complete child sum minus four times the complete parent
sum, divided by4X^2. Child parameters are recomputed at2X. No algebraic
localization replaces E, deletes a mixed product, or deletes W(1).

The outstanding condition is a single fixed program, some integer q0>=1,
and, for every integer q>=q0, a finite Cq independent of every dyadic X>=1
such that W(2X)-W(X)<=Cq*ceil(X^(1/q)). Fixed positive powers do not suffice.
An additive uniformly bounded error in W changes this condition only by an
additive constant in Cq, because ceil(X^(1/q))>=1. This permits the exact
bounded-remainder reductions below, but does not prove the reduced condition.

IDs33,35,37: point-weighted first differences, orders k=2,3,6
----------------------------------------------------------------
Define
 D(t)=M(t)-M(floor(t/2)), D(0)=0,
 h_k(t)=floor(t^(1/k)), a(t)=mu(h_k(t)) for t>=1,
 A(t)=a(t)D(t), A(0)=0.
The max1 in the old point-query argument makes its t=1 query valid. Its
other factor is D(0)=0, so the AST is exactly
 U(t)=A(t)-A(t-1).
This state has no X parameter; the child has no additional cutoff correction.

Let delta(t)=D(t)-D(t-1). Exact floor endpoints give
 delta(t)=mu(t)-1_{2|t}mu(t/2).
For odd t, delta(t)^2=mu(t)^2. For even t=2n,
 delta(2n)^2=(1+3*1_{n odd})mu(n)^2.
Indeed mu(2n)=-mu(n) for odd n, whereas mu(2n)=0 for even n. Thus the
square of the local D increment is an explicit unsigned squarefree statistic,
not an independently varying signed cancellation term.

The exact discrete product rule is
 U(t)=a(t)delta(t)+(a(t)-a(t-1))D(t-1).
The coefficient can change only at t=m^k, m>=2. Away these boundaries,
 U(t)^2=mu(h_k(t))^2*delta(t)^2<=4.
At a boundary, mu(m^k)=0. If k>=3 and t is even, m^k/2 is also squareful;
if t is odd there is no half increment. Consequently delta(m^k)=0 at EVERY
boundary for k=3,6. For k=2 the same holds except m=2,t=4: if even m>=4,
m^2/2=2*(m/2)^2 is squareful. Hence, apart from that one finite exception,
 U(m^k)=(mu(m)-mu(m-1))*D(m^k-1).
These are root-boundary impulses involving unresolved long-tail prefixes.

This yields an exact and useful all-scale reduction. Set
 S_k(X)=X^-2 sum_{m>=2, X<=m^k<=2X-1}
                  (mu(m)-mu(m-1))^2*D(m^k-1)^2.
For k=3,6,
 V(X)=S_k(X)+X^-2 sum_{X<=t<=2X-1, t not a boundary}a(t)^2 delta(t)^2.
For k=2 add the single correction
 1_{X<=4<=2X-1}*(1+4D(3))/X^2.
To check that correction, at4 we have a=-1, its jump=-2 and delta=1, so
U(4)^2-4D(3)^2=1+4D(3). Its absolute value is at most9 by |D(3)|<=2.
No asymptotic argument is needed to handle this endpoint.

Thus V-S_k is uniformly bounded (indeed at most4/X in absolute value for
k=3,6, and at most4/X+9/X^2 for k=2). The complete cofinal drift condition
for each of IDs33,35,37 is equivalent, up to constants, to that for E+S_k.
This identifies the actual remaining mechanism: positive sparse sampling of
the long-tail energy at perfect powers, rather than generic first-difference
cancellation. D(v)^2 still contains the full cross term
 M(v)^2-2M(v)M(floor(v/2))+M(floor(v/2))^2.
The weight (mu(m)-mu(m-1))^2 cannot erase these source correlations.

Unconditionally |D(m^k-1)|<=m^k/2 and the weight is at most4. Each boundary
therefore contributes at most4 to S_k on its block. The number of boundaries
is at most(2X)^(1/k)+1, giving V=O(X^(1/k)). This is a genuine improvement
over an unrestricted O(X) potential bound, but still a fixed positive power.
Sharper signed E+S_k transport, or sharper sparse-prefix energy control,
remains unproved by these identities.

Unsquared telescoping does hold exactly:
 sum_{t=a}^b U(t)=A(b)-A(a-1).
It does not telescope the stored squares. The precise square identity is
 sum_{t=a}^b U(t)^2=A(b)^2-A(a-1)^2-2 sum_{t=a}^b A(t-1)U(t).
Its residual correlation must be kept. This is why merely naming U a discrete
derivative does not supply a dissipative signed W drift.

IDs34,36,38: bilinear moving differences, orders k=2,3,6
----------------------------------------------------------------
Let r=floor(X^(1/k)), s=(t-r)_+, and
 F_k(u)=M(u)*M(h_k(u)), F_k(0)=0.
The exact AST is
 U_X(t)=F_k(t)-F_k(s).
On the actual block r<=X<=t, s=t-r>=0; the clipping still matters at X1.
The one-step product rule gives, for u>=1,
 F_k(u)-F_k(u-1)=mu(u)M(h_k(u))
                    +1_{u=m^k}mu(m)M(m^k-1).
The root increases by one at each perfect kth power. At every m^k,m>=2,
mu(m^k)=0, so the first term vanishes right at the boundary. At u1 the
second term iszero because M(0)=0.

The moving difference therefore localizes EXACTLY as
 L_X(t)=sum_{s<u<=t}mu(u)M(h_k(u)),
 J_X(t)=sum_{m: s<m^k<=t}mu(m)M(m^k-1),
 U_X(t)=L_X(t)+J_X(t).
Its whole square is L^2+2LJ+J^2. On any window containing no root boundary,
h_k is constant and this further simplifies to
 U_X(t)=M(h_k(t))*[M(t)-M(t-r)].
This is a short-interval state, not a root-prefix envelope for the whole product.

There is at most ONE root boundary in each window (t-r,t]. To verify this
without an asymptotic exception, r^k<=X and
 X-r>=r^k-r>=(r-1)^k.
Relevant boundaries have m>=r, and consecutive such kth powers are more than
r apart. Thus the boundary windows [m^k,m^k+r-1] are disjoint. Put
 ell_X(m)=max(0,min(2X-1,m^k+r-1)-max(X,m^k)+1).
Then the impulse square has the exact diagonal-only form
 S_k^bil(X)=X^-2 sum_m mu(m)^2*M(m^k-1)^2*ell_X(m).
Here diagonal-only is JUSTIFIED by disjoint window support, rather than by
dropping cross terms of simultaneously active bank indices. The mixed term
2LJ remains fully present. ell includes partial windows at both block ends.

Elementary uniform bounds make a stronger reduction possible for ID38.
We have |L|<=r*(2X)^(1/k)<=2X^(2/k), |J|<=2X, and its support has at most
r*((2X)^(1/k)+1)<=3X^(2/k) locations. Consequently
 X^-2 sum L^2<=4X^(4/k-1),
 |2X^-2 sum LJ|<=24X^(4/k-1),
 |V(X)-S_k^bil(X)|<=28X^(4/k-1).
These coarse constants follow from inequalities, not response fitting.
For k=6 the remainder is O(X^(-1/3)), hence uniformly bounded. ID38's
cofinal complete drift condition is therefore equivalent, up to constants,
to that for E+S_6^bil. Its unresolved part is the NONNEGATIVE sparse energy
of M(m^6-1), with squarefree weights and short-band multiplicities. Bounded
smooth-region storage and bounded mixed L/J correction do not control this
impulse energy or its drift. For k=3 the remainder estimate is O(X^(1/3));
for k=2 it is O(X). Those estimates are not cofinal-small-power bounds.
The impulse estimate alone is only S_k^bil=O(X^(2/k)).

Two endpoint/cutoff identities must accompany any later proof. First, fixed-r
unsquared telescoping is
 sum_{t=X}^{2X-1}U_X(t)=sum_{u=2X-r}^{2X-1}F_k(u)
                            -sum_{u=X-r}^{X-1}F_k(u).
For the square one must retain the residual -2F_k(t-r)U_X(t) in
 U_X(t)^2=F_k(t)^2-F_k(t-r)^2-2F_k(t-r)U_X(t).
Second, the child uses r'=floor((2X)^(1/k)), not r. For the frozen parent
extension Ubar_X(t)=F_k(t)-F_k((t-r)_+) on the child block,
 K_X(t)=U_{2X}(t)-Ubar_X(t)
       =F_k((t-r)_+)-F_k((t-r')_+).
This is the sum of the same one-step increments over the additional window.
The complete transition contribution is
 (4X^2)^-1 sum_{t=2X}^{4X-1}[2Ubar_X(t)K_X(t)+K_X(t)^2].
The root impulses in this additional window cannot be replaced by smooth bounds.

ID39: divisor-output bank, moving square-root cutoff
---------------------------------------------------
Set R=floor(sqrt(X)). Its state is exactly
 U_R(t)=sum_{d<=R,d|t}mu(d)M(t/d).
For1<=X<4, R=1 and U=M(t), so this regime is an exact target copy. At any
prime output t>=X>=2, only d1 is admitted and again U_R(t)=M(t). Those
output energies are not canceled by a generic divisor-inversion identity.

The full untruncated function is H(t)=(mu*M)(t) as a Dirichlet convolution
of the arithmetic function M with mu. It satisfies sum_{d|t}H(d)=M(t),
not H(t)=1. In particular H(p)=M(p)-1 at a prime p. The truncated bank is
 H(t)-sum_{R<d|t}mu(d)M(t/d).
That exact complement must survive any full-convolution rewrite. The kernel
here is M(t/d); the inverse-divisor identity with floor(t/d) is different.

All square terms admit a useful exact rearrangement. The diagonal is
 X^-2 sum_{d<=R}mu(d)^2
            sum_{n=ceil(X/d)}^{floor((2X-1)/d)}M(n)^2.
If d divides X its unweighted normalized inner sum is E(X/d)/d^2; otherwise
the explicit integer endpoints remain. For every d<e, let g=gcd(d,e),
L=lcm(d,e). The cross term is exactly
 (2mu(d)mu(e)/X^2)
 sum_{n=ceil(X/L)}^{floor((2X-1)/L)}M((e/g)n)M((d/g)n).
No common-multiple correlation has been discarded. The d1 diagonal is E(X)
itself, and the signed cross terms are the unresolved possible coupling.

Minkowski and |M(n)|<=n give an unconditional O(X) total bound. For each d,
there are at most2X/d admitted outputs and |M(t/d)|<2X/d. Its normalized
squared contribution is at most8X/d^3. Since sum d^(-3/2)<=3,
 V(X)<=72X.
This avoids an unnecessary cutoff-count loss but does not give a cofinal drift.

There is a better unconditional lemma for the child cutoff SQUARE alone.
Let R'=floor(sqrt(2X)) and
 K(t)=sum_{R<d<=R',d|t}mu(d)M(t/d), t in[2X,4X-1].
All admitted d exceed sqrt(X). Using |M(t/d)|<=4X/d and the count of multiples
of L=lcm(d,e) in this block, which is at most4X/L because L<=de<=2X,
 (4X^2)^-1 sum_child K(t)^2
 <=16X sum_{R<d,e<=R'}gcd(d,e)/(d^2 e^2)
 <=32 H_{R'}, H_N=sum_{n=1}^N1/n.
For the last step use d^2e^2>X^2 and
sum_{d,e<=R'}gcd(d,e)<=R'^2 H_{R'}<=2X H_{R'}.
Thus the new-index square costs only O(log X), which individually can be
bounded by Cq*X^(1/q) for every fixed q. This is a substantive boundary lemma.
It does not bound the full correction: 2Ubar_R*K remains. Cauchy-Schwarz with
the elementary old-bank bound gives only O(sqrt(X log X)). An independently
signed common-multiple estimate or a justified negative transport absorption
is still required; the old bank contains the unresolved target energies.

ID40: gcd/quotient commutator, moving cube-root cutoff
----------------------------------------------------
Set R=floor(X^(1/3)), q_d=floor(t/d), g_d=gcd(t,d). On the actual block,
1<=d<=R<=t, so both point-query arguments are positive. The exact state is
 U_R(t)=sum_{d<=R}mu(d)[mu(q_d)M(g_d)-mu(g_d)M(q_d)].
The kernel in brackets is antisymmetric in its two arguments, but the argument
pairs here do not come with a reversed partner. For R>=2, t>=X>=R^3 gives
q_d>=R^2>R>=g_d. Thus no equality q_d=g_d or within-bank argument-swap
cancellation exists in these regimes. Individual equality would make a term
zero, but does not occur there. The d1 contribution is exactly
 mu(t)-M(t)=-M(t-1).
For1<=X<8, R=1 and this is the entire state.

For every nonzero summand d is squarefree, hence g_d is squarefree and
 mu(d)mu(g_d)=mu(d/g_d).
Consequently the state separates exactly into A_R-B_R, where
 A_R(t)=sum_{d<=R}mu(d)mu(q_d)M(g_d),
 B_R(t)=sum_{d<=R,d squarefree}mu(d/g_d)M(q_d).
If d|t and d is squarefree, that particular contribution is
 mu(d)mu(t/d)M(d)-M(t/d).
The coefficient of the large prefix is then -1, independently of the sign of
mu(d). Nondisor terms must remain; the full bank is not a divisor-only bank.

The small-gcd component A_R has an UNCONDITIONAL uniformly bounded normalized
square. This is stronger than the pointwise O(R^2) estimate. Write
 tau_R(t)=sum_{h<=R,h|t}1. The elementary divisor identity for gcd gives
 |A_R(t)|<=sum_{d<=R}gcd(t,d)
          =sum_{h<=R,h|t}phi(h)*floor(R/h)<=R*tau_R(t).
Since lcm(h,j)<=R^2<=X, counts of its multiples in the block are at most
2X/lcm(h,j). Therefore
 sum_{t=X}^{2X-1}tau_R(t)^2<=2X sum_{h,j<=R}gcd(h,j)/(hj)
                           <=2X H_R^3,
 X^-2 sum A_R(t)^2<=2R^2 H_R^3/X<=2H_R^3/R<=54.
The final elementary bound follows from H_R<=3R^(1/3). The sharper expression
2R^2 H_R^3/X tends tozero as X grows. This bound uses only |mu|<=1 and
|M(g)|<=g; it assumes no estimate for large M(q).

Nevertheless the WHOLE potential is
 V_R=||B_R||_X^2-2<A_R,B_R>_X+||A_R||_X^2,
with every d/e pair contained in these terms. B_R includes M(t) from d1 and
many large quotient prefixes. A bounded A component does not bound the mixed
term with B. The elementary estimate is only ||B_R||_X^2<=4X H_R^2;
Cauchy-Schwarz reintroduces its positive-power size. A Young inequality can
compare V_R to B_R energy up to constants and a multiplicative loss, but
pointwise energy comparison does not order their signed innovations.

The child uses R'=floor((2X)^(1/3)); at common t its correction is exactly the
sum of the same commutator terms over R<d<=R'. Expanding 2Ubar_R*K+K^2
retains old/new, new/new and A/B products. The new A component satisfies the
same bounded-small-gcd lemma on the child scale. No corresponding unconditional
cofinal bound for the large-quotient component or its cross terms is obtained.
