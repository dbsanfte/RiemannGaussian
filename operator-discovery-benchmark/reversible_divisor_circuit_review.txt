Reversible divisor circuits: independent analytical theorem review

Scope and conclusion
Pure analytical review of the proposed family. No numerical responses, actual
source tables, tests, hashes or frozen files are read or changed. The finite
inverse and norm/recovery theorem is valid with the qualifications below. It
gives a conditional all-scale moment-to-RH admission rule, not a signed moment
bound. The family avoids the growing-average near-identity mechanism but still
preserves, rather than weakens, the unresolved weighted energy requirement.

1. Coefficients and the negative-fractional case
Fix one function chi on primes with |chi(p)|<=1, independent of output n and
of the function being transformed. It may query a fixed actual-Mobius source.
For rational alpha in[-2,2] define multiplicative coefficients by
 a_alpha,chi(p^k)=chi(p)^k*(alpha)(alpha+1)...(alpha+k-1)/k!,
 a(1)=1. The local generating function is(1-chi(p)x)^(-alpha).
Set r=ceil(|alpha|), with r=0 when alpha=0. For every integer j>=0,
 |alpha+j|<=r+j.
Thus for k>=1 and r>=1,
 |a(p^k)|<=(r)(r+1)...(r+k-1)/k!=d_r(p^k).
This argument also covers negative nonintegral alpha; it does not require its
alternating coefficient signs to have a special pattern. Zero factors at
negative integral alpha cause no problem. Multiplicativity gives |a(n)|<=d_r(n).
For alpha=0 the coefficient is the convolution identity and T is the identity.

2. Exact finite inverse and fixed-source requirement
Define(T_alpha,chi f)(n)=sum_{d|n}a_alpha,chi(d)f(n/d).
The formal local product of powers alpha and-alpha is1. Therefore
a_alpha,chi*a_-alpha,chi=epsilon as an exact Dirichlet-convolution identity.
At each n, every sum in T_-alpha T_alpha is finite over pairs ab|n. For n<=T,
all needed inputs n/(ab)<=T. Consequently restriction to a finite initial
interval commutes with the triangular transform and its inverse: no tail
beyond T is omitted. The diagonal coefficient is1, so there is no loss at1.

The inverse uses the SAME chi(p). If coefficients are changed when the input
changes, derived from current intermediate values, or made output-dependent,
the Euler-convolution inverse is no longer established. Frozen source queries
are fixed coefficients, not new values of the transformed source. Candidate
source descriptions need an all-positive-integer mathematical meaning even
when their saved finite evaluation has only a bounded source horizon.

Point phases S(n)=+1 or-1 are self-inverse isometries. A zero-valued source mask
such as mu(n), or a coprime indicator, is not such a phase. A phase derived from
these predicates must explicitly choose one of the two unit signs at every n.
For a finite circuit applied in forward order, undo the operations in reverse
order, replacing alpha by-alpha and preserving every phase and chi source.
Recovery covers one reversible circuit, or a nonnegative energy stack retaining
one such channel with a fixed positive weight. A signed sum of circuit outputs
inside one square need not be invertible; identity minus identity vanishes.

3. Finite discrete norm theorem
Use the UNSQUARED norm associated with
 H_sigma,T(f)=sum_{1<=n<=T}|f(n)|^2/n^(2sigma+1).
The divisor-supported dilation D_d f(n)=1_{d|n}f(n/d) satisfies EXACTLY
 H_sigma,T(D_d f)=d^(-2sigma-1)H_sigma,floor(T/d)(f).
Triangle inequality and the coefficient domination therefore give
 ||T_alpha,chi f||_T
 <=sum_d |a(d)|d^(-sigma-1/2)||f||_T
 <=zeta(sigma+1/2)^r||f||_T.
For sigma=1/2+epsilon, epsilon>0,
 zeta(1+epsilon)<=1+1/epsilon,
so both T and its inverse have norm at most(1+1/epsilon)^r. No assumption of
finite infinite energy, Mertens growth or RH enters this proof.

For a fixed circuit with R=sum ceil(|alpha_i|), phases costing0,
 ||Circuit f||_T,||Circuit^-1 f||_T<=(1+1/epsilon)^R||f||_T.
The corresponding SQUARED H recovery bound is(1+1/epsilon)^(2R), not the
unsquared factor. Passing to infinity is by monotonic limits of finite bounds.
The blow-up as epsilon decreases is allowed in a qualitative RH bridge;
the circuit and R must remain fixed. Changing the circuit with epsilon or X
would not establish one admissible all-scale candidate.
At n=1 every transform preserves its input and every phase has unit modulus,
so on actual M the observed initial energy is exactly1. Additional initial
storage costs must also be retained in telescoping.

4. Essential distinction: transform M directly, not mu then prefix
The theorem concerns f(n)=M(n) as its INPUT SEQUENCE. Transforming mu instead
and then summing the output produces
 sum_{k<=n}(a*mu)(k)=sum_{d<=n}a(d)M(floor(n/d)).
This is an ordinary quotient sum with no1_{d|n} support. Its integral dilation
norm lacks the additional d^-1/2 from discrete divisibility density, and the
displayed zeta(1+epsilon) bound does not apply to it. The two constructions
are not interchangeable. Likewise a continuous extension must apply the
finite integer circuit first and then use its floor-step extension; it cannot
replace divisibility support by a continuous scaling formula.

5. Exact dyadic-moment-to-integral-to-zeta bridge
Let rho be the output of ONE fixed admissible circuit on actual M, and define
 E_rho(X)=X^-2 sum_{X<=n<2X}|rho(n)|^2 on dyadic X.
For the discrete weighted norm, each block satisfies
 2^(-2sigma-1)X^(1-2sigma)E_rho(X)
 <=sum_block |rho(n)|^2/n^(2sigma+1)
 <=X^(1-2sigma)E_rho(X).
Thus E_rho(X)<=C_delta X^delta with delta<2sigma-1 gives finite global H_sigma.
For the contract's direct moment gate, an unbounded set of positive integers q
with finite Cq and E_rho(X)<=Cq ceil(X^(1/q)) suffices: for each epsilon choose
one such q with1/q<2epsilon. It need not be every integer above a threshold.
The same holds if a full nonnegative storage W=E_rho+V obeys a cofinal dyadic
drift with finite W(1), one fixed circuit, some q0 and finite constants Cq
independent of X for every integer q>=q0:
 DeltaW(X)<=Cq ceil(X^(1/q)).
Telescoping yields E_rho(X)<=W(1)+2Cq(X^(1/q)-1)/(2^(1/q)-1).
For sigma=1/2+epsilon choose q>=q0 with1/q<2epsilon. Recovery then makes the
global discrete H_sigma(M) finite. Only finitely many tested q do not suffice.

For step extension M(x)=M(floor(x)), the continuous weighted integral is<=the
discrete norm; its cell weights are integral_n^(n+1)x^(-2sigma-1)dx. They also
bound it below by2^(-2sigma-1) times the discrete norm. For Re(s)>1/2 choose
1/2<sigma<Re(s). Cauchy-Schwarz implies absolute/local-uniform convergence of
 F(s)=s integral_1^infinity M(x)x^(-s-1)dx.
It is holomorphic there and equals the ordinary sum mu(n)n^(-s)=1/zeta(s)
where Re(s)>1, by ordinary absolute convergence and summation by parts.
The continuation identity(s-1)zeta(s)F(s)=s-1 excludes zeros of the ordinary
Riemann zeta function to the right of1/2. Its functional equation/symmetry
then gives RH. Standard final symmetry references are NIST DLMF25.4.E3 and
25.10.i, also documented in residual_observer_bridge_review.txt.

This bridge is conditional. It does not assert a proved cofinal rho moment or
ordinary Mobius-series convergence at every Re(s)>1/2 directly from moments;
the analytic integral continuation is sufficient. If the experiment instead
retains original E_M as a positive anchor in W, that anchor itself gives this
moment implication; observer recovery must not be double-counted as proof.

6. What reversibility does and does not change
For every sigma>1/2, H_sigma(rho)<infinity if and only if H_sigma(M)<infinity.
This is honest preservation of the target analytic class, not a weaker goal.
Unlike growing normalized averages, an unnormalized divisor convolution need
not become the identity at high inputs. For an impulse at N, T produces value
a(d) at dN; its relative weighted difference from the identity includes
 |a(2)|^2*2^(-2sigma-1), independent of N whenever that output is retained.
Thus the family can express persistent prime/divisor interactions at arbitrarily
large scales. This is a real additional search ability, not a moment theorem.

Each convolution has diagonal1. A circuit of convolutions and unit phases has
diagonal the product of its phases, of modulus1. Its other terms use proper
divisor inputs<=n/2. A coherent fresh target block is therefore present in the
observer with full diagonal magnitude; a source-free norm/past proof cannot
make it disappear. Fixed-source coefficients can encode genuine arithmetic
constraints, but those constraints must be used to prove the signed moment.
Invertibility alone does not establish them. This is the whole-target version
of the fresh-block obstruction, rather than the blindness of old quotient banks.

7. Algebraic degeneracies that should remain visible
Same-chi convolution nodes obey T_alpha,chi T_beta,chi=T_alpha+beta,chi; inverse
pairs cancel. All divisor convolutions commute, even with different coefficient
sources, though different sources generally do not merge into one Euler power.
The order of noncommuting phase nodes is where new circuit interactions arise.
alpha=0 or chi identically0 gives the identity. On a prime domain mu(p)=-1 and
mu(p^2)=0, so such queries can be a constant coefficient source or an identity
source, rather than new independent signs.

If S is a COMPLETELY MULTIPLICATIVE unit sign, then
 S T_alpha,chi S=T_alpha,chi*S,
where the new prime source is chi(p)S(p). Hence such phase conjugation folds
into coefficients and is not intrinsically a new noncommuting mechanism.
A generic parity/source phase need not be completely multiplicative; this
reduction must not be asserted merely from its pointwise unit modulus.
Nonmultiplicative source-conditioned phases retain factorization-dependent
interactions S(n)S(n/d), which need explicit signed analytic estimates.

Full divisor inverse illustrates another important non-alias. T_1,1 has divisor
coefficient1 and T_-1,1 has coefficientmu. On source mu, T_1,1 mu=epsilon.
On the actual observer input M they instead give divisor sums of M, not the
bounded full quotient identity. At a prime p,
 (T_1,1 M)(p)=M(p)+1, (T_-1,1 M)(p)=M(p)-1.
Consequently one cannot certify a bounded observer by applying the source
inverse identity to the prefix sequence. Transforming a source then prefixing
is the different, nonadmitted operation from section4.

8. Where analytic simplification could actually come from
The useful possibilities are exact divisor-source identities, complementary
channels kept intact, and nonmultiplicative phase/divisor interactions whose
complete signed square can be controlled. A commutator circuit involving a
unit phase and a convolution is legitimately reversible and can retain those
interactions; if the phase is a multiplicative character it reduces as above.
These are generic mechanisms, not a favorite formula or expected result.

An analytic proof must bound the fixed observed square for all dyadic scales,
including its diagonal, divisor-pair, phase and source-complement terms. Full
divisor inversion, prime-conditioned laws and fresh nonmultiple prefixes can
provide identities; none authorizes dropping a remaining channel or importing
an unknown Mertens-strength estimate. The recovery factor explicitly guarantees
that a truly easier proved observer estimate would prove the original result.
It does not guarantee that any generated representation is easier to estimate.

9. Read-only interface consistency check
The new reversible_divisor_circuit_contract.txt and the independent
check_reversible_divisor_circuits.py were read as source, without execution.
They use prefix M as input, prime-factorized generalized-binomial coefficients,
all output divisors, fixed original source values for every phase, strict unit
phase syntax and the correct unsquared recovery-power metadata. The checker
admits the restricted theorem schema by independent reconstruction; it is not
a proof assistant or source-specific moment certificate. No mathematical
interface inconsistency was found in this read. Producer/root workflow and
development execution are outside this analytical review receipt.

Final disposition
Finite inverses, generalized-binomial domination and polynomial-in1/epsilon
norm recovery are sound for fixed divisor-supported circuits on M with genuine
unit phases. The dyadic-to-ordinary-zeta/RH bridge is sound under the unproved
fixed-candidate cofinal moment premise. The family is mathematically justified
and does not suffer the same growing-average near-identity limitation, but
whole-target and algebraic alias obstructions remain. No signed all-scale bound
or RH conclusion is established by this review.
