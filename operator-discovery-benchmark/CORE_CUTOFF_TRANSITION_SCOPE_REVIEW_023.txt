1. Findings that should change the next search

The current global low-height leaf is mathematically sufficient, but is a
substantial arithmetic assertion. Its fixed-height portion already implies
a uniform power-saving Mertens estimate and a fixed zero-free half-plane.
It should not be presented as a routine finite-polynomial calibration.

There are two useful executable alternatives. First, generate finite inverse
heads for every residual degree, center those finite heads completely, and
pay their storage by a bounded-prefix rule. This uses actual mu*One=delta
and is a genuine reusable arithmetic construction. Second, search localized
physical storage or cutoff-transition bounds at the SAME physical horizon,
rather than automatically requiring an all-x global Mellin norm. A global
recurrence which closes the current leaf inherits its strong arithmetic
consequences; changing its notation does not weaken the goal.

None of these observations closes the tail, the complete signed core, or RH.
The existing low leaf remains a valid optional sufficient route. The new
languages should retain its history instead of silently replacing an open
goal by a weaker claim with the same certificate name.

3. Fixed-height coercivity: the low leaf implies a Mobius L10 bound

Choose a fixed small t0<=1 such that both zeta(sigma+it) and
zeta(sigma+u+it) have positive modulus lower bounds on |t|<=t0. This is
possible without a zero-free region: zeta(a)<0 for real 0<a<1 by its
alternating eta representation, and continuity supplies the small interval.
The fixed lines are sigma=9/20 and sigma+u=47/60. On this compact set zeta
also has a finite upper bound. The inherited |c_U|<=2 is uniform in U.

Since E_U=1+zeta(s)c_U-zeta(s)A_U, there is a fixed threshold B such that
|E_U(s)|>=C_0|A_U(s)| whenever |A_U(s)|>=B. The shifted zeta lower bound
and the fixed upper bound on |s| then give, for ALL complex A_U values,

 |A_U(sigma+it)|^10 <=C_1[1+|F_U(sigma+it)/(sigma+it)|^2].

Thus (2.1), just on this fixed compact interval, implies

 integral_(-t0)^(t0)|A_U(sigma+it)|^10 dt <=C_2 U^(12/5).       (3.1)

The large H_U is not the sole source of difficulty. Even the fixed-height
head with exponent 12/5 already asks for strong actual Mobius cancellation.
Generic harmonic cancellation alone cannot provide it, as review021 shows.

4. From local vertical L10 to the actual weighted Mobius prefix

A pointwise conclusion cannot be obtained by treating an arbitrary L10
function as sampleable. The following finite-polynomial reproduction proof
supplies the missing step, with an arbitrarily small U exponent loss.

Let P_U(t)=sum_(n<=U)a_n n^(-sigma-it), |a_n|<=1. Assume its L10 norm on
[-t0,t0] has tenth power O(U^beta), uniformly U. Fix delta>0 and set
T=U^delta log(2U). For a fixed integer L>=0 use the Fourier multiplier

 psi_(L,T)(w)=exp[-w^2/(4T^2)] sum_(k=0)^L [w^2/(4T^2)]^k/k!.

Its inverse Fourier kernel K_(L,T)(t) is a Gaussian times a fixed polynomial:
with K_T(t)=T/sqrt(pi)*exp(-T^2 t^2), it equals

 sum_(k=0)^L (-1)^k/(4^k T^(2k) k!) * derivative_t^(2k) K_T(t).

Consequently ||K_(L,T)||_(10/9)=C_L T^(1/10), and the integral of its
absolute value outside any fixed t0 is bounded by a polynomial in T times
exp(-c t0^2 T^2). Also, for 0<=w<=log U,

 |1-psi_(L,T)(w)|<=C_L (log U/T)^(2L+2).

Expand the finite polynomial before integrating. Its reproduction error at
zero is at most C_L U^(1-sigma-2delta(L+1)). Choose the fixed L large
enough that this exponent is negative. The outside-interval integral costs
at most U^(1-sigma) times the exponentially small kernel tail. On the inside,
Holder gives

 |P_U(0)|<=C_L T^(1/10)
                [integral_(-t0)^(t0)|P_U(t)|^10dt]^(1/10)+O(1).

Given ANY epsilon>0, choose delta sufficiently small, then L sufficiently
large, and absorb log(2U)^(1/10) into the remaining epsilon. The constants
may depend on epsilon,t0,sigma,L but not U. Therefore

 |P_U(0)|=O_epsilon(U^(beta/10+epsilon)).                       (4.1)

This is an elementary finite Gaussian argument, not a new zeta moment
import. It uses |a_n|<=1 for the reproduction error and remote tail, and a
genuine neighborhood norm for the local term. It does not assert exact
point sampling of an arbitrary Mellin L2 auxiliary.

Apply (4.1) to (3.1), beta=12/5. We obtain the conditional implication

 A_U(9/20)=O_epsilon(U^(6/25+epsilon)), EVERY U>=1.              (4.2)

Partial summation, using the same actual mu at ALL cutoffs, gives exactly

 Mertens(U)=U^sigma A_U(sigma)
            -sigma integral_1^U A_t(sigma)t^(sigma-1)dt,

and hence

 Mertens(U)=O_epsilon(U^(69/100+epsilon)).                      (4.3)

For every s with Re s>69/100, choose epsilon small enough that Abel tails
of sum mu(n)n^(-s) converge locally uniformly. This holomorphic series equals
1/zeta(s) on Re s>1. Analytic continuation of zeta(s) times that series is
1 throughout the half-plane, with the usual removable product at its pole.
Thus (2.1) implies that zeta has no zeros with Re s>69/100. This is an
implication of a presently OPEN arithmetic hypothesis, not a newly proved
zero-free region or an RH equivalence. It is a useful hardness diagnostic.

For a GENERAL p,m version with compact coercivity degree 2(p+m), an all-U
global leaf U^beta similarly implies a Mertens exponent
sigma+beta/[2(p+m)]+epsilon. The detector can generate this scope annotation
algebraically instead of mistaking the leaf for a low-cost calibration.

5. Global centering can overpay the physical target

The reviewed Gaussian Mellin bridge identifies F_U(s)/s with the transform
of the COMPLETE centered staircase

 C_U(x)=P_U(x)-(M_U/theta)x^theta, ALL x>0,
 P_U(x)=sum_(n<=x)(K*A^2*E^3)(n).

For 0<x<1, this is -(M_U/theta)x^theta. The global norm therefore includes
|M_U|^2/[2theta^2(theta-sigma)] from an artificial below-unit centering tail.
It is mathematically required by that Mellin identity, but physical storage
on distant blocks does not need to pay it at the same U exponent. The old
integer/history error uses the weaker residue estimate
|M_U|^2<=C U^(10/3), then divides its storage by X; that is already paid.

If one instead defines the centered function to vanish below 1, its transform
is F_U(s)/s-M_U/[theta(s-theta)]. That subtraction is legitimate only with
this CHANGED physical definition, all compensations explicitly restored.
It is not a second residue subtraction from the original global bridge.
It removes one unnecessary global cost, but does not itself establish a
localized bound or erase compact-height arithmetic difficulty.

More generally the global norm integrates all x, including prefixes near
the finite cutoff and far beyond the actual finite source horizon N. The
source needs storage near X about U^24, plus exactly its channel histories.
A global bound is sufficient and convenient; it need not be necessary.
Generic global sharpness from review021 is not a lower bound for that
prescribed physical block, projected channels, or the joined signed core.

6. A precise physical localization language

Put G_U(t)=F_U(sigma+it)/(sigma+it) and
f_U(y)=exp(-sigma*y)C_U(exp y), interpreted by the reviewed L2 Mellin limit.
Then f_U(y)=(1/(2pi))integral G_U(t)exp(ity)dt. A continuous block storage is

 X^(-2) integral_X^(2X)|C_U(x)|^2dx
 =X^(2sigma-1) integral_0^(log2)
       exp[(2sigma+1)v]|f_U(log X+v)|^2dv.                    (6.1)

Its Mellin expression is a DOUBLE Hermitian form in G_U(t),G_U(w), with
kernel

 exp[i(t-w)log X] integral_0^(log2)exp[(2sigma+1)v]exp[i(t-w)v]dv.

This is positive as a whole, retains all off-diagonal heights and source
phases, and is different from the diagonal global integral integral|G|^2.
Use Gaussian regulators or L2 operator limits for the double expression;
do not assume absolute double-integral convergence merely from G in L2.
Dropping its off-diagonal terms is not a localization theorem.

The actual finite channel/cell operator can be compiled more concretely.
For each integer n>=1 its averaged prefix kernel is

 Q_n(t)=integral_n^(n+1)x^(sigma+it)dx
       =[(n+1)^(sigma+1+it)-n^(sigma+1+it)]/(sigma+1+it).

Each complete-history polynomial channel is an EXACT finite linear map on
q_n=(1/(2pi))integral G_U(t)Q_n(t)dt, using every n up to the horizon and
q_0=0. Compose the map with the saved cell means and parent/child storage.
The resulting finite-rank positive kernel is a sum of products of these
linear kernels. The separate analytic-residue averaging error is paid by
the reviewed rule. This construction avoids unsupported L2 sampling and
keeps all earlier prefixes, units and low/high block crosses. It also gives
an honest executable object on which arithmetic signs can be searched.

The localized kernel must use the original complete signed coefficients,
both parent and child with the same N and cutoffs. A local restart at X,
discarded history, or a Gaussian height diagonal substituted for this
physical quadratic form changes the problem. A finite calibration of the
kernel is evidence for a conjectured all-scale rule, not its proof.

7. A reusable actual inverse-head construction is provably paid

For all integers 1<=n<=U, actual mu*One=delta implies

 (A^p*E^m)(n)=c_U^m d_(m-p)(n) if m>=p,
               c_U^m mu^(p-m)(n) if m<p.

Powers mean Dirichlet convolution, with zero power delta. Let h_head be
this EXACT coefficient array truncated to n<=U, and h_tail=A^p*E^m-h_head.
Then h_tail(n)=0 for n<=U. This is a finite-support identity, not a claim
that the whole global function equals the infinite divisor function.

Here is a general paid rule for ANY finite h with finite absolute mass L.
For u=1-theta in (0,1), integral comparison gives the exact safe bound

 |sum_(k<=y)k^(-u)-y^theta/theta|<=1/theta, EVERY y>0.          (7.1)

For y<1 the sum is zero and the displayed bound still holds. Consequently
the completely centered K*h prefix equals

 sum_d h(d)[sum_(k<=x/d)k^(-u)-(x/d)^theta/theta],

and is bounded in absolute value by L/theta for EVERY x>0, including ALL
d>x. The analytic moment is the ordinary finite sum M_h=sum_d h(d)d^(-theta).
No divisor or small-prefix term is omitted.

For a channel polynomial p(t)=sum c_l t^l, let P0=sum|c_l|, P1=sum l|c_l|,
eta>=0. Complete discrete Abel summation and
sum_(k<n)1/k / log(2n)<=2 give the uniform channel bound

 |channel_n|<=2^ceil(eta)(P0+2P1)L/theta.

Thus its block/projected energy is O(L^2/X), with fixed explicit safe channel
constants and unchanged history. The finite inverse heads above have
L<=C U(1+log U)^(r-1) for fixed r=|m-p|>=1, using |c|<=2 and the elementary
divisor mass bound; absolute mu^r coefficients are bounded by d_r. For r=0
the head is c^m delta and its mass is bounded independently of U.
At alpha=1/24, every fixed-degree head therefore has storage
O(U^2 polylog(U)/X), strictly paid since 2alpha-1=-11/12. This covers all
current residual degrees, rather than an analyst-nominated E^3 head alone.

For p2,m3 specifically, h_head=c^3 One*1_(n<=U), L<=8U, and its ALL-prefix
bound is <=12U. Its finite residue is M_head=c^3 sum_(d<=U)d^(-theta).
The exact decomposition is

 centered(K*A^2*E^3)
   =centered(K*h_head)+centered(K*h_tail).

When subtracting the newly paid centered head from a source residual, its
opposite (M_head/theta)J_theta drift must remain in the exact signed join.
The original scalar +18, same cutoffs and all 144 recovery bindings still
apply. A publication needs to compile/check that bookkeeping before calling
it a new payment; this review does not modify the existing publication.

The tail support gap is useful arithmetic information, but its ANALYTIC
centering again contributes -(M_tail/theta)x^theta below U. Therefore a
support gap alone does not bound the low Gram or avoid all residue costs.
Subtracting an INFINITE c^3 One instead of the finite head introduces a new
pole at 1 and a different tail; complete finite restoration is compulsory.

There is a stronger unconditional global low-norm bound for these heads.
Let D_theta(y) be the bracket in (7.1). Direct integration gives

 ||D_theta||_(x^(-2sigma-1)dx)^2 <= B_(sigma,theta)
 :=(1/(2theta^2))[1/(theta-sigma)+1/sigma], 0<sigma<theta.

The below-unit part is integrated exactly; the above-unit part uses (7.1).
For any finite h, scaling y=x/d and Minkowski imply

 ||centered(K*h)||^2 <=B_(sigma,theta)[sum_d |h(d)|d^(-sigma)]^2.    (7.2)

The finite-head Gaussian Mellin identity identifies this complete norm with
integral_R |zeta(s+u)h(s)/s|^2dt/(2pi). No twisted theorem or ordinary
infinite coefficient moment is needed; h has finite support. For h=c^a d_l
restricted to n<=U, l>=1, elementary divisor mass and partial summation give

 sum_(n<=U)|h(n)|n^(-sigma)
 <=[2^a/(1-sigma)] U^(1-sigma)(1+log U)^(l-1).

For mu^l the same absolute divisor envelope applies; for l=0 the mass is
just <=2^a. At sigma9/20,theta2/3, the safe B is exactly 100/13. Hence a
general primitive head has complete global AND low-window norm bounded by

 [100/13]*4^a/(1-sigma)^2 *U^(11/10)(1+log U)^(2l-2),             (7.3)

with a constant bound in the delta case. For the specific c^3 One head the
safe rational coefficient is 2560000/1573. Exponent 11/10 and any fixed log
loss are strictly harmless relative to the desired 12/5. This rule is
robust at every integer cutoff, including the unit and all below-support x.

The difference of two such finite heads at U<=U'<=dU is bounded in complete
norm by twice the sum of their squared norms, so its entire moving-window
shell is paid with the same 11/10 exponent and fixed logs. This pays ONLY
finite-head corrections, not the raw A^2 E^3 cutoff shell. It also proves
that raw and head-corrected GLOBAL low targets with exponent12/5 are
equivalent up to fixed constants: each is at most twice the other plus the
paid head norm. Head subtraction changes the arithmetic representation,
not the strength of that global goal or its Mertens consequence. A physical
localized goal is the separate route which may be genuinely weaker.

8. Exact cutoff transitions with all compensations

Let U'>=U and define the actual shell polynomial
D(s)=A_(U')(s)-A_U(s), delta_c=D(1), Q(s)=delta_c-D(s).
Exactly

 c_(U')=c_U+delta_c, T_(U')=T_U+Q, Q(1)=0,
 E_(U')=E_U+zeta(s)Q(s).

For p2,m3 the full component transition is

 Delta h =sum_(0<=r<=2,0<=k<=3,(r,k)!=(0,0))
   binom(2,r)binom(3,k) A_U^(2-r)D^r E_U^(3-k)(One*Q)^k.       (8.1)

For arbitrary p,m use the same generated binomial formula. The corresponding
F difference is zeta(s+u) times its Dirichlet function. Its residue difference
is obtained by evaluating the SAME expression at theta, and the centered
transition includes -(Delta M/theta)J_theta with unit 1. These identities are
global identities of the specified COMPONENTS, not of a truncated full inverse
word beyond its certified source horizon N.

Although D has no coefficients at n<=U, Q has a unit coefficient delta_c.
Thus One*Q starts immediately. For the actual p2,m3 head,
Delta h(n)=(c_(U')^3-c_U^3)One(n), n<=U. A shell-only recurrence which sets
this head to zero has dropped the compensation. The trivial shell bound
|delta_c|<=sum_(U<n<=U')1/n is O(1) for U'/U bounded, not a negative U
power. Any stronger shell cancellation must remain an explicit arithmetic
premise; harmonic nullness Q(1)=0 alone does not provide it.

The complete joined source is an exact identity on the common finite horizon
n<=N. Different truncated inverse programs may discard different support
terms beyond N. Their cutoff invariance cannot be promoted to a global
Dirichlet-function identity without carrying those discarded terms. Other
source modes/cutoffs, original drifts, transport -e, old paid pieces and
correlations also have to change by their exact identities, not just (8.1).

For a one-step compiler let q>=2, U=q-1, U'=q, x=mu(q), c=c_(q-1),
Z(s)=q^(-s), L(s)=zeta(s)(q^(-1)-Z(s)). Then A'=A+xZ, E'=E+xL.
The binomial transition (8.1) reduces, using x in {-1,0,1} and x^3=x, to
x*J_odd+x^2*J_even. These are generated by grouping all odd and positive
even powers of x, not by guessing a replacement polynomial.

The finite-head difference is EXACTLY

 H_q-H_(q-1)=c^3 delta_q
       +x(3c^2/q+1/q^3)One_(<=q)+x^2(3c/q^2)One_(<=q).

Thus the head-corrected tail transition requires

 J0=-c^3 delta_q,
 Jo=J_odd-(3c^2/q+1/q^3)One_(<=q),
 Je=J_even-(3c/q^2)One_(<=q),
 Delta h_tail=J0+x*Jo+x^2*Je.                                  (8.2)

In particular J0 remains when mu(q)=0: the finite head boundary moved even
though A and c did not. Omitting it falsely preserves the support-gap tail.
Each finite-head atom in (8.2) has its own finite analytic residue; the
corresponding J_theta compensator and opposite drift must be included.
Formula (7.2) pays J0 at a squared-norm cost O(q^(-2sigma)); the odd head
correction has the same order because its 1/q factor multiplies a weighted
One head of size q^(1-sigma), while the even correction is smaller. Summing
their norms over q<=U gives O(U^(1-sigma)), square O(U^(11/10)). This is
another exact way to verify that head corrections are harmless. None of
these estimates bounds J_odd,J_even of the raw unknown shell.

For an arbitrary primitive c^a d_l head, the independent-of-x boundary atom
is c^a d_l(q)delta_q; expand (c+x/q)^a-c^a on the NEW finite d_l head and
reduce powers of x by x^3=x. A delta head (l=0) has no boundary atom for
q>=2. The compiler must preserve this distinction instead of forcing a
nonzero J0 for every primitive.

9. Moving height boundaries and noncircular recurrence closure

For the low norms H_U<H_(U'), an exact decomposition is

 I_(U')=I_U
  +(1/(2pi))integral_(H_U<=|t|<H_(U'))|F_U(s)/s|^2dt
  +2 Re[(1/(2pi))integral_(|t|<H_(U'))(F_U(s)/s)conj(Delta F(s)/s)dt]
  +(1/(2pi))integral_(|t|<H_(U'))|Delta F(s)/s|^2dt.             (9.1)

The OLD-F annulus is paid by its old high theorem, <=C U^(-1/16). This does
not pay the CHILD-F annulus automatically: (9.1) retains Delta F and its
cross over the WHOLE enlarged interval. Alternatively a child high provider
can cover that annulus after verifying its own length premise, with fixed
cutoff-ratio constants and any needed strict-margin adjustment. One cannot
use a new-polynomial length premise at the old threshold without checking it.

Suppose a NEW, independently proved uniform transition estimate gives
the last norm in (9.1) <=D U^beta for all U<=U'<=2U, beta=12/5. Minkowski
then gives a genuine telescoping recurrence for sqrt(I_U), with the old
annulus cost included. It closes I_U=O(U^beta) from a finite base because
sum of geometric U^(beta/2) increments is O(U^(beta/2)). This shell norm
hypothesis remains OPEN; the identity (8.1) has not proved it.

Equivalently a recurrence I_(2U)<=q I_U+D U^beta closes with a fixed constant
only when q<2^beta. Equality gives a log U loss, and q>2^beta gives a larger
exponent. For rational beta and q, the detector can check the comparison by
exact integer powers. A recurrence only at U=2^k does not establish (2.1)
for every integer; require uniformly controlled intermediate cutoffs or an
explicit arbitrary-U halving induction. Its constants and base are fixed.

If the shell estimate was obtained from ||Delta F||<=||F_(U')||+||F_U|| and
the desired original target, this is circular. A proof graph must keep the
shell signed-Gram bound as an open leaf and reject a cycle back through I.
Global recurrence closure, even with exact shell algebra, still implies the
Mertens consequence in section 4. It is not an easier goal by definition.

For a PHYSICAL localized recurrence, keep X fixed while changing U, with a
state domain such as 1<=U<=C X^(1/24), using the same physical operator,
channel history, N and coefficients. A transition norm bound
||Delta||_storage<=D U^b X^(-eta), b>0, eta>=b/24, telescopes geometrically
to a bounded component; strict extra saving absorbs fixed log costs. For
b=0, plain addition incurs log U unless a genuine contraction, orthogonality
or signed correlation rule is proved. Base U=1 must be bounded uniformly
in X. Comparing instead X(U) with X(2U) silently moves the observed physical
block by a factor about 2^24 and requires an additional scale rule. Exact
cutoff transitions alone do not supply self-similarity of Mobius weights.

10. Suggested executable polynomial auxiliary/recurrence contract

The grammar should generate bounded-degree polynomials in old A,T,E and new
shell D,Q, with Q=D(1)delta-D; derive their head constraints by exact inverse
algebra; and compile the full binomial transition, residue map, finite head
subtraction, and either global or physical localized Hermitian kernel.
No chosen shell amplitude, cutoff or asserted cancellation is an input.
All relation rows and discarded support terms have exact scope labels.

Identity nodes are PROVED by coefficient/algebra replay. A proposed signed
Gram inequality, contraction coefficient, or shell power gain is CONDITIONAL
until an all-U/all-X arithmetic rule proves it. The recurrence-closing node
depends on its genuine base, shell inequality, moving-boundary cost and exact
strict multiplier comparison. It cannot depend on its own desired bound.
Analytic and ordinary moments remain separate; all compensation units and
opposite drifts remain. Existing 144 source bindings are carried unchanged
unless a separately checked exact new recovery actually pays an auxiliary.

Finite actual arithmetic calibration may rank these generated constructions
and record complete signed values. It must include both old and new windows,
every j,k and ordered divisor pair, all history operators and residues. It
cannot convert a fitted scale exponent into a uniform theorem or treat
observed positive slack as a proof of a contraction at all cutoffs. A smaller
local target may be worthwhile even when the global low leaf does not fit.
The detector should report which grammar it actually searched, rather than
claiming a general recurrence or arithmetic-bound synthesizer already exists.
