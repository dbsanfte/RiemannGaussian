2. A block-energy theorem with no asymptotic expansion assumption
---------------------------------------------------------------
For a real step function Q(n), define
  E_Q(X)=X^(-2)*sum_(n=X)^(2X-1) |Q(n)|^2,
and M_Q(s)=int_1^infinity Q(x)*x^(-s-1) dx when it converges. Suppose
  E_Q(2^j)<=K*(1+j)^p  for all sufficiently large j, p>=0.             (2.1)
For s=1/2+delta+i*tau, 0<delta<=1/4, Cauchy on each dyadic block gives
  |int_(2^j)^(2^(j+1)) Q(x)*x^(-s-1)dx|
       <=sqrt(E_Q(2^j))*2^(-delta*j).
Indeed its unweighted L2 integral is 2^(2j)*E_Q(2^j), while the integral
of x^(-2Re(s)-2) is at most 2^(-j*(2Re(s)+1)). Therefore
  |M_Q(1/2+delta+i*tau)|
       <=K_head+sqrt(K)*sum_(j>=0)(1+j)^(p/2)*2^(-delta*j)
       =O(delta^(-1-p/2)).                              (2.2)
The elementary last bound follows by comparison with the exponential
integral after substituting y=delta*t. This also proves holomorphy of
M_Q in Re(s)>1/2 by locally uniform absolute convergence.

If its analytic continuation has a nonzero leading singularity
  M_Q(rho+delta)=c*delta^(-ell)*(1+o(1)), c!=0, ell>=1,
then (2.2) forces
  p>=2*ell-2.                                         (2.3)
This is a necessary condition only. It neither proves a matching bound
nor controls other zeros. No pointwise asymptotic, prime randomness,
positivity of Q, or RH assumption is required. For ell=2, degrees p=0
and p=1 are impossible. A model oscillation x^(1/2)*log(x) at frequency
Im(rho) illustrates why degree two is compatible with the local pole;
it is not asserted to be the complete actual-source asymptotic.

4. Log-Abel preserves the leading oscillatory pole
-------------------------------------------------
Put B(x)=sum_(n<=x) f(n)*log(n), and define the continuous version
  P(x)=B(x)/log(x) for x>1, P=0 on [1,2).
The value at x=1 is immaterial. In the absolute-convergence half-plane,
  int_1^infinity B(x)*x^(-s-1)dx=-D_f'(s)/s,
and hence
  M_P'(s)=D_f'(s)/s.                                   (4.1)
This derivative identity is exact. In particular it does not replace
B by an approximate pointwise derivative or assume a Mertens estimate.

Suppose a polylog energy claim for the actual discrete log-Abel output
holds. The continuous and discrete transforms differ by a function
holomorphic in Re(s)>0, as follows. The proved coefficient envelope is
  |f(n)|<=C_f*d_2(n), C_f=3*beta+R_u,
and the elementary divisor sum gives
  |B(n)|<=C_f*n*(1+log n)*log n.
For x in [n,n+1), n>=64,
  |B(n)/log(n)-B(x)/log(x)|
    <=C_f*(1+log n)/log n<=2*C_f.                      (4.2)
Here B(x)=B(n), and the reciprocal-log difference is at most
1/[n*(log n)^2]. Below64 the evaluator's G(n)=F(n) is merely a
finite-interval modification. Its Mellin transform is entire. Explicitly,
for dyadic X>=64 the continuous block energy satisfies
  X^(-2)*int_X^(2X) |P(x)|^2 dx
       <=2*E_G(X)+8*C_f^2/X.
This uses the square triangle inequality and retains the entire difference
rather than discarding its cross term. The proof of section2 applies
unchanged to this continuous block energy. Thus a polylog energy bound
for discrete G gives the same log degree for P and a finite head.
M_P is holomorphic
in Re(s)>1/2, and (4.1) continues there by the identity theorem (if an
interior pole prevented this, the proposed bound would already fail).

More generally let D_f(s)=d_ell*(s-rho)^(-ell)+lower terms, ell>=2.
Integrating (4.1) along the rightward horizontal approach yields
  M_P(rho+delta)=(d_ell/rho)*delta^(-ell)
       +O(delta^(1-ell)*(1+|log delta|)).               (4.3)
This follows by multiplying D_f' by the Taylor expansion of 1/s at
rho!=0 and integrating the resulting Laurent terms. Any residue term
produces only a lower-order logarithm, and the integration constant is
bounded. Therefore (4.3) has exactly the same leading coefficient as
D_f(s)/s. The bounded difference (4.2) has a transform holomorphic near
rho and cannot cancel it. This proves the discrete output's leading
double-pole barrier without presuming exact log-degree preservation by
the separate Volterra inverse theorem.
