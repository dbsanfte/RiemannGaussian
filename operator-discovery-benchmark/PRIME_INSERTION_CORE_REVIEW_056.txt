Scope and disposition
=====================
This new review preserves every frozen 055 and earlier file. No scientific
source values, numerical integrals, tests, final scores, tables, or hashes
were computed. The results are exact source identities, unconditional tail
bounds, conditional recovery rules, and a separately scoped conditional RH
bridge. The intermediate-parameter signed source estimate remains OPEN.
No full original bound or converse from RH is claimed.

Fix 0<alpha<1, a finite set S of distinct actual primes, s=|S|, and
R=product_{p in S}p, with empty product 1. For n>=2 let

 K(n)=-sum_{d|n}mu(d)(log d)^alpha,
 K_S(n)=D_S K(n),
 D_S=product_{p in S}(I-E_p), E_p f(n)=f(pn),
 B_S(X)=sum_{2<=n<=X}K_S(n),
 t_S(N)=[B_S(4N)-3B_S(2N)+2B_S(N)]/alpha, integer N>=2.

These are unrounded ordinary-mu sources. The 055 algebra gives

 K_S(n)=0 if gcd(n,R)>1;
 otherwise K_S(n)=I_alpha^-1 int_0^infinity
       R^-t product_{p|n}(1-p^-t)*t^(-alpha-1) dt>0,
 I_alpha=int_0^infinity(1-exp(-u))*u^(-alpha-1) du.               (1)

The external coprime mask is mandatory. The unit is excluded from B_S;
its actual mixed difference is -(log R)^alpha for nonempty S. Idempotence
and commuting Boolean identities apply to radical-invariant states, not
arbitrary functions. Repeated primes do not generate a new difference order.

A. Arbitrary rational parameter cutoff and complete tail
========================================================
Define the exact binary weight

 W(y)=floor(4y)-3floor(2y)+2floor(y), |W(y)|<=1,
 H_S(N,t)=sum_{d<=4N, gcd(d,R)=1}mu(d)d^-t
                           *sum_{e|R}mu(e)W(N/(de)),
 h_S(N,t)=R^-t H_S(N,t).

Finite divisor expansion and coprime cofactor counting prove

 t_S(N)=1/(alpha I_alpha) int_0^infinity
                              h_S(N,t)t^(-alpha-1) dt.          (2)

Equivalently h_S is the filter of R^-t product_{p|n}(1-p^-t), over n>=2
coprime to R. The n=1 correction cancels because 2-3+1=0. Formula (2)
includes every d, cofactor e, prime power, gcd mask, and floor endpoint.
Near zero its finite exact cancellations must be kept; an absolute infinite
Dirichlet series is not being used there.

For the empty S, d=1 contributes W(N)=0 at INTEGER N. For nonempty S,
d=1 need not vanish: its cofactor sum is bounded by 2^s, and the external
R^-t must be kept when integrating it. Dropping that factor before the d=1
integration would lose a uniform-in-alpha bound.

Let tau>0 be a fixed rational cutoff and

 M_tau=max(tau^-1,tau^-2),
 E_S(N;tau)=1/(alpha I_alpha) int_tau^infinity
                              h_S(N,t)t^(-alpha-1) dt.

For t>=tau, t^-alpha-1<=M_tau, uniformly 0<alpha<1. The elementary
normalizer inequality alpha I_alpha>=1-exp(-1)>1/2 implies
1/(alpha I_alpha)<2<=3. Also log 2>=1/2. For an individual denominator d,
integrating the retained exponential (Rd)^-t gives (Rd)^-tau/log(Rd).
Thus the exact mask/unit handling above gives the common valid bound

 |E_S(N;tau)|
 <=6 M_tau 2^s R^-tau sum_{1<=d<=4N}d^-tau.                   (3)

For empty S, the real sum starts at d=2; the displayed d=1 term is only an
enlargement AFTER its zero contribution has been treated. For nonempty S,
log(Rd)>=log2 pays the true d=1 term. There is no use of convergence of an
infinite mu series for tau<=1. The finite denominator horizon is essential.

Dropping R^-tau<=1 gives conservative R-independent rational constants:

 0<tau<1:
 |E_S|<=24 M_tau 2^s*(2-tau)/(1-tau)*N^(1-tau).                (4a)

 tau=1:
 |E_S|<=18*2^s*(1+log N).                                    (4b)

 tau>1:
 |E_S|<=6*2^s/(tau-1).                                      (4c)

Proof: sum_{d<=D}d^-tau<=1+int_1^D x^-tau dx. Below 1 this is at most
[(2-tau)/(1-tau)]D^(1-tau); use D=4N and 4^(1-tau)<=4. At 1, the sum
is at most 1+log(4N)<=3(1+log N). Above 1 it is at most
1+1/(tau-1)=tau/(tau-1), while M_tau=1/tau. All constants are rational
functions of rational tau and the integer 2^s, and are uniform in alpha.
The sharper factor R^-tau in (3) can be retained if its enclosure is licensed;
none is required for (4).

In particular the entire interval t>=1/2 has an unconditional square-root
bound

 |E_S(N;1/2)|<=288*2^s*sqrt N.                                (5)

For tau>1/2 its bound is smaller than sqrt N asymptotically; at 1 it is
logarithmic, and above 1 it is bounded. Therefore a square-root or cofinal
subpower source proof only has to solve the joined low-parameter integral
0<t<1/2. The constants in (5) are not the old selected constant C=2.
This is a paid reduction of the difficult parameter range, not that stronger
fixed-budget statement.

At noninteger N, W(N) may be nonzero, so the empty-set d=1 step in (3)
needs a separate treatment. The primary theorem here deliberately uses all
integers N>=2; the subsequent source bound can use the paid 053 jump transfer.

B. Recovery of the whole target and all energy crosses
======================================================
Write t_i=l_i+e_i, with l_i the 0<t<tau integral and e_i the tail above.
For fixed signed companion coefficients a_i and lambda>=0 put

 v_low=sum_i a_i l_i, e_v=sum_i a_i e_i,
 F=[t0^2+lambda*(sum_i a_i t_i)^2]/N.

The full exact expression is

 F=[l0^2+lambda v_low^2]/N
   +[2l0 e0+2lambda v_low e_v+e0^2+lambda e_v^2]/N.             (6)

A tail estimate controls e0 and e_v, not the sign of either cross. For any
fixed rational u>0 the positive quadratic identity gives

 F <=(1+u)F_low+(1+1/u)F_tail,
 F_low<=(1+u)F+(1+1/u)F_tail,
 F_tail=[e0^2+lambda e_v^2]/N.                                (7)

This is a valid recovery rule, with constants. At tau=1/2, (5) makes F_tail
uniformly bounded. Thus bounded or polylogarithmic normalized energy of the
full and low-core states is equivalent up to paid constants and additive
bounded forcing. Above 1/2, F_tail decays: for tau<1 its power is
N^(1-2tau); at tau=1 it is O(log(N)^2/N); above 1 it is O(1/N).
Neither direction of (7) preserves the SAME drift constant or automatically
transfers a candidate recurrence; all crosses in (6) remain part of that
separate proof task.

For the scalar target alone, a low-core bound
|l0(N)|<=A sqrt N*(1+log N)^eta, eta>=0,
at tau=1/2 implies the full bound with constant A+288. The same rule for a
different S uses 288*2^s. These are conditional implications from a specific
unpaid low-core bound. Conversely a proved full bound gives the same growth
class for its low core by subtracting the paid tail. No assumption about
unknown companion RH bounds enters this reduction.

C. Exact first Taylor row and finite-prime exceptions
====================================================
Let G_n(t)=product_{p|n}(1-p^-t). At t=0, G_n(0)=0 for n>=2. Its first
derivative is log p when n=p^e and zero when n has at least two distinct
prime factors. Therefore

 h_S(N,0)=0,
 h'_S(N,0)=J_S(N)
   =psi_S(4N)-3psi_S(2N)+2psi_S(N),                           (8)

where psi_S(x)=sum_{p^e<=x, p not in S}log p, with every e>=1. The derivative
of R^-t contributes nothing because the original product vanishes at zero.
These first Taylor rows do not depend on alpha; alpha enters the integral.

Write Psi(N)=psi(4N)-3psi(2N)+2psi(N), and

 V_p(N)=log p*[floor(log_p(4N))-3floor(log_p(2N))
                                        +2floor(log_p N)].

Then J_S=Psi-sum_{p in S}V_p exactly. Each dyadic shell (N,2N] or (2N,4N]
contains at most one p-power for every prime p>=2. Hence

 |V_p(N)|<=2 log p, uniformly all real N>=1,
 V_2(N)=-log2 EXACTLY, uniformly all real N>=1.                 (9)

The last identity uses floor(log_2(2N))=floor(log_2 N)+1. The p=2 column
is thus a known constant, not a generic fluctuating prime-power mode.
No primality distribution or RH estimate is used in (9).

For any fixed signed mixture a_S,

 sum_S a_S J_S
  =(sum_S a_S)Psi-sum_p A_p V_p,
 A_p=sum_{S containing p}a_S.                                 (10)

If sum_S a_S=0, the common ordinary-prime mode disappears and the remaining
first row has uniform bound 2 sum_p |A_p|log p. If every A_p also vanishes,
the first row is identically zero. A_p at p=2 contributes the explicit
constant +A_2 log2. A compiler can represent rows exactly as integer incidence
vectors (1,-1_{p in S}), plus the licensed constant-column simplification.
Exact nullspace computation then gives real uniform identities. A finite
sample nullspace is not an exact row certificate, and further relations
among unproved prime modes must not be inferred from data.

D. Remainder scope: jet cancellation is not fixed-cutoff integral control
========================================================================
For a fixed N the integrand is a finite analytic exponential sum. Taylor
expansion is exact locally, but its derivatives grow with log N and its
source horizon. On a fixed interval such as 0<t<1/2, t log N is not small.
Canceling the first row alone does not pay the higher-jet remainder.

There is a useful explicit, coarse unconditional remainder. Put L=log(4N).
For all t>=0,

 |G_n(t)-t Lambda(n)|<=t^2*(log n)^2/2, n>=2.

For a prime power this is the second-order exponential remainder; otherwise
choose two distinct prime factors, bound their product by t^2(log n)^2/4,
and keep the other factors at most 1. Also 1-R^-t<=t log R. The filter has
absolute coefficient count 4N across its two disjoint shells. Consequently

 |h_S(N,t)-t J_S(N)|
       <=2N*t^2*L*(L+2 log R).                               (11)

After normalized integration over 0<t<epsilon,

 |core_micro-J_S*epsilon^(1-alpha)/[(1-alpha)alpha I_alpha]|
 <=6N*L*(L+2 log R)*epsilon^(2-alpha)/(2-alpha).                (12)

For a zero-sum mixture, substitute the paid bounded row (10) and sum (11)
with absolute a_S. Taking epsilon_N=min(1/2,N^(-1/(2-alpha))) makes its
remainder only O(log(N)^2), with explicit finite-prime constants. This pays
a shrinking microscopic interval. The interval epsilon_N<t<1/2 remains
OPEN; a local nullspace must not be presented as control of that interval.
The common first row for the retained original target is still Psi. Dropping
it by changing to an auxiliary without a recovery theorem drops the target.

E. Boolean faces E_R: exact higher-jet source and microscopic bound
=================================================================
For a nonempty fixed squarefree prime product R, k=omega(R),

 E_R K(n)=K(nR)=sum_{S subset primes(R)}(-1)^|S|D_S K(n).        (13)

For k>=2 this is a complete first-row nullspace face; for k=1 its first
row is V_p. More generally its pointwise product G_(nR)(t) has a zero of
order at least k at t=0, because all k primes of R occur. Thus every Taylor
row below order k vanishes EXACTLY, not merely numerically. The compensating
Boolean face must be complete; omitting one subset changes these statements.

Let h_E(N,t) be the full same quadratic filter of G_(nR)(t), n>=2. Then

 |h_E(N,t)|<=4N*t^k*product_{p|R}log p,
 |normalized integral over 0<t<epsilon|
 <=12N*product_{p|R}log p*epsilon^(k-alpha)/(k-alpha).           (14)

Choosing epsilon_N=min(1/2,N^(-1/(k-alpha))) pays this microscopic integral
by a constant depending only on the fixed R and alpha. Its high parameter
tail t>=1/2 is paid by applying (4)-(5) to the complete finite combination
(13). Hence only epsilon_N<t<1/2 remains as this new source's open parameter
range. These are unconditional bounds; no source observations were used.

A weaker microscopic target is sufficient for square-root recovery and can
be compiled with exact rational exponents. For any fixed rational gamma>0,
take epsilon_N=min(1/2,N^-gamma). Since product_{p|R}log p<=R, (14) gives

 |normalized microscopic integral|
 <=[12R/(k-alpha)]*N^[1-gamma*(k-alpha)].                       (14g)

All constants and the exponent test are rational when alpha and gamma are
rational. The piece is paid at square-root scale exactly when
gamma*(k-alpha)>=1/2. Equality is allowed. A finite denominator limit for
gamma is a resource constraint on enumeration, not a stronger analytic
condition. The O(1) choice above corresponds to gamma=1/(k-alpha), but
the detector need not insist on that stronger paid component.

The complete Boolean-face tail has the explicit convenient bound
288*3^k*sqrt N, since sum_{S subset primes(R)}2^|S|=3^k. Therefore a genuine
all-integer bound for the middle integral, with constant C_middle and
growth sqrt N*(1+log N)^eta, eta>=0, yields the full bound with constant
12R/(k-alpha)+288*3^k+C_middle whenever the rational exponent test holds.
This triangle recovery retains the complete integrand before splitting;
for squared potentials the crosses still require (6)-(7). The NEW middle
inequality remains the unpaid source hypothesis, not an implication of the
near-zero exponent test or the high-tail certificate.

This E_R source is pointwise no larger than K for n>=2. That fact gives
no upper recovery of the original K from E_R: the insertion projection has
a nullspace. A separate conditional RH bridge for E_R is nevertheless
available, as proved next. It licenses a new RH-sensitive source goal, not
the original fixed-constant bound or a norm inverse.

F. Separately scoped conditional RH implication for fixed E_R
=============================================================
The only imported zeta background is the same meromorphic/Euler/zero/
reflection facts used in frozen 052:
[DLMF 25.2](https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.2),
[DLMF 25.4](https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.4),
[DLMF 25.10](https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.10).
The following singular-integral argument is derived here.

Fix a NONEMPTY finite R once and for all, k=omega(R)>=1. Define
A_R(x)=sum_{2<=n<=x}K(nR). For Re(s)>1 its complete Dirichlet transform is

 F_R(s)=I_alpha^-1 int_0^infinity
          [U_R(s,t)*zeta(s)/zeta(s+t)-G_R(t)]*t^(-alpha-1) dt,
 U_R(s,t)=product_{p|R}(1-p^-t)/(1-p^(-(s+t))),
 G_R(t)=product_{p|R}(1-p^-t).                                (15)

The local factor follows from the full p-adic orbit: n=p^e contributes
G_(nR) the inserted p factor for EVERY e>=0, replacing the original factor
(1-p^(-(s+t)))/(1-p^-s) by (1-p^-t)/(1-p^-s). The subtraction G_R is the
actual n=1 term, K(R), not zero. Absolute Fubini follows from K(nR)<=K(n)
for n>=2 and the finite inserted unit. This identity is initially Re(s)>1.

The real pole at 1 has positive residue
I_alpha^-1 int U_R(1,t)/zeta(1+t)*t^(-alpha-1)dt. It is finite at zero
and infinity; a fixed scale filter P with P(q)=0 removes this real pole.
No fitted residue is needed for the numerical arithmetic statistic.

If ordinary RH is false, choose rho with Re(rho)>1/2, nonzero imaginary
part, RIGHTMOST at its fixed height, as in 052. All ordinary denominator
zeta(rho+t), t>0, then avoid zeros. The factors 1-p^(-(s+t)) also avoid
zero along this ray, since Re(rho)>0. Compact separation plus Euler tails
provides the needed continuation tubes; no global rightmost zero is assumed.

Let rho have any finite multiplicity d>=1 and write
zeta(rho+z)=z^d g(z), g(0)!=0. Near delta=0, t=0,

 U_R(rho+delta,t)=t^k u(delta,t),
 u(0,0)=product_{p|R}log p/(1-p^-rho)!=0,
 zeta(rho+delta)/zeta(rho+delta+t)
     =[delta/(delta+t)]^d*g(delta)/g(delta+t).

The combined amplitude u*g(delta)/g(delta+t) is analytic near (0,0).
Put beta=k-alpha, so k-1<beta<k. The elementary singular integral is

 J_(d,beta)(delta)=delta^d int_0^epsilon
                             t^(beta-1)/(delta+t)^d dt.

If d>=k, substitution t=delta v immediately gives the noninteger term
delta^beta times the strictly positive finite integral
int_0^infinity v^(beta-1)(1+v)^-d dv, with remainder O(delta^k).

If d<k, subtract the integrable analytic terms l=0,...,k-d-1 inside the
integral. The remaining coefficient of delta^beta is

 B_(d,beta)=int_0^infinity v^(beta-1)*
 [(1+v)^-d-sum_{l=0}^{k-d-1}(-1)^l binom(d+l-1,l)v^(-d-l)]dv. (16)

At zero the worst power is v^-alpha; at infinity the remainder is
O(v^-alpha-1). Thus (16) is absolutely convergent. Its bracket has STRICT
sign (-1)^(k-d), by the Taylor remainder for (1+z)^-d at z=1/v. It is
therefore nonzero. No assumption of simple zeros enters this argument.
If desired its value is Gamma(beta)Gamma(d-beta)/Gamma(d), with negative
Gamma arguments interpreted solely via finitely many Gamma recurrences;
the positive/finite integral proof (16) already establishes the needed sign.

Analytic amplitude corrections do not erase that term. Expand the amplitude
in delta and t to finite order k. A monomial delta^a t^b with a+b>=1 shifts
the fractional exponent to beta+a+b>k, apart from analytic Taylor terms.
The t^k remainder has k uniformly integrable delta derivatives: its bound
after differentiation is a constant times t^(beta-1), which is integrable.
The delta^k remainder is O(delta^k). Hence, after subtracting an actual
polynomial of degree at most k-1, the complete local expansion is

 F_R(rho+delta)=Q_(k-1)(delta)+C_R,rho*delta^(k-alpha)
                                        +O(delta^k),           (17)
 C_R,rho=u(0,0)*B_(d,beta)/I_alpha!=0.

The part t>=epsilon is analytic near rho by the fixed-height ray argument;
the unit subtraction is a constant. They only alter Q and analytic higher
terms. There is NO additional factor (-1)^d: the local zero ratio is
delta^d/(delta+t)^d. The sign in the finite-part integral already accounts
for d<k.

Now suppose a fixed nonzero polynomial scale filter of A_R, with P(q)=0,
satisfies an all-integer square-root/polylog bound. The exact finite-head
Mellin identity and integer-to-real transfer from 053 apply because
K(nR)<=K(n)<=log(n)^alpha for n>=2. They make P(q^s)F_R(s)/s holomorphic
on Re(s)>1/2. At rho its analytic multiplier has finite order j>=0.
Subtract the analytic product with Q in (17); a nonzero term
delta^(k+j-alpha)+O(delta^(k+j)) remains. Holomorphy would force all Taylor
coefficients through k+j-1 to vanish, hence O(delta^(k+j)), a contradiction.
Thus this precise NEW source-bound premise implies RH. Annulus exclusion
of polynomial roots is again unnecessary; their integer orders cannot erase
a noninteger branch. The strict fixed range 0<alpha<1 is indispensable.

This implication neither proves the source premise nor its converse from RH.
It does not recover the original K target norm or its selected constant.
R must be fixed: a scale-dependent inserted prime set is not a single
Dirichlet source and is not licensed by this continuation proof.
