1. Exact source and physical versus periodic prefix

Fix an INTEGER U>=1. A(d)=mu(d)1_(d<=U), c=sum A(d)/d. Dirichlet convolution
is denoted *. The actual inverse facts give |c|<=2. Define
 E=delta-One*A+c One, C=1+(M(U)-c)/2, e=E-C delta.
For integers n>=1, the COMPLETE prefix of e is
 Q^+(n)=Q(n)=sum_(a<=U)A(a)B_a(n),
 B_a(n)={n/a}-(a-1)/(2a).
The physical Q^+(0)=0 differs from the periodic Q(0)=1-C. B_1=0.
Every B_a is discretely centered on an a-period. Hence |Q(n)|<=U/2
on ALL integers, including zero; its period divides lcm(1,...,U).
This enormous period is never needed as a computational object.

For a finite real or complex h supported in 1..D put
 F_h(n)=sum_(d<=D)h(d)Q(floor(n/d)), all integer n>=0.
The actual convolution prefix is EXACTLY
 P_(h*e)(n)=F_h(n)-(1-C)sum_(d>n)h(d).                 (1.1)
The correction is supported at n<D. No argument at zero, unit, previous
prefix, or shorthead can be discarded. In particular n>=D is a sufficient
certificate for equality with the periodic expression, not an assumption
that can be inferred from a long-looking numerical block.

2. Floor elimination and coefficient collection BEFORE magnitudes

For positive integers a,d and integer n>=0,
 {floor(n/d)/a}={n/(ad)}-{n/d}/a,
 B_a(floor(n/d))=B_(ad)(n)-B_d(n)/a.                 (2.1)
Write n=ad k+d j+r, 0<=j<a,0<=r<d, to verify both fractional parts;
the centering constants give the second equality exactly.
Consequently
 F_h(n)=sum_(m<=UD)v(m)B_m(n), v=h*A-c h=-h*T,
 T=c delta-A.                                       (2.2)
All floor-lift frequency collisions have become ordinary signed coefficient
collection. The formula is valid at n=0 as well. It does NOT remove (1.1).
Its complete harmonic null is
 sum_m v(m)/m=hat(h)(1)[hat(A)(1)-c]=0.               (2.3)
Here hat(h)(1)=sum h(d)/d, not the unit coefficient h(1).

For h=A^p, v=A^(p+1)-c A^p, maximum denominator H=U^(p+1).
In particular h=A^2 gives v=A^3-cA^2 and H=U^3. The compiler can derive
the power and support from convolution nodes rather than nominate U^3.
Arbitrary finite signed h, weighted factors, and finite linear combinations
use the same identity. Collect the entire h or v before applying triangle.

3. Exact signed covariance: conductor and gcd descriptions agree

For a common-period average denoted by < >,
 <B_m B_l>=(gcd(m,l)^2-1)/(12ml).                    (3.1)
One proof uses the discrete Fourier coefficients
 -1/[m(1-exp(-2pi i j/m))], 1<=j<m. Only equal reduced frequencies
survive averaging. The reduced denominator r contributes J_2(r)/12,
where J_2(r)=r^2 product_(p|r)(1-p^(-2)); summing r|gcd(m,l), r>1,
uses sum_(r|g)J_2(r)=g^2. This also proves the identity for m or l=1.
Equivalently it follows by averaging integer residues on the common period.

For finite v,w, the exact covariance is
 (1/12)[sum_(m,l)v(m)conj(w(l))gcd(m,l)^2/(ml)
              -hat(v)(1)conj(hat(w)(1))].           (3.2)
The subtraction is NOT zero for an arbitrary coefficient state. For (2.2)
it vanishes by the certified harmonic null. Positivity of the remaining
GCD Gram does not make its individual signed cross terms nonnegative.

Let K_r(v)=sum_(k<=floor(H/r))v(rk)/k. Orthogonality also gives
 Var(F_h)=sum_(r=2)^H |K_r(v)|^2 J_2(r)/(12r^2).      (3.3)
This is an exact finite arithmetic Gram, retaining every prime/composite,
gcd and repeated-valuation sector. Its bilinear version, or a block matrix
for v_1,...,v_j, preserves every cross between generated proof branches.
Different supports may be embedded in their common maximum H. Equal
frequency coefficients must be collected before any squared magnitude.

4. Explicit divisor-envelope provider; no n^epsilon constant

For integer k>=1 let d_k be the ordered k-fold divisor function. Two
elementary rules are
 d_k(ab)<=d_k(a)d_k(b), d_k(n)^2<=d_(k^2)(n).         (4.1)
Both reduce to prime powers. With f_k(e)=binom(e+k-1,k-1), the ratios
f_k(e+1)/f_k(e)=(e+k)/(e+1) decrease, proving submultiplicativity.
For the square inequality compare consecutive ratios:
 (e+k)^2<=(e+1)(e+k^2), whose difference is e(k-1)^2>=0;
both sides start at1. Multiplying over primes proves (4.1).
Also sum_(n<=x)d_k(n)<=x(1+ln x)^(k-1), x>=1, by eliminating one
factor in the ordered product count and using harmonic sums for the rest.

Define R_j=j! sum_(i=0)^j 1/i!, an INTEGER; R_0=1,R_6=1957.
For integer j>=0 and H>=1, Stieltjes summation gives
 sum_(r<=H)d_k(r)(1+ln(H/r))^j
 <=R_j H(1+ln H)^(k-1).                              (4.2)
Indeed, writing D_k(t)<=t(1+ln t)^(k-1), the terminal contribution is
H(1+ln H)^(k-1). The remaining positive integral is bounded by the same
quantity times j int_0^infinity e^(-y)(1+y)^(j-1)dy=R_j-1.
This proof includes r=1,H=1, all floors, and nonintegral integration limits.

If |v(n)|<=C0 d_k(n) and support<=H, then
 |K_r(v)|<=C0 d_k(r)(1+ln(H/r))^k.
Applying (4.1),(4.2) to (3.3) yields the reusable rule
 Var <=[C0^2 R_(2k)/12] H(1+ln H)^(k^2-1).           (4.3)
An empty spectrum, in particular H=1, has variance0; the positive bound
remains safe. C0 can itself be an explicitly paid cutoff/log envelope.

Actual h=A^2 has |v|<=d_3+2d_2<=3d_3. Thus
 Var(F_(A^2))<=(5871/4)U^3(1+3ln U)^8.              (4.4)
The constant5871/4 belongs to THIS logarithm. Replacing it by (1+ln U)^8
requires the extra factor3^8. More generally h=A^p uses k=p+1,C0=3
(d_0 means delta if p=0). This is a proved two-U-power improvement over
the old variance bound U^5/2, though its logarithmic constant is larger.

For ANY weights |w|<=1, c=hat(A)(1) only satisfies |c|<=1+ln U, and the
same bound uses C0=2(1+ln U). It has the SAME U power with two extra logs.
The actual complete-inverse fact |c|<=2 saves logs; it does not create the
power improvement. The improvement comes from exact floor/cofactor
collection and cutoff-sensitive summation, not new Mobius cancellation.

5. Genuine coefficient-l2 providers and their limitations

Cofactor Cauchy in (3.3), using sum k^(-2)<=2, gives
 Var <=(1/6)sum_(n<=H)d_2(n)|v(n)|^2.                (5.1)
All input coefficients may remain exact signed source coefficients until
this last square. This is a real alternative to ||h||_1^2. It is not a
uniform constant times ||h||_2^2: the merge v=h*A-c h still has a cost.
For example the safe weighted Dirichlet-Young estimate is
 ||v||_(l2(d2))<=[sum_(a<=U)|A(a)|sqrt(d2(a))+|c|]
                         ||h||_(l2(d2)).            (5.2)
Each dilation obeys d2(an)<=d2(a)d2(n), and Minkowski proves the formula.
For actual source the bracket is <=U sqrt(1+ln U)+2. Applying this to
h=A^2 is usually worse than (4.4); the planner should compare costs.

An actual-source counterexample to a U-independent constant times ||h||2^2
is h=delta. For every squarefree r in (U/2,U], K_r(A)=mu(r); each such
conductor contributes >=1/24 to Var(Q). There are at least
U/8-sqrt U-1 such squarefree integers: count the interval and subtract all
multiples of k^2, k>=2, using sum_(k>=2)k^(-2)<=3/4. For U>=256 this
count is >=U/32. Hence Var(Q)>=U/768 whereas ||delta||2^2=1. The cutoff
or the merged-coefficient cost cannot simply disappear in an l2 lift.

An unweighted l2 rule is possible with an explicit loose logarithmic cost:
 Var <=[3^28/12](1+ln H)^2 sum_(n<=H)|v(n)|^2,
 H>=2.                                               (5.3)
Here is a self-contained proof, with no external GCD-sum import. The positive
GCD kernel in (3.2) equals product_p p^(-|v_p(m)-v_p(l)|). The single-prime
Toeplitz row sum is <=(1+1/p)/(1-1/p). A finite prime-tensor Schur bound
therefore gives operator norm <=product_(p<=H)(1-1/p)^(-2).

To bound that product elementarily, let vartheta(x)=sum_(p<=x)ln p.
Every prime n<p<=2n divides binom(2n,n), so dyadic summation gives
vartheta(x)<=4 ln2*x for x>=2. Partial summation implies
sum_(p<=H)(ln p)/p <=4 ln2*(1+ln H). Set s=1+1/ln H. The logarithmic
Euler-factor difference at1 and s is bounded by
2 sum_(p<=H)(p^(-1)-p^(-s))
 <=(2/ln H)sum_(p<=H)(ln p)/p <=8 ln2+8<14.
The factors at s multiply to at most zeta(s)<=1+ln H, by positive geometric
expansion and the integral test. Thus product_(p<=H)(1-1/p)^(-1)
 <=e^14(1+ln H)<3^14(1+ln H), proving (5.3).
The harmonic subtraction in (3.2) can only decrease the quadratic, so this
provider is safe even without null; equality with the positive kernel still
requires null. Infinite exponent boxes are only a proof device, not an
allocation or actual-source computation.

Actual mu enters the exact v, its support, complete harmonic null and

6. Finite windows, head recovery and proof costs

All reduced frequencies now have denominator<=H. Their circular separation
is at least H^(-2). The elementary geometric-kernel row bound from Review030
therefore proves, for ANY integer window of length L>=1,
 sum_window |F_h(n)|^2
 <=[L+H^2(1+2ln H)] Var(F_h).                         (6.1)
The proof counts neighbors at distances j/H^2 on the two circle directions
and sums the harmonic bound on the kernel. It does not assume a complete
period or random sample. The period/GCD providers compete with direct
pointwise |F_h|<=(U/2)||h||_1. For h=A^2 the latter gives U^6 L/4.

Combining (4.4),(6.1) has costs U^3 L times log^8 and U^9 times log^9,
rather than the old U^5 L and U^11 log costs. Both comparisons retain
the physical shorthead (1.1). If a window includes n<D, a separate safe
correction bound is |1-C| ||h||_1<=(3U/2)||h||_1 and its squared sum
is <=(9/4)U^2||h||_1^2 min(L,D). Triangle can add twice each squared cost,
or the exact head cross can remain in the proof target. For h=A^2 and
physical scales X with X>=U^2 the correction is identically absent.

The spacing term is a proved upper cost, NOT a lower bound for the actual
window norm. A failure to pay its exponent is a proof-language failure;
it is not a counterexample to arithmetic cancellation or RH.

7. Complete hyperbola geometries for e^2 and e^3

For sequences f_i with prefixes P_i, integer cutoffs t_i>=1 and n>=0,
if product_i(t_i+1)>n, inclusion-exclusion covers every tuple with product
<=n by at least one coordinate <=t_i. Explicitly the complete prefix of
f_1*...*f_j equals the sum over nonempty coordinate sets S, coefficient
(-1)^(|S|+1), of
 sum_(a_i<=t_i,i in S) product_(i in S)f_i(a_i)
       P_(product of remaining f_i)(floor(n/product_(i in S)a_i)).        (7.1)
The empty remaining convolution is delta, whose prefix is1_(argument>=1).
Thus a fully finite intersection STILL has its product<=n indicator unless
product t_i<=n. This is the complete reciprocal region, with no tail lost.

For t=floor(sqrt n), n>=1,
 P_(e^2)(n)=2 sum_(a<=t)e(a)Q^+(floor(n/a))-Q^+(t)^2. (7.2)
For t=floor(n^(1/3)),
 P_(e^3)(n)=3 sum_(a<=t)e(a)P_(e^2)(floor(n/a))
            -3 sum_(a,b<=t)e(a)e(b)Q^+(floor(n/(ab)))
            +Q^+(t)^3.                              (7.3)
The squares/cube here are rectangle intersections of prefixes. They are NOT
an identity e^3(n)=Q(n)^3, nor a pointwise replacement of convolution.
At n=0 use the literal empty physical prefix. For unequal thresholds retain
the literal finite-intersection indicator in (7.1).

For machine search on a WHOLE parent/child range n<=N, fixed thresholds
are preferable: prove support gaps, not a different period at every n.
Let e_t=e 1_[1,t]. If (r+1)^2>N, coefficientwise on 1..N,
 e^2=2 e_r*e-e_r^2.
If also (t+1)^3>N, substitution in the complete cube cover gives
 e^3=(6 e_t*e_r-3 e_t^2)*e + e_t^3-3 e_t*e_r^2.     (7.4)
This equality is exact on EVERY coefficient <=N, hence on every prefix
<=N, and after convolution by any positive-support state for coefficients
<=N. The general asymmetric pair rule is
 e^2=(e_r+e_s)*e-e_r*e_s if (r+1)(s+1)>N.             (7.5)
Combining (7.1),(7.5) provides selectable split geometries with exact finite
supports. All thresholds use exact integer roots and strict support tests.
An n-dependent threshold is allowed in the literal arithmetic identity,
but its whole-window covariance is a NEW target, not (6.1) for fixed v.

For A^2*e^3 define, using (7.4),
 h=A^2*(6 e_t*e_r-3 e_t^2),

Then P_(A^2*e^3)(n)=sum_d h(d)Q^+(floor(n/d))+P_k(n), n<=N. (7.6)
The first term floor-collects to v=h*A-c h with head (1.1). The second is

9. Where actual Mobius interactions remain, and the next complete leaf

For squarefree n, A^j(n)=mu(n) N_j(n;U), where N_j counts ordered j-factor
allocations of the prime factors with each factor<=U. All allocations have
the SAME mu(n) sign. Thus within-product prime permutations do not cancel
the leading A^3 branch. Repeated prime valuations are at most j in A^j
and have their complete signed binomial/allocation contributions; none is
discarded by the floor identity. For v=A^3-cA^2,
 v(n)=mu(n)[N_3(n;U)-c N_2(n;U)] on squarefree n.
This does allow exact branch cancellation at a fixed product, but generic
divisor counting does not prove it quantitatively. The conductor sum mixes
DIFFERENT products r k, so its signed offdiagonal correlations are a genuine
arithmetic opportunity not already decided by parity in one allocation fiber.

There is also a source-specific obstruction to lowering the U POWER of the
FULL-PERIOD norm for h=A^2. For U>=5 let P_U be the primes in (U/2,U]. For
three distinct members p1,p2,p3 put r=p1p2p3. Each of the three short A
factors must contain exactly one of these primes: two such primes in one
factor exceed U. Thus A^3(r)=-6, A^2(r)=0. No additional integer k>=2 can
be absorbed by any of the three already occupied factors, so v(rk)=0 for
every k>=2. Therefore K_r(v)=-6 EXACTLY. Different unordered triples have
different r. Since J2(r)/r^2>=1/zeta(2)>=1/2, (3.3) proves the all-U rule
 Var(F_(A^2)) >=(3/2) binom(|P_U|,3).                (9.1)
This exact finite prime-count lower certificate requires no asymptotic input.

If an explicit prime-number-theorem import is permitted, |P_U|~U/(2ln U)
and hence (9.1) is Omega(U^3/(ln U)^3). One primary proof/formalization
reference for the imported statement pi(x)~x/ln x is Mario Carneiro,
