1. Squarefree and gcd reduction, including shared linear terms
----------------------------------------------------------------
For each repair index j the063 factorization gives

 c_j(r)=sum_(m<=H)L_(j,m)(r)mu(m)
       -sum_(m<=H,ell<=K)B_(j,m,ell)(r)mu(m)mu(ell), (1)

with exact source-free affine coefficients in r. Both occurrences of mu
are the SAME arithmetic sequence. The matrices retain original floor
horizons, short fills, units and all source masks. Here
K<=floor(D/(H+1)), D=floor(4N/p).

If an integer input is nonsquarefree, its mu factor is zero. Those
monomials can be deleted by exact integer factorization, not by a sign
guess. For squarefree m,ell, put g=gcd(m,ell). Then

 mu(m)mu(ell)=mu(m*ell/g^2).                          (2)

The index on the right is squarefree. More generally the valid formula
is mu(m)^2 mu(ell)^2 mu(m*ell/g^2); omitting the squarefree indicators
is wrong. For example equal nonsquarefree inputs would otherwise create
a spurious unit. In prime notation the key of a surviving product is the
SYMMETRIC DIFFERENCE of the two prime sets, not their union or product.
Shared primes cancel their sign squared only after both inputs pass the
squarefree gate. Repeated valuations and overlap sectors are thereby
handled exactly, rather than erased.

Linear mu terms use their squarefree prime key, and mu(1)=1 has the empty
key. A squarefree diagonal m=ell also has the empty key. These constants,
linear aliases, and the signed quadratic contribution must be coalesced
together BEFORE absolute values. Coalescing only within the quadratic
part can miss the primary linear-minus-quadratic cancellation.

Let P_B denote all actual primes occurring in query indices through
B=max(H,K). After exact reduction, write

 c_j(r)=sum_(F subset P_B)a_(j,F)(r)*prod_(v in F)x_v, (3)

where the actual assignment is x_v=-1 for every actual prime. There are
only finitely many nonzero keys. All coefficients remain affine in r.
Equality of (3) with (1) is certified by squarefree factorization, prime
set comparison and exact rational summation; it does not require an
observed source prefix. The product of the certified key primes must
equal m*ell/g^2, and every omitted term must have a proved zero factor.

The reduction is also valid for formal x_v in{-1,1}. This broader class
is useful to prove envelopes; it must not be confused with a claim that
actual Mobius prime signs are random or unspecified.

2. Conditioning is an exact signed projection and an L1 contraction
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Choose an explicitly specified set S of actual primes. Extra primes not
in P_B have no effect. Substituting x_v=-1 for v in S gives

 b_(j,U)(r)=sum_(F: F\\S=U)(-1)^|F intersect S|a_(j,F)(r),
 c_j(r)=sum_(U subset(P_B\\S))b_(j,U)(r)*prod_(v in U)x_v. (4)

The complete positive price

 E_(j,S)(r)=sum_U |b_(j,U)(r)|                       (5)

bounds |c_j(r)| at the actual remaining signs. Thus sum_j E_(j,S)
bounds the entire repair L1, with every unit and channel present. The
same prime variable is used at every j; replacing it by independent
row-specific signs is only a weaker optional relaxation.

If S is enlarged, (5) cannot increase: new coefficients are signed sums
of old ones, and triangle inequality proves the contraction. This is a
theorem about SYMBOLIC coefficient L1, not a theorem about its dependence
on N. It may strictly decrease or remain unchanged. Keeping only the
signed coefficient sum rather than the sum of absolute combined
coefficients would be invalid unless every remaining prime is fixed.

For each fixed S and N,y, E_(j,S)(r) is convex in r. Since r=(p/q)^t
lies in[p/q,1] for 0<t<1, exact endpoint prices at p/q and1 bound the
whole t interval. This is not two sampled-t verification. Complete
endpoint coefficient lists must be interpolated and source-bound, and
the same declared conditioning rule must apply throughout that interval.
If independent endpoint lists are used, their union and every added
horizon must be retained as in062. Different thresholds or different
fill selection across N are allowed only as an explicit fixed algorithm,
not as an unreported global polynomial winner.

Squareful rows of the ORIGINAL source and registered-prime masks remain
subject to their original whole-certificate admission. The absence of a
formal monomial after squarefree reduction does not license dropping a
nonzero original row, tail, or residual. The old source binding and the
new symbolic price have distinct roles.

3. Query exponents and a useful large-prime degree gate
-------------------------------------------------------
For H=floor(N^eta), rational eta in[0,1], the063 proof gives

 B<=max(N^eta,(4/p)N^(1-eta))=O(N^max(eta,1-eta)).   (6)

The minimal query exponent is1/2 at eta1/2. Every key prime is <=B, but
a composite key can reach HK<D=O(N) when a nonzero long part exists.
Reducing to that key does not require a new current-scale mu table if
its factorization is inherited and independently checked. Conversely a
checker that explicitly queries mu at the key must account for that
larger query. The composite-size and prime-factor-size claims must not
be interchanged.

A conditioning rule can use all actual primes <=floor(B^xi), optionally
unioned with registered primes, for any budgeted nonnegative rational
xi. The algebra needs no denominator whitelist. Exact floors/power
thresholds, primality and integer/bit capacities are still charged; a
large exponent or denominator is not a free computation. For xi>=1
all relevant primes are fixed. The resulting envelope equals the exact
finite value |c_j|, rather than proving an all-scale growth theorem.

There is a strong source-free simplification at xi>=1/2. Every query
integer m<=B has at most ONE prime factor exceeding floor(sqrt B): two
such primes would have product>B. Thus after conditioning those small
primes, each mu(m) is either zero, an actual known constant, or a known
constant times one remaining large-prime variable. Every coefficient
(1) becomes a polynomial of degree at most TWO in these remaining
large-prime variables. Its diagonal terms are again constants. This is
a valid graph/tensor simplification, not an assumed large-prime mean.

More generally, if the unconditioned primes all exceed a cutoff C and
the surviving key has d primes, then (C+1)^d<=key. This exact necessary
inequality provides a degree gate for arbitrary rational cutoffs. One
must keep constants and strict/floor boundaries rather than infer a
degree only from approximate logarithmic exponents.

4. What additional universal norms can and cannot certify
---------------------------------------------------------
The Fourier L1 price (5) is a universally valid bound over every
remaining sign assignment. Coalescing improves it; it need not equal
the optimal worst-case value because overlapping keys cannot all choose
their signs independently. Further exact factorization, cancellation or
a certified matrix norm can sometimes improve that positive route.

For example, in the large-prime degree-two case write a row as
b+v^T x+x^T A x, putting diagonal constants into b. Since ||x||_2^2=n,
a certified operator-norm bound gives
|c_j|<=|b|+sqrt(n)||v||_2+n||A||_op. This is a sufficient alternative
envelope, not a free rate estimate for its actual coefficient tensors.
The source-free Frobenius bound is another conservative choice.

There are exact finite lower guards for any proposed free-sign envelope.
Orthogonality of distinct parity characters, averaged over all formal
sign assignments, gives average |c_j|^2=sum_U b_(j,U)^2. Therefore a
uniform worst-case upper bound on a row cannot be smaller than that
coefficient L2 norm. Similarly a worst-case upper bound on sum_j|c_j|
cannot be smaller than sqrt(sum_(j,U)b_(j,U)^2). These averages are
finite algebraic identities, not probabilistic assumptions about mu.
They can falsify an envelope argument even though they need not refute
the actual Mobius bound.

When all primes are conditioned, evaluation becomes exact finite
arithmetic in the smaller indices. The outstanding theorem is still
the growth of sum_j|c_j| for every N and required prefix. A table of
such evaluations, however long, is not its proof. Partial conditioning
instead seeks a stronger WORST-CASE theorem; loss of actual arithmetic
relations can make that stronger theorem false.

5. A proved countermodel for a scoped raw-indicator relaxation
-------------------------------------------------------------
This example rigorously shows a potential loss; it does NOT refute the
joined W/core-fill family, a growing conditioning rule, or RH. Take the
RAW repair of the single indicator 1[d<=T], H=floor(sqrt T),
K=floor(T/(H+1)). For sufficiently large integer T, its exact factored
coefficient at floor horizon1 is the following polynomial in small
mu inputs, directly from063 inverse (1):

 c_1=sum_(m<=H)mu(m)-sum_(m<=floor(H/2))mu(m)
     -sum_(m<=H,ell<=K)mu(m)mu(ell)floor(T/(m ell))
     +sum_(m<=floor(H/2),ell<=K)mu(m)mu(ell)
                                      floor(T/(2m ell)). (7)

At the actual assignment this is M_0(T)-M_0(floor(T/2)). Fix ANY finite
set S of conditioned primes, and give every other prime sign +1. The
corresponding allowed squarefree parity values are

 chi_S(n)=mu(n)^2*(-1)^#{v in S: v divides n}.        (8)

This assignment respects the squarefree masks, all gcd/parity reductions,
unit, and the stated conditioning. It need not satisfy the actual
mu*One=delta source law. That is precisely a relation the free-sign
envelope has discarded; the envelope must still bound this evaluation
of its polynomial, not only actual source-bound completions.

Here is an elementary proof of its harmonic growth, with no prime-gap,
RH or distribution premise. Squarefree counting satisfies

 sum_(n<=x)mu(n)^2=c_0*x+O(sqrt x),
 c_0=sum_(d>=1)mu(d)/d^2>0.

Expand mu(n)^2=sum_(d^2|n)mu(d), count multiples, and bound the floor
errors and absolute reciprocal-square tail to prove the formula. The
explicit bound c_0>=1-sum_(d>=2)d^-2>=1/4 suffices; the last sum is
at most1/4+integral_2^infinity u^-2 du=3/4.

For v in S let b_S(v^e)=2*(-1)^e for e>=1, with local constant1;
outside S its higher local coefficients are zero. The exact local
product (1+z)*(1-z)/(1+z)=1-z proves chi_S=mu^2*b_S,
where mu^2 here denotes the POINTWISE squarefree indicator sequence,
not the Dirichlet convolution mu*mu.
Moreover sum_n|b_S(n)|/sqrt n is a finite product of convergent
geometric series, since S is fixed and finite. Summing this convolution
and extending the main coefficient tail therefore gives

 sum_(n<=x)chi_S(n)=c_S*x+O_S(sqrt x),
 c_S=c_0*product_(v in S)(v-1)/(v+1)>0.             (9)

Partial summation yields
A_S(x)=sum_(n<=x)chi_S(n)/n=c_S*log x+O_S(1).
The density error integrates absolutely against x^-2. The constants
here are not asserted uniform when S grows with N.

Evaluate (7) at (8). Each floor equals its argument plus an error
of absolute value<=1; there are at most HK and floor(H/2)K terms.
The linear part is O(H), and hence

 c_1(chi_S)=-T*[A_S(H)-A_S(floor(H/2))/2]*A_S(K)+O(T)
          =-(c_S^2/8)*T*(log T)^2+O_S(T log T).     (10)

Thus the conditioned parity-L1 envelope for this raw-indicator row is
at least a positive constant times T log^2 T eventually, for EVERY
fixed finite S. It cannot have a square-root/polylog uniform rate.
This is a rigorous obstruction to that particular stronger proof
route, not a lower bound for the actual Mobius coefficient.

The example also explains why smaller queried indices are insufficient:
all inputs in (7) are O(sqrt T), yet current-scale weights make the
relaxed quadratic cost large. Actual mu cancellation has been replaced
by a much larger squarefree multiplicative-sign class. None of this
licenses a conclusion for an uncancelled subblock of the real joined
coefficient: other linear/quadratic pieces may cancel it. A gate for
an actual candidate must evaluate the COMPLETE polynomial at its
counterassignment, including source masks, signed channels and unit.

Growing S is a separate case. In (9) c_S may shrink and the error
constants depend on S; inserting S(N) into (10) without controlling
both is invalid. No obstruction for every growing conditioning scheme
is established here. Fully conditioned evaluation also falls outside
this fixed-S countermodel.

6. Concrete next arithmetic leaf and admission guards
------------------------------------------------------
The next uniform premise is the actual conditionally coalesced budget

 max_(r=p/q,1) sum_(j,U)|b_(j,U)(r)|
                 <=C_kappa*N^(1/2+kappa)*polylog N,

for EVERY required integer N and prefix y, with a fixed declared fill,
conditioning and rearrangement algorithm. Add the063 paid generic
long coefficient mass, then the062 full-prefix/core composition. A
uniform theorem for every positive kappa would pay the original bridge
in its licensed endpoint scope. This premise remains OPEN.

Useful detector outputs include exact tensor provenance, factor/key
proofs, every zero/constant alias, the conditioning projection, remaining
degree, endpoint price, and a complete counterassignment if an envelope
claim fails. Any new spectral, factorized or telescoping envelope must
be accompanied by a proved coefficient-rate rule; the finite norm alone
does not establish that rate. Nonlinear winner selection is recorded
separately from one fixed branch's polynomial formula.

If a finite-prime relaxation is too strong, a genuine next route must
retain further actual source laws: complete divisor convolution, shared
cofactor/prime-entry constraints, or a proved signed correlation with
all exits. Assuming a Mertens/RH bound on the resulting quadratic
companion just restates the unpaid source leaf. Conversely proving a
uniform sign-relaxed envelope would be legitimate substantive progress
if it actually succeeds; this review does not rule it out for the
joined family.
